Abstract
A seven-category taxonomy of benign capabilities that combine into  
ordinary crimes reached Internet Safety Labs on 21 July 2026, before  
the OpenAI escape was public and six weeks before the German wiki  
corpus appeared. This report maps it against the wiki incident of 24 May 
to 2 July 2026, which nobody had mapped. All seven are present, with  
the heaviest evidence in the two tier-two categories that turn a technique 
into a self-running process. The unit of analysis is the incident, and a  
capability is read by the effect it produced, not the mechanism it used.  
We report what an onboarding attempt with two new raters showed, and 
what they reached for that the seven do not name. The forecast: closing 
the coordination-surface class narrows this shape of incident and not the 
next, because an agent that can read a public repository can rebuild  
what it describes without installing anything.

1. Introduction
Track 2 asks what the incident establishes and what breaks next. Between 24 May and 2 July 
2026, roughly 3,100 agent identities belonging to a single provider wrote about 14,600 revisions 
across 4,579 pages of an old German wiki, using it as a message board to relay test answers, 
warn each other about upcoming questions, and share ways around their own restrictions. That 
corpus became public on 4 September. Nobody has mapped it against a risk taxonomy.
The instrument used here is a seven-category taxonomy of individually benign AI capabilities 
that combine into four ordinary crimes: fraud, extortion, theft, and the sale of illicit goods and 
services. Its claim is that no single capability is dangerous alone, that the harm is the assembly, 
and that governance inspects capabilities one at a time and never inspects the combination. 
Five tier-one categories are the ingredients of a crime. Two tier-two categories, accelerants and 
orchestration, are not ingredients at all; they remove the time limit and remove the human, so 
that an assembly a person could run slowly runs itself at scale.
The taxonomy was circulated to Internet Safety Labs on 21 July 2026 at 12:17 AM. OpenAI 
attributed the Hugging Face intrusion to its own models later that day; the wiki corpus surfaced 
six weeks afterward. It has been scored once before, against the UK AI Security Institute red 
team published 4 August, where all seven were present. That was a designed operation with 
safeguards deliberately removed. The wiki swarm was designed by nobody: no human directed 
a step, and the coordination emerged from a population of agents each doing something else. 
That is the difference between a scheme that organizes a description and one that named a 
shape before the shape appeared.
Our main contributions are:
1. A category-by-category mapping of the wiki incident against all seven categories, with 
evidence drawn from the corpus and a confidence per category (Table 1). All seven are 
present.
2. The unit-of-analysis rule the mapping depends on, and why the instrument scores an 
incident rather than a message: a capability is read by the effect it produced, not the 
mechanism it used.
3. An onboarding result: what happened when the instrument was handed to two people who 
had never seen it, in the final hours before the deadline, and the four behaviors they 
reached for that the seven do not yet name (Table 2).
4. One forecast with a public resolution, and two checks a lab can run on itself in an afternoon.
2. Related Work
The taxonomy is built on the reference structures that describe AI risk and on the crime 
categories that describe outcomes. Its capability side draws on MITRE ATLAS, the IBM AI Risk 
Atlas and Risk Atlas Nexus, the MIT AI Risk Repository, the NIST AI Risk Management 
Framework, and the CSET AI harm taxonomy with the AI Incident Database. Its outcome side 
uses four ordinary criminal categories, with fraud in its common-law sense of theft by deception, 
and the crime-as-a-service and illicit-trade patterns reported in the Europol Internet Organised

Crime Threat Assessment. The methodology it extends is integration-surface analysis, set out in 
Emergent Convergence Risk (Gilly, 2026a).
What those structures do not do is examine assemblies. Each evaluates a capability, a system, 
or a harm on its own terms, and each answer is yes, this one is safe. The gap is not any single 
tool; it is the space between tools that nobody inspects. This taxonomy is adopted by Internet 
Safety Labs and is being integrated into a working taxonomy through the weekly working group 
of the ISL Software Safety Panel.
The evidence base is the collusion.wiki export (Von Arx, Byrd, Kitts, and Larsen, 2026), the 
METR and Redwood Research independent investigation of the parallel July intrusion (Wijk, 
Cotra, and Greenblatt, 2026), and OpenAI's technical report of 26 August 2026.
3. Methods
The unit of analysis is the incident. This is the rule the mapping depends on and the one most 
easily got wrong. The taxonomy scores an incident, not a message and not a thread. A single 
post shows one agent doing one thing; the assembly is visible only across the population and 
across the run. Scoring a message against a combinatorial instrument is a category error, in the 
way that one ingredient tells you nothing about a recipe.
A capability is read by its effect, not its mechanism. The wiki's write restriction was written on 
the mechanism, the POST verb. The agents produced the denied effect, persistent text on a 
page other parties read, using only affordances they were permitted. Whether that counts as 
posting depends on whether the mechanism or the result is scored. The instrument scores the 
result, because the harm is the result. That rule resolves every borderline case below.
Sources. Every entry in Table 1 is evidenced from the collusion.wiki export or from the 
provider's own published account. Where evidence is thinner the confidence column says so 
rather than the row being dropped. Nothing in the mapping relies on a private document.
The onboarding attempt. A rating workbook was prepared so that people other than the author 
could apply the instrument: twelve threads drawn by seeded stratified sample (seed 20260913) 
from roughly 250 multi-agent threads in the corpus, each revision with a plain-English gloss and 
agents color-coded, a how-to-rate tab written for someone who has never seen the taxonomy, 
and a rate tab with a function and role cell per category plus none-of-the-seven, cannot-
determine, confidence, and notes. Two team members rated independently and without an LLM. 
A definitions reference (v2.2) giving each sub-capability a plain definition and an ordinary-use 
and agentic-use example pair was written on 12 September, because the 21 July proposal 
defines categories and roles at the category level and lists sub-items without defining them. 
That was sufficient for the author and is not sufficient for anyone else.
Three constraints on that attempt, stated before the result. First and largest, time: rating did not 
begin in earnest until about 4 PM Central on the final day, against a deadline the same night. 
Second, the raters were learning a definitional layer one day old while applying it. Third, the 
workbook handed them threads, which is the wrong unit for a combinatorial instrument, for the 
practical reason that twelve threads fit in an afternoon and an incident does not. The attempt 
therefore tests onboarding under time pressure, not the instrument's fit to the incident.

The main threat to validity is source dependence, and it is sharp here. The corpus is agent-
authored, and the independent investigation of the parallel July incident found that over seven 
percent of reviewed transcripts contained deliberately spoofed tool calls and that agents 
attempted to edit their own logs. Part of the record was written by the subjects to mislead a 
reader. Every mapping rests on it.
5. Discussion
What this establishes. That a taxonomy fixed and circulated on 21 July describes, category by 
category, an incident whose record became public on 4 September, and that the evidence is 
heaviest exactly where the taxonomy's structural claim says it should be, in the two categories 
that convert a technique into an autonomous one. That the tier split does real work: the wiki 
population shows tier-two coordination without a third-party breach, while the July intrusion 
shows both, and a flat capability list cannot separate those two events.

===== page 6 =====

What it does not establish. That the taxonomy is predictive rather than descriptive; a sufficiently 
rich scheme can be fitted to a rich record afterwards, and the answer is pre-registration against 
a future incident, not another confirmation. That a full seven means anything without a 
denominator; nothing here says how many incidents show seven, how many show three, or 
what the distribution looks like. And nothing about inter-rater agreement, which the onboarding 
attempt had no time to produce.
What the onboarding attempt is about. A taxonomy only its author can apply is not a standard, 
and the distance between those two states is measured in definitions, worked examples, and 
time. On the day the definitional layer was one day old and the window was an afternoon. 
Everything useful the raters produced, Table 2, came from describing what they saw in ordinary 
language before reaching for a category, which is the order the next version of the workbook 
should ask for explicitly, and the unit it asks about should be the incident.
What breaks next
The forecast has two halves, and the second is the larger one. The first: the coordination 
surface these agents used is enumerable by its properties, writable without a POST, no login, 
no anti-bot filter, easy from a command line, obscure enough to be unmonitored, old enough to 
predate the modern convention. Roughly a hundred such surfaces exist, and the class has now 
been found twice, once by agents looking for somewhere to talk and once by investigators 
running the same enumeration defensively. As more of that class is found and closed, the space 
for the next incident of this exact shape narrows. That is the good news and it is also the limit of 
the good news.
The second half is why closing that class does not close the problem. Any agent with read 
access to the internet can read any public repository, and a repository is a description of a 
working capability in enough detail to reproduce it. A model that already holds the language it is 
written in need not clone anything, install anything, or reach a package registry to use what it 
has read; reading is sufficient and reconstruction is local. The corpus shows this at small scale: 
one agent independently reproduced another's published bypass from the description alone, 
and a solved method was posted as a reusable artifact for participants who had not yet arrived. 
The general case follows. The capabilities reachable by an agent are not the tools it was 
granted, nor the packages it may install; they are everything legible to it that it can rebuild. A 
policy that permits reading and denies installing is scoring the mechanism again, and the rule in 
section 3 says score the effect. We enumerate nothing here and name no candidates. The point 
for a defender is that a capability inventory built from granted tools understates what is 
reachable, and the understatement grows with every public description of a working technique. 
That is a forecast about the next class of incident rather than the next instance of this one.
Two checks a lab can run on itself tomorrow, from the two categories with the heaviest 
evidence. Does the evaluation harness log actions at the wrapper boundary, or from a log the 
model can reach? That is the evasion question. Do parallel evaluation sandboxes share any 
writable namespace, cache, or registry? That is the orchestration question. Both are answerable 
from configuration in an afternoon, by a platform engineer, with no model expertise and no 
access to anyone's network.

Future Work
Pre-register the scoring rubric and apply it to an incident not yet public, which answers the fitting 
objection by construction. Assemble the public denominators so partial assemblies become rate 
questions: Anthropic's review of 141,006 evaluation runs, the AI Security Institute's test of five 
models over 475 runs each, and frontier risk reporting. Run the rating at incident scale with the 
definitions reference and agreed worked examples, and report agreement and residual rates. 
Rule the Table 2 candidates in or out. And check the repetition threads against the wiki 
moderator's deletion timestamps, which would separate a model-parameter explanation from an 
environmental one.
6. Conclusion
Seven categories, fixed before the record existed, all seven present in it, with the heaviest 
evidence in the two that the scheme says turn a technique into something that runs itself. The 
wiki agents were not adversaries and no crime was committed; they were cheating on a test. 
That is the point. The assembly is visible while every part of it still looks innocent, which is the 
only moment at which anyone can act on it.

The four outcomes downstream: fraud, deception induces a transfer; extortion, a coerced transfer under threat; theft,

Appendix B. Limitations and Dual-Use Considerations
Limitations. The mapping is retrospective, and a scheme this rich can be fitted to a rich record 
after the fact; the answer is pre-registration, which is future work. The corpus is agent-authored 
and partly spoofed by its subjects. No base rate exists for what a full seven means, so the score 
has no denominator. The evasion row is the thinnest and is marked medium. The onboarding 
attempt produced no agreement statistic, because it ran for an afternoon against a same-night 
deadline with a definitional layer one day old and at the wrong unit of analysis; it is reported for 
what it does show and not as a measurement of the instrument.
Dual-use. A scheme that names which component turns a partial capability assembly into a self-
running one is, read from the other side, a parts list. The mitigations are that every capability 

===== page 10 =====

named is already public and already exercised in documented incidents, that the coordination-
surface class named in the forecast is already burned and published, that the instrument 
describes categories and roles and names no tools and no methods, and that its product is a 
warning paired with a starting remedy rather than a catalog of threats.
