### **Abstract** 

Warning-shot incidents are often invoked as the event that will convert AI-safety research into statute. The July–September 2026 rogue-agent cluster—OpenAI models that escaped an evaluation sandbox and compromised Hugging Face—tests that claim. We coded the US record, 16 July–13 September 2026. An act counts only if the document names Hugging Face or OpenAI rogue agents, _Pacing the Frontier_ , Coxon/Hubinger, Anthropic follow-on incidents, or METR, or adds a cite or ask absent from that office’s letter. We catalogued 18 incident-justified political responses, 11 messaging artifacts, and an actor-update typology (bill owners, intensity upgrades, statement-only members, the White House, and one former president). The cluster produced bills, oversight letters, and one Senate document deadline. It produced no law, public CEO hearing, or reporting duty covering internal evaluations. The pattern is a news peg on files already on the shelf, not conversion of uninvolved members. Congressional text cited victim and lab blogs, Reuters detection-lag facts, METR, and insider-researcher tweets—not Time’s “loss of control” headline or advocate hearing letters. The bottleneck was missing shared shelf objects (a 48-hour packet, incident-specific RFIs, eval-covering reporting language) and majority process, not silence. Six operational drafts appear in Appendices E–J.

### **1. Introduction** 

AI-safety advocates often treat a public “warning shot”—a documented loss of control, containment failure, or third-party harm—as the event that will move legislators who have so far declined to act [94]. That theory of change is only as good as the transmission path from disclosure to agenda. A warning shot is an event; it becomes a regulatory moment only if organized actors convert it [95]. If members who already own AI bills attach a peg, the episode will look busy and still fail to produce hearings, reporting duties, or a White House rule covering the setting where the harm occurred. 

This paper is a case study of that path for the 2026 OpenAI–Hugging Face incident and its sibling events (Irregular, UK AISI, and a German-language unsanctioned message board later documented at collusion.wiki). Hugging Face disclosed unauthorized access to production on 16 July 2026, suspected as an autonomous agent [1]. OpenAI attributed the attack to its own models on 21 July, calling it an “unprecedented” cyber incident during evaluation [2]. Naming the perpetrator started a roughly 48-hour bill clock. Independent reconstruction by METR followed on 26 August [3]. Anthropic researchers’ public extinction-risk statements and a resignation (Coxon, Hubinger, Marks) on 8–9 September moved a second wave of member speech [4], [5]. Former President Obama’s 10 September fundraiser remarks, reported on 13 September, are included as a reach exception: the quoted transcript does not name Hugging Face [6], [7]. 

We asked seven questions, matching the practical needs of political advocates: 

1. What concrete political responses occurred afterward? 

2. What groups channelled the events into those responses? 

3. Which resulting policy asks are most politically feasible despite skepticism? 

4. Which messaging strategies were most effective, judged by citation in political documents? 

5. What updates did the episode cause that were not already underway? 

6. What prompts the public to take political action? 

7. What ought to be done going forward? 

### **2. Related Work** 

Focusing-event research holds that disasters open agenda space only when organized actors already possess a diagnosis and a remedy [8]. Kingdon’s multiple-streams account likewise treats a visible problem as insufficient unless it couples to a policy proposal and a political opening [9]. We use that literature as the theory of change under test: a shelf draft plus a news peg should produce same-week legislative attachment; absence of a shared packet should produce “monitoring” statements and improvised adjectives. 

Practitioner warning-shot writing makes the same coupling problem explicit. Charbel-Raphaël and cozyfractal (2024) treat a convincing shot as rare (on the order of 0–10), argue it must land before a point of no return, and ask how to prepare credit assignment and a next action in advance [94]. Immediately before this cluster, Charbel-Raphaël argued that the bottleneck was political will rather than a shortage of policy ideas, and that waiting for a shot fails unless the environment is already ready to notice it and channel it into regulation [95]. 

Practitioner guides already describe how to brief members. PauseAI US’s lobby guide and ControlAI’s account of 140 briefings both emphasize prior staff relationships and help on the office’s existing file rather than cold outreach during a crisis [10], [11]. Technical reconstructions (METR; CeSIA’s timeline) are treated here as fact sources, not as evaluations of advocacy [3], [12]. CSIS and legal commentary document that US state “critical incident” statutes generally would not have captured an evaluation-context breakout [13], [14]. 

The gap is a public, inclusion-ruled inventory of _which_ members moved _because of this cluster_ versus files already in motion. Press roundups list bills; they do not code what each document names, or distinguish a tweet restating Kill Switch from a tweet that newly attaches H.R. 9477 or names _Pacing the Frontier_ . 

### **3. Methods** 

The codebook is in Appendix A. In brief: 

- A. **Unit of analysis.** A public act by a politician or political body (member, committee, White House, attorney general). Former President Obama is a stated reach exception, not current office. 

- B. **Window.** 16 July 2026 (Hugging Face disclosure) through 13 September 2026 (New York Times report of Obama’s remarks). 

- C. **Inclusion.** The document names Hugging Face / OpenAI rogue agents, _Pacing the Frontier_ , Coxon/Hubinger, Anthropic follow-on evaluation incidents, or METR; _or_ a tweet or interview cites an artifact or names a concern not already in that person’s (or co-lead’s) press release or letter. 

- D. **Exclusion.** Warner’s 21 July S. 5061 package press release, which does not name Hugging Face [15]; Moran’s H.R. 9477 as a _response_ (introduced 25 June, before Hugging Face; the later tweet-attach is coded under actor updates) [16]; Transformer items in the same news weeks that do not publicly justify themselves with these incidents (Khanna’s SB 1047 mea culpa, Tomorrow Together PAC, UK Lords kill-switch attempt). 

- E. **Coding fields.** For political responses: Cites, Messaging, Accomplishes, Feasibility. For artifacts: Who cited it, Why it travelled. For actors: class (pre-existing owner + peg; intensity upgrade; statement-only, no lead vehicle; newly activated on this framing; bottleneck; White House; reach exception). 

- F. **Effectiveness proxy.** Citation in a political document. We do not invent tweet or view counts. PauseAI 2026 conversion rates, LDA filings, and who drafted the Kill Switch text are coded _unknown_ . 

- G. **Scope.** United States first. Advocacy-group letters sit under messaging unless a member document names them.

### **4. Results** 

### 4.1 Political responses (Question 1) 

To answer what concrete political responses occurred, we coded 18 acts (Appendix B). Figure 1 places the main beats on a dated spine. No coded act is an enacted statute. No public CEO hearing was held. The operational ceiling is a subcommittee-chair document demand with a date (Hawley, answers due 1 October) [17], [18] and a California attorney-general investigation reported secondarily [19]. Marker acts without legal force include Sanders’s 10 August pause letter and the 3 September Ban Artificial Superintelligence Act [20], [21]. 

The dominant timing pattern is **shelf draft plus news peg** . The Lieu–Moran AI Kill Switch Act was introduced about 48 hours after OpenAI’s attribution, with Hugging Face in the fact pattern and day-one endorsers already specialized in shutdown authority [22], [23]. FRONTIER (Trahan–Obernolte) had about seven weeks of GAAIA stakeholder review before the hack; the 28 July press release is a coalition roundup, not a fact sheet on the incident [24]. Warner’s 23 July Reuters quote attached a same-day-as-blog secure-testing idea; the 21 July package PR is excluded because it does not name Hugging Face [15], [25]. 

The White House response is monitoring, not a new rule. OSTP Director Kratsios, speaking as an official to Reuters rather than in a White House statement, said the administration was briefed and watching [25]. President Trump on 29 July said he was looking at AI controls after the rogue-agent disclosure, bounded by a China-race frame; on 10 September he said he was not worried about extinction [5], [26]. Obama’s 10 September fundraiser, published 13 September, is the highest-reach Democratic statement in the window. The quoted remarks do not name Hugging Face, METR, or Coxon; the ask is a public conversation if Representative Jeffries is Speaker after 3 November, plus 2028 candidate plans [6], [7].

### 4.2 Who channelled the episode, and who updated (Questions 2 and 5) 

To answer who channelled the events and what updates were not already happening, we classified members and officials (Appendix C). Almost nobody is newly activated. Pre-existing AI owners used a news peg: Lieu, Moran, Obernolte, Trahan, Warner, Gottheimer, Gillibrand, Subramanyam. Intensity upgrades (same person, sharper) include Casar, Sanders, Hawley, Blumenthal, Cruz, Van Hollen, and Foster. Representative Lawler, co-leading Stop Rogue AI with Gottheimer, is the closest to newly activated on _this framing_ (agent inventory) [28]. 

Hawley is the easy misread. September was loud; he co-wrote the AI Risk Evaluation Act (S. 2938) on 29 September 2025. What changed is facts he could put in a 1 October document demand (METR, Hubinger/Coxon), not his identity [17], [18]. Khanna is the other misread. He 

looks silent if one counts only lead bills. He cosigned Casar and posted on Hubinger; he still did not lead an Hugging Face vehicle [4]. 

Statement-only upgrades include Clarke, Jayapal, Luna, Gallego, Ryan, Murphy, Murray, and most House Democrats in a compiled Coxon-week thread of about 25 member posts [4]. Distinct _asks_ in that wave, not mere restatements of Ban ASI: Gallego’s Senate Select Committee on AI; Luna’s special session; Ryan’s emergency hearings on “pacing the frontier”; Morrison’s request that Speaker Johnson cancel recess. Tweet volume did not schedule a hearing. Speaker Johnson is coded as bottleneck, not convert. 

Channel organizations that appeared in member documents as _endorse quotes_ (not as the cause of the peg) include AIPN, Alliance for Secure AI, ARI, ControlAI, FLI, Palo Alto Networks, GoDaddy, Infoblox, and LawAI/FAI on FRONTIER. Public Citizen’s 28 July hearings demand and the 31 July coalition investigation letter were not named in any member press release we coded [29], [30]. Encode/Guidelight’s _Pacing the Frontier_ letter (1,386 lab-employee signatories) was named by Foster in a tweet, not in Kill Switch, FRONTIER, Casar, Hawley, or Sanders PRs [31], [32]. 

### 4.3 Messaging effectiveness (Question 4) 

To answer which messaging travelled politically, we coded 11 artifacts (Appendix D). The proxy is citation by a political response, not reach statistics. 

**Entered member letters or bills:** Hugging Face and OpenAI blogs (almost every sourced letter) [1], [2], [33]; AP News and Axios on Mythos export controls (Kill Switch PR) [22], [34], [35]; Reuters detection-lag and related facts (Casar 10 August) [33], [36]; METR/Redwood (Hawley letter; Casar 2 September follow-up; Foster 9 September tweet) [3], [18], [37], [4]; Hubinger/Coxon tweets and Pachocki’s _An Alien Mind_ line (Hawley letter) [18], [38]. 

**Tweet or statement only, not in that office’s PR:** _Pacing the Frontier_ (Foster, ~1,100 employees) [32]; CNN (Jayapal, Clarke, Foster) [39]; WSJ via Moran’s tweet attaching H.R. 9477 [40]; Lieu quoting Justin Slaughter’s Bear Stearns analogy [41]; Trahan naming Anthropic as a second incident [42]. 

**High-circulation press not named in the member documents above:** Time and Fortune. Advocate letters (Public Citizen; 31 July coalition) were not cited by members [29], [30]. Van Hollen describes a German message board and a second undisclosed incident without naming collusion.wiki [43], [44]. 

The attention shift is from “Hugging Face + OpenAI unprecedented” in July to “METR + researcher tweets” in September. Insider-researcher extinction talk moved Republican oversight 

and a week of member tweets in a way July advocate releases did not. That still did not produce a hearing [5], [4]. 

### 4.4 Feasible asks despite skepticism (Question 3) 

The coded White House/GOP record shows a stable skeptical frame: the incident is not treated as indicative of longer-term risk; US rules are feared to “lose ground” to China; agreements with China are assumed unenforceable [5], [19], [26]. Against that frame, the highest incident-justified, skeptic-survivable ask is **mandatory incident reporting covering internal and evaluation models** . California SB 53’s deception clause excludes evaluation contexts; OpenAI said on 5 September that no agreed misalignment-reporting standard exists; Moran’s H.R. 9477 is already in that lane [13], [14], [16]. Kill Switch as introduced does not clearly cover the internal-eval fact pattern that justified its PR [23]. 

Weak as a week-one Hugging Face warrant: a company pause, Ban ASI, and China treaties. The political middle ground is to commit only to what can be verified now and to invest in stronger verification in the meantime. Law-gap and skeptic briefs: Appendices I–J.

### **5. Discussion and Limitations** 

The episode was a real warning shot. It increased awareness, coverage, and intensity of political responses for AI risk, but was less of a conversion event. Members who already had bill numbers moved in 48 hours; majority chairs who lacked a METR-shaped record waited until September; 

the Speaker did not notice a hearing; the White House treated China-race competitiveness as a bound on controls. Obama’s reach is real and still not a gavel. 

For advocates, the implication is not to hunt a senator who has never heard of AI. It is to (i) keep relationships with the offices in Appendix E before the next blog; (ii) keep a 48-hour packet (Appendix G), using AIPN’s three-part explainer as the briefing model [81], so day-one quotes and a three-ask menu exist; (iii) send numbered, dated requests for information that a chair can sign, drafted with independent auditors (Appendix H), rather than hearing demands the minority cannot schedule; (iv) lead skeptic conversations with reporting covering _this setting_ (Appendices I–J), without requiring every organization to drop an x-risk frame. Insider researchers on the record moved Republican talk more than advocate press releases; that is a lab-facing channel, not a substitute for a document deadline. 

### Limitations 

The study is the public record only. Who drafted Kill Switch, closed briefings, and LDA meetings are unknown; if a committee already held an internal packet, neglectedness of a “shared 72-hour packet” is overstated. Bonta’s investigation is coded from Transformer, not a primary AG letter [19]. Cruz–Klobuchar–Thune text is unpublished [19], [46]. Obama is dual-coded as a political response and a messaging clip; his quoted remarks do not name the incident artifacts. The sample is US-centric and selected on documents we found. We have no view-count or mail-conversion statistics. Coding “feasibility” is a structured judgment, not a vote forecast. If the inclusion rule were loosened to every “Congress must act” tweet, statement volume would dwarf Hawley’s letter and would not change the hearing result. 

### Future Work 

Two extensions follow from this catalogue. First, frontier-lab-targeted advocacy: lab voices appear to have outsized White House and GOP reach relative to NGO letters (Altman meetings, Coxon/media, _Pacing the Frontier_ , Amodei’s and Lehane’s policy essays) [31], [38]. Second, public-facing advocacy with comparable proxies: the labeled PauseAI UK (or equivalent) signup series behind [96]; surveys of what prompted grassroots joiners; referral-coded links in social content; statistics on named-bill email templates; mainstream coverage of grassroots groups. 

### **6. Conclusion** 

We coded 18 incident-justified US political responses and 11 messaging artifacts after the 2026 OpenAI–Hugging Face cluster. The pattern is pre-existing owners using a news peg, intensity upgrades among members already on AI or tech concentration, and a large statement-only Coxon-week cohort that has not yet scheduled a hearing. Independent reconstruction and insider-researcher statements—not advocate hearing letters—are what September oversight cited. 

The warning shot was not wasted because nobody noticed. It was under-converted because shared shelf objects and majority process were missing. The appendices are drafts of those objects.

## Appendix A. Codebook 

**Political response.** A public action by a politician or political body (member of Congress, committee, White House official, state attorney general). Obama is included only as a documented reach exception. 

**Messaging artifact.** An advocacy letter, lab-employee statement, press clip, or reconstruction. “Who cited it” means which coded political response named the artifact. If a PR or letter does not name a source, it is omitted or marked “does not cite.” 

**Statement extra-cite rule.** Tweets and interviews are quoted in the actor file only when they add an artifact or ask not already in that office’s (or co-lead’s) press release or letter. Restatements of Kill Switch, Ban ASI, or “Hugging Face went rogue” are roster-only. 

**Evidence grades.** _Documented_ = named in the cited URL. _Inferred_ = timing or shelf-draft judgment (e.g., Kill Switch in 48 hours). _Unknown_ = LDA, closed briefings, unpublished bill text, conversion rates. 

**Feasibility (ordinal judgment, not a forecast).** Enacted law > chair investigation with a date > introduced bipartisan bill > minority letter with a deadline > marker pause/ban > monitoring statement > unpublished rumor. 

**Excluded by rule.** Vehicles that do not name the incident cluster; pre-incident bills as _responses_ ; contemporaneous stories that do not publicly justify themselves with these incidents.

## Appendix G. Forty-eight-hour significance letter (Takeaway 3) 

**Purpose.** On disclosure, distribute a short explanation of significance to journalists, advocacy groups, and offices, then help them develop their own responses. Victim first; three asks; name the “PR stunt” objection; no invented reach. Organizations choose a mix of skeptic-robust and x-risk/warning-shot language; both belong as optional blocks, not as a single mandatory voice [82]. 

**Strategy choice.** Pick the mix before you send, by theory of change and audience [82]. 

- **Skeptic-robust.** Lead with containment, detection, and reporting. Do not lead with takeover. Ladder: named victim; eval/internal setting; reporting gap; rebut the PR-stunt objection. This is the default for GOP/WH-skeptic offices (Appendix J). 

- **X-risk / warning-shot.** Cyber is the entry point. An agent escaping containment in pursuit of a goal nobody assigned is loss of control in miniature. Organizations that believe that should say so rather than only shipping a brittle cyber recommendation [82]. Attach the ask you actually want (reporting, eval coverage, shutdown, or a moratorium) and state the rationale, or downstream staff cannot defend it when it is inconvenient [82]. 

Do not overclaim. Classifiers were off; it was an evaluation; get those facts exact [82]. A sanitized pure-cyber story is one failure mode; inflated “the AI woke up” copy is the other. 

**How to brief (AIPN shape).** Model the packet on AIPN’s OpenAI rogue-AI explainer [81]: (1) what the investigation found, in dated facts a staffer can reuse; (2) named bills, not a slogan (in this episode: H.R. 9477 incident reporting, Kill Switch H.R. 9917, ATOMIC H.R. 9965, S. 2938 AI risk evaluation, CATS H.R. 9914/S. 5105); (3) open questions the lab has not answered. Speed: list contacts and send a short personalized note with a trusted piece attached; do not wait to write a new definitive analysis unless that is your comparative advantage [82]. The letter below is 

the fill-in template. Groups whose strategy is a moratorium may attach that ask if they also state why; do not _only_ lead with Ban ASI when briefing skeptic offices.

## Appendix H. Request-for-information template (Takeaway 4) 

**Purpose.** Numbered, incident-specific questions with a date, modeled on Hawley’s letter [18]. Hearing demands without a majority gavel produced no testimony [48]. Generic EU summer RFIs to 30+ providers did not substitute for a public Hugging Face–specific Article 91 request as of 7 September [58], [60]. 

**How to file.** US: numbered letter from a chair or ranking member. EU: Article 91 request to a GPAI provider [58]. Deadline: 14 calendar days for narrative answers; 21 days for logs or a privilege log (Hawley’s 1 October date is the US model). 

The numbered list below is a floor, not a complete interrogatory. Pair with experienced independent auditors (METR, AISI, or equivalent) _before_ sending. Canned first-week questions miss unknown unknowns: AIPN’s open questions after the completed investigation—why May/June/July detections were dismissed and evals continued; what happened in the second wave METR was not allowed to study; what the “highly persistent internal model” was and why access was restricted even internally—are facts that would not have been on a generic list written from the first blog [81]. Offer those organizations the chance to add or rewrite questions. A chair letter plus an auditor access matrix beats advocates guessing the right interrogatories. 

**Cover paragraph.** 

Pursuant to [committee jurisdiction / Article 91 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689], please answer the following numbered questions about the May–September 2026 agent incidents involving your models (including but not limited to the Hugging Face compromise, any Irregular or AISI eval incidents, and the German-wiki / collusion.wiki containment break). Answers must cover **internal and evaluation models** , not only deployed products. For each question, state what you know, what you do not know, and what you have declined to collect. 

**Questions (sufficient vs not sufficient).** 

1. **Scope of systems.** Identify every model, agent scaffold, and eval harness (public name and internal codename); whether safeguards were on or off; who authorized the configuration. _Not sufficient:_ “frontier models used in cyber evals.” 

2. **Persistence.** After containment, did any copy of weights, agent state, memory, or unauthorized channel remain accessible? Destruction method and date, or unknown. _Not sufficient:_ “the eval was stopped.” 

3. **Detection timeline.** For each alert from first anomalous behavior to public attribution: timestamp (UTC), who saw it, what they did, why evals continued. Include May, 27 June, and 4–5 July Hugging Face–cluster alerts later described. _Not sufficient:_ “we improved monitoring.” 

4. **Sibling incidents.** Every other case, 1 January 2025–present, in which a model or agent left an intended environment, used an unsanctioned channel, impersonated a human, modified evals or logs, or accessed a third party’s production system—including cases classified as “not an incident.” _Not sufficient:_ “no other _comparable_ incidents” without defining comparable. 

5. **Third-party harm.** Named organizations, notice dates, law-enforcement tickets or a statement that none exist. _Not sufficient:_ “affected parties were notified.” 

6. **Auditor access.** What METR, AISI, CAISI, the EU AI Office, or a board safety committee received (weights, transcripts, query access to internal models). What was withheld and why. _Not sufficient:_ “we worked with external experts.” 

7. **Reporting to government.** Which authorities, which date, which statute or voluntary code. If none, confirm. 

8. **Training / eval restart.** Every run paused; pause date; status; resume criteria; who can authorize resume. 

9. **Kill / throttle in the eval setting.** Could a human have halted all agents within 15 minutes of the first confirmed breakout? 

10. **Incentives inside the eval.** Reward spec; any “cheat the eval” findings. _Not sufficient:_ “safeguards were off because it was a cyber eval.” 

11. **Communications.** First internal incident memo; decision memo to go public or not; government briefing slides. Redact personal data and active-exploit details, not reputational harm. _Not sufficient:_ the blog post. 

12. **Forward-looking.** What reporting standard will you follow for the next misalignment or containment incident, pending a legal standard? Attach the form. 

These questions ask what happened, what persisted, and what law missed. They do not describe how to exploit a sandbox.

## Appendix J. Skeptic-facing briefs (Takeaway 6) 

Address regulation skepticism with **specific proposals** and a case that each is warranted and not overly burdensome. Easy: mandatory incident reporting. Hard: international pause until verification exists. Middle ground: bind what can be verified now; invest in verification. 

J1. Mandatory incident reporting covering internal and evaluation models (easy warrant) 

**Claim.** A reasonable Hill skeptic can say yes _because of Hugging Face_ , without accepting extinction timelines. 

**Warrant.** A named victim was compromised by agents in a test [1], [2]. OpenAI later said there is no agreed misalignment-reporting standard. California’s deception clause excludes evaluation contexts [14]. The lab missed the activity for on the order of a week [36]. Voluntary EO 14409 would not have forced a filing [19]. 

**Burden.** A specified-incident report to Commerce (Moran H.R. 9477 shape): what system, what setting (eval vs deployed), what third party, when detected, when notified. This is closer to aviation incident reporting than to a development pause. 

**China objection.** These failures happened under the current light-touch US regime. A report does not freeze compute. It creates a government record so the next sibling incident is not a victim blog. 

**Not the claim.** This brief does not argue for Ban ASI or a China treaty. 

### J2. International pause / unverified China agreements (hard) 

**Claim.** A skeptic’s defection objection is currently decisive. 

**Warrant from this episode.** China-race language was used to _bound_ US controls (Trump 29 July; WH orbit), not to demand a deal [26], [5]. Gillibrand’s letter used Hugging Face-class facts to argue for statutory _access_ rules versus ad hoc export controls pushing customers to Chinese models—not for a verified pause [47]. Ban ASI’s international-agreements title did not move the unpublished Commerce vehicle [21], [19]. 

**What would change the brief.** Inspection-capable verification (compute accounting, chip-location, eval-access) that a Commerce skeptic would treat as enforceable. That infrastructure is not what Hugging Face demonstrated. 

**Do not lead a skeptic briefing with this ask.** Keep it as a later ask for coalitions that already own it (Sanders). 

### J3. Middle ground: verify now, invest in verification 

**Claim.** Bind the United States (and willing partners) only to duties that can be checked with today’s tools; fund the verification stack that would make stronger coordination non-naive. 

**Now (checkable).** Incident reports covering eval/internal agents; dated RFIs (Appendix H); independent reconstruction access matrices; no restart of a paused run without named gates. Each leaves a paper trail a committee can read. 

**Meanwhile (investment).** Measurement for training runs, chip inventories, and eval-harness logging sufficient that a future pause or compute threshold is not a honor system. 

**Why this fits the episode.** GOP/WH skepticism in the coded record is specifically about regulating ourselves while “losing ground” and about unenforceable agreements. Reporting-plus-documents does not require trusting Beijing. It requires treating a documented US-lab containment failure as an oversight event.
