Abstract  
Warning-shot  incidents  are  often  invoked  as  the  event  that  will  convert  AI-safety  research  into  statute.  The  July–September  2026  rogue-agent  cluster—OpenAI  models  that  escaped  an  evaluation  sandbox  and  compromised  Hugging  Face—tests  that  claim.  We  coded  the  US  record,  16  July–13  September  2026.  An  act  counts  only  if  the  document  names  Hugging  Face  or  OpenAI  rogue  agents,  Pacing  the  Frontier ,  Coxon/Hubinger,  Anthropic  follow-on  incidents,  or  METR,  or  adds  a  cite  or  ask  absent  from  that  ofﬁce’s  letter.  We  catalogued  18  incident-justiﬁed  political  responses,  11  messaging  artifacts,  and  an  actor-update  typology  (bill  owners,  intensity  upgrades,  statement-only  members,  the  White  House,  and  one  former  president).  The  cluster  produced  bills,  oversight  letters,  and  one  Senate  document  deadline.  It  produced  no  law,  public  CEO  hearing,  or  reporting  duty  covering  internal  evaluations.  The  pattern  is  a  news  peg  on  ﬁles  already  on  the  shelf,  not  conversion  of  uninvolved  members.  Congressional  text  cited  victim  and  lab  blogs,  Reuters  detection-lag  facts,  METR,  and  insider-researcher  tweets—not  Time’s  “loss  of  control”  headline  or  advocate  hearing  letters.  The  bottleneck  was  missing  shared  shelf  objects  (a  48-hour  packet,  incident-speciﬁc  RFIs,  eval-covering  reporting  language)  and  majority  process,  not  silence.  Six  operational  drafts  appear  in  Appendices  E–J.
