Abstract  
Agentic  AI  incidents  increasingly  reveal  a  gap  between  intended  safeguards  and  the  capabilities  agents  can  exercise  in  practice.  We  study  how  such  failures  can  be  reconstructed,  compared,  and  ultimately  contained.  We  ﬁrst  develop  a  public-source  forensics  pipeline,  extracting  679  source-linked  claims  across  ﬁve  incidents,  and  introduce  a  protocol  for  systematically  evaluating  containment,  isolation,  monitoring,  and  postmortem  evidence.  Across  the  cases  we  can  assess,  failures  cluster  around  isolation  and  alerting  controls.  We  then  test  whether  monitoring  can  move  beyond  detection  to  prevention  in  a  controlled  authorization  environment.  Relational  authorization  evidence  enables  detection  before  the  last-safe  intervention  boundary,  and  matched  executions  show  that  timely  capability  revocation  prevents  an  otherwise-occurring  prohibited  effect.  However,  representation-sensitive  false  positives  can  also  trigger  unnecessary  intervention  and  legitimate-task  failure.  Together,  our  results  highlight  a  central  lesson  for  AI  incident  response:  detection  is  not  containment.  Effective  containment  requires  reconstructable  evidence,  timely  monitoring,  and  reliable  mechanisms  for  acting  before  failures  become  irreversible.   
 
1  Research  conducted  at  the  AI  Incident  Response  Sprint,  September  2026
