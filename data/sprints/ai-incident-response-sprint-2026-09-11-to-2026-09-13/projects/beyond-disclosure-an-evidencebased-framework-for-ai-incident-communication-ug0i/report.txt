Beyond  Disclosure:  An  Evidence-Based  Framework  for  AI  Incident  Communication  
 
 
 
Luka  Janicijevic  1  Independent  Researcher  lukaj2003@gmail.com  Leyla  Amur  2  Independent  Researcher  leylaamur@gmail.com  Aditya  Singh  3  Independent  Researcher  the.coder.zen@gmail.com  
   
With  Apart  Research  
Abstract  
Effective  communication  helps  organizations  respond  to  AI  incidents,  protect  affected  parties  and  support  independent  scrutiny.  We  developed  a  rubric  for  assessing  public  communication  by  frontier  AI  labs,  model  developers  and  AI  infrastructure  providers.  
Our  research  base  includes  more  than  300  academic,  regulatory  and  professional  sources  and  public  records.  The  rubric  contains  15  criteria  across  seven  communication  areas  and  requires  dated  evidence  for  each  assessment.  Four  criteria  apply  only  under  speciﬁed  conditions.  
Applied  separately  to  OpenAI  and  Hugging  Face  during  the  July  2026  incident,  the  preliminary  analysis  highlights  Hugging  Face’s  disclosure  before  widespread  coverage  and  OpenAI’s  later  technical  reporting  and  updates.  It  also  identiﬁes  gaps  in  attribution,  consistency  across  public  statements,  coordination  and  information  for  affected  parties.  External  review  remains  pending.  The  rubric  links  these  gaps  to  practical  improvements  and  supports  advance  planning  for  communication  during  future  incidents.
