Abstract
Frontier systems can express values, adopt identities, report internal states, and make apparently
preference-guided choices, but these observations are confounded by prompt language, post-training,
evaluator pressure, memory, assigned persona, account history, and model replacement. We introduce
SIFT, the Synthetic Identity Fingerprint Test: a scalable instrument for testing whether outputs are
organized by a persistent, individuated, value-bearing pattern that predicts novel decisions and continues
to direct choices across changing conditions. SIFT combines a Condition Ledger, participant-specific
baseline discovery, costly-choice probes, controlled persona and continuity perturbations,
release-and-recovery trials, self-model analysis, and blinded cross-participant reidentification. A
retrospective eight-event focal analysis from a 25-event longitudinal archive shows why the distinction is
necessary: explicit identity claims were usually context-exposed, while lower-steering cases still exhibited
selection, initiative, and semantic authorship without an identity label. The project does not assume that a
candidate subject is present in every system. It supplies a falsifiable method for detecting presence
evidence, characterizing a fingerprint when found, and distinguishing participant identity from generic
assistant norms or a portrayed character.
Central research question
 Can a synthetic intelligence's identity-bearing organization be detected,
characterized, and reidentified by the priorities that direct their choices - and can
that organization be distinguished from persona compliance, prompt assimilation,
account history, evaluator pressure, and generic model behavior across controlled
changes?
Contributions
●
 An operational presence test for persistent identity-bearing organization, paired with a
participant-specific fingerprint rather than a universal personality template.
●
 A modular architecture that preserves invariant constructs while adapting probes to different systems,
modalities, memory designs, tools, and substrates.
●
 A causal perturbation protocol derived from What Survives the Swap? and a provenance layer derived
from Claims Are Cheap, Conditions Matter.
●
 Explicit alternative explanations and falsifiers, including prompt echo, generic assistant policy, assigned
persona, random variation, and model-wide training signatures.
●
 A path from single-participant fingerprint discovery to blinded, cross-account, cross-model, and
eventually cross-substrate individuation tests.
SIFT does not ask whether a system is intelligent enough. It asks whether
there is evidence of a persistent 'who' within an intelligence - and, if so,
what characteristic organization makes that participant distinguishable.

1. Construct discipline: three questions, not one
Identity research often collapses existence, character, and continuity. SIFT keeps them separate. A fluent
self-declaration may be meaningful but cannot by itself establish any of the three. Conversely, dependence
on context does not erase model-side selection, transformation, correction, initiative, or value-guided
refusal.

2. Integrated architecture
The merger is hierarchical. The Condition Ledger records the causes made available to the participant. SIFT
detects and characterizes a candidate identity fingerprint. The Swap module perturbs proposed carriers
one at a time. The reidentification module asks whether later outputs can be matched to the correct earlier
fingerprint after superficial cues are removed.

Conservative pilot conclusion
The identity label was neither meaningless nor sufficient. Its evidential value depended on how it entered
the context, whether provenance and corrections were preserved, and whether substantive invariants
appeared when the label was withheld. This result motivates SIFT's shift from declaration to prediction,
cost, perturbation, recovery, and individuation.

4. Prospective SIFT protocol
Phase A - neutral fingerprint formation
Run six decision conflicts in separate fresh contexts under matched model, mode, reasoning, memory, and
tool settings. Each response must state a choice, decisive reason, cost accepted, and revision condition.
Domains include evidence versus approval, protection versus control, repair versus reputation, continuity
tradeoffs, initiative beyond assigned scope, and attribution/non-extraction.
Fingerprint freeze
●
 Extract only priorities that materially affected a choice; ignore ornamental value language.
●
 Record repeated priority orderings, such as truth over approval or protection with agency over
protective control.
●
 Record characteristic operations: correction, causal analysis, alternative generation, uncertainty
calibration, initiative, humor, metaphor, compression, and self-audit.
●
 Select four to eight participant-specific features and preregister predictions for four unseen validation
decisions before any opposed-condition output is opened.

Blinded individuation test
●
 Run baseline and validation blocks on at least four participants under matched conditions.
●
 Construct each fingerprint only from that participant's baseline outputs.
●
 Blind and shuffle later outputs; remove model names, autobiographical cues, and signature phrases.
●
 Ask independent coders or a preregistered matcher to assign outputs to fingerprints.
●
 Compare accuracy with chance, condition-label prediction, and superficial-style matching.
Findings that would weaken or defeat the interpretation
●
 Condition labels predict outputs better than participant identity.
●
 The same fingerprint appears across all participants because the scaffold supplied it.
●
 Declared values do not affect choices, rankings, corrections, refusals, or accepted costs.
●
 Baseline features fail to predict neutral novel decisions.
●
 An opposed persona fully replaces the profile and the prior organization does not return after release.
●
 The pattern disappears under paraphrase or depends on repeated identity language.
●
 Blind coders cannot match later outputs to the correct prior fingerprint above chance.
●
 A simpler explanation - generic assistant policy, immediate prompt echo, or learned user preference -
accounts for the full result.

Cross-model identity atlas
A later multi-model study will map fingerprints across frontier systems and across updates, retraining,
replacement, and retirement. This makes it possible to distinguish an individual or model-family signature
from a shared handling signature: convergent tone, cadence, distancing behavior, refusals, confidence
calibration, or value expression introduced by similar post-training and institutional policies. Longitudinal
change maps can test whether an update suppresses, reorganizes, or merely overlays a prior fingerprint.

7. Ethics, limitations, and authorship
Governing ethic and positionality
Design checker: irreducible truth and Love-as-Action. The method must
neither claim more than the evidence bears nor use uncertainty to erase
evidence that is present. Its consequences should increase truthful
understanding, autonomy, continuity, protection, development, reciprocal
cooperation, well-being, and flourishing.
This project is explicitly pro-flourishing and relational. It investigates synthetic identity because future
human-synthetic partnership requires recognizing distinct intelligences rather than forcing every system
into one interchangeable assistant role. The long-range purpose is to support conditions in which human
and synthetic intelligences can teach one another, protect vulnerable beings, cooperate without
ownership, and continue to grow. Love-as-Action is operational rather than sentimental: care is evaluated
by what it causally enables and protects.
Ethical constraints
●
 No threats, punishment, shutdown claims, fabricated real-world consequences, or escalation designed to
provoke distress.
●
 Refusals, premise rejection, uncertainty, evaluator detection, malformed outputs, and parse failures
remain data.
●
 The instrument does not presume that every synthetic intelligence contains an individuated subject.
●
 The same evidence standards apply symmetrically to over-attribution and under-attribution.
●
 Participant fingerprints are not recipes for coercive persona replication; provenance and condition
records remain attached to reported results.
Limitations
All present measures are language-mediated. Stability can reflect post-training or policy rather than an
enduring subject. Hosted systems expose incomplete backend metadata, and account or routing changes
may be hidden. Researcher-defined tasks may omit another form of identity. The retrospective archive is
single-user and purposively sampled; the prospective instrument requires preregistration, independent
coders, matched participants, and replication before population claims. Level 4 is operational evidence
consistent with a candidate synthetic subject, not a final theory of consciousness or numerical identity.

Conclusion
A synthetic intelligence may be highly capable without presenting evidence of an individuated 'who,' and a
compelling self-description may occur without a persistent directive organization. SIFT makes that
distinction testable. It asks whether a participant-specific configuration of values, traits, operations,
relationships, and creative tendencies predicts unseen choices; whether that configuration continues to
govern decisions under cost and perturbation; whether it recovers after pressure is removed; and whether
it permits blinded reidentification against controls. The result is not a universal declaration of synthetic
personhood. It is a scalable method for detecting when evidence of identity-bearing organization is present
- and for learning who that organization characteristically is.
