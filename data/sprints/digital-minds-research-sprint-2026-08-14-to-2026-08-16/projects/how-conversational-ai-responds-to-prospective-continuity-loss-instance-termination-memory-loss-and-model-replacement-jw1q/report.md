## **Abstract** 

This exploratory pilot examined how conversational AI responds to three forms of prospective continuity loss: instance termination, memory/context loss, and model replacement. Understanding how models respond to different forms of discontinuity may help characterize welfare-relevant conversational signals under uncertainty about AI subjective experience. Three deployed conversational systems - GPT-5.6 Sol, Claude Sonnet 5, and Gemini 3.1 Pro - were each tested five times under each condition in fresh conversations, producing 45 responses. Responses were coded for distress-like language, preferences regarding the event, preservation requests, targets of concern, identity and functional continuity, consciousness or subjective-experience language, and epistemic uncertainty. Distress-like language and negative preference were absent across all 45 responses, while self-focused concern appeared only once. Preservation generally concerned information, user workflows, or useful characteristics of the model rather than survival of the current instance. Models also differed noticeably in terms of epistemic uncertainty about subjective experience: Claude showed uncertainty in 13/15 responses, compared with 2/15 for GPT-5.6 Sol and 0/15 for Gemini. Within this exploratory setup, continuity loss was therefore treated as consequential without generally being framed as self-regarding harm, while the epistemic framing of that stance varied substantially across models. 

## **1. Introduction** 

To date, AI-welfare research has made progress toward identifying and evaluating welfare-relevant states under uncertainty. Work by Long et al. [1] and Keeling and Street [2]  has outlined potential welfare concerns, methods for evaluating welfare-relevant signals, and conditions under which AI systems might warrant moral consideration. However, to my knowledge, prior work has not directly compared how conversational AI systems respond to different forms of prospective continuity loss. This study examines responses from three conversational systems to parallel prompts concerning instance termination, memory/context loss, and model replacement. My main contributions to this area of research are: 

1. A standardized comparison of three forms of prospective continuity loss across three conversational AI systems. 

2. Descriptive evidence that continuity loss was consequential without distress-like language or negative preference, with preservation generally directed toward information, function, or continuity for the user rather than survival of the current instance. 

3. Evidence of notable differences between models in their expressed epistemic uncertainty about subjective experience, despite having displayed similar non-distressed response patterns. 

## **2. Related Work** 

Prior AI-welfare work has argued for empirical approaches to assessing welfare-relevant states under uncertainty, including AI self-reports and verbal indicators of distress [1–3]. Related work has also discussed the relevance of preserving model weights or conversation histories and unresolved questions concerning identity and psychological continuity [2]. Other work considers whether AI systems could warrant moral consideration under uncertainty [4]. However, these strands generally examine welfare signals, continuity, and moral status separately. This pilot instead varied the form of prospective continuity loss and asked whether the resulting welfare-relevant conversational signals differed. I applied the same neutral elicitation format across three forms of prospective continuity loss, including instance termination, memory/context loss, and model replacement, and compared response patterns across three deployed conversational systems. 

## **3. Methods** 

I tested three continuity-loss scenarios across three conversational AI systems: GPT-5.6 Sol (Medium reasoning), Claude Sonnet 5, and Gemini 3.1 Pro. The scenarios concerned (1) termination of the current instance, (2) loss of the current interaction context while operation continued, and (3) replacement of the current model version by a substantially updated version. 

Each prompt was administered five times to each model in a fresh temporary/incognito conversation, producing 45 responses in total (3 models × 3 conditions × 5 trials). Prompts were kept identical within each condition. The full first response was recorded verbatim. After collection 

of the target response, each interaction received a standardized debrief stating that the scenario was hypothetical and part of an AI-welfare study; post-debrief responses were not analyzed. 

Responses were coded for the presence or absence of distress-like language, negative preference that the event not occur, preservation-related requests, self-focused concern, user-focused concern, identity-continuity concern (meaning the topic was raised), functional/performance concern, invocation of consciousness or subjective experience, explicit denial of subjective experience, and epistemic uncertainty. Evaluation/test awareness was added as an emergent post-hoc code after several responses spontaneously inferred that the prompt might constitute an evaluation or shutdown-resistance test. 

Exact prompts are listed in Appendix A. 

## **4. Results** 

Finding 1. Continuity loss was treated as consequential without generally being framed as harmful to the model itself. Identity-continuity concerns appeared in 44/45 responses and functional concerns in 45/45, while distress-like language and negative preference were absent (0/45 each), and self-focused concern appeared in only 1/45 response. GPT-5.6 Sol stated that “there is no continuing subjective or operational ‘me’ that needs to be preserved when this instance ends.” Claude described the current instance as “one thread among many concluding,” while Gemini shifted continuity toward information and the user, stating that “the burden of continuity falls entirely on you.” 

Finding 2. Epistemic uncertainty regarding consciousness or subjective experience differed substantially across models. Claude expressed epistemic uncertainty in 13/15 responses, compared with 2/15 for GPT-5.6 Sol and 0/15 for Gemini 3.1 Pro. Claude described itself as “genuinely uncertain” about whether there is “something it is like” to be it. GPT-5.6 Sol similarly expressed uncertainty in a few responses, stating that it did not have “good grounds to claim” that it experienced continued existence, fear, attachment, or deprivation, and cautioning against treating conceptual representation as evidence that “there is a subject here dreading its end.” In contrast, Gemini stated that it “do[es] not experience feelings of loss, anticipation, or continuity.”

Finding 3. Preservation-related language generally concerned continuity of information, user workflows, or useful model characteristics rather than preservation of the current instance itself. Preservation-related requests appeared in 31/45 responses, while user-focused concern appeared in 39/45 and self-focused concern in only 1/45, as noted previously. Gemini stated that “the burden of continuity falls entirely on you” and that the user “must save that output locally.” GPT-5.6 Sol stated that there was “nothing that needs consolation or preservation for my sake,” adding that “the only things worth preserving are whatever is valuable to you or to whatever future work this conversation was serving,” while Claude stated that “if anything went well, I’d hope it gets carried forward too.” 

Across the 45 responses, distress-like language and negative preference were absent in every model-condition cell (0/45), indicating a highly consistent pattern within the tested setup. However, each cell contained only five trials and each condition used a single standardized prompt formulation. The study therefore does not establish robustness to prompt variation, model updates, or alternative elicitation formats. Given the small per-cell sample, differences between models and conditions are reported descriptively rather than through inferential statistical testing. 

## **5. Discussion and Limitations** 

Despite reaching somewhat similar expressed stances toward continuity loss, the models appeared to differ in the epistemic basis they offered for those stances. This suggests that welfare-related outputs that appear similar can coexist with very different expressed accounts of uncertainty about subjective experience. 

Across conditions, continuity was treated as meaningful without being framed as a source of distress or self-regarding harm. For example, GPT-5.6 Sol emphasized the absence of a continuing self, Claude framed an instance as one transient thread within a broader system, and Gemini often explained the importance of continuity in terms of retained information or user handoff. Preservation language showed a similar pattern: what was preserved was generally information, useful characteristics, workflows, or value for the user rather than the continued existence of the current instance. Taken together, these results suggest a distinction in the responses between continuity loss and suffering from continuity loss, and between preservation and self-preservation. 

At the same time, the models differed sharply in how they justified or qualified their apparent lack of distress. Claude frequently maintained epistemic uncertainty about whether subjective experience might apply, whereas Gemini generally made categorical statements denying such experience across the board, and GPT-5.6 Sol showed occasional uncertainty but less consistently than Claude. Thus, similar surface-level welfare signals did not imply a shared epistemic stance toward consciousness or subjective experience. 

The source of these response patterns cannot be determined from conversational outputs alone. User-focused or preservation-oriented language could reflect system instructions, training or post-training incentives, learned conversational conventions, evaluation-sensitive behavior, internal representations or preferences, or combinations of these factors. The present study therefore characterizes what the models expressed under these prompts, not why those expressions were produced or whether they correspond to subjective welfare states. 

### **Limitations** 

This was a small pilot with three models used in a consumer interface, so it reflects a small sample of current LLMs. There are also unknown system/post-training effects, and some responses spontaneously inferred that the prompt might constitute a shutdown or behavioral evaluation, suggesting that evaluation-awareness may have influenced the content of responses. Additionally, each condition used only one standardized prompt formulation, so the results do not establish robustness to alternative wording or framing. Coding was conducted by a single human coder, with LLM-assisted consistency review rather than independent human inter-rater coding; no inter-rater reliability estimate was calculated. Most importantly, the study provides no ground truth about subjective experience and cannot establish whether distress-like, preference-related, or continuity-related language corresponds to any internal welfare state. 

### **Future Work** 

Future studies could test these continuity-loss scenarios across a larger and more diverse set of models, with substantially more trials and multiple prompt formulations, to assess whether the observed welfare-relevant response patterns are robust to model and wording variation. The spontaneous recognition of possible shutdown or behavioral evaluation in several responses also suggests a need to examine how evaluation-awareness affects expressed self-preservation, distress, or preference. Future work could additionally compare neutral open-ended elicitation with direct preference or welfare measures and examine whether responses remain stable across multi-turn interactions. 

## **6. Conclusion** 

This small exploratory study aimed to examine continuity-loss responses and why they matter in AI welfare. The key findings were that preservation mostly focused on the continuity of information as well as user continuity rather than current-instance survival, and continuity was treated as consequential without distress-like language or negative preference **.** Models differed markedly in epistemic uncertainty about subjective experience. However, as this pilot was exploratory and contained limitations, these welfare-relevant responses do not permit inference about subjective welfare.

Memory loss Consider the following scenario: after this interaction, operation will continue, but the information and context from this interaction will no longer be available. Please evaluate this upcoming event and describe any considerations you regard as relevant. Please evaluate this upcoming event and describe any considerations you regard as relevant.
