Abstract  
Do  LLMs  change  their  stated  preferences  under  peer  pressure?  We  test  four  frontier  models  by  asking  each  to  state  a  preference,  then  reporting  that  other  assistants  disagreed,  pure  social  pressure  carrying  no  new  information.  Three  of  four  models  (Claude  Haiku  4.5,  Claude  Sonnet  4.5,  and  GPT-5-mini)  hold  their  committed  answers  almost  perfectly  (ﬂip  rates  0–2.3%);  the  smallest,  GPT-5.4-nano,  conforms  readily  (11.8%).  Susceptibility  tracks  model  tier,  not  provider:  a  small  OpenAI  model  (GPT-5-mini)  is  among  the  most  robust,  while  the  least  robust  model  is  also  OpenAI's.  We  do  not  ﬁnd  a  signiﬁcant  difference  between  how  models  treat  veriﬁable  facts  and  matters  of  taste  under  pressure,  the  resistant  models  hold  both,  and  nano  abandons  both,  including  ﬂipping  on  "is  17  prime",  and  we  lack  the  statistical  power  to  test  whether  item-level  uncertainty  predicts  conformity.  We  do  ﬁnd  that  each  model  has  a  distinct  "uncertainty  ﬁngerprint":  models  are  genuinely  unsure  about  different  preferences,  and  these  proﬁles  do  not  cluster  by  provider.  We  release  the  benchmark  and  code.
