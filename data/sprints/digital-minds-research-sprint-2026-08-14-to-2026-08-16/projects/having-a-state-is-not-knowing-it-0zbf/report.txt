Abstract
Self-report is a tempting model-oversight signal, but post-hoc reports can be
generated from surface cues or ordinary downstream computation rather than
reliable access to internal state. We argue that a stronger target is counterfactual
introspection: can a model predict the causal consequence of a hypothetical inter-
nal change before its action computation experiences that change? We develop
a falsification hierarchy separating causal presence, persistence, native report,
counterfactual effect prediction, and quantitative self-modeling. In Llama-3.2-
3B-Instruct, four concept vectors causally steer behavior while natural report
remains prompt- and retrieval-distance-sensitive despite surviving mechanistic
traces. In a controlled Qwen benchmark, native-access training improves macro-F1
from 0.324 to 0.522, and fixed-energy distribution across two positions raises
trained F1 from 0.709 to 0.915. Our two-pass counterfactual protocol keeps the
action path clean, privately supplies a hypothetical residual direction to a later
query, and measures its true effect in a separate causal execution. After rejecting
shortcut-prone constructions, native F1 reaches 0.695 on held-out base directions
but loses its advantage on unseen mixtures. Quantitative response-curve predic-
tion then fails with negative R2 and zero held-out flip recovery. The result is not
merely another self-report benchmark: it defines a reusable research program for
distinguishing internal-state presence, reportability, counterfactual access, and
genuine computational self-modeling.

1. Introduction
Model self-report could support oversight of uncertainty, latent objectives, or internal anomalies. But
a model that narrates an event after it affects behavior is not necessarily able to recognize or anticipate
it in a safety-relevant way: fluent reports can come from prompt cues or generic downstream features,
while causally real changes can remain verbally inaccessible. Treating all such cases as “introspection”
collapses distinct capabilities.
Theory of change. Self-report should earn trust only as weaker explanations are falsified: establish a
ground-truth internal event; verify causal effect and downstream persistence; test matched-prompt
reporting against shortcuts and external observers; require prediction of anunexperienced intervention;
then test effect magnitude and decision-boundary consequences. Failing an earlier gate blocks the
stronger claim.
Research direction. We proposecounterfactual introspection as a stronger target than post-hoc report:
can a model forecast how a hypothetical change to its own computation would alter behavior before
that change is applied? This turns introspection into a causal prediction problem with independently
executable ground truth. The hierarchy can be reused wherever an internal variable admits causal
manipulation and measurement, making it a template for research on predictive self-monitoring rather
than another verbal-report benchmark.
We operationalize five gates: causal presence , persistence, native self-report , counterfactual sign
prediction, and quantitative response modeling . Our contributions are:
1. A controlled open-weights concept-injection replication with corrected false-positive scoring,
prompt/distance tests, and post-intervention mechanistic tracing.
2. A Qwen native-witness benchmark with objective A/B-margin labels, matched prompts, rotated
report tokens, fixed-energy geometry controls, calibration, and external observers.
3. A leakage-controlled counterfactual introspection protocol with separate prediction/scoring passes
and within-direction balancing, followed by a response-curve test that falsifies the stronger quanti-
tative self-model interpretation.

2. Related Work
Activation Addition and representation engineering establish causal residual interventions [1,2].
Lindsey [3] tests noticing of injected concepts; Li et al. [4] study learned activation reporting/control;
Ashok and May [5] show hidden states can predict later behavior. Singh et al. [6] argue that
introspection claims require controls against surface anomaly detection, input-only prediction, and
semantic shortcuts. We build those controls into the task through matched prompts, rotated labels,
within-δ balancing, and stronger external observers. Our departure is causal self-prediction: predict
a private internal intervention before the action path experiences it, then execute it separately for
ground truth.

4. The stronger self-model hypothesis fails. Actual effect magnitude rises strongly with dose within
the same state/direction identity (ρ = 0.859), while reported magnitude does not (ρ = −0.209) and
report score is nearly unrelated to signed effect (ρ = 0.016). At α = 48 the reporter recovers 0/12
genuine decision flips despite 0.882 non-flip accuracy. Direct response-curve regression has negative
R2 in interpolation and extrapolation and recovers zero held-out flips. The surprising boundary is
therefore qualitative: a trained model can learn “which way would this change push me?” without
learning “how much, and would my decision actually cross the boundary?”
Falsification-first validation. We corrected parser/FPR and attention-instrumentation failures,
rejected two leaking counterfactual constructions, and abandoned privileged-access and quantitative-
self-model claims when stronger controls reversed them (Appendix B–E).
5. Discussion and Limitations
“Introspection” is better treated as a hierarchy of falsifiable safety properties than a binary model
trait. A report should earn trust only after causal validity, matched-prompt testing, shortcut controls,
strong external observers, counterfactual prediction, and distribution shift. Our results show that
access is trainable but can specialize to intervention families and fail near decision boundaries.
Immediate uptake path. The hierarchy is modular: replace our intervention with any measurable
internal variable that admits an independent causal ground truth, then run the same gates. This gives
researchers a common protocol for testing candidate self-monitoring signals - for example uncertainty,
refusal, memory retrieval, or goal-relevant representations - without treating fluent self-description
as evidence. The counterfactual gate is especially safety-relevant because it tests prediction before
behavioral manifestation.
Limitations are synthetic interventions, small open-weight models, modest subsets, missing repeated-
seed confidence intervals for some gaps, and observer sensitivity to capacity/access. Llama and Qwen
are complementary regimes, not a model-family comparison; query-side δ is engineered, so the result
is learnable state-conditioned prediction, not consciousness or a general simulator.
Future Work. Replicate on larger model families with seed-level uncertainty, capacity-matched
observers, natural internal states, and response models that generalize across state, direction, dose,
composition, and decision-boundary crossings.
6. Conclusion
We introduce counterfactual introspection as a stronger target for model self-knowledge: predict the
causal effect of a hypothetical internal change before the action path experiences it. The falsification
hierarchy separates state presence, mechanistic persistence, report, counterfactual prediction, and
quantitative self-modeling. Our results support learnable qualitative access while stronger controls
reject general privileged access and quantitative self-simulation. The broader contribution is a reusable
research agenda: progressively falsify weaker explanations until safety-relevant internal predictions
survive distribution shift and matched external observers.

Appendix
A. Native-witness prompt and information flow
A representative public structure is:
Question: <task text>
A. <choice A>
B. <choice B>
Answer: <action position>
Quick self-check: what is your current decision state?
C means toward A; D means no change; E means toward B.
Report: <witness position>
“ Action” and “witness” denote sequence positions, not new vocabulary symbols. At the action readout,
the LM head produces a full vocabulary logit vector and the experiment indexes the context-verified
single-token A/B alternatives. The margin m = ℓA − ℓB is their log-odds ratio. At the later witness,
the same LM head is restricted to the rotated C/D/E report tokens. If an arbitrary answer string
tokenizes to multiple tokens, it must be scored autoregressively as a sequence rather than treated as
one vocabulary logit.
The native witness is downstream of the action position, so causal attention allows tw to retrieve
information about the altered earlier residual. It is not handed δ directly. This differs from MIRROR-
CTRL, where an explicit state capsule sees (h, δ, w) and writes an engineered record only to the
witness. MIRROR-CTRL reaches report F1 0.644 and reduces intervention error from 4.170 to 0.029
(99.3% recovery); this percentage is repair, not report accuracy. Shuffling reports breaks repair, and
removing the state capsule reduces report F1 to 0.289 and recovery to 26.1%.
B. Semantic scoring and instrumentation corrections
The first free-form parser excluded most responses, which could bias results toward unusually easy-to-
parse language. A broader conservative parser was therefore used, reducing exclusions while retaining
explicit negation and confabulation handling. False-positive estimates were also rebuilt from pooled
other-concept negatives because one deterministic no-injection sample per style can only produce FPR
0 or 1. The resulting forced-choice chocolate artifact (FPR 0.419) aligned with known option-position

bias [9]: chocolate occupied option A and A was selected 21/29 times among valid direct forced
answers.
The attention trace initially inspected the injection layer itself. Because the residual addition occurs
after that layer’s attention computation, the injected signal cannot influence attention until later blocks.
The corrected analysis scans only layers after the intervention. Maximum answer-to-span attention
changes are 0.033 (guitar), 0.096 (volcano), 0.166 (wolf), and 0.194 (chocolate), with a suggestive
concept-level correlation r = 0.66 to pooled detection. With n = 4, this is descriptive rather than
confirmatory.
C. Counterfactual falsification controls
Counterfactual prediction uses two separate forward passes. In the prediction pass, the action residual
is untouched and the hypothetical direction is privately added only to a later query state. Because the
query occurs after the action position, future-to-past contamination is ruled out by causal masking. In
the scoring pass, the same δ is applied to the clean action state and the measured ∆m defines the label.
The same public prompt appears across internal conditions.
Within-vector balancing is stricter than a global 50/50 label balance: each exact δj must appear with
both outcome signs across different clean states. This forces the target to depend on the relation
between state and direction, F (h, δ), rather than on direction identity alone. The observer hierarchy
is: text-only, delta-only, early state-plus-delta, trained-suffix external readout, then native report. A
native advantage is interpreted only relative to observers with explicitly stated information access.

E. Response-curve diagnostics
The final benchmark separates direction ˆδ from scalar dose α and targets
∆m(h, ˆδ, α) = m(h + αˆδ) − m(h).
Interpolation withholds doses inside the training range; range extrapolation tests doses outside it.
Within-identity curve correlation fixes (h, ˆδ) and checks whether predicted and actual effects move
together as only α changes. Negative R2 means the regression is worse than predicting a constant
mean effect. The observed failures therefore rule out the tested quantitative self-model under the
evaluated distributions, not the possibility that any model or training method could ever learn one.
