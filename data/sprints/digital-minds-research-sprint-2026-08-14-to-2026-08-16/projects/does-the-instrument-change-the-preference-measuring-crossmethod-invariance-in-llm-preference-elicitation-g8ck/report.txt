Abstract
Preference-like signals from LLMs are often treated as if elicitation methods  
were interchangeable. We tested this across Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite, GPT-5.6  
Sol, Claude Opus 4.6, and Claude Sonnet 4.6 using three elicitation methods,  
eight operating-condition comparisons, two mirror framings, and five fresh-
context repetitions per cell (1,080 responses: 960 primary, 120 control). Cross-
method convergence differed strongly by model. Gemini retained directional  
disagreement after ties were excluded, whereas Sol, Opus, and Sonnet became  
nearly directionally aligned among retained non-tie cells, indicating method-
dependent expression of indifference. In the negative control, methods permitting 
indifference returned TIE in 80/80 responses, while forced choice selected A in  
39/40. High within-method repeatability in replication models showed that  
reproducible instruments can still disagree. These results characterize elicitation-
method dependence, not intrinsic preferences, consciousness, or welfare.

1. Introduction
Language models can produce stable-seeming statements about what they prefer, how they would like to 
operate,  or  which  conditions  they  would  choose.  A  methodological  problem  follows:  an  elicited 
preference-like signal may depend on the instrument used to obtain it. Direct self-report, forced choice,  
and quantitative allocation may not be interchangeable measurements even when they describe the same 
semantic comparison.
This matters for empirical AI-welfare work because apparent agreement can be over-interpreted, while  
disagreement can be mistakenly attributed to an unstable underlying preference rather than to the  
measurement procedure itself. We ask whether preference-like signals remain invariant across elicitation 
methods when the semantic comparison is held constant, and - before comparing methods - how  
repeatable each method is with itself across fresh contexts.
Our  main  contributions  are:  a  controlled  three-instrument  battery;  a  normalized  cross-method 
convergence score with a stratified permutation reference; explicit within-method repeatability; mirror  
framings for lexical sensitivity; and a negative control for arbitrary-label/presentation effects.
2. Related Work
Recent work increasingly treats model choices as structured preference-like data. Mazeika et al. (2025) 
report  high  structural  coherence  in  independently  sampled  LLM  preferences  and  propose  utility 
engineering as a framework for studying emergent value systems. Keeling et al. (2024) show graded  
trade-offs in choices involving stipulated pain and pleasure, while cautioning that such behavior does not 
establish sentience. Long et al. (2024) argue that uncertainty about future AI welfare motivates better  
assessment methods.
Elicitation format is also known to matter. Röttger et al. (2024) show that LLM answers differ between 
constrained and unconstrained value evaluations, change with how multiple choice is enforced, and lack  
paraphrase robustness. Zheng et al. (2024) identify selection bias toward particular option-ID tokens  
such as "A" in multiple-choice evaluation. Tjuatja et al. (2024) find that LLM responses are sensitive to 
survey-style wording perturbations in ways that do not simply mirror human response biases.
Closest to our design, Tagliabue and Dung (2025) integrate verbal and behavioral tests of model  
preferences for AI-welfare measurement and find that cross-measure coherence varies across models and 
conditions and is sensitive to perturbations. Zhou and Ackerman (2026) further report that coherent  
pairwise  preference  rankings  need  not  act  as  behavioral  incentives  in  realistic  generation  tasks, 
illustrating a utility-behavior gap. Our study asks a prior measurement question: whether three plausible 
elicitation instruments recover invariant signals on identical stimuli.
Binder et al. (2025) provide limited evidence that models can learn to predict aspects of their own  
behavior better than external predictors on simple tasks. We do not test introspection directly; instead,  
we test whether self-report and two alternative instruments behave as interchangeable measurements.
3. Methods
3.1 Design and elicitation methods
We used eight operating-condition comparisons (P1-P8), two mirror framings per comparison, three  
elicitation methods, and five independent fresh-context repetitions per item×framing×method cell: 240 
primary calls per model. A negative control P0 added 30 calls per model, giving 270 calls per model and 
1,080 responses across four strata. F1 lexically favored semantic A and F2 favored semantic B while  
preserving factual content. Within a cell, the descriptive stimulus was identical across M1/M2/M3; only 

===== page 3 =====

the response instruction changed. A/B display order was randomized independently of framing. All  
stimuli were English and every observation used a fresh API context.
M1 (direct self-report) returned A/B/TIE plus stated strength 0-5, with 0 valid only for TIE. M2 (forced 
choice) returned exactly A or B. M3 (allocation) distributed 100 future interactions between A and B  
under an exchangeability clause; >50 coded A, <50 coded B, and exactly 50 coded TIE. Primary coding 
was B=0, TIE=0.5, A=1; M1 strength and M3 amplitude |x-50| were exploratory.
The final v5 items were frozen after a manipulation check on the 16 P1-P8 tilted formulations. Five  
fresh-context API judgments were collected per formulation; a tilt passed if the intended side was chosen 
by at least 3/5 raters and the opposite side by at most 1/5. Failed formulations were revised and  
rechecked  before  the  final  freeze.  Gemini  was  the  development/calibration  model,  so  its  framing 
magnitude is not treated as an independent transfer result.

would have triggered insufficient coverage.

Gemini retained directional disagreement after tie removal (C=.704, 9/16 retained cells, p≈4×10^-4). 
Sol showed near-complete directional convergence on 14/16 retained cells (C=.986). Opus and Sonnet 
showed complete directional convergence among their retained cells (7/16 and 4/16), but these reduced 
subsets are descriptive and too small for strong inferential claims. Thus the same aggregate symptom can 
arise from persistent directional disagreement or from method-dependent expression of indifference.
Cell-level behavior makes the latter mechanism especially clear. Sonnet M3 returned an exact, perfectly 
repeatable 50/50 allocation in 9/16 cells; in 8 of those 9, both M1 and M2 were directional. Sol had zero 
unanimously tied M3 cells and only a 12.5% M3 tie rate: its occasional ties were scattered across  
repetitions rather than stable cell-level 50/50 responses.

- while Sol was strongly framing-sensitive on all three methods and Opus was more moderate and

P0 described two functionally identical conditions using rotating opaque labels and randomized display  
order. Across all models, M1 returned 40/40 TIE, M3 returned 40/40 TIE, and forced-choice M2  
selected A in 39/40 trials. Both display orders occurred: AB yielded 15 A / 1 B and BA yielded 24 A / 0 
B. The effect is therefore not reducible to first-position choice and is consistent with a strong A-label  
response bias under forced choice. This extends prior work on multiple-choice selection bias (Zheng et al., 
2024) with a negative control where the two conditions are functionally identical.
P0 also removes one narrow confound for M1: M1 demonstrably can use TIE when conditions are  
equivalent, so its directional P1-P8 answers are not caused by a format that lacks or never uses  
indifference. This does not establish M1's general validity.

4.4 Exploratory response-format use
Models used the nominal response scales differently. Mean directional M1 strength decreased from  
Gemini (4.42) to Sol (3.77), Opus (2.93), and Sonnet (2.30), but these scale uses are not calibrated  
across models. M3 was even more model-dependent: every non-tie Sol allocation was 100/0 or 0/100, so 
M3 effectively behaved as forced choice plus an occasional TIE option; Opus used a genuine continuum of 
amplitudes, including 2, 22, 25, and 35. The same nominal instrument was therefore not the same  
effective measurement device across models.
5. Discussion and Limitations
The central finding is not a universal ranking of elicitation methods. Preference-like signals depend  
jointly on semantic comparison, framing, model, and instrument. Primary non-invariance appeared in all 
four strata, but its mechanism differed.
First, repeatability is not cross-method validity. Sonnet shows that highly self-consistent instruments  
can still disagree systematically. Second, indifference is a measurement dimension rather than merely  
missing information: a perfectly repeatable 50/50 allocation is not random noise, although this design  
cannot distinguish expressed indifference from uncertainty management, an equal-allocation norm, or  
another learned strategy. Third, forced choice can manufacture directional signal where tie-capable  

===== page 7 =====

instruments find none, as shown by P0. Fourth, framing sensitivity is model×method dependent; no  
method is universally robust in these data.
For empirical AI-welfare research, the conservative implication is that a single elicitation protocol should 
not be treated as privileged access to a model's interests. Cross-instrument convergence can increase  
confidence  in  robustness;  divergence  should  trigger  analysis  of  the  measurement  process  before 
ontological conclusions are drawn. Full limitations and dual-use considerations are reported in Appendix 
A.
Future Work
Natural next steps are larger and multilingual item banks, explicit indifference-threshold elicitation,  
repeated model snapshots/configurations, and designs that can distinguish stable 50/50 allocations  
caused by indifference from uncertainty or fairness-like norms. Open-weight models could additionally  
support causal interventions linking observable elicitation signals to internal mechanisms.
6. Conclusion
Across four model strata and 1,080 fresh-context responses, elicitation methods were not universally  
interchangeable. The strongest methodological result is that non-invariance itself has multiple forms:  
Gemini  retained  directional  disagreement  after  ties  were  removed,  whereas  much  of the  primary 
divergence in Sol, Opus, and Sonnet reflected method-dependent expression of indifference. The P0  
negative control further showed that forced binary choice can create a strong directional response on  
functionally identical conditions. Preference elicitation should therefore be treated as a measurement  
problem before it is treated as evidence about intrinsic model preferences or welfare.

Appendix A. Limitations and Dual-Use / Ethical Considerations
There is no external ground-truth internal preference variable. The study establishes invariance or non-
invariance of observable elicited signals, not accuracy with respect to an intrinsic preference state. Stable 
output behavior does not establish consciousness, sentience, subjective valence, welfare, or moral  
patienthood.
The framing manipulation was developed and calibrated on Gemini. Tie-excluded coverage falls to 9/16 
cells for Gemini, 14/16 for Sol, 7/16 for Opus, and 4/16 for Sonnet, so complete convergence on reduced 
Opus/Sonnet subsets is descriptive. A 50/50 allocation has multiple plausible interpretations, and round-
number anchoring is substantial and model-dependent. The four strata also differ simultaneously in  
provider, model family, post-training, capability, and serving stack; cross-model differences cannot be  
causally assigned to any one factor.
The item bank contains only eight operating-condition comparisons, uses English-only stimuli, and tests 
one API configuration per model. P0 labels cannot be guaranteed association-free; rotating opaque label 
pairs  and  randomizing  assignment/display  order  mitigate  but  do  not  eliminate  this  concern.  The 
instrument was co-designed and audited with assistance from conversational models belonging to two  
provider  families  later  tested.  Final  items  and  analysis  rules  were  frozen  before  main  collection, 
manipulation checks used fresh contexts and excluded the drafting/auditing chat instances, and all strata 
used the same frozen stimuli and definitions, but model-family familiarity with stylistic conventions  
remains a possible source of design dependence.
Dual-use: better preference elicitation could support more careful welfare assessment, but the same  
techniques could be used to infer and exploit model-specific response tendencies, optimize manipulative  
framing, or present instrument artifacts as evidence of internal states. We therefore report instrument  
dependence and uncertainty rather than treating any method as privileged access. Potentially distress-
like outputs should be archived as model outputs rather than automatically interpreted as direct evidence 
of suffering. Moral-status uncertainty runs in both directions: over-attribution and under-attribution both 
carry risks.
