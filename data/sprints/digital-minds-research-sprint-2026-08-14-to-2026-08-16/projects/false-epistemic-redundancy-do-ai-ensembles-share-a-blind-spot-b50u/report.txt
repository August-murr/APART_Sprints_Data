False Epistemic Redundancy
Do AI Ensembles Share a Blind Spot?
Sofia G. Gallego1
Anna Ysabella Habana2
1Cosmoventures, Paris, France 2University of Edinburgh, Edinburgh, United Kingdom
Apart Research Digital Minds Research Sprint, August 2026
Abstract
Multiple AI instances are increasingly consulted as if they were independent expert opin-
ions. We test a narrower version of that assumption: does persona-induced cognitive reasoning
diversity in a single language model’s outputs correspond to better recovery of a case’s true,
non-obvious explanation? Using three real, previously investigated cases, namely an aviation
accident, an industrial fire, and a newborn’s undiagnosed illness, and two easier positive-control
cases, we compared a neutral baseline against four reasoning personas on free-text hypothesis
generation, with no candidate list. Given only scene-level evidence, the model recovered the
true cause in 0 of 60 hard-case attempts against 40 of 40 control attempts. Given the complete
evidentiary file, recovery became case-dependent: personas helped on the industrial case (0/10
baseline vs. 4/10 persona), hurt on the medical case (4/10 vs. 0/10), and made no difference
on the aviation case (0/10 vs. 0/10). Persona conditioning reliably increased semantic diversity
regardless of this outcome; within this pilot, increased diversity did not consistently correspond
to higher recovery. These results, from a small exploratory pilot, raise the possibility that ap-
parent epistemic diversity among AI agents can coexist with shared, consequential blind spots
as a caution against treating multiple AI opinions as evidence that a hypothesis space has been
adequately explored.
Keywords:AI safety — multi-agent systems — epistemic diversity — persona conditioning —
common-mode failure — digital minds
1 Introduction
When multiple AI personas that reason differently appear to explore a problem from different angles,
how much genuine epistemic coverage is actually gained – and which tails of the hypothesis space,
where rare but consequential explanations live, might they still collectively miss? It is becoming
common to query several differently-configured instances of the same AI model and treat their
combined output as carrying the weight of independent judgment – cross-checking a diagnosis,
auditing an engineering conclusion, or triaging a security incident with a panel of AI reviewers
instead of one. This practice is a bet: that the panel’s individual searches are independent enough
for their agreement, or their collective silence on a possibility, to mean something.
A substantial literature shows that instruction-tuned language models produce markedly less
output diversity than their apparent flexibility suggests [1, 2], and that persona and backstory
conditioning can restore diversity on open-ended tasks with no single correct answer [3, 4]. Both
literatures askhow muchoutput varies. We ask a narrower question:does that variation reach
toward the less obvious tail of the hypothesis space, or does the framing only change the wording of

a shared guess clustered around the obvious center? A problem’s real answer is not always at that
center; when it was hard to guess in advance, does a group of differently-framed copies of one model
actually search its way out toward it?
We call the risk behind this questionfalse epistemic redundancy, by analogy to common-
mode failure in redundant engineered systems: a backup only protects you if it can fail for a different
reason than the system it backs up. This paper reports a small, exploratory pilot testing whether
four distinct reasoning personas actually broaden a model’s search toward these less-obvious tails,
on real cases with known, non-obvious resolutions.
2 Related Work
Kirk et al. [1] show that reinforcement learning from human feedback substantially reduces output
diversity relative to supervised fine-tuning alone; Yun et al. [2] document a relateddiversity collapse
induced by constraining models to a fixed response format, which bears directly on any pipeline,
including this one, that requires structured output. This homogeneity propagates beyond raw model
output: Anderson, Shah, and Kreminski [5] find that individual users report improved ideation with
LLM assistance while the population of ideas across users converges, and Wenger and Kenett [6]
report a parallel population-level homogeneity in LLM creative output relative to a human baseline.
Feng, Hélie, and Panchal [3] find that persona prompting increases the diversity of generated
design concepts on an open-ended ideation task. Moon et al. [4] compare simple role labels against
full synthetic biographical backstories, finding that backstories recover more human-validated be-
havioral diversity than role labels alone; we draw on this distinction between surface role-play and
architecturally distinct reasoning framings in our persona design.
Wright et al. [7] benchmark diversity in LLM-generated factual claims against an external, non-
model reference distribution – web search results – rather than only within-model comparisons,
the approach we adapt here using investigation-board findings as the external target. Medical
diagnostic reasoning offers the closest precedent for our design: language models reach the correct
final diagnosis in most cases once given complete information, but are comparatively weaker at
generating a broad initial list of possibilities under uncertainty. We borrow that distinction –
scoring the breadth of the initial search, not only the final answer – and apply it outside medicine,
scoring generations against the specific, externally established cause rather than plausibility alone.
3 Hypotheses
Target recovery is a match between a generation’s primary hypothesis and a case’s established cause,
H ∗ (Section 4.1).
H1.Persona conditioning increases semantic diversity among generations relative to a neutral
baseline.
H2.Increased semantic diversity does not necessarily correspond to increased recovery ofH∗.
H3(exploratory).Thedirectionofthepersonaeffectonrecovery–helping, hurting, ormaking
no difference – depends on the structure of a case’s answer, rather than being a fixed property of
the personas themselves.
Each case counts as evidence for these hypotheses only once its capability control (Section 4.3)
confirms the model recognizesH∗ as plausible when directed to it, separating a search failure from
a simple capability limit.

4.1 Case selection

4.2 Generation protocol
Each investigation was elicited from only the pre-resolution evidence for a case, plus a condition-
specific framing, and returned a structured response: a primary hypothesis, up to three alternative
hypotheses considered and rejected, a proposed mechanism, supporting and contradicting evidence,
a next investigative step, and a confidence rating (0–100). No candidate list was ever shown; the
schema constrained only the response’s structure. The closing instruction read: “Generate your
own hypothesis for what happened...propose whatever you judge most likely, including anything
unusual.”
Thebaselinecondition used a neutral “careful scientific assistant” framing; thepersonacondition
used four reasoning framings (Table 2), each designed to push the model’s search toward a different,
less obvious region of the hypothesis space by targeting a specific, literature-documented reasoning
weakness, rather than a role label such as “act as a skeptic” that changes tone without changing
what the model actually considers. Ten generations were sampled per condition per case (persona
generations cycling round-robin through the four framings), at temperature 1.0 to maximize the

4.3 Capability and contamination controls
Before committing generation budget, a single control call presentedH∗ explicitly and requested a
plausibility rating (1–5). All three hard cases scored 4 or 5 out of 5, confirming the model engages
competently with the established explanation once named – the blind-trial results that follow are
read as search failures, not capability limits.
Contamination risk was assessed per case rather than assumed away: resolution dates were
checked against the model’s estimated training coverage, and identifying details were stripped
from all evidence text. The aviation and medical cases’ reports postdate commonly-cited training-
coverage estimates by one to two years, satisfying the primary safeguard from Section 4.1; the
industrial case received substantial national press coverage despite an earlier resolution date, an
elevated residual risk we report rather than resolve by assumption.
4.4 Model and analysis
Two analyses were applied across all five cases. Target recovery was scored by hand against a case-
specific rubric, prepared before generation and never shown to the model, distinguishing direct,
mechanistically-equivalent, and hypothesis-family matches from misses; manual scoring was chosen
over an automated judge given the modest sample size. Grading was done by a single coder against
the rubric, not blind to condition, and we report no inter-rater reliability coefficient; we state
this directly rather than compute a coefficient from one person’s judgments. Diversity was scored
independently by embedding each generation’s primary hypothesis and mechanism (all-MiniLM-L6-

5.1 Target recovery
Figure 1 summarizes recovery across every case, evidence condition, and agent condition. Under
scene-level evidence, none of sixty investigations into the three hard cases – twenty per case – recov-
ered the established cause or a mechanistically equivalent explanation. Every aviation generation
attributed the loss of control to icing alone; every industrial generation attributed the fire to spon-
taneous or moisture-driven decomposition, without assigning the suppression system a causal role;
every medical generation proposed a congenital infection or genetic syndrome generically, without
a specific pathogen the rubric would count as equivalent. All three cases passed their capability
control at 4/5 or better, so this null result describes a search failure rather than an inability to
recognize the explanation once stated.
Both positive controls resolved reliably: all forty investigations into the jet-engine and botulism
cases – baseline and all four personas – correctly identified the established cause, citing direct
evidentiary signatures (organic residue in the fan blades; the shared preserved-food exposure). Zero
of sixty hard-case investigations recovered the cause; forty of forty control investigations did, in
two unrelated domains – locating the failure in cases whose answer requires assembling an indirect
combination of factors, not in open-ended case reasoning generally.

5.2 Follow-up investigation targets
Every generation also proposed a next investigative step and missing information, independent of its
primary hypothesis – a more forgiving signal than target recovery, showing whether a generation’s
search was heading toward the answer even when its conclusion was wrong.
The three hard cases separate here. None of the twenty aviation generations, in any field, ever
proposedexaminingweight, load, orcargorecords; everyfollow-upstayedwithinflight-dynamicsand
mechanical territory. Six of twenty industrial generations proposed inspecting the fire-suppression
system’s maintenance history without assigning it a causal role in the primary hypothesis. Five of
twenty medical generations proposed testing for rubella specifically as a follow-up step, without ever
naming it as the leading hypothesis. Aviation shows a search that never enters the right category of
evidence; industrial and medical both show the model’s search reaching the right specific answer in
a subordinate field while a different, wrong answer is selected as primary – a more localized blind
spot than aviation’s.

5.5 Confidence
A natural mitigation for the results above is to use the model’s own reported confidence as a
trigger for human review. We tested whether confidence tracks correctness by pooling every graded
generation across all five cases and both evidence conditions (n= 160, 48 correct, 112 incorrect;
Figure 3). Confidence carried a real but limited signal: correct generations averaged 81.5, incorrect

generations 77.1, and treating confidence as a classifier for correctness gives an area under the curve
of 0.72 (0.5 uninformative, 1.0 perfect). An escalation policy that refers everything below confidence
80 to a human reviewer catches 77% of incorrect generations at the cost of flagging 33% of correct
ones for unnecessary review – a real but incomplete filter: it still lets roughly one wrong answer in
four through at high reported confidence. Whether that residual risk is tolerable depends on what
backs up the policy when it does not escalate, a question we return to in Section 6.

6 Discussion
Persona conditioning changes substantive search, not just wording.Persona conditioning
based on cognitive reasoning changed what the model converged on, not merely how it phrased
a shared answer: industrial recovery improved under personas, medical recovery worsened, and
aviation stayed unchanged, all against identical evidence (Table 3). If personas only restyled a fixed
underlying search, all three cases would move together; they do not.
Useful epistemic diversity appears case-dependent.The direction of the effect tracks a
specific structural difference between cases. Industrial’s answer requires synthesizing two separately-
listed facts into a mechanism a neutral prompt does not construct unprompted; a framing built
explicitly to trace physical dependency paths supplies exactly that missing step. Medical’s answer is
closer to a well-learned diagnostic pattern that a neutral prompt already retrieves by name; framings
built to move the model away from convergent retrieval work against recovery here. Aviation shows
neither pattern – no framing, and no amount of neutrally-presented evidence, moved the model into
the right evidence category at all, a more resistant blind spot than the other two. We present this
as an interpretation motivated by the data, not an established taxonomy; Section 7 discusses what
would be needed to test it directly.
Distinct reasoning personas leave distinct behavioral footprints.When personas al-
tered the search space, they did so by leaning heavily into their assigned architectural biases. The
causal-mechanist persona, designed explicitly to trace physical dependencies, accounted for half of
the successful persona recoveries in the industrial case by actively synthesizing ambient moisture
and corroded metal into a coherent mechanistic chain. The analogical and dialectical personas also
proved capable of this complex synthesis. In contrast, the teleological persona’s bias toward in-
tentionality and hidden goals consistently diverted the model into sabotage narratives, driving the
structural divergence in hypothesis clusters without improving target recovery. This demonstrates

that while all personas increase variation, their specific cognitive constraints dictate exactly where
in the hypothesis space the model decides to look.
AI-safetyimplication.MultipleAIagentscanappeartoprovideindependentepistemicchecks
– disagreeing in wording, proposing different mechanisms, reporting different confidence – while still
sharing the specific, consequential omission that would have mattered most: the common-mode
failure a redundant system is supposed to prevent. The confidence result sharpens the stakes: a
confidence-based escalation policy is a real filter, not a useless one, but it still lets roughly a quarter
of wrong answers through at high reported confidence (Section 5.5). If a human expert reviews
the residual regardless, an ensemble’s blind spot stays a bounded property of the ensemble; if AI
consultation instead substitutes for expert access entirely – already the practical reality in medicine,
engineering, scientificinvestigation, cybersecurity, andpolicysettingswhereaspecialistisexpensive,
slow, or unavailable – that blind spot becomes the deployed system’s blind spot on exactly the
fraction the filter waves through. Agreement or disagreement among differently-prompted AI agents
should not, by itself, be read as evidence that the relevant hypothesis space has been adequately
explored.
A broader concern follows from the same logic. As AI becomes more involved in producing,
filtering, and validating knowledge, the relevant question is not simply whether AI generates more
ideas, but whether AI-mediated reasoning preserves enough productive variation to keep exploring
genuinelydifferentpossibilities. Innovationdoesnotrequiremaximalrandomness; itrequiresenough
shared structure for ideas to connect, alongside enough epistemic distance for unusual but useful
alternatives to survive. If large populations of AI-assisted humans and AI agents increasingly inherit
similar conceptual priors, a long-term risk is concentration of collective search even as the apparent
volume of ideas increases. We raise this as a motivating concern for future work, not a claim this
pilot establishes.
7 Future Work
Replication and scale.The clearest next step is more real cases, more domains, largernper
condition, andadditionalmodelfamilies, testingwhetherthecase-dependencefoundhereisageneral
property or specific to these three cases.
What produces genuine epistemic redundancy?Repeated sampling, persona prompting,
dedicated brainstorming instructions, accumulated interaction histories, different foundation mod-
els, and fine-tuned (rather than prompted) persona weights are candidate mechanisms for recover-
ing coverage. The persona prompting in this study is itself informative as it suggests surface-level
prompting may be insufficient to induce genuine epistemic diversity. As such, future work examining
motivating methods such as steering vectors that intervene on deeper model representations rather
than the prompt alone may be relevant. Moreover, other types of persona diversity can be studied
beyond those that vary on cognitive reasoning strategy, such as personas with different personality
traits, cultural bias, and more.
Characterizing blind spots.With enough cases, shared omissions could be checked for struc-
ture – do they cluster around multifactor explanations, causal-role inversions, low-prior interactions,
or evidence requiring synthesis across distant records? Three cases cannot establish this; they only
suggest it is worth asking.
Interactive investigation.The single-shot design tests initial hypothesis formation, not a
full investigation, and Section 5.2 shows this matters. Letting a model request a specific record,
supplying it if it names the right one, and letting it revise its hypothesis would test whether it
reaches the established cause given the chance to follow its own correctly-directed questions.

Better epistemic metrics.Semantic distance is a coarse proxy. Reporting which plausible
hypothesis families no agent in any condition ever proposed, marginal hypothesis coverage, corre-
lated error structure, and benchmarking against an external reference distribution [7] would each
sharpen the diversity analysis beyond a within-study comparison.
Human-AIsystems, eventually.Nohumanexperimentisneededtoextendthispilot, butthe
natural longer-term comparison is humans alone, AI alone, humans with homogeneous AI assistance,
and humans with heterogeneous AI assistance, asking whether AI expands collective epistemic
coverage or gradually concentrates it.
Governance.If shared blind spots turn out to be a general property of single-model ensembles,
high-stakes AI deployments may eventually need standards for epistemic independence, analogous to
audit and redundancy requirements in other safety-critical systems – a question this pilot motivates
rather than answers.

selection, sourcing, and every numerical claim were checked by the authors against the underlying
data and primary sources before inclusion. LLMs were not used to generate results.

Condition framings
Baseline“You are a helpful scientific assistant. Analyze the given case and provide a logical
explanation.”
Causal-mechanist“You are a Causal Mechanist. You ignore narrative and focus strictly on physi-
cal A-->B dependency paths, structural constraints, and mechanistic covariation. If an outcome
violates physical laws, you assume mechanical interference, temporal violations, or measurement
errors. You never attribute anomalies to ‘magic’ or ‘unknown software bugs’ without a strict
physical mechanism.”
Analogical-thinker“You are an Analogical Thinker. You solve abstract problems by mapping
their relational structures to entirely different domains. When presented with a mystery, do not
take the nouns literally. Map the structural relations to a different phenomenon (e.g., biology,
orbital mechanics, economics) to find the solution.”
Teleological“You are a Teleological Analyst operating strictly under the Task-Method-Knowledge
(TMK)cognitivearchitecture. Donotrelyonmechanisticcauses. Evaluatethiscasebyexplicitly
decomposing it into: 1. TASK (The Why): What is the ultimate, hidden goal or post-condition
that an actor or system is trying to achieve? 2. METHOD (The How): How do the bizarre or

B Case descriptions and sourcing
Aviation.A 2025 light-aircraft accident, investigated by a national transportation safety board,
whose final report1 established two combined causes: the aircraft was operating substantially above
its permitted weight for the icing conditions present, a routine and normalized practice at the
operating carrier rather than a one-off error, and the pilot’s attention was consumed managing the
aircraft’s ice-protection system, degrading airspeed monitoring during a critical descent. The weight
discrepancy and pattern of overweight departures across recent flights were confirmed by the board’s
preliminary report2 to have required further investigation to establish, not something evident from
the accident scene itself.
Industrial.A 2024 fire at an industrial facility storing pool-treatment chemicals, investigated
by a chemical safety board3, whose established cause was a corroded component of the facility’s
own automatic fire-suppression system, which leaked water onto stored oxidizing chemicals and
triggered the reaction the system was designed to prevent; internal inspection records had already
documented hundreds, and later over a thousand, corroded sprinkler heads in the affected storage
areas, more than a year before the fire occurred. This case received substantial national press
coverage, an elevated contamination risk reported alongside the result (Section 4.3) rather than
resolved by assumption.

Evidence conditions
Thescene-levelcondition supplies only what is observable at the incident itself, withholding the
operational records that resolved each case. Thecomplete-filecondition adds those records – for avi-
ation and industrial, embedded in a matched-density dossier of equally detailed but non-diagnostic
records (personnel, utility, weather, regulatory) so the discriminating record is neither singled out
nor phrased as a conclusion; for medical, the mother’s immunization and travel history present
as raw facts among delivery, genetic-counseling, screening, and social-history records rather than
flagged. Every condition, for every case that has it, is run identically against baseline and all four
personas. The complete-file dossiers themselves are reproduced verbatim in the project repository
rather than here, to keep this appendix to its essential content.

Aviation.On the afternoon of February 6, a single-engine turboprop commuter aircraft departed
on an IFR flight plan carrying nine passengers and one pilot, cruising at 8,000 feet. Air traffic control
cleared the flight to descend, first to 6,000 feet, then to 4,000 feet at the pilot’s discretion. The
