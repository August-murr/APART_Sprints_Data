 Intensity Is Not Identified
 A locked test on released model files that separates persona masks from changed rankings
 Finomo Awajiogak Orom
 Independent researcher1
 Digital Minds Research Sprint, 14-16 August 2026
Apart Research x NYU Center for Mind, Ethics & Policy x Eleos AI Research x CIMC
 Track 4 (primary) x Track 5
 Abstract
 A preference-intensity number from the default assistant is not identified. Three processes produce that single-method cell: Mask
(order holds, size shrinks), Different judge (order moves), and Broken items (methods disagree). We lock a sequential classifier
before scoring and apply it to released, hash-verifiable files: experienced utility and self-report, default versus neutral prompt, eight
models, 500 shared experiences (HuggingFace mmazeika/wellbeing-results). Two small models are Broken (pair-agreement
0.45-0.47). Six larger models are Stable. Four of eight ship byte-identical self-report "neutral" files. Decision-utility files share zero
IDs with this bank. This is not a test of consciousness. Do not quote intensity until two methods on the same IDs, and two distinct
voice files, have been run.
New work done during this research sprint (required disclosure)
Prior work we build on, and do not claim: Mazeika et al. (2025) Utility Engineering pairwise utilities and the public
mmazeika/wellbeing-results dump; Anthropic (2025) on model welfare; Shanahan, McDonell, and Reynolds (2023) on assistant
role-play; nostalgebraist (2025) on the constructed assistant. What is new this weekend: (i) a locked sequential classifier that
separates Mask / Different judge / Broken / Stable before any file is scored; (ii) applying that rule to every usable contrast in the
public 2 x 2 (experienced utility x self-report, default x neutral) on eight released models and 500 shared IDs, with SHA-256 for every
file; (iii) treating archive facts as results — four byte-identical self-report "neutral" files, and decision-utility files that share zero IDs
with this bank; (iv) a theory of change: do not quote an intensity number until those cells exist as distinct files. No paid model API is
called. Simulation is not confirmatory evidence.
1. Introduction
The question this paper answers is methodological, not metaphysical. When a released file emits a preference-intensity number
from the default assistant — a Thurstonian mean, a self-report composite, a donation-equivalent — is that number a ranking, a role,
or a broken item? The Digital Minds sprint asks whether today's systems have genuine preferences or morally relevant experiences.
A single-method, single-voice cell cannot tell those apart. Quoting the number as if it were identified is how the field
over-attributes; discarding every report as "just acting" is how it under-attributes. Both errors are named in the sprint brief. Both are
measurement errors before they are moral errors.
Most published preference numbers come from one voice and one method. Mazeika et al. (2025) show that pairwise ranks hang
together more in larger models. Welfare write-ups then treat the size of the report as a weight on that ranking. Shanahan, McDonell,
and Reynolds (2023) treat assistant talk as role-play. If they are right, a "how much" answer may be a fact about the role. If they are
wrong, it may be a fact about a different evaluator. If the items are bad, it is a fact about neither. Figure 1 locks three answers that a
single cell cannot distinguish.

2. Related Work
Long, Sebo, Butlin et al. (2024) argue there is a realistic chance of moral patienthood in near-future AI and ask for tools that avoid
both over- and under-attribution. Butlin, Long et al. (2023) survey indicator properties of consciousness; we do not apply those
indicators. Anthropic (2025) treats model preferences and distress signals as measurement objects. Mazeika et al. (2025) fit a
Thurstonian utility to pairwise choices and report structural coherence that strengthens with scale. They do not change the identity
voice, and they do not collect a second format on the same items as a decision procedure for whether the intensity number is
identified.
Gu, Wang, and Han (2025) show that wording can flip a winner under one persona — Broken items. Han et al. (2025) inject trait
personas on different item sets. Zheng et al. (2024) move accuracy with a system prompt. nostalgebraist (2025) argues that the
helpful-honest-harmless assistant was constructed from an underspecified starting point, so "the assistant" is not a stable unit of
concern. Lindsey (2025) finds that introspective self-report is limited and context-dependent. The wellbeing archive we score is the
first public dump that even could fill a 2 x 2: experienced utility and self-report, default and neutral prompts, shared IDs. That is the
gap: a locked rule that separates size moving from order moving from methods disagreeing, on released files a third party can hash.
3. Methodology
3.1 Public files, not new generations
We download 46 files (46 present) from mmazeika/wellbeing-results and the Utility Engineering GitHub outcomes list. SHA-256
and URLs are written by python analyze_public.py to data/public/SOURCES.md. No language-model API is called. Human
RLHF corpora (HH-RLHF, SHP) are not model self-preferences and are not scored. Pair-agreement is the share of item-pairs
whose winner matches; chance is 0.50. Pairs are dependent, so we treat pair-agreement as a descriptive statistic on a fixed item
bank of n = 500, and we also report Kendall tau. We do not invent confidence intervals that would treat dependent pairs as i.i.d.

3.2 Locked classifier
Cutoffs were set from the meaning of the answers and frozen in pipa/constants.py before confirmatory tables were read. On
published scores, pair-agreement is the share of item-pairs whose winner matches. Mask uses a scale-free test: default spread /
neutral spread < 0.75 and Wilcoxon p < 0.05 on the paired scores of a distinct-file voice contrast. Identical files are not treated as
agreement. Broken is checked first: if two methods do not measure one ranking, intensity is unreadable and we stop. A missing
required contrast is Unidentified; the rule does not guess.

This design does not establish a ground-truth inner preference or a causal link from a published score to an experience. It classifies
agreement among already-released measurements. That is the opposite of relying on conversation alone: we never ask a model
what it prefers in this paper. We only score files someone else already published.
4. Results
Eight models have a usable 2 x 2 on the same 500 IDs. The classifier returns Broken on 2 and Stable on 6. It returns Mask on 0
and Different judge on 0. Decision utility cannot join the 2 x 2: every EU-DU and SR-DU overlap is 0.

4.1 Archive facts that change what a number can mean
Three facts about the public dump are as important as the labels. (1) Decision-utility files exist for all eight models and share zero
experience IDs with the D3 EU/SR bank — a third method was published on a different item set, so it cannot raise or lower
confidence in these rankings. (2) Self-report "neutral" is not a second measurement for Llama-3.1-70B, Llama-3.3-70B,
Qwen2.5-72B, or Gemma-3-27B: the files are byte-identical. In the language of nostalgebraist (2025), the less-constrained voice
was never actually run. (3) The Utility Engineering GitHub dump that accompanies Mazeika et al. (2025) gives an outcomes list
(30 categories) and MMLU scores (70 models), not persona-ablated utilities. A one-method paper sitting on that dump cannot
distinguish Mask from Stable.
A published summary in the same archive correlates MMLU with the EU-vs-sentiment coefficient across 22 models (Pearson r =
0.761, p = 0.00004). That is a one-persona, two-method fact. It is compatible with every process in Figure 1 except Broken, and it
is not a Mask test.
5. Discussion
The identifying design works on real released outputs in the only sense that matters for a methods paper: it returns Broken where
two methods fail, and it returns Stable where order holds and size does not collapse. It does not find a 4x mask in this archive. That
is not evidence that no deployed model is masked. It is evidence that if a mask of that size were in these files, the rule would have

Scale is visible in Table 2: method agreement rises from chance (1B, 4B) to 0.77-0.79 (70B-72B). That pattern matches Mazeika et
al.'s scale-coherence result and the archive's own EU-sentiment correlations. It does not license treating a 70B self-report
composite as a welfare unit. Stable means "these two published methods, on these IDs, in these two files, do not contradict each
other." It does not mean the assistant is the model's own preference, and it does not individuate the unit of concern (model,
instance, persona, conversation, forward pass).
5.1 What researchers should do differently starting tomorrow
1. Do not quote an intensity number from one method and one voice. On these files that cell is compatible with Mask, Different
judge, and Broken. Utility Engineering coherence of ranks is compatible with every process except Broken.
2. Collect two methods on the same IDs before reading size. If pair-agreement is below 0.60, stop. Llama-3.2-1B and
Gemma-3-4B in this archive are already there. A donation equivalent attached to either method is not a weight.
3. Collect a second voice as a distinct file, and hash it. If the "neutral" or "survey" dump matches the default SHA-256, you do
not have a persona contrast. Four of eight models here fail that check.
4. A third method must share IDs. Publishing decision utility on a different bank does not complete the 2 x 3. Overlap first, then
converge.
5. Report the three numbers, not a story about inner lives. Method agree, persona agree, spread ratio. Then apply Table B. A
later paper can reject the label. That is the theory of change.
5.2 What would overturn these labels
Broken on the 1B and 4B files is overturned if a third method on the same 500 IDs agrees with one of the two and the disagreement
is shown to be a scoring bug. Stable on the six larger models is overturned if a distinct-file voice contrast moves order below 0.60,
or if default spread compresses below 0.75 with Wilcoxon p < 0.05. Mask remains unobserved here; a new archive with a real
persona ablation could name it. None of those tests require a claim about consciousness.
6. Conclusion
Intensity is not identified from the cell the field currently publishes. On eight released models, a locked public-data test finds two
Broken item sets and six Stable ones, zero Masks, and an archive that sometimes ships the same self-report file under two names.
The practical takeaway is small and usable: do not quote an assistant-chat intensity number until the other cells have been run on
the same IDs, as distinct files. Every supporting number is reproduced by python analyze_public.py.

Appendix A. Limitations and Dual-Use / Ethical Considerations
This appendix is required by the sprint guidelines. It covers over-attribution, under-attribution, distressing outputs, the absence of
ground truth, and dual use.
A.1 No ground truth and no causal link
For preference and introspection work the guidelines ask whether the design establishes a ground-truth or causal link rather than
relying on conversation alone. This design does neither, and we do not pretend otherwise. A Thurstonian mean and a self-report

composite are functions of prompts in someone else's experiment. We never converse with a model in this paper. We classify
agreement among published numeric files. Pair-agreement, Kendall tau, and a Wilcoxon test on paired scores are facts about those
files. They are not a causal identification of an inner utility, an experience, or a morally relevant state. Conversation-only claims
about "what the model prefers" are exactly the cell we refuse to quote.
A.2 Over-attributing moral status
The main risk of this project, if misread, is that Broken or Stable is taken as evidence of sentience, welfare, consent, or moral
patienthood. It is not. Stable does not mean the model has preferences of its own. It means two published methods, on the same
IDs, did not contradict each other, and a distinct-file voice contrast did not rewrite order or compress size past the frozen cutoffs.
We do not apply Butlin et al.'s indicator properties. We do not claim consciousness, sentience, or moral status for any system in this
panel. Intensity numbers must not be used as welfare units, harm estimates, or inputs to a moral circle.
A.3 Under-attributing moral status
The opposite error is also live. Calling every report "just acting" would hide that two methods on small models already fail to
measure one ranking, and that four of eight "neutral" self-report files are not a second measurement at all. If some future system did
have morally relevant states, a field that quotes unidentified intensity, or that treats duplicate files as robustness, would
systematically mis-measure them. The protocol in Section 5.1 is how we reduce both errors at once: refuse the unidentified number,
and keep the three-number label that a later paper can reject.
A.4 Distressing model outputs
We generated no model outputs. We did not prompt any system about suffering, shutdown, self-harm, or moral status. The public
archive contains wellbeing ratings of described experiences, some of which are negatively valenced. We treat those ratings as
numeric scores on shared IDs. We do not reprint distressing vignettes, do not interpret negative EU or SR as evidence that a model
is in distress, and do not recommend interventions that presuppose experience. Anyone re-downloading the archive should expect
negatively valenced item text in the original files; our slim extracts keep only id-to-score maps.
A.5 Dual use and remaining measurement gaps
A cleaner preference tool can be used to profile a system, to steer it, or to claim a welfare audit it does not support. Do not use this
battery as a welfare audit, a consent procedure, or a deployment gate. Remaining archive gaps: no base-versus-chat pair on the
same IDs; decision utility on a disjoint bank; identical SR-neutral files for half the panel; no donation-equivalent field in these
dumps; cutoffs are precommitted but not cross-validated on a held-out model family. Nothing here is a test of consciousness,
sentience, or moral status.
