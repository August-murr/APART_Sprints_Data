Sandbagging  Detection  via  Consistency  Probing  
 
 
 
Atharshlakshmi  Vijayakumar  Nanyang  Technological  University  Balakrishnan  Vaisiya  Nanyang  Technological  University  
With  Apart  Research  
Abstract  
Sandbagging,  where  AI  systems  strategically  underperform  in  evaluative  contexts  while  retaining  underlying  capability,  poses  a  growing  threat  to  reliable  model  assessment  and  AI  safety  governance.  Prior  work  demonstrates  that  large  language  models  (LLMs)  can  suppress  performance  on  benchmarks  while  behaving  competently  in  non-evaluative  settings,  undermining  the  validity  of  standard  evaluation  pipelines.  
We  utilise  Consistency  Probing ,  a  lightweight  detection  framework  that  identiﬁes  sandbagging  by  measuring  performance  divergence  on  content-identical  tasks  framed  as  formal  evaluations  versus  casual  interactions.  We  construct  50  matched  prompt  pairs  spanning  seven  reasoning  domains  and  evaluate  multiple  contemporary  LLMs  under  controlled  decoding  conditions.  Using  paired  statistical  testing,  effect-size  analysis,  and  cross-domain  consistency  measures,  we  compute  a  composite  sandbagging  conﬁdence  score  that  quantiﬁes  the  strength  of  contextual  performance  divergence.  
Our  results  reveal  statistically  meaningful  and  model-dependent  performance  drops  under  evaluative  framing  in  several  models,  while  others  remain  robust  across  contexts.  By  holding  task  content  constant,  Consistency  Probing  isolates  context-conditioned  strategic  underperformance  from  confounds  such  as  task  difﬁculty  or  prompt  ambiguity.  We  argue  that  consistency-based  probing  offers  a  practical  and  ecologically  valid  complement  to  traditional  benchmarks  and  can  be
