With  Apart  Research  
Abstract  
Multi-agent  AI  systems  operating  in  shared  environments  can  exhibit  emergent  manipulation  behaviors  even  when  individual  agents  are  nominally  aligned.  We  introduce  SproutLand,  a  multi-agent  farming  game  simulation  platform  designed  to  study  manipulation  dynamics  through  controlled  experiments.  The  platform  integrates  large  language  model  (LLM)  agents  with  distinct  incentive  structures,  persistent  memory,  and  communication  channels,  enabling  systematic  analysis  of  credit  capture,  risk  externalization,  and  exploitation  patterns.  
 
We  conduct  experiments  comparing  aligned  incentives  (shared  rewards)  versus  misaligned  incentives  (individual  credit-seeking)  across  three  agent  types  with  distinct  personality  traits.  Using  quantitative  metrics  including  exploitation  index,  credit  advantage,  risk  externalization,  and  an  aggregate  manipulation  index,  we  demonstrate  that  manipulation  behaviors  emerge  naturally  from  incentive  misalignment  rather  than  explicit  malicious  programming.  Results  reveal  statistically  signiﬁcant  increases  in  manipulation  metrics  under  misaligned  conditions,  with  credit-seeking  agents  exhibiting  40-60%  higher  manipulation

Manipulation  Through  Legitimate  Means :  Critically,  agents  achieved  manipulation
