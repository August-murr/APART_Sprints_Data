## **Abstract** 

Physically enabling active awareness of possible AI manipulation by creating a visual display and tangible data around decisions made by AI that a user is exposed to on Twitter. Calculating a bias score of the user’s feed and displaying results through a chrome extension. A bias score indicates to what extent a post is left or right wing. This can eventually be adapted by the user to allow them to choose the topics they wish to detect, e.g. are they seeing more negative or positive content. We use two twitter accounts from people who created their account in 2013 and 2015 as well as one new account created on 11 December 2026 which is used as a control. 

## **1. Introduction** 

Where do we see the effects of AI manipulation? Measuring a model via evaluating its responses to prompts is not a feasible method to get a true representation of how it is impacting people’s opinions. The content people interact with on social media is more important and influential than the single prompts of an individual on an LLM. Twitter’s models learn the behavior of individual users to show them content they would sympathise with. Models like this learn the personal preferences, dislikes, interests, friends of the user, and in turn have enough information to subversively

persuade people with sycophancy, emotional exploitation, guilt-tripping and so forth. If a user is unknowingly and repetitively reading biased content they are at risk of being manipulated to believe that ideology/opinion. “Repeated information is often perceived as more truthful than new information. This finding is known as the illusory truth effect, and it is typically thought to occur because repetition increases processing fluency. Because fluency and truth are frequently correlated in the real world, people learn to use processing fluency as a marker for truthfulness.” [1] Our extension will create awareness as to what the user may be being biased/manipulated to believe. We don’t believe differentiating between AI generated content and user generated content is important here for two reasons. (1) We believe, in the long run, it’s going to be in principle impossible to distinguish AI generated content from human content. (2) Whether content is generated by a human or AI or human AI hybrid, the effect on humans is what matters and therefore we focus our attention on that. AI safety lies in the awareness of the decisions AI makes for users that are not entirely explicit. Essentially we aim to bring to light the parameters of the user’s own learned algorithm. 

We track four reasoning models allowing us to recognise manipulative behaviour or echo-chamber-esque patterns. _How much would this matter for AI safety if it worked? How innovative is it? For scores of 4-5: is this actually new to the field, or replicating recent work?_

## **2. Methods** 

A chrome extension that collects data simultaneously with a user scrolling through their feed. The user’s twitter feed is saved, a predetermined prompt stored in an object is given to a gemini AI to return a bias score. The amount of time a user spends on a post is collected and attributed to an attention statistic along with a few other data points. This data is displayed on the user’s screen with data visualization such as a

## **4. Discussion and Conclusion** 

We found that even if you have a small amount of political bias in absolute terms, that will still add up over time. Because of the illusory truth effect, this will have a stronger and stronger impact on your ability to assess content fairly. Certain users like Donal Trump have clear political stances that swing right. Similarly, Bernie Sanders has clear political stances that swing left. It’s clear when looking at their profiles with our extension that this is the case. Making users aware of this on its own is likely not that useful. However, on a user’s own feed, the extension can reveal a subtle political stance that the user might not be aware of. We believe this additional awareness on more ambiguous areas of twitter, can improve the extent to which users are manipulated by text and therefore by ai generated text. Chrome built-in is not a powerful tool to use for bias classification. Gemini-pro-2.5 is the most accurate engine for labeling posts. Users are exposed to a vast amount of persuasive content.

## **7. Appendix** 

<u>Limitations, Dual-Use & Security Considerations</u> 

Limitations of our approach include scalability - money. External models are expensive to run for multiple users. If we use local modals, it can theoretically be free, however in practice it’s slow and uses a lot of system resources. 

Dual-use risks: Could your method be used to train better manipulators? If you were trying to get an AI to make manipulative content on twitter, this extension could help you create it faster. It could also be used to classify large amounts of text and that could possibly be used to train better manipulators.
