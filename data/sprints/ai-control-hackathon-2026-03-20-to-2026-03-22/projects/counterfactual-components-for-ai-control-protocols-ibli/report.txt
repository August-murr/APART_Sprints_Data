Abstract  
Current  AI  control  protocols  rely  heavily  on  monitors  scoring  suspiciousness  —  a  single  point  of  failure  vulnerable  to  prompt  injection  and  capability-gap  degradation.  We  present  two  composable  components  based  on  counterfactual  reasoning  that  move  beyond  subjective  suspicion  scoring.  Both  ask:  what  would  happen  if  something  were  different?  The  Counterfactual  Monitor  (CM)  generates  a  trusted-model  reference  action  and  uses  an  LLM  judge  to  score  intent  divergence.  Counterfactual  Perturbations  (CP)  generates  controlled  task  variants  and  ﬂags  solutions  that  remain  suspiciously  invariant  across  perturbations.  We  evaluate  CM  on  ControlArena's  APPS  setting  across  three  conﬁgurations  and  three  attack  categories.  The  combined  conﬁguration  matches  the  standard  monitor's  safety  of  0.83  at  2%  audit  budget  while  maintaining  0.89  usefulness;  the  counterfactual-only  variant  achieves  0.93  safety  above  5%  budget.  CP  is  implemented  and  proposed  for  future  evaluation.  Together,  these  components  demonstrate  that  counterfactual  reasoning  is  a  promising  composable  direction  for  AI  control.

The  Counterfactual  Perturbations  Monitor,  shown  in  Figure  1(b),  targets  a  speciﬁc  structural

6.  Conclusion  
We  presented  two  composable  components  for  AI  control  protocols,  both  grounded  in  
counterfactual
 
reasoning.
 
The
 
Counterfactual
 
Monitor
 
(CM)
 
asks
 
what
 
a
 
trusted
 
model
 
would
 
do
 
on
 
the
 
same
 
task
 
and
 
scores
 
intent
 
divergence;
 
evaluated
 
across
 
three
 
attack
 
categories,
 
the
 
combined
 
conﬁguration
 
matches
 
the
 
standard
 
monitor’s
 
safety
 
of
 
0.83
 
at
 
realistic
 
audit
 
budgets
 
while
 
the
 
CM-only
 
variant
 
achieves
 
0.93
 
safety
 
when
 
budgets
 
exceed
 
5%.
 
Counterfactual
 
Perturbations
 
(CP)
 
asks
 
what
 
would
 
change
 
if
 
the
 
task
 
itself
 
were
 
different,
 
exploiting
 
context
 
isolation
 
to
 
make
 
coordinated
 
attacks
 
exponentially
 
harder;
 
it
 
is
 
implemented
 
and
 
proposed
 
for
 
future
 
evaluation.
  Together,  these  building  blocks  demonstrate  that  counterfactual  reasoning  -  whether  across  model  
outputs
 
or
 
task
 
perturbations
 
-
 
is
 
a
 
promising
 
and
 
composable
 
direction
 
for
 
AI
 
control,
 
offering
 
detection
 
signals
 
that
 
are
 
structurally
 
complementary
 
to
 
standard
 
monitoring.
 
The
 
components
 
can
 
be
 
composed
 
with
 
each
 
other
 
and
 
with
 
existing
 
protocols,
 
enabling
 
layered
 
defenses
 
tuned
 
to
 
speciﬁc
 
deployment
 
requirements.
