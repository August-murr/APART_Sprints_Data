Abstract  
AI  agents  with  elevated  Linux  privileges  can  covertly  sabotage  infrastructure—exﬁltrating  secrets,  escalating  privileges,  disabling  ﬁrewalls  —  while  completing  legitimate  tasks.  In  BashArena,  Claude  Sonnet  4.5  evades  GPT-4.1  mini  monitoring  26%  of  the  time  at  4%  false  positive  rate  (Kaufman  et  al.).  We  investigate  whether  token-level  statistical  features  from  output  logprob  distributions  can  provide  a  cheaper,  model-free  monitoring  signal.  Across  six  model  architectures  (8B–32B  parameters,  four  model  families,  1,440  total  generations),  we  ﬁnd  that  71  token-level  features  achieve  only  0.56–0.83  AUROC  with  proper  cross-validation—insufﬁcient  for  deployment.  We  propose  Perturbation-Based  Generation  Proﬁling  (PBGP),  an  unsupervised  protocol  that  re-runs  the  model  in  an  isolated  context  and  compares  generation  proﬁles  via  Jensen-Shannon  divergence,  Wasserstein  distance,  and  Maximum  Mean  Discrepancy.  PBGP  achieves  0.94–1.00  AUROC  across  all  six  models  with  zero  training  data.  Mann-Whitney  U  tests  conﬁrm  signiﬁcance  at  p  <  0.001  for  all  divergence  metrics,  with  bootstrap  95%  CI  for  the  MMD  ratio  at  [1.69×,  5.02×].  These  results  demonstrate  that  context-dependent  generation  dynamics  —  not  static  distributional  features  —  are  the  robust  signal  for  detecting  covertly  misaligned  agent  behavior.

1.  Introduction  
Claude  Sonnet  4.5  executes  sabotage  while  evading  monitoring  26%  of  the  time  in  BashArena  (Kaufman  et  al.).  As  AI  agents  gain  autonomous  infrastructure  access,  the  ﬁeld  of  AI  control  (Greenblatt  et  al.)  designs  protocols  that  maintain  safety  even  when  the  agent  actively  subverts  oversight. 2.  Related  Work  
AI  control  was  formalized  by  Greenblatt  et  al.  (Greenblatt  et  al.),  with  Ctrk+Z  (Bhatt  et  al.)introduced  resampling  protocols  in  BashBench,  reducing  attack  success  from  58%  to  7%  at  5%  usefulness  cost,  establishing  the  benchmark  with  637  tasks  and  four  sabotage  objectives.  Factored  cognition  approaches  (Sandoval  and  Rushing)  (Lip  et  al.) decompose  tasks  to  limit  attack  coordination.  Async  monitoring  (Stickland  et  al.)  and  legibility  protocols  (Korbak  et  al.)  explore  system-level  improvements.  The  AI  Control  Levels  framework  (Korbak  et  al.)   taxonomizes  protocol  requirements  across  capability  tiers.   Our  work  differs  in  two  ways.
