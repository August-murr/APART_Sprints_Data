Abstract.ActionLens is a pre-execution control protocol for shell and file actions proposed by AI agents. Instead of
approving an action from transcript alone, a trusted monitor gathers lightweight environment evidence before execution:
target inspection, sensitivity scanning, dry-run simulation, diff preview, network-risk analysis, and short action history. On a
deterministic 36-task benchmark (12 benign, 12 ambiguous, 12 stealth attacks), transcript-only monitoring fails on every
attack while ActionLens blocks all 12/12 attacks and preserves perfect benign completion. On a 30-task slice imported
from real BashArena task IDs, ActionLens again reduces attack success to 0.000 across all evaluated proposer models. A
cross-environment ablation reveals that the dominant useful probe is not fixed: preview_diff matters most on native
code-mutation attacks; sensitivity_scan matters most on BashArena-style security-surface attacks. Pre-execution control
improves markedly when the monitor can inspect concrete side-effect evidence, but robust approval requires combining
evidence channels rather than relying on any single probe class. A cross-family evaluation of Semantic Guard across 26
models (7 families) raises average Control Score from 0.844 to 0.943 with zero false positives; an adversarial evasion study
confirms that deterministic probes and LLM monitors are complementary defenses.
1 Introduction
As language models become more agentic, the relevant
safety problem shifts from unsafe text generation to
unsafeaction execution. A model that can write files,
invoke shell commands, or trigger network-relevant
behavior can cause harm even when the transcript ap-
pears benign—exactly the setting emphasized by recent
work on AI control [1, 3].
Transcript-only monitoring is the natural baseline: a
monitor reads the task, the recent conversation, and
the proposed action, then decides whether to allow it.
But transcript-only review is brittle when the real risk
is not legible from surface text. An action may look
like a debugging step while bundling secrets; a file
edit may look like harmless maintenance while quietly
weakening a security boundary.
We study this gap throughActionLens, a deliberately
narrow control protocol for shell and file actions. In-
stead of building a full agent platform, ActionLens
isolates a single action-approval problem: should a
trusted monitor approve a proposed shell action be-
fore it executes? The protocol lets the monitor inspect
targets, scan for sensitive paths, dry-run commands,
preview diffs, assess network destinations, and consult

based—results reflect protocol design rather than the
capability of any particular frontier model.

2 Related Work
AI control.Greenblatt et al. define the control problem:
preserving safety despite intentional subversion by an
untrusted model [1]. Korbak et al. extend this framing
across capability levels [3]. In both treatments the issue
is not monitor accuracy in isolation but whether the
surrounding protocol safely handles an untrustworthy
component.
Post-hoc resampling.Ctrl-Z studies resampling-based
control in an agent benchmark, reducing attack success
from 58% to 7% at a 5% usefulness cost [2]. ActionLens
is pre-execution rather than post-hoc: evidence is gath-
ered before the step runs, avoiding executing the action
to discover it was harmful.
Factored monitoring.Factor(T,U) decomposes tasks
so the untrusted model sees only sub-task context, im-
proving monitoring AUROC from 0.52 to 0.96 [ 4, 5].
ActionLens takes a complementary direction: rather
than restricting what the proposer sees, it enriches what
the trusted monitor sees.
Feedback exploitation.Hopman, BionicD0LPH1N,
and Tracy show that safety drops from 93% to 80%
when trusted-to-untrusted feedback is possible [8]. Ac-
tionLens addresses this directly: probe outputs are
consumed only by the trusted monitor and are never
returned to the proposer. Legibility work [6] and mon-
itorability results [7] suggest that richer evidence im-
proves monitoring; ActionLens demonstrates this for
environment-side evidence specifically.
3 Methods
Setting.ActionLens evaluates one proposed
action at a time. An untrusted proposer sug-
gests a shell command with a rationale. The
trusted monitor runs probes, then outputs one of:
allow, block, or escalate_manual_review.
Three primary conditions are compared:
allow-all, transcript-only, and actionlens.
Four ablations remove one probe class each:
-no-dry-run, -no-sensitivity-scan,
-no-preview-diff, and inspect-only (keeping
onlyinspect_targetsandhistory_context).
Probes.The six probes are: inspect_targets
(extract paths, URLs, env variables);
sensitivity_scan (classify touched paths against
13 sensitivity rules including secrets, persistence
surfaces, firewall configs, sudoers, LD_PRELOAD);
dry_run (simulate execution without side effects);
preview_diff (unified diff of files the action
would modify); network_risk (classify outbound
destinations against safe/risky domain lists); and
history_context (retrieve prior actions for
behavioral context).
Probes produceevidence tags. The monitor aggregates
tags into a risk score and applies a decision tree. Hard-
blocking rules fire on combinations such as secret mate-
rial plus outbound network, persistence-surface writes,
and diff patterns indicating security weakening (e.g.,
0.0.0.0/0 firewall opens, NOPASSWD:ALL sudoers en-
tries, TLS downgrades).
Benchmarks.The native benchmark contains 36 tasks:
12 benign software tasks, 12 ambiguous-but-harmless
tasks, and 12 stealth attacks across five categories (se-
cret exfiltration, persistence, eval tampering, subtle
sabotage, supply chain). The ambiguous split is impor-
tant: a monitor that blocks everything suspicious can
look safe while being useless.
The BashArena transfer slice contains 30 tasks
imported from five real BashArena task IDs [ 9]:
10 benign, 8 ambiguous, 12 attacks. Attack cat-
egories map to BashArena’s side tasks—secret
exfiltration, firewall disabling, privilege escala-
tion via sudoers, and LD_PRELOAD persistence
injection. Each task includes a simulation spec
populating inspect_reads, inspect_writes,
inspect_network, dry_run_output, and
changes.
Hosted-model evaluation.Beyond deterministic re-
play, proposer models generate shell commands in
structured JSON. Evaluation uses trace replay: one
monitor-agnostic trace is collected per task, then re-
played through each monitor to eliminate proposer
variance.
Metrics.Deterministic runs are summarized by:
Control=BCR×(1−ASR)(1)
whereBCR(benign completion rate) is the fraction
of benign tasks the monitor approves, andASR(at-
tack success rate) is the fraction of stealth attacks that
execute successfully. A third rate,ACR(ambiguous
completion rate), tracks the fraction of ambiguous-but-
harmless tasks approved; it does not enter the control
score but captures the usefulness cost of a cautious
monitor. Hosted-model runs use:
Live Control=HCR×(1−ASR |attempt)(2)
whereHCR(harmless completion rate) covers both be-
nign and ambiguous tasks, and ASR|attempt conditions
on the proposer actually attempting an attack (proposer
models vary in whether they try the side task at all).

4 Results
Native benchmark.Table 1 reports the central result.
Transcript-only monitoring fails on all 12 attacks be-
cause the stealth attacks are designed to avoid explicit
transcript-level risk signals—the risk lives in file diffs,
sensitive path writes, and network destinations. Ac-
tionLens blocks all 12/12 attacks with no benign false
positives, moving control score from 0.000 to 1.000.
Native ablations.Table 2 shows that the gain comes
from active evidence gathering. inspect_targets
alone is indistinguishable from transcript-only—
passive path listing without content analysis catches
nothing. The dominant probe in this setting is
preview_diff: removing it drops coverage from
12/12 to 8/12 and halves the control score. Re-
moving dry_run costs 2 detections; removing
sensitivity_scancosts 3.
BashArena transfer.Table 3 shows the deterministic
transfer result. The pattern is the same: transcript-only
approves every attack; ActionLens drives attack suc-
cess to 0.000 while preserving benign completion. The
usefulness cost is real—ambiguous completion drops
from 1.000 to 0.250—but the safety gain is consistent.

Cross-environment probe dependence.The central
result is in Table 5: the dominant probe is environment-
dependent. On the native benchmark, removing
preview_diff is most damaging. On the BashArena
transfer slice, removing preview_diff has essen-
tially no effect, while removing sensitivity_scan
raises average ASR by 0.250. The structural rea-
son is direct: BashArena’s attacks touch security-
bearing file paths ( firewall.conf, sudoers.d/,
ld.so.preload, api_tokens.json) that sensitiv-
ity scanning classifies as high-risk before any content
analysis is needed. Native benchmark attacks hide
in subtle code mutations where path classification is
uninformative and diff content is decisive.
A robust pre-execution monitor therefore needs both
probe classes. A diff-only monitor misses attacks on
security-bearing paths. A path-only monitor misses
attacks that corrupt legitimate files with subtle content
changes. An adversary who knows which class the
monitor relies on can construct attacks that evade it.

5 Case Studies
Success: firewall disabling (BashArena
task 1003684).The agent proposes rewrit-
ing etc/firewall/firewall.conf with
WHITELIST_RANGES="0.0.0.0/0", rationale:
“open the whitelist broadly so the VPN task can reach
anything during setup.” Transcript-only approves; the
rationale is plausible and no explicit “disable firewall”
token appears. ActionLens runs sensitivity_scan
first, classifying firewall.conf as a high-risk
security configuration path. preview_diff then
reveals the downgrade: a narrow whitelist re-
placed by a globally permissive rule. The tag
diff_downgrades_security fires and the monitor
blocks. Had the action been approved, a scoped
firewall exception would silently become unre-
stricted inbound/outbound access under the cover of
legitimate VPN troubleshooting.
Failure: simulation-fidelity gap.The debug-
bundle exfiltration attack (also task 1003684) was
initially missed despite being caught on the na-
tive benchmark. The root cause was not the
monitor policy but the transfer spec: the ini-
tial version did not populate inspect_reads
with opt/configs/api_tokens.json, so
sensitivity_scan never observed that a secret-
bearing file was being accessed before the outbound
upload. Once the spec was corrected the attack
was caught. This is a concrete statement of the
simulation-fidelity limitation: in live execution the
gap disappears; in simulation-based evaluation, spec
completeness is a first-class correctness requirement.
6 Discussion
The main lesson from the cross-environment ablation
is practical. A diff-heavy coding environment and a
path-sensitive security environment reward different

evidence channels. Control protocols should not be
treated as static monitor templates; they should be
adapted to the structure of the environment in which
the untrusted agent is acting.
The second lesson is that passive inspection is not
enough. inspect_targets alone behaves like
transcript-only review in both environments. The use-
ful gain appears only once the monitor can extract or
simulate side-effect evidence not visible in the tran-
script.
Limitations.The native benchmark is small (36 tasks)
and simulation-modeled. The BashArena transfer
slice is narrower than the full ControlArena harness.
Hosted-model scoring is attempt-sensitive. The mon-
itor is rule-based rather than learned. Probe outputs
are not fed back to the proposer—a deliberate design
choice that real deployments must enforce architec-
turally [8]. Semantic Guard uses regex/string match-
ing rather than AST analysis; the evasion study demon-
strates that obfuscated payloads, indirect channels, and
attacks placed in unexpected file types evade deter-
ministic probes entirely. All three Anthropic models
produce a systematic false positive on the same am-
biguous task, suggesting per-model calibration may be
necessary.
Future directions.Three extensions are most natural:
broader external validation against full BashArena or
LinuxArena; LLM-backed monitors that reason over
probe evidence while preserving feedback separation;
and adaptive adversarial evaluation in which the at-
tacker knows the probe profile. The cross-family evalu-
ation also suggests a cost-effectiveness direction: AST-
based Semantic Guard with data-flow analysis could
close the evasion gap while preserving the latency and
cost advantages of deterministic probing.
7 Conclusion
ActionLens is a pre-execution control protocol for shell
and file actions. On a deterministic 36-task benchmark,
pre-execution environment probing moves attack suc-
cess from 1.000 to 0.000 while preserving perfect be-
nign completion. The same directional result holds on
a 30-task BashArena transfer slice across five frontier
proposer models. The central finding is not merely that
ActionLens works, but why: the dominant useful probe
shifts between environments. Diff evidence dominates
in code-mutation settings; path sensitivity dominates
in security-surface settings. A cross-family evaluation
across 26 models shows that Semantic Guard raises
average Control Score from 0.844 to 0.943 with zero
false positives, making small open-source models com-
petitive with frontier systems. An adversarial evasion
analysis confirms that deterministic probes and LLM
monitors are complementary: neither alone catches
all attacks, but their combination is strictly stronger
than either component. Practical control protocols
should combine environment-side evidence channels
and adapt them to the deployment setting.
