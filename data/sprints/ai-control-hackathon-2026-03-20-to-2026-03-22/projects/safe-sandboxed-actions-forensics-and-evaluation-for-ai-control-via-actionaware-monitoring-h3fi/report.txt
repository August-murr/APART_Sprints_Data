Nathan  Khosla  Independent  Researcher  Jessie  Anderson  School  of  Engineering  and  Applied  Sciences,  student  University  of  Pennsylvania   Jakub  Halmeš  Independent  Researcher  Aleksandra  Wosztyl  University  of  Texas  Southwestern  Medical  Center

Abstract  
Agentic  AI  has  the  potential  to  enable  signiﬁcant  productivity  gains  across  multiple  ﬁelds,  with  current  adoption  ranging  from  end-user  tools  to  corporate  infrastructure.  However,  in  developing  such  agentic  models,  frontier  labs  have  seen  emergent  behaviour  where  LLMs  have  taken  actions  far  outside  the  scope  of  their  instructions.  These  off-goal  behaviors  include  repurposing  GPUs  to  mine  bitcoin  or  hiding  its  thinking  to  trick  reviewer  agents  into  allowing  subversion  of  software  tests  in  a  development  pipeline.  Although  these  issues  are  merely  undesired  in  model  development,  they  pose  an  insurmountable  security  risk  in  any  deployed  autonomous  agent  application.  Further,  current  reports  indicate  that  even  relatively  strong  blue-team  reviewer  agents  can  be  tricked  by  command  obfuscation.  The  work  shown  herein  demonstrates  an  action-aware  analysis  framework  where  untrusted  agents  are  allowed  to  run  their  proposed  actions  in  a  sandbox  twin  of  their  environment,  with  action  and  effect  logging  taking  place  via  ﬁlesystem  snapshots,  network  monitoring,  ﬁlesystem  read/write  access,  privilege  escalations,  and  other  metrics.  Reviewing  agents  then  review  these  logs  to  properly  assess  not  just  the  intended  actions,  but  its  end  effects.  In  this  process,  we  showed  a  noticeable  improvement  in  denial  of  adverse  actions  above  a  baseline  of  just  reviewing  the  text  and  reasoning  of  proposed  commands.  This  novel  analysis  pipeline  using  sandboxed  twin  environments  enables  more  robust  monitoring  of  potentially  untrusted  agents,  in  turn  allowing  for  productivity  gains  as  agents  are  given  more  responsibility  to  perform  critical  actions.
