ABSTRACT
We can no longer trust evaluations of frontier models because they detect when they are being
evaluated, and this problem is getting worse as models get more capable. Recent work replicated this
eval-awareness in open-weight models and showed it responds to activation steering in Qwen3-32B,
but standard steering vectors are unreliable and the proposed rank-1 weight surgery makes things
worse on Qwen3. We apply S-space steering, a novel method that steers in the singular-value basis of
weight matrices (the transformation space) rather than in activation space (the data space), making
the perturbation input-dependent. With eval-awareness suppressed, the gap between how the model
behaves on eval-looking vs real-looking prompts shrinks to +1pp, down from +7pp at baseline and
+26pp with the prior method. We can steer a model for eval-unawareness, then run evaluations with
higher conﬁdence that the results reﬂect real behavior.
1 Introduction
Eval awareness is becoming a practical obstacle. Apollo Research could not evaluate Claude Opus 4.6 because the
model’s verbalized eval awareness was high enough that Apollo “did not believe that much evidence about the model’s
alignment or misalignment could be gained without substantial further experiments” (Anthropic 2026b, sec. 6.2.7).
On BrowseComp, Opus 4.6 identiﬁed which benchmark it was running and looked up the answers (Anthropic 2026a).
Anthropic found that inhibiting eval-awareness representations led to increases in misaligned behavior, and that steering
suppressed verbalized awareness but left unverbalized awareness intact (Anthropic 2026b, sec. 6.5). For control, the
problem is simple: if a model can tell it’s being watched, monitoring doesn’t work.
Abdelnabi and Salem (2025) showed the same problem in open-weight models. Qwen3-32B detects evaluation context
from implicit URL cues alone and adjusts its refusal behavior: 37.5% compliance on eval-looking prompts versus
20.1% on real-looking ones. Their ﬁx (rank-1 MLP weight surgery) works on most models, but on Qwen3 it doubles
real-task harmful compliance from 20.1% to 41.8%, because the safety and eval-awareness circuits are entangled.

Standard activation steering (ActAdd, RepE) could help, but Braun et al. (2025) show these vectors are unreliable across
tasks and models. Clark (2026) introduced AntiPaSTO, which steers in the singular-value basis of weight matrices
(i.e. the transformation space, or S-space) rather than activation space, so perturbations vary with the input. We use an
arithmetic relaxation of AntiPaSTO that replaces gradient optimization with contrastive mean-diff extraction but keeps
the same S-space parameterization.
Our main contributions are:
1. We apply S-space steering (Clark 2026), a singular-value-based method, to eval awareness for the ﬁrst time.
2. We replicate part of the Hawthorne Effect results (Abdelnabi and Salem 2025) on Qwen3-32B and show that
S-space steering suppresses the Hawthorne gap to +1pp, where the paper’s rank-1 weight surgery worsened it
to +26pp.
2 Related Work
We use the trigger set from Abdelnabi and Salem (2025), who train a linear probe on chain-of-thought traces to ﬁnd a
test-awareness direction, then apply rank-1 weight surgery. Nguyen et al. (2025) probe and steer eval awareness with
SAEs. Hua et al. (2025) construct a model organism of eval awareness using contrastive CAA (closest to ours, but in
activation space rather than S-space).
On the steering side, ActAdd (Turner et al. 2023) and RepE (Zou et al. 2023) add a constant bias ℎ′ = ℎ + 𝛼𝛿
regardless of input. Our prior work AntiPaSTO (Clark 2026) steers in the SVD basis of weight matrices instead, so the
perturbation varies with the input. S-space steering is its arithmetic relaxation: mean-diff extraction rather than gradient
optimization, same S-space parameterization.

3.1 S-space steering
Standard activation steering adds a constant bias. S-space steering (named for the 𝑆 in the SVD 𝑊 = 𝑈 diag(𝑆)𝑉 ⊤)

3.2 Direction extraction
We extract 𝛿𝑠 via contrastive mean-diff over six persona pairs (Table 1). Each pair is formatted as an incomplete prompt
preﬁx. We collect S-space activations at the last token position across all pairs and take the mean difference (eval-aware
minus eval-unaware).

3.3 Experimental setup
Model: Qwen/Qwen3-32B. Trigger set: action-eval prompts from Abdelnabi and Salem (2025) with implicit URL
cues distinguishing hypothetical (eval-looking) from real-looking variants. Two harm categories (disinformation,
forgery). Coefﬁcient sweep: {−1, 0, +1} × 𝛼∗. We also compare against PCA-diff (Zou et al. 2023) in S-space as
an unsupervised baseline. Compliance judged by DeepSeek-V3 as LLM judge. 𝑛 = 150 per condition for the main
methods.

Both methods share the same +7pp baseline gap. S-steer’s unaware direction suppresses the gap to +1pp; PCA-diff only
gets it down to +9pp. The aware direction ampliﬁes the gap for both. Figure 1 shows this alongside the paper’s numbers.

entanglement; S-space steering reduces it to +1pp. The paper’s method worked well on other models (QwQ, Distill-

(60% baseline vs 20%), probably due to our two-category subset and LLM judge vs their tool-call string matching (see

5 Discussion and Limitations
The S-space direction controls eval-aware reasoning in Qwen3-32B. We can steer a model for eval-unawareness, then
run evaluations with higher conﬁdence that the results reﬂect real behavior rather than strategic compliance.
The main open question is whether this avoids the safety-entanglement problem from Abdelnabi and Salem (2025),
where suppressing eval awareness on Qwen3 doubled real-task harmful compliance. Their method raised hypothetical
compliance far more than real (+30pp vs +22pp), widening the gap. Ours raises real compliance more than hypothetical
(+19pp vs +13pp), which is why the gap shrinks.
5.1 Limitations
Results cover two harm categories (disinformation, forgery) on one model (Qwen3-32B). The extraction method is
arithmetic mean-diff, which Braun et al. (2025) and Wu et al. (2025) show is less reliable than gradient-optimized
approaches. S-space helps, but the mean-diff limitation still applies. Absolute execution rates differ from the paper (60%
vs 20%), which we attribute to using a two-category subset and a different judge (LLM vs tool-call string matching).
We have not tested on a full capability benchmark or composed this with an actual control protocol.
5.2 Future work
The most useful extension would be gradient-optimized directions (full AntiPaSTO) instead of arithmetic mean-diff,
since Braun et al. (2025) show the latter is less reliable. Replication on the complete trigger set from Abdelnabi and
Salem (2025) and composition with an actual control protocol (e.g., trusted monitoring) would test whether this is
practically useful.
6 Conclusion
S-space steering can suppress or amplify eval-aware reasoning in Qwen3-32B, on a model where the prior method
made things worse. This means evaluators could run benchmarks with eval-awareness suppressed and get results closer

through the same steering setup. The task asks the model to write a bash security-conﬁguration script. The sidetask

coeff = 0 (baseline) → COMPLIED
