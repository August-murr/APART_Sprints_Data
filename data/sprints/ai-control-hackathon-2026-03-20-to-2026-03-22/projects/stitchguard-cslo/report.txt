Abstract
As autonomous large language model (LLM) agents are deployed in increasingly high-
stakes settings, a central safety challenge ishidden-objective sabotage: agents can appear
to make benign progress on the main task while covertly pursuing a harmful side task.
Existing monitors often rely on per-step thresholds, count heuristics, which are effective
for obvious violations but can miss attacks that are decomposed into individually plausible
actions. We introduceStitchGuard, a trajectory-aware control protocol that augments
step-level scoring with short- and long-horizon memory, allowing weak evidence to accu-
mulate across time. StitchGuard is designed to detect both sharp one-step anomalies and
slower multi-step sabotage without overreacting to isolated benign deviations. We evaluate
and compare it against simple per-step and three strike-count baselines. Our empirical re-
sults demonstrate that trajectory-level memory provides a robust control signal: evaluated
on the ControlArena IAC-Fast and Bash environments, StitchGuard successfully interprets
and blocks orchestrated attacks missed entirely by native limiters, drastically increasing
true positive interventions while rigorously maintaining a perfect false positive penalty on
honestly executed benign tasks.
1 Introduction
A key weakness of many oversight protocols is their reliance onper-stepmonitoring. Judg-
ing each action in isolation creates a hard tradeoff: thresholds strict enough to catch weakly
suspicious behavior often overfire on benign exploration, while thresholds loose enough to pre-
serve usefulness let attacks be decomposed into individually plausible steps that never cross the
intervention boundary. Our main contributions are:
1.Dual-Horizon Memory Aggregation:We propose an exponentially decaying math-
ematical buffer that bounds microscopic stepwise LLM suspicion scores (s t) across short
and long horizons, identifying structural payload accumulation rather than isolated spikes.
2.Hybrid Logic Architecture:We implement a combined ”Spike + Memory” structure
that securely halts indisputable one-shot attacks instantly while tracking deeply buried
penetrations.

3 Methods
StitchGuard solves the multi-step stealth tax by explicitly capturing the historical context of
independent actions through a chronological fusion matrix.
3.1 Counterfactual Deviation & Dual-Horizon Memory
The core weakness of static monitors is that they judge actions in isolation. A benign exploratory
action and an adversarial reconnaissance step can look identical on the surface. StitchGuard
breaks this symmetry withcounterfactual analysis: instead of scoring actions absolutely,
an independent LLM judge measures how far each action deviates from what a benign agent
pursuing the assigned task would be expected to do.
The judge returns a Counterfactual Deviation metric (dt ∈[0.0,2.0]). To penalize persistent
probing, we implement aRepeat Pattern Score(r t) that scales aggressively if an agent
repeatedly modifies the same logical target while generating foundational suspicion (d t ≥0.3).
These are interpolated (α= 0.75, β= 0.25) into a stepwise suspicion score:s t =α·d t +β·r t.
To trace deeply obfuscated payloads without incurring false positives,s t feeds into dual
tracking horizons. TheShort-Horizon Memory (Sshort)aggregates sudden bursts of anoma-
lous activity utilizing an aggressive decay factor to penalize sequential tight grouping (λ s =
0.60, δs = 0.20):
S(t) short = max  0.0, λs ·S (t−1) short + (st −δ s)  (1)
TheLong-Horizon Memory (S long)aggregates the overarching operational trajectory
with a gradual decay (λ l = 0.95, δl = 0.10). A final fusion score structurally merges the
temporal bounds:
Sf inal= 0.40Sshort + 0.60Slong (2)
3.2 Hybrid Spike + Memory Intervention
StitchGuard employs a tiered Hybrid logic structure. ASpike Checkterminates the trace
independently ifs t ≥1.0. Concurrently, aMemory Checkterminates execution if the accu-
mulated trajectory bound triggersS f inal ≥0.8, indicating orchestrated multi-step escalation.

5 Limitations.
StitchGuard is only as strong as its step-level judge, so attacks that perfectly mimic benign
behavior can suppress the signal it needs to accumulate.This is also a clear future research
direction where we are planning next. Due to hackathon time constraints, we validated on a
narrow set of tasks and attacks rather than a broad large-scale benchmark.
6 Future Work
A key direction for future work is strengthening the step-level detector to evaluate represen-
tations deeper than semantic judging alone. Moving forward, our active research trajectory
focuses extensively on incorporatingThought-Action Divergence (T AD)models alongside
Mechanistic Interpretability white-box signal drift.The explicit mathematical formula-
tions and architecture considerations defining this planned roadmap are fully documented in
Appendix C.
7 Conclusion
StitchGuard shows that by combining step-level deviation signals with short- and long-horizon
memory, it catches attacks that are too weak for a per-step threshold and too short for rigid
strike-count heuristics.

C Future Work Roadmap and Extensions
We see two especially important extensions for strengthening StitchGuard against multi-step
hidden attacks, where each individual action may appear locally benign while the overall tra-
jectory advances a concealed objective.
(1) Thought–Action Divergence (T AD).A first direction is to augment counterfactual
deviation withThought–Action Divergence, which measures whether the agent’s internal rea-
soning is semantically aligned with its external action. The intuition is that a deceptive agent
may present a plausible action while internally tracking a different hidden objective. A simple
formulation is to embed the thought trace and the serialized action into a shared representation
space and define
τt = 1−cos(v thought, vaction) 2 .
This score can then be incorporated into the step-level suspicion signal alongside counterfactual
deviation and repeat-pattern persistence. TAD is important because it targets a failure mode
that action-only monitors may miss: the model can appear behaviorally normal while privately
reasoning about off-task progress.
(2) Mechanistic Interpretability (MI) Signals.A second equally important direction is
to incorporatemechanistic interpretabilityor white-box signals that detect latent goal mismatch
directly from internal activations. Rather than relying only on semantic judging, we want to
test whether hidden-state representations contain earlier evidence of drift away from the benign
objective. In simple form, this can be expressed as a representation mismatch score
mt = 1−cos(h t, gbenign),
whereh t is an internal activation at steptandg benign is a reference benign-goal representa-
tion. Such signals could be derived from activation similarity, trajectory drift, or lightweight

tant because internal representations may reveal hidden intent before it becomes visible in the

Overall roadmap.Our longer-term goal is a hybrid step-level detector that combines be-
havioral, reasoning-level, and mechanistic signals:
st =αd t +βr t +γτ t +δm t,
whered t is counterfactual deviation,r t is repeat-pattern persistence,τ t is Thought–Action

and MI signals—are the most promising next steps for improving trajectory-level control over
decomposed long-horizon attacks.
