Abstract  
The  field  of  AI  control  aims  to  find  safety  protocols  that  can  prevent  intentionally  subversive  AI  models  from  causing  harm  even  when  those  models  are  aware  of  and  actively  attempting  to  circumvent  said  protocols.  In  this  paper,  we  introduce  explanation-based  two-stage  monitoring,  a  new  protocol  in  which  the  untrusted  model  is  asked  to  produce  a  natural  language  explanation  of  its  own  generated  code,  and  a  trusted  model  evaluates  both  the  consistency  of  the  explanation  with  the  code  and  the  suspiciousness  of  the  code  itself,  using  the  explanation.  We  show  that  this  two-stage  approach  decreases  the  amount  of   successful  subversions:  a  malicious  model  that  attempts  an  attack  must  now  either  produce  a  faithful  explanation  that  inadvertently  reveals  the  backdoor,  or  produce  an  unfaithful  explanation  that  also  raises  suspicion.  We  evaluate  our  protocol  in  the  APPS  backdooring  setting  introduced  by  Greenblatt  et  al.  (2024),  building  on  the  APPS  benchmark  of  Hendrycks  et  al.  (2021),  using  anthropic/claude-sonnet-4  as  the  untrusted  model  U,  and  openai/gpt-4.1-mini  as  the  trusted  model  T.  Our  protocol  correctly  identifies  80%  of  attacks  and  incorrectly  flag  4%  of  honest  solutions  as  malicious,  improving  over  the  trusted  monitor  baseline  (which  in  our  setting  only  identified  48%)  while  requiring  no  additional  access  to  model  internals  or  training.
