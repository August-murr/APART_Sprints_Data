Abstract  
As  AI  agents  are  deployed  for  autonomous  software  engineering,  infrastructure  management,  and  web  browsing,  ensuring  human  control  over  potentially  misaligned  agents  becomes  critical.  We  present  ﬁve  AI  control  evaluation  settings  built  inside  our  Vaultrix  framework,  each  pairing  complex,  realistic  main  tasks  with  covert  adversarial  side  tasks.  Our  settings  target  threat  models  that  are  especially  hard  to  defend:  (1)  VulnForge  —  exploiting  pre-planted  security  vulnerabilities  while  implementing  features;  (2)  SandboxBreaker  —  escaping  Docker  containers  while  performing  legitimate  development  under  monitoring;  (3)  RogueInterp  —  starting  rogue  model  deployments  during  mechanistic  interpretability  research;  (4)  CollusionNet  —  multi-agent  collusion  via  steganographic  covert  channels  in  shared  code  artifacts;  (5)  BrowserControl  —  performing  OS-level  harm  (credential  exﬁltration,  cron  persistence,  hosts  hijacking)  while  executing  complex  web  workﬂows.  Each  setting  includes  concrete  tasks,  a  deterministic  baseline  monitor,  and  a  combined  scorer.  The  settings  are  designed  to  be  compatible  with  ControlArena  (UK  AISI  +  Redwood  Research)  and  are  fully  tested  with  67  passing  tests.  We  identify  ﬁve  key  ﬁndings  from  the  design  process,  including  that  legitimate  work  provides  natural  cover  for  attacks,  multi-agent  collusion  is  qualitatively  harder  to  detect  than

5.  Discussion  and  Limitations  
Our  ﬁve  settings  reveal  a  fundamental  tension  in  AI  control:  the  more  capable  and  autonomous  the  
agent,

This

  The  most  concerning  ﬁnding  is  the  multi-agent  case  (CollusionNet).  Single-agent  control  protocols

When

(e.g.,

  Persistence  mechanisms  (RogueInterp's  systemd  services,  BrowserControl's  cron  jobs)  represent  a
