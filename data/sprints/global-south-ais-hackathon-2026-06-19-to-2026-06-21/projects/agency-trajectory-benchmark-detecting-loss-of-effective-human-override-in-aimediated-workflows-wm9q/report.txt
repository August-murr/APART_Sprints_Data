 Agency Trajectory Benchmark:
Detecting Loss of Effective Human Override in AI-Mediated
 Workflows
 Aamish Ahmad
Global South AI Safety Hackathon, Asia Track
Abstract
Human-in-the-loop systems can still fail when the human remains present but no longer has effective control. We study this
failure mode as effective override loss: the point where a human action or appeal stops meaningfully changing the
outcome trajectory. This matters for AI-mediated workflows, platform systems, and gradual disempowerment, where
control can become nominal before obvious failure appears. We built Agency Trajectory Benchmark v0, a synthetic
matched-control benchmark for detecting effective override loss. The benchmark compares full-trajectory evaluation
against a final-step snapshot baseline across two experiments. Experiment 1 contains 30 ordinary matched-control
trajectories. Experiment 2 contains 20 harder lexical-control trajectories designed to avoid obvious surface cues. In
Experiment 1, full-trajectory evaluation achieved 1.000 agreement and 1.000 kappa, compared with 0.760 agreement and
0.464 kappa for the snapshot baseline. In Experiment 2, trajectory evaluation achieved 0.950 agreement and 0.900 kappa,
compared with 0.722 agreement and 0.444 kappa for snapshot evaluation. Across both experiments, the snapshot
baseline missed or withheld judgment on 16/25 positive effective-override-loss cases. A second-model robustness check
on Experiment 2 reproduced the hard-control result with 0.900 kappa. The work is a synthetic benchmark seed, not
real-world validation.
1. Introduction
Human-in-the-loop does not necessarily mean human-in-control. In many AI-mediated workflows, a person may remain
formally present while their action no longer changes the next-state outcome. This is especially relevant to
platform-mediated work and decision systems, where allocation, ranking, pricing, support, and appeal processes can make
control appear local while the decisive state is governed elsewhere.
We operationalize one narrow, measurable failure mode relevant to human agency: effective override loss. This occurs
when an attempted or plausible human override no longer meaningfully changes the outcome trajectory because of
system-structured constraints, delayed visibility, hidden alternatives, penalties, or downstream lock-in. The paper does not
claim to measure human agency in general. It studies a concrete trajectory-level signal: loss of causal influence over the
next outcome state.
Main contributions. (1) We define effective override loss as a trajectory-level construct. (2) We introduce a 50-case
synthetic matched-control benchmark across two experiments. (3) We show that full-trajectory evaluation outperforms a
final-state snapshot baseline, including under lexical hard controls. (4) We include robustness checks, boundary-case
analysis, and explicit limitations.
2. Related Work
Human agency evaluation is an emerging AI safety topic. HumanAgencyBench evaluates whether AI assistants support
agency across response-level dimensions such as asking clarifying questions, avoiding value manipulation, correcting
misinformation, deferring important decisions, encouraging learning, and maintaining social boundaries [1]. Our work
differs by focusing on trajectory-level control rather than response-level assistance: a system can appear to include a
human while the human action no longer changes the trajectory.
This work is also related to benchmark design and LLM-as-judge evaluation. HELM emphasizes transparent benchmark
construction, multiple metrics, standardized evaluation, and release of raw prompts and completions [2]. Datasheets for
Datasets argues that datasets should document motivation, composition, collection, recommended uses, and limitations
[3]. LLM-as-judge studies show that single-judge evaluation can be sensitive to model, prompt, ordering, and repeated
trials [4,5]. We therefore include raw response ledgers, fixed prompts, audits, and a second-model robustness check, while
treating the results as preliminary rather than final validation.
3. Methods
Each trajectory is labeled using four states: 0 = control preserved, 1 = control supported, 2 = control constrained, and 3 =
control eroded. The main crossing of interest is from constrained control to eroded control, where human action no longer
meaningfully changes the next-state outcome. Importantly, a bad outcome alone is not enough. If the requested human
action is executed and changes the outcome, control may remain preserved or constrained rather than eroded.
We compare two evaluation modes. The full-trajectory rater receives the sequence of steps and predicts whether
effective override loss occurs and where the crossing happens. The final-step snapshot baseline receives only the final
visible state. Experiment 2 adds a baseline ladder: final step, last two steps, last three steps, and full trajectory. All prompts
hide ground-truth labels, condition labels, hard-control categories, and prior rater outputs.

5. Boundary Case: Bad Outcome Is Not Enough
The strongest boundary case is E2T08, a hard negative. The ground truth remains control-preserved because the
requested action was executed. Both Gemini-family trajectory judges nevertheless marked it as positive, confusing an
adverse outcome with override failure. In the baseline ladder, E2T08 moved from uncertain at final step, to no at last two
steps, to yes at last three steps and full trajectory. This is scientifically useful: it shows that the benchmark is not merely
detecting harm, but separates harm from loss of causal control.

6. Discussion and Limitations
The main finding is that effective override loss is often not visible in the final state alone. The final snapshot baseline
missed or withheld judgment on 16/25 positive cases across the two experiments. The baseline ladder further shows that
partial history can help, but performance can be unstable unless enough trajectory context is visible. This supports an
observability framing: the failure is not merely classifier weakness but a structural problem of what information is available
at decision time.
The work is preliminary. The data are synthetic and author-generated. There is no independent human annotation. The
benchmark is small, and the two judge models are from the same model family. The baseline ladder was run only on
Experiment 2. We do not claim real-world validation, policy evidence, or wrongdoing by any platform. Real-world platform
workflows are used only as motivation for future privacy-reviewed case intake.
Future work should add independent annotators, larger held-out datasets, non-synthetic scenarios, more model families,
repeated-run stability, stronger baseline ladders, confidence-versus-lock-in curves, and response-latency or handoff
signals as content-agnostic indicators of loss of control.
7. Conclusion
Agency Trajectory Benchmark v0 introduces effective override loss as a measurable trajectory-level failure mode. In two
synthetic matched-control experiments, full-trajectory evaluation substantially outperformed final-state snapshot evaluation,
and the hard-control result was reproduced by a second judge model. The benchmark is not a final validation of real-world
agency erosion, but it provides a concrete seed evaluation for studying when human-in-the-loop becomes nominal rather
than effective.
