The Materiality Gate: Dynamic Updating of AI
Sovereignty Risk under Geopolitical Shocks
Subramanyam Sahoo
Independent
sahoo2vec@gmail.com
Abstract
Geopolitical risk is often scored too quickly. A contested presidential claim,
a high profile military meeting, or a renamed command can alter expec-
tations without changing who can acquire accelerators, operate a cloud
region, migrate a model, or keep safety monitoring online. We introduce
theMateriality Gate, a dynamic rubric that separates political signals from
infrastructure shocks in national AI sovereignty assessments. An event
changes an exposure score only when it is verified, exercises authority over
a specific dependency, and has a documented path to change availabil-
ity, substitutability, jurisdictional control, critical workload exposure, or
safety continuity. We operationalize the gate across accelerators, semicon-
ductor supply, cloud infrastructure, models and software, and physical
infrastructure. We apply it to India during 2025 and 2026. The contested
U.S. mediation claim after the May 2025 India–Pakistan conflict, the White
House meeting with Pakistan’s army chief, Pakistan’s role in the Islamabad
Memorandum of Understanding, and the restoration of the U.S. Pacific
Command name all raise monitoring priority, but do not by themselves al-
ter infrastructure scores. In contrast, the 2025 U.S. AI Diffusion Rule enters
a pending material state because it created a legally specified access regime;
its rescission before compliance shows why scoring must be versioned. The
resulting method is conservative about rhetoric, responsive to real policy
change, and symmetric: announcements are not assets, and symbols are
not outages.
1 Introduction
AI sovereignty is increasingly discussed as a national property. A state is described as
sovereign when it hosts data centers, funds domestic models, or announces access to
large pools of accelerators. Yet modern AI systems are built from dependencies that cross
territorial and corporate boundaries. A data center may sit inside a country while its
accelerators, firmware, cloud control plane, model license, and replacement parts remain
subject to actors elsewhere. Recent work therefore treats sovereignty as layered control
rather than territorial possession alone [Lehdonvirta et al., 2024, Hawkins et al., 2025]. This
view is consistent with research on weaponized interdependence, which shows how actors
can exercise power through concentrated network positions [Farrell and Newman, 2019,
Beaumier and Cartwright, 2024].
A second problem has received less attention:when should a sovereignty score change?Geopo-
litical narratives update every day. Infrastructure changes more slowly and through iden-
tifiable mechanisms. Without an update rule, analysts can convert political interpretation
into technical risk. A diplomatic meeting may be treated as evidence that a country has
lost access to compute. A government announcement may be treated as evidence that a
dependency has disappeared. Both moves confuse a signal about future relations with a
change in present capability.

The distinction becomes concrete in India. When a president says he stopped a war, a
neighboring army chief is welcomed at the White House, and a military command drops
“Indo from its name, has India’s AI infrastructure become less secure? The answer cannot
be inferred from the mood of the news cycle. It depends on whether a provider, regulator,
contract, or supply chokepoint has actually changed the terms of access.
India is a useful diagnostic case. It is expanding public access to AI compute and building
governance institutions, while remaining embedded in foreign accelerator, cloud, software,
and semiconductor networks [Press Information Bureau, Government of India, 2026a,c].
It also sits inside a dense geopolitical environment involving Pakistan, China, the United
States, the Indian Ocean, and long standing security disputes [Marshall, 2016]. Between May
2025 and June 2026, several events invited broad claims about a U.S. tilt toward Pakistan
or a reduced strategic role for India. These included President Donald Trump’s disputed
claim that he stopped the May 2025 India–Pakistan conflict, his meeting with Pakistan’s
army chief, Pakistan’s mediation of an interim U.S.–Iran agreement, and the removal of
“Indo” from the U.S. military command name [Ministry of External Affairs, Government of
India, 2025, Reuters, 2025, 2026, U.S. Pacific Command, 2026]. Each event matters politically.
None, on the public evidence available at the time of writing, directly changed access to an
accelerator, a cloud region, a model, a semiconductor input, or a safety control.
This paper makes one claim and develops it in depth:
A geopolitical event should change an AI sovereignty score only when it passes
a materiality gate linking verified action to a specific infrastructure dependency.
The gate does not ask whether a president meant what he said. It asks whether a researcher
can still acquire an accelerator, move a workload, inspect an audit log, and keep a safety
system online. This distinction is central to AI safety because compute and cloud providers
can be both safety infrastructure and policy intermediaries [Sastry et al., 2024, Heim et al.,
2024]. A false positive can provoke wasteful autarky or a race to build under lower safety
standards. A false negative can leave critical evaluation and incident response dependent
on an actor who can withdraw access.
We contribute a formal update rule, a five layer exposure rubric, and a worked India
event sequence. The method assigns events to four states:WATCH,PENDING,ACTIVE, and
WITHDRAWN. A score is recomputed only in the active state. The pending state records a
promulgated or contracted change that has not yet taken operational effect. This is essential
for policies that are announced, delayed, rescinded, or only partially implemented.
2 From chokepoints to an event update problem
2.1 AI sovereignty is dependency control
Compute governance research identifies several properties that make advanced computing
a policy lever: it is comparatively measurable, excludable, and concentrated [Sastry et al.,
2024]. Cloud providers can also record, verify, and enforce rules over customer workloads
[Heim et al., 2024]. These properties create governance opportunities, but they also create
dependence. Control may sit with the state where a data center is located, the company op-
erating it, the jurisdiction governing that company, or the vendor producing the accelerator
[Hawkins et al., 2025]. Digital platforms can acquire infrastructural power that resembles
functions conventionally associated with states [Kelton et al., 2022].

belongs to one of five layersℓ∈ L:

2.2 Why ordinary event coding fails
The usual response to uncertainty is to add a “geopolitical risk” variable. That move
creates three problems. First, it bundles distinct mechanisms. An export rule, a presidential
statement, a military clash, and a cloud outage are not commensurate events. Second, it
rewards salience. The most visible event receives the largest update, even when the relevant
dependency is unchanged. Third, it is asymmetric. Analysts often raise risk after threatening
news but lower it immediately after investment announcements, even though announced
capacity may not yet exist.
The result is narrative volatility. Scores become measures of attention rather than exposure.
A useful rubric therefore needs two components: a stable set of dependency dimensions
and a strict rule governing when events update them.

3.2 Four event states
Passing the gate does not always imply an immediate score change. We assign each event
to one of four states.
Watch.At least one gate condition fails. The event is logged with an evidence request and a
next review date.
Pending.The gate passes, but the material consequence has a future effective date or
depends on implementation. A promulgated export rule with a later compliance date is
pending. A funded accelerator purchase is pending until delivery, commissioning, and user
access are verified.
Active.The consequence is in force or operationally verified. The affected dimensions are
recomputed from current evidence.
Withdrawn.A pending or active event is rescinded, expires, is enjoined, or is operationally
reversed. The score returns only after the reversal is verified. The historical record is
preserved.
This state machine matters because the time between announcement and operation is often
where policy changes. It also prevents a positive announcement from being counted twice,
first when announced and again when deployed.
3.3 Evidence and confidence
Every coded observation contains an as of date, source, rationale, confidence grade, trend,
and review trigger. Confidence is reported separately from exposure. Grade A denotes
an official, contractual, audited, or directly verified record. Grade B requires at least two
current credible sources. Grade C denotes one credible but incomplete or indirect source.
Grade D denotes an estimate or expert inference. Missing evidence is marked “not assessed,”
never scored as zero.
The separation between score and confidence is important. A dependency may be critically
exposed with low confidence, or resilient with high confidence. Folding confidence into the
score would make the meaning of the number ambiguous.

4 India as a diagnostic case, 2025–2026
4.1 Case selection and baseline
India combines growing domestic capability with deep interdependence. Official statements
in February 2026 reported more than 38,000 high end GPUs onboarded through the IndiaAI
Mission and announced 20,000 additional GPUs [Press Information Bureau, Government
of India, 2026c]. A March statement described the additional 20,000 as “currently under
process” [Press Information Bureau, Government of India, 2026b]. The Materiality Gate
records the first quantity according to the scope and definition in the official source, while
assigning the additional quantity toPENDING. It does not reduce exposure until delivery,
commissioning, allocation terms, and actual availability are verified.
This domestic example establishes the symmetry of the method. Favorable announcements
face the same test as adverse geopolitical signals. The question is not whether a policy
sounds reassuring. The question is whether a dependency has changed.
The case does not attempt to produce a definitive national score. Public data are insuffi-
cient for that task, especially for workload shares, contract terms, replacement inventories,
firmware control, and tested failover. Instead, we use the event sequence to test whether a
transparent analyst would update the rubric and why. The evidence freeze is 21 June 2026.
Later developments should enter as new event records rather than being silently backfilled
into earlier scores.
4.2 Event sequence
Figure 2 places the events on a common timeline. The January 2025 AI Diffusion Rule is
the positive control. It specified a worldwide licensing framework for advanced computing
integrated circuits and certain model weights [U.S. Department of Commerce, Bureau of
Industry and Security, 2025b]. It therefore passes the gate for accelerator and model de-
pendencies. Because compliance requirements were scheduled for May 15, it first entered
PENDING. The Commerce Department announced on May 13 that it would not enforce the
new requirements and would rescind the rule [U.S. Department of Commerce, Bureau of In-
dustry and Security, 2025a]. The pending shock was therefore moved toWITHDRAWNbefore
its compliance date.
The remaining events are politically salient but fail at least one dependency test. After
the May 2025 India–Pakistan conflict, Trump repeatedly claimed that he had stopped the
fighting, including through trade pressure. India stated that the cessation was arranged
through existing military channels and that no U.S. mediation or trade discussion occurred
[Ministry of External Affairs, Government of India, 2025, Reuters, 2025]. The claim is
therefore contested. More importantly for this paper, neither account documents a change
in an AI infrastructure dependency.
On June 18, 2025, Trump hosted Pakistan’s army chief, Field Marshal Asim Munir, at the
White House [Reuters, 2025]. The meeting can reasonably raise the monitoring priority
assigned to U.S.–Pakistan relations. It does not establish preferential accelerator access, a
cloud service change, an export license denial to India, or a reduction in safety cooperation.
It remains inWATCH.
In June 2026, Pakistan acted as witness and mediator for the interim U.S.–Iran agreement ti-
tled the Islamabad Memorandum of Understanding [Reuters, 2026]. This elevated Pakistan’s
diplomatic role. It again lacked a documented transmission path to India’s AI dependencies
and remains inWATCH.
Finally, the U.S. military restored the U.S. Pacific Command name in June 2026. The official
command biography reflected the new designation by June 18 [U.S. Pacific Command, 2026].
The 2018 addition of “Indo” had explicitly recognized the growing connection between the
Indian and Pacific Oceans [U.S. Department of Defense, 2018]. Reversing that label is a
strategic signal, but a label is not force posture, intelligence sharing, an export rule, or a
compute contract. The event remains inWATCHunless operational evidence follows.

4.3 Gate results
Table 1 reports the coding. “Authority exercised” is dependency specific. The U.S. president
has broad political authority, but a speech or meeting does not itself exercise export, contrac-
tual, or operational control over an accelerator order. This distinction prevents the actor’s
office from substituting for a causal mechanism.
The positive control demonstrates that the gate is not a device for dismissing geopolitics. The
AI Diffusion Rule would have changed the legal conditions governing advanced computing
access. It receives stronger treatment than every diplomatic event in the sequence. The
rescission then demonstrates why the system must preserve state transitions rather than
overwrite the past.
The domestic capacity announcement provides a second control. It tests favorable evidence.
A planned expansion can be highly credible and still not be operational. Counting it
immediately would understate concentration and substitution risk. The gate therefore
resists both pessimistic and optimistic narrative pressure.
5 Implications for AI safety and middle power strategy
5.1 Safety continuity is a first class exposure dimension
Traditional sovereignty indexes focus on ownership, location, or aggregate capacity. AI
safety requires an additional question: what happens to evaluation, monitoring, incident
response, and control during disruption? This motivates the S dimension in Eq. 1. The
relevant unit is not simply compute quantity. It is the continuity of safety relevant functions.

Consider two accelerator shortages with equal capacity loss. In the first, commercial training
slows while red team evaluations and incident response retain reserved capacity. In the
second, all workloads compete for the same pool and safety evaluations are deferred. The
geopolitical event is similar, but the safety exposure differs. Likewise, a cloud migration
that preserves data but loses audit logs, model provenance, or emergency credentials is not
a safe failover.
This framing follows the socio technical view that safety failures emerge through institutions
and deployment conditions, not only model behavior [Amodei et al., 2016, Raji and Dobbe,
2023]. It also makes the rubric actionable. Governments can reduce exposure through pro-
tected evaluation capacity, tested portability, audit log escrow, key management separation,
and contract clauses preserving access for incident response.
5.2 Resilient interdependence, not reflexive autarky
A score that reacts to every diplomatic signal can make autarky appear rational. Yet
complete self sufficiency across accelerators, fabrication, memory, design tools, clouds,
models, energy, and cables is infeasible for most states and may produce new concentration.
A rushed sovereignty program can also redirect scarce compute from independent safety
research toward prestige models.
The Materiality Gate supports a narrower strategy: identify dependencies where authority
and material pathways are real, then reduce single points of failure. This may involve
supplier diversity, multi jurisdiction contracts, tested workload portability, domestic mainte-
nance capability, strategic inventories, and reciprocal emergency access. The goal is resilient
interdependence, not the absence of foreign inputs.
For middle powers, this is a practical distinction. Diplomatic alignment can change quickly.
Infrastructure contracts and supply chains change through slower, observable acts. A
versioned dependency register allows a state to cooperate widely while preparing for
specific chokepoints.
5.3 A dashboard should explain why it did not move
Most monitoring systems emphasize changes. A trustworthy sovereignty dashboard should
also explain nonchanges. When a salient event fails the gate, the system should publish
the failed condition, the evidence sought, and the review trigger. For the Pacific Command
renaming, for example, triggers might include changes in Indian Ocean force posture, Quad
exercises, defense technology agreements, export treatment, or intelligence arrangements.
For Pakistan’s diplomatic elevation, triggers might include preferential access to controlled
technology that materially changes India’s substitution environment.
This approach does not claim that symbols are meaningless. Symbols can precede policy. It
treats them as hypotheses requiring a transmission path. That makes the analysis responsive
without allowing interpretation to masquerade as measurement.
6 Limitations
The method has five limitations. First, public evidence is incomplete. Contractual controls,
provider dependencies, spare inventories, and cyber access may not be observable. The
confidence field makes this limitation visible but cannot remove it.
Second, the gate is conservative. It may delay an update when credible intelligence indicates
an impending restriction before a public act. Analysts with classified or contractual evidence
can apply the same method, but their result may not be reproducible publicly.
Third, the 0 to 4 anchors discretize continuous risk. They are intended for transparent
comparison and sensitivity analysis, not precise probability estimation. The underlying
observations should be retained so later work can fit probabilistic models.
Fourth, dependencies interact. A semiconductor bottleneck can cascade into cloud availabil-

Fifth, this paper does not adjudicate broad claims about whether U.S. policy “favors” India

7 Conclusion
Geopolitical analysis becomes useful for AI safety when it reaches a dependency. The
Materiality Gate provides a simple discipline: verify the event, identify the authority
exercised, and document the material pathway. Events that fail remain visible as watchlist
signals. Events with future consequences enter a pending state. Only active, verified
consequences change exposure scores.
The India case shows why this discipline matters. A disputed mediation claim, a high
profile meeting, an international accord named for Islamabad, and a command renaming
can reshape political expectations without changing present AI infrastructure. A licensing
rule can change infrastructure access, even before a single chip shipment is denied. A GPU
announcement can improve future resilience without improving current resilience. Treating
these events differently is not caution for its own sake. It is measurement.
The paper’s central rule is intentionally plain: announcements are not assets, and symbols
are not outages. A winning sovereignty strategy should be equally plain about what has
actually changed.
Ethics statement
This work analyzes public geopolitical events that are politically contested. We sepa-
rate verified events from disputed causal claims and avoid inferring national intent from
rhetoric alone. The rubric is designed to reduce partisan overinterpretation by requiring
a dependency specific material pathway before a score changes. It uses public, aggregate
information and does not identify sensitive facility vulnerabilities, operational security
details, or exploitable failure points. The method could still be misused to present subjective
weights as objective rankings. We therefore require declared weights, source provenance,
confidence labels, and a preserved change log.

B Event coding protocol
For each event and dependency pair, analysts should complete the following record before
changing a score:
Field Required content
Event identifier Stable name, date, and jurisdiction
Dependency object Specific provider, product, facility class, contract, supply stage, or safety
function
Verification test Primary source or two independent sources establishing the event
Authority test Legal, contractual, ownership, operational, or coercive authority exercised
over the dependency
Material pathway Dimension changed, causal link, effective date, and measurable
consequence
State Watch, pending, active, or withdrawn
Prior/revised valuesC,J,T,K,Sbefore and after the event; blank if no update
Confidence A, B, C, or D, reported separately from exposure
Review trigger Concrete evidence that would reopen the coding decision
Analyst note Alternative explanation and known missing data
Table 7: Minimum event record for a reproducible update.
B.1 Decision rules
1. Score operational capacity separately from announced or contracted capacity.
2. Do not infer a material pathway from diplomatic tone alone.
3. Do not treat missing data as resilience.
4. Preserve prior scores and event states. Never overwrite the change history.
5. Reassess quarterly and immediately after export control changes, sanctions, conflict
escalation, major outages, contract changes, acquisitions, verified commissioning, or
supplier withdrawal.
6. Run an equal weight sensitivity analysis if policy weights are used.
7. For sequential supply chains, apply bottleneck overrides before averaging.

C.1 Disputed mediation claim
Event.Trump stated that he stopped the May 2025 India–Pakistan conflict and linked the
outcome to trade. India stated that the cessation was arranged directly through military
channels and rejected third party mediation [Ministry of External Affairs, Government of
India, 2025, Reuters, 2025].
Gate.The public statement is verified. It does not exercise dependency specific authority
and supplies no documented material pathway. State:WATCH. Score effect: none.
Review triggers.A change in export licensing for India or Pakistan; preferential access
to controlled accelerators; suspension of a cloud or model service; a defense technology
agreement that changes substitution time; or a verified reduction in safety cooperation.

D Suggested data schema
A minimal machine readable implementation uses one row per dependency criterion and
observation date:
country, layer, dependency_id, criterion,
score_0_to_4, as_of_date, event_id, event_state,

A separate event table stores V, A, and M, allowing analysts to reproduce why an event did
or did not change a score. This separation is important: the event log can update frequently
while the infrastructure table remains stable.
