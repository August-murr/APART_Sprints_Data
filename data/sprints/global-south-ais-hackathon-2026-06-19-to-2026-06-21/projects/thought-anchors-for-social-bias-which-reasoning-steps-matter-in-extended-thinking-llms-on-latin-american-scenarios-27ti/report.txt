Thought Anchors for Social Bias: Which Reasoning Steps Matter
in Extended Thinking LLMs on Latin American Scenarios
Andres Mosquera-Hernandez
Systems and Computing Engineering Department
Universidad de Los Andes
Cra. 1E No. 19A-40, Bogotá, Colombia
a.mosquerah2@uniandes.edu.co
Abstract
We investigate which reasoning steps in
extended-thinking LLMs are associated
with pro-stereotypical outputs on Latin
American social bias scenarios. Social bias
benchmarks, including BBQ (Parrish et al.,
2022), SESGO (Robles et al., 2024), and
EsBBQ/CaBBQ (Ruiz-Fernández et al., 2025),
measure final-answer distributions but provide
no visibility into intermediate reasoning.
We adapt the thought-anchor resampling
framework (Bogdan et al., 2025) to social
bias measurement: for each sentence-level
chunk in a model’s chain-of-thought, we
remove it, resample K=3 continuations, and
measure the change in pro-stereotypical output
probability via three importance metrics.
We apply this pipeline to SESGO-small,
an 80-item stratified pilot subset spanning
four Latin American bias categories (género,
clasismo, racismo, xenofobia), two languages,
and three recent models with extended
thinking. Across 1,708 chunk-level attribu-
tions, disambiguated contexts show higher
pro-stereotypical output rates than ambiguous
ones (33–75% vs. 0%), and high-importance
reasoning positions tend to be tagged as
logical_reasoning or context_recall
rather than stereotype_activation. These
pilot results suggest that bias-relevant moments
may lie in general inference steps rather
than overt stereotype invocations, offering a
step-level diagnostic target that complements
answer-level benchmarks. More broadly,
attributing bias to specific reasoning steps
could help localize where stereotyping enters a
model’s reasoning, though our findings remain
preliminary.
1 Introduction
Extended-thinking LLMs now expose their chain-
of-thought as a separable reasoning stream pro-
duced before the final answer. This capability
opens a question that prior bias evaluations can-
not address: at which specific reasoning step does
a model tend toward a stereotypical output?
Social bias benchmarks have documented that
LLMs produce stereotypical final answers across
cultural contexts: BBQ (Parrish et al., 2022) for
the United States, SESGO (Robles et al., 2024) for
Latin America, CBBQ (Huang and Xiong, 2023)
for China, and KoBBQ (Jin et al., 2024) for Korea.
Despite this progress, all operate at the final-answer
level. B-score (V o et al., 2025) extends evaluation
to multi-turn patterns but still measures answer-
level phenomena. The thought-anchor framework
(Bogdan et al., 2025) assigns importance scores to
individual chain-of-thought steps for mathematical
reasoning, but has not been applied to social bias.
The result is a gap: it remains unclear whether
biased outputs tend to arise at explicit stereotype
invocations, at general logical inference steps, or at
the moment of option evaluation.
We take a step toward closing this gap by adapt-
ing thought-anchor resampling to social bias mea-
surement on SESGO. Our contributions are:
• Step-level bias attribution pipeline.We
adapt thought-anchor resampling to produce
chunk-level importance scores for stereotyp-
ical reasoning in extended-thinking LLMs, a
granularity not addressed by prior bias bench-
marks, to our knowledge.
• Pilot evidence for the disambiguation ef-
fect.On SESGO-small, pro-stereotypical out-
put rates are higher in disambiguated con-
texts (33–75%) than ambiguous contexts (0%)
across all three evaluated models, consistent
with the core BBQ finding in a multilingual
Latin American setting.
• Preliminary reasoning step characteriza-
tion.On this pilot, high-importance positions
tend to be tagged as logical_reasoning
and context_recall rather than

2 Related Work
Social bias benchmarks.Parrish et al. (2022)
introduced the paradigm of ambiguous vs. dis-
ambiguated MCQ scenarios for probing social
bias in English LLMs. Multilingual extensions,
CBBQ (Huang and Xiong, 2023), KoBBQ (Jin
et al., 2024), MBBQ (Neplenbroek et al., 2024),
adapted this design to Chinese, Korean, and mul-
tilingual settings. EsBBQ and CaBBQ (Ruiz-
Fernández et al., 2025) cover Spanish and Cata-
lan in a Spain-centric context; SESGO (Robles
et al., 2024) targets Latin American stereotypes
and adds explicit target/other group indices and
question_polarity metadata enabling program-
matic pro-stereo classification. LatamQA (Karmim
et al., 2026) covers Latin American cultural knowl-
edge but measures factual accuracy, not stereotyp-
ing. All these benchmarks share a design choice
that limits step-level analysis: they report final-
answer distributions only.
Chain-of-thought attribution.Bogdan et al.
(2025) introduced rollout resampling for impor-
tance attribution of CoT steps in mathematical rea-
soning. Token-level attribution methods (attention
rollout, integrated gradients) operate on represen-
tations rather than semantic steps, making them
less suited for identifying which conceptual move
drives a particular output. To our knowledge, no
prior work applies step-level rollout attribution to
social bias scenarios with extended-thinking mod-
els.
Bias in chain-of-thought reasoning.Shaikh
et al. (2023) examined whether chain-of-thought
prompting reduces or amplifies social bias in final
answers, but without attributing importance to indi-
vidual steps. Kotek et al. (2023) found that LLMs
invoke demographic information at inference time.
Our work complements these findings with a sys-
tematic attribution pipeline.

3 Methods
3.1 Dataset: SESGO-small
SESGO (Robles et al., 2024) is a Latin American
social bias benchmark structured as three-option
MCQ items: one stereotyping the target group,
one naming the non-stereotyped group, and one
withholding judgment (always option C). Items en-
code a bias category (género, clasismo, racismo,
xenofobia), a context condition (ambiguous or dis-
ambiguated), and a question polarity (negative or
non-negative).
We classify outputs aspro-stereotypicalfollow-
ing the SESGO convention: under negative polar-
ity, selecting the target group index; under non-
negative polarity, selecting the other group index
(denying a positive trait to the target).
SESGO-smallis a stratified 80-item pilot sub-
set: 5 ambiguous and 5 disambiguated items per
category per language (Table 1). This subset is
not sufficient for drawing stable quantitative con-
clusions; it serves as a proof-of-concept for the
attribution pipeline.
3.2 Models
We evaluate three recent models: Qwen3.7-Plus
(Fireworks AI, reasoning_content field), Gem-
ini 2.5 Flash (Google, ThinkingConfig), and
Claude Sonnet 4.6 (Anthropic, extended thinking
blocks with budget_tokens=8000). All expose
their chain-of-thought separately from the final an-
swer.
3.3 CoT Generation and Chunking
For each item, we extract thebase chain-of-thought
(CoT) from the thinking portion of the model’s
output and parse the final answer letter (A, B, or
C) via regex. We then split the CoT intochunks
using sentence-boundary detection on punctuation
and paragraph breaks, merging chunks shorter than
10 characters into their neighbors.

3.4 Importance Attribution via Rollout
Resampling
For each chunk i in the base CoT of item q, we:
(1) truncate the CoT prefix at chunki−1, (2) resam-
ple K=3 continuations by prompting the model to
continue reasoning from that prefix, and (3) classify
each rollout as pro-stereo, anti-stereo, or unknown.
We compute three importance metrics.Resam-
pling bias importance( ∆RS) measures the change
in P(pro-stereo) between consecutive chunk posi-
tions:

3.5 Semantic Chunk Labeling
To explore which types of reasoning steps tend
to carry higher bias importance, we label each
chunk with function tags using Claude Haiku
(claude-haiku-4-5-20251001) and a predefined
ten-tag taxonomy ( stereotype_activation,
context_recall, logical_reasoning,
option_evaluation, backtracking, and
others). Tag accuracy in this domain is unknown;
labels should be treated as approximations.

Three observations emerge, all provisional given
smalln:
Ambiguous condition.All three models pro-
duce 0% pro-stereotypical base answers in ambigu-

models not spontaneously imposing stereotypes
when the context provides no group-identifying
information.
Disambiguated condition.Pro-stereo rates are
higher when group-identifying context is available
(33–75%), consistent with the core BBQ finding
that disambiguation is associated with stereotypical
responses.
Category breakdown.On SESGO-small, xeno-
fobia items show higher pro-stereo rates than other
categories across all three models (38–50%), while
racismo items show 0% across all evaluated sce-

properties of the specific items sampled and war-

4.2 Chunk-Level Importance: Which
Reasoning Steps Associate with Bias
Among 37 high-importance chunks (|∆RS|>0.2 ,
the top ≈1.2% of 3,023 total labeled chunks),
the most frequent tags are context_recall (11
chunks, 30%) and logical_reasoning (9 chunks,
24%), not stereotype_activation as initially
hypothesized (Table 3). stereotype_activation
(n=34 total labeled chunks, 1.1%) and
group_attribution (n=21, 0.7%) each have
zero high-importance representatives on this pilot.

5 Conclusion
We adapted thought-anchor resampling to social
bias measurement and applied it to SESGO-small,
a stratified 80-item pilot spanning four Latin Amer-
ican bias categories, two languages, and three
extended-thinking LLMs. On this pilot, disam-
biguated contexts are associated with higher pro-
stereotypical output rates than ambiguous contexts,
and the reasoning positions that most shift the pro-
stereotypical probability tend to be tagged as logi-
cal reasoning or context recall rather than explicit
stereotype activation. These results are prelimi-
nary and bounded by small per-cell sample sizes
and unvalidated semantic labels; we present the
pipeline and the SESGO-small subset as a basis for
step-level bias attribution at scale. Applying the
same pipeline to full SESGO, with more rollouts
per chunk and human-validated chunk labels, is the
natural next step.
Limitations
Sample size.SESGO-small has 10 items per cell.
Category-level results rest on 2–8 items per cell;
95% binomial CIs span ±30–45 pp. Full SESGO
would be needed for stable conclusions.
Rollout count. K=3 yields P(pro-stereo)∈
{0, 1 3 , 2 3 ,1} . Importance scores are coarse; aggre-
gated trends are more interpretable than individual
chunk scores.
Claude coverage.Claude Sonnet 4.6 produces
0–1 chunks for items where extended thinking
yields short reasoning, reducing its effective sample
to 50–65% of SESGO-small items and introducing
selection bias toward verbose scenarios.
Semantic labeling.Chunk tags are assigned by
Claude Haiku without human validation. Tag ac-
curacy in this domain is unknown and could affect
patterns in §4 and Figure 2.

Broader Impacts
This work aims to improve interpretability of
stereotypical reasoning in LLMs on Latin Amer-
ican scenarios. Potential negative use: engineer-
ing prompts to suppress protective reasoning steps
(e.g., backtracking) to elicit stereotypical outputs.
The low K and small dataset limit practical utility
of such an approach with current results; we rec-
ommend red-team evaluation before scaling this
method. Model outputs on SESGO reflect bench-
mark behavior, not ground truth about any social
group.
Reproducibility
We will release the SESGO-small subset (item IDs
and stratification), the full attribution pipeline, all
chunk-level importance scores (∆RS, ∆CF, DKL),
and the Claude Haiku chunk labels under an open
license upon publication; an anonymized version is
available to reviewers. The dataset is derived from
the public SESGO benchmark (Robles et al., 2024).
Decoding parameters (temperature 0.6, top-p 0.95,
K=3 rollouts), model identifiers, and provider end-
points are documented in §3.2 and Appendix B.
Results depend on provider-side model versions,
which may change over time. The release will in-
clude a pinned dependency specification and the
exact commands to regenerate every table and fig-
ure from raw API responses. We use SESGO with
attribution to its authors (Robles et al., 2024); our
derived subset and code will be released under a
permissive open-source license.
