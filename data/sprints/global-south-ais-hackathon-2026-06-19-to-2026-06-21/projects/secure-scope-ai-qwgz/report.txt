Abstract 
Scanners such as Trivy and OWASP Dependency -Check produce large volumes of context -free 
findings that teams must review, prioritize, and fix manually; as a result, many vulnerabilities remain 
unremediated. We present Secure Scope AI, an agent that integrates natively into DevSecOps 
pipelines and works with any AI model —in the cloud or locally via Ollama —, pres erving data 
sovereignty. Unlike traditional scanners, the agent does not detect vulnerabilities itself: a deterministic 
correlation engine consolidates, normalizes, and deduplicates findings before any inference, and only 
then does the model contextualize,  prioritize, and propose remediations, safe versions, or 
compensating controls. We validated the platform with three reproducible pipelines over OWASP 
NodeGoat, which correlated 366 vulnerabilities per run (108 critical and 528 high accumulated) in ~3–
4 mi nutes, with AI analysis in seconds; the bottleneck is SCA, not the AI. The system explicitly 
separates deterministic evidence from AI interpretation and degrades gracefully when no provider is 
configured, showing that it is feasible to integrate AI into DevSecOps in an auditable, offline-first way, 
especially in Global South settings with limited resources. 
1. Introduction 
Development teams rely on tools such as Trivy and OWASP Dependency -Check to detect 
vulnerabilities in source code, dependencies, and con tainer images. However, these tools produce 
numerous findings that must be reviewed, prioritized, and fixed manually, which is time -consuming 
and requires specialized staff; as a result, many vulnerabilities go unremediated or are addressed late, 
increasing the attack surface. 
We present Secure Scope AI , an AI -powered cybersecurity agent that integrates natively into 
DevSecOps pipelines and can connect to any AI model, in the cloud or run locally, preserving data 
sovereignty. The agent runs the analysis, co rrelates findings, prioritizes by risk, and generates 
actionable recommendations: it proposes dependency updates and simulates their sanitization without 
modifying the repository, and when a direct fix is not feasible, it recommends compensating controls 
and specific mitigations. 
Our main contributions are: 
1. A provider-agnostic, AI-based security agent that integrates with commercial and local models 
inside DevSecOps pipelines. 
2. A deterministic correlation and prioritization engine for vulnerabilities that reduces manual effort 
and the risk of LLM hallucinations. 
3. A remediation system that proposes safe updates, simulates sanitization, and generates 
compensating controls when a direct fix is not possible. 
2. Related Work 

===== page 2 =====

2 
Software Composition Analysis (SCA) and container-scanning tools such as Trivy and OWASP 
Dependency-Check are the state of the art for identifying vulnerabilities in dependencies and images, 
backed by CVE and GHSA identifiers and CVSS metrics. However, they are limited to detecting and 
reporting: correlating results, removing duplicates, prioritizing, and defining remediation remain the 
team's responsibility. LLMs are already used to assist with these tasks, but work on prompt injection, 
multilingual jailbreaks, and the OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications shows that they can hallucinate 
or be manipulated through untrusted data; moreover, most commercial solutions depend on the cloud, 
which limits adoption where sovereignty or connectivity constraints exist. 
Secure Scope AI closes this gap by combining consolidated scanners with an agent able to operate 
with multiple AI providers or local models. It does not replace the detection engines; rather, it turns 
their results into actions: it correlates findings from  several sources, prioritizes by project context, 
recommends safe updates, simulates sanitization, and generates compensating controls when there 
is no direct fix. An organization would choose it over traditional solutions to move from a model of 
“vulnerability detection” to one of “AI -assisted remediation,” with contextual prioritization, verifiable 
recommendations, and the ability to operate entirely in local environments. 
3. Methods 
Secure Scope AI is a reproducible DevSecOps platform. The backend (Djang o, DRF, Celery, 
PostgreSQL, Redis) orchestrates the pipeline; the frontend (React) provides real-time monitoring and 
visualization; and an Nginx gateway centralizes access over HTTPS and isolates the internal services. 
Detection uses Trivy (container image s) and OWASP Dependency -Check (SCA), executed in an 
isolated security-tools container. The core is a seven -stage pipeline —cloning, dependency build, 
Docker build, container run, dependency analysis, Trivy scan, and intelligent analysis —; in the last 
stage the agent correlates, deduplicates, prioritizes, and recommends. The agent is decoupled from 
the AI provider (commercial models or local via Ollama), and each stage produces auditable logs and 
telemetry, enabling reproducibility and traceability (Figure 1, Appendix). 
3.1 Deterministic correlation and reliability 
A central decision is that the AI does not detect vulnerabilities. Before any inference, a deterministic 
engine consolidates the findings from Trivy and Dependency -Check: it unifies identifiers (CVE, 
GHSA), normalizes severities, extracts CVSS scores, and removes duplicates; when both tools detect 
the same vulnerability, it is correlated and the highest CVSS is kept. The result is a single, validated 
list that acts as the source of truth for the rest of the pipeline, preventing the model from inventing or 
altering findings and reducing the risk of hallucinations, while improving traceability and auditability. 
3.2 Multi-AI engine, sanitization, and security 
The Multi-AI Engine supports DeepSeek (default), OpenAI, Gemini, Azure OpenAI, and local models 
via Ollama (Llama 3.2, Mistral, Code Llama), plus custom endpoints; in our runs it operated in live 
mode with DeepSeek (Figure 2, Appendix). To minimize hallucinations, the AI only analyzes already-
validated vulnerabilities and uses structured responses, low rand omness, and post -hoc validation, 
complementing each finding with references to OWASP, CWE, and NIST. A sanitization module 
assesses which libraries could be fixed and simulates the outcome (“Fixable simulated”), generating 
a patch only as a local ZIP witho ut touching Git or the database (Figure 4, Appendix). The platform 
includes industry -aligned controls —JWT with rotation, Argon2, 2FA (TOTP), role -based access 
control, auditing, and API hardening—; by offline-first design, it can be deployed locally with Docker 
Compose, preloaded images, and vulnerability databases, using OWASP NodeGoat as the reference 
application.

Separating evidence from interpretation. We distinguish two layers: (1) reproducible deterministic 
observations —the 366 vulnerabilities, their severity, and the per-CVE correlation come from Trivy + 
Dependency-Check, independently of the mod el—; and (2) the interpretive layer, generated live by 
DeepSeek, which produces the executive summary (Figure 3, Appendix) and the prioritized 
remediation plan (Figure 6, Appendix). The system maintains this separation explicitly and, if no 
provider is configured, degrades gracefully to a deterministic CVSS-based fallback without halting the 
pipeline. As a robustness check, the repository's QA suite reports 104 tests passed and 2 skipped 
(API contract, authentication, permissions, errors, and integration). 
5. Discussion and Limitations 
Secure Scope AI shows that AI can be integrated into DevSecOps without granting it absolute 
authority: LLMs are most useful as an analysis and recommendation layer over data already validated 
by deterministic tools, not as primary detectors. From this we derive three principles for AI systems in 
security: use verifiable sources of truth before inference, validate and structure the model's responses, 
and maintain controlled degradation when the AI is unavailable. The offline -first approach further 
broadens access for organizations with connectivity, budget, or sovereignty constraints. We observed 
that the main challenge is not inference but the volume of findings: correlation and risk -based 
prioritization were effective at reducing noise. 
5.1 Limitations 

===== page 4 =====

4 
The evaluation focused on OWASP NodeGoat, so results could vary on larger applications or other 
languages; although the runs already execute in live mode with DeepSeek, we did not quantitatively 
compare against human analysts nor formally measure the precision of the LLM's recommendations. 
Although we incorporated controls, we do not fully resolve threats such as prompt injection or incorrect 
recommendations, and we assume Trivy and Dependency -Check are a reliable baseline: their false 
positives or negatives would propagate to later stages. Finally, running untrusted repositories inside 
the pipeline requires more robust isolation for production. 
5.2 Future Work 
We plan to evaluate with multiple real applications, incorporate metrics for precision, usefulness, and 
hallucination rate, and compare against human experts. We will also explore defenses against prompt 
injection, automatic validation of recommendations with NVD and OSV, remediation via controlled 
patches, and stronger isolation plus support for new languages and tools. 
6. Conclusion 
Secure Scope AI offers a practical architecture for integrating AI into DevSecOps pipelines in a secure, 
reproducible, and auditable way. Instead of replacing traditional tools, it combines deterministic 
scanning, finding correlation, and AI -assisted reasoning to turn technical results into remediation 
actions. By relying on verifiable sources, structured validation, and local or cloud deployments, it 
constitutes a replicable model of secure AI, adaptable to organizations w ith diverse capabilities and 
especially useful in Global South settings.

LLM Usage Statement 
During the development of Secure Scope AI, several LLM -based assistants were used to support 
specific project tasks. ChatGPT was used for overall architecture planning, component definition, and 
structuring the development flow. Antigravity supported backend implementation, task automation, 
and the generation of supporting code. DeepSeek was used to design and implement the intelligent 
agent's API and some components of the analysis engine. Cursor served as a programming assistant 
for code review, technical documentation, refactoring, and validation of architecture decisions. 
The AI models were used solely as development -assistance tools. All design, integration, 
implementation, testing, and final validation decisions were made by the authors. Likewise, all results, 
metrics, security analyses, and claims presented in this work were manually reviewed and verified by 
the team before being included in the project and in this report.
