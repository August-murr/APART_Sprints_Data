 NeuroTrace:  Spec-Aware  Neural  Network  Runtime  
 
 
                             Robert  Joseph  George                                    California  Institute  of  Technology    
With  Apart  Research  
Abstract  
AI-written  neural-network  code  can  run,  train,  export,  and  even  receive  a  veriﬁer  result  while  silently  drifting  from  the  speciﬁcation  the  user  intended.  This  creates  a  false-assurance  failure  mode:  a  proof  may  apply  to  the  wrong  runtime,  output  head,  input  domain,  training  behavior,  or  optimized  implementation.  We  introduce  NeuroTrace ,  a  spec-driven  observability  framework  for  AI-written  neural  models.  To  our  knowledge,  NeuroTrace  is  the  ﬁrst  framework  that  turns  neural  architecture  and  veriﬁcation  specs  into  live  runtime  contracts  for  PyTorch  execution,  training,  optimized  implementations,  and  veriﬁer  applicability.  It  generates  TorchLean-style  formal  architecture  artifacts,  PyTorch  runtimes,  hook  and  torch.fx  traces,  training-time  SpecTrace  logs,  robustness  evidence,  and  SpecFuzz  mutations.  During  execution,  it  records  layer  paths,  tensor  shapes,  dtypes,  activation  summaries,  gradient  ﬂow,  mode  assumptions,  and  artifact  provenance.  If  the  runtime,  export,  optimized  path,  or  property  binding  departs  from  the  accepted  spec,  NeuroTrace  emits  a  replayable  violation  artifact  and  rejects  veriﬁer  applicability.  We  evaluate  the  prototype  on  MLP,  CNN,  and  attention-style  models,  plus  controlled  reproductions  of  documented  bug  classes  such  as  ONNX/export  drift,  logits/probability  confusion,  input-contract  violations,  dropout/mode  drift,  and  broken  gradient  ﬂow.  The  long-term  goal  is  to  scale  this  into  a  practical  observability  layer  for  larger  ML  systems,  where  formal  specs  help  detect  real  implementation,  training,  compiler,  and  deployment  bugs  before  veriﬁcation  evidence  is  trusted.
