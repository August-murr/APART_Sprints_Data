Abstract  
The  AI  safety  and  governance  community  is  making  progress  on  deﬁning  red  lines  around  existential  risk  from  advanced  AI  systems,  and  building  veriﬁcation  infrastructure  to  support  this  objective.  However,  this  is  only  half  the  battle.  Implementing  these  red  lines  requires  unprecedented  international  coordination,  and  enforcing  them  requires  credible  commitments  from  national  governments,  all  during  a  period  of  increased  geopolitical  tensions.   
We  present  a  whitepaper  that  makes  the  case  that:  
●
 
Implementation
 
and
 
enforcement
 
of
 
red
 
lines
 
is
 
as
 
much
 
a
 political  question  as  it  is  a  technical  one  
●
 
While
 
studies
 
exist
 
showing
 
general
 
public
 
support
 
for
 
AI
 regulation,  the  depth  of  domestic  political  support  among  major  powers  for  costly  international  AI  regulation  and  willingness  to  make  real  sacriﬁces  to  enforce  these  red  lines  is  understudied  
●
 
Rigorous
 
policy-credible
 
surveys
 
of
 
public
 
opinion
 
in
 
this
 
area
 would  both  enhance  the  AI  safety  community’s  efforts  to  prioritize  scarce  resources  to  the  most  effective  action  areas  and  provide  advocates  a  valuable  source  of  evidence  to  point  to  when  lobbying  national  governments  and  international  organizations  
And  therefore  proposes  and  develops  the  methodology  for  AIRAS.  
1  Research  conducted  at  the  Technical  AI  Governance  Challenge,  2026

1.  Introduction  
 The  AI  governance  community  is  converging  on  an  important  idea:  some  AI  capabilities  and

These

AI

should

The  concept  has  rapidly  gained  institutional  traction.  This  is  no  longer  a  debate  about  whether  
boundaries

It

debate

which
 
boundaries
 
to

how

enforce
 
them,

whether

political
 
will
 
exists
 
to
 
do
 
so.

1.  A  survey  framework  (AIRAS)  that  measures  comprehension,  risk  perception,  policy

institutional
 
trust,
 
and
 
mobilization
 
potential
 
for
 
speciﬁc
 
AI
 
safety
 
red
 
lines.
 
 2.  A  proof-of-concept  validation  using  synthetic  respondent  proﬁles:  we  generated  diverse

3.  Methods 
 AIRAS  is  built  on  ﬁve  design  principles  derived  from  limitations  in  existing  AI  public  opinion  
research:
 
Comprehension  Before  Evaluation.  Every  red  line  assessment  begins  with  a  comprehension  gate.  
Respondents
 
explain
 
the
 
concept
 
in
 
their
 
own
 
words
 
and
 
rate
 
their
 
conﬁdence.
 
Downstream
 
responses
 
can
 
be
 
weighted
 
by
 
comprehension,
 
and
 
comprehension
 
rates
 
themselves
 
become
 
a
 
key
 
output
 
metric.
 
Decompose  Risk  Perception.  We  separately  elicit  perceived  likelihood,  perceived  severity,  and  
relative
 
importance.
 
A
 
respondent
 
might
 
report
 
moderate
 
concern
 
because
 
they
 
think
 
a
 
risk
 
is
 
unlikely
 
but
 
catastrophic,
 
or
 
likely
 
but
 
manageable—these
 
have
 
different
 
implications
 
for
 
political
 
viability.
 
Test  Support  Under  Tradeoff  Conditions.  After  measuring  raw  policy  support,  we  test  whether  
support
 
persists
 
under
 
explicit
 
tradeoff
 
conditions:
 
slower
 
AI
 
development,
 
higher
 
consumer
 
prices,
 
national
 
competitive
 
disadvantage,
 
taxpayer
 
funding
 
requirements.
 
The
 
gap
 
between
 
raw
 
and
 
conditional
 
support
 
reveals
 
cost
 
sensitivity.
 
Map  the  Accountability  Landscape.  We  elicit  both  responsibility  attribution  (who  should  act)  and  
trust
 
ratings
 
(conﬁdence
 
in
 
each
 
actor).
 
The
 
gap
 
between
 
"should
 
act"
 
and
 
"trusted
 
to
 
act"
 
identiﬁes
 
legitimacy
 
deﬁcits
 
that
 
may
 
impede
 
implementation.
 
Measure  Mobilization  Potential.  We  elicit  behavioral  intentions  across  a  range  of  actions  with  
varying
 
cost—from
 
discussing
 
with
 
friends
 
to
 
participating
 
in
 
protests—producing
 
a
 
mobilization
 
potential
 
score
 
that
 
distinguishes
 
latent
 
from
 
activated
 
support.
 
See  the  whitepaper  for  full  methodology  details.

5.  Discussion  and  Limitations  
Limitations
 
This  is  a  whitepaper  proposing  a  methodology  for  AIRAS  and  how  the  results  will  be  useful  to  the  
AI
 
safety
 
community
 
and
 
to
 
policymakers.
 
It
 
was
 
not
 
possible
 
to
 
conduct
 
the
 
survey
 
with
 
a
 
meaningful
 
number
 
of
 
human
 
participants
 
during
 
the
 
allotted
 
time,
 
so
 
synthetic
 
results
 
were
 
used
 
to
 
illustrate
 
the
 
potential
 
value.
  
Future  Work  
We  propose  a  phased  implementation  path.  Phase  1  (concept  validation)  validates  the  theory  of  
value
 
through
 
expert
 
consultation,
 
methodology
 
reﬁnement,
 
and
 
small-scale
 
trials.
 
Phase
 
2
 
(publishable
 
pilot)
 
produces
 
a
 
peer-reviewable
 
single-country
 
study
 
with
 
full
 
analysis
 
and
 
media
 
circulation.
 
Phase
 
3
 
(deﬁnitive
 
study)
 
deploys
 
cross-nationally
 
across
 
key
 
AI
 
powers
 
(US,
 
China,
 
UK,
 
EU)
 
to

policy-credible
 
evidence
 
for
 
international
 
coordination
 
discussions.
 
This

to

resources.

6.  Conclusion  
AI  governance  red  lines  require  political  will  to  enforce.  AIRAS  provides  a  systematic  framework  
for
 
assessing
 
whether
 
that
 
political
 
will
 
exists
 
by
 
measuring
 
public
 
comprehension,
 
risk
 
perception,
 
policy
 
support
 
under
 
tradeoff
 
conditions,
 
enforcement
 
appetite,
 
institutional
 
trust,
 
and
 
mobilization
 
potential
 
for
 
speciﬁc
 
proposed
 
prohibitions.
 
The
 
resulting
 
political
 
viability
 
scorecards
 
enable
 
advocates
 
to
 
identify
 
where
 
to
 
push
 
now

foundational
 
work
 
is
 
needed,
 
complementing
 
the
 
technical
 
governance
 
infrastructure
 
being
 
developed
 
across
 
the
 
AI
 
safety
 
community.
 
The
 
gap
 
between
 
expert
 
consensus
 
on
 
AI
 
risk
 
thresholds

the
 
political
 
conditions
 
for

current
 
governance

AIRAS
 
is

to

that
 
gap—providing

political
 
intelligence
 
that

red
 
lines
 
to

to
 
implementation.
