Abstract  
The  governance  of  frontier  artiﬁcial  intelligence  systems  faces  a  
fundamental  veriﬁcation  paradox:  regulatory  bodies  require  
evidence  of  compliance  with  compute  thresholds,  safety  
evaluations,  and  organizational  commitments,  yet  AI  laboratories  
legitimately  resist  disclosing  information  that  could  compromise  
competitive  advantages  or  reveal  security  vulnerabilities.  This  
tension  has  stalled  progress  on  international  AI  coordination,  
undermined  the  enforceability  of  frameworks  such  as  the  EU  AI  
Act,  and  reduced  Responsible  Scaling  Policies  to  exercises  in  
self-attestation.  We  present  ZK-GovProof,  a  cryptographic  
framework  that  resolves  this  paradox  through  zero-knowledge  
succinct  non-interactive  arguments  of  knowledge  
(zkSNARKs)—enabling  laboratories  to  demonstrate  regulatory  
compliance  without  revealing  the  underlying  sensitive  data.  Our  
system  contributes  three  novel  elements:  working  Groth16  circuits  
for  compute  thresholds,  safety  evaluation  attestation,  and  policy  
compliance  veriﬁcation;  a  composable  proof  architecture  that  
bundles  multiple  governance  requirements  into  uniﬁed  submission  
artifacts  with  3×  reduction  in  veriﬁcation  overhead;  and  a  
systematic  veriﬁability  gap  analysis  that  delineates  the  boundaries  
of  what  zero-knowledge  cryptography  can  and  cannot  guarantee  
for  AI  governance.  We  validate  our  implementation  against  real  
regulatory  frameworks—the  EU  AI  Act  Article  55  obligations  for
