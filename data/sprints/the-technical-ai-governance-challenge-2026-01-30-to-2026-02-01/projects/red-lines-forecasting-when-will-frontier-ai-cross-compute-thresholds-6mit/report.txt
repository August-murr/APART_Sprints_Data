Red Lines Forecasting: When Will Frontier AI Cross
Compute Thresholds?
Kwesi Amanfu
Independent
With
Apart Research
Abstract
We forecast when frontier AI training runs will cross compute thresholds relevant
to AI governance. Frontier models are those trained with the largest computational
budgets each year; we measure training compute in FLOPs (floating-point opera-
tions, the total computational work used to train a model). Using Epoch AI data,
we find that both policy-defined thresholds (EU AI Act 10 25 FLOP, US EO 10 26
FLOP) have already been crossed. Compute milestones at 10 ×, 100×, and approx-
imately 3000× the US EO threshold, which are expected within one to three years.
We validate forecasts with rolling origin cross-validation and calibration correction.
We distinguish policy thresholds from compute milestones. The latter are training
budget forecasts, not capability claims.
NOTE: This work was completed over 36 hours as part of a hackathon and the claims require
additional validation.

1 Introduction
AI governance frameworks increasingly rely on compute thresholds to trigger regulatory re-
quirements. The EU AI Act flags models with more than 10 25 FLOPs, triggering systemic
risk assessment requirements. US Executive Order 14110 (now rescinded) previously required
reporting above 10 26 FLOP. We retain this as a reference threshold given its influence on AI
governance discourse.
Policymakers set thresholds but lack tools to forecast when they will be crossed. This
creates a gap between regulatory intent and practical implementation. Our work addresses this
gap by providing empirically grounded forecasts of when frontier training runs will cross these
thresholds and milestones.
The Epoch Capabilities Index (ECI) aggregates performance across major AI benchmarks
into a single score. Epoch defines 165 ECI as roughly PhD-level problem solving, meaning
the ability to solve novel research-level problems. We use ECI to cross-validate our compute
forecasts with capability trends.
Main Contributions
Our main contributions are:
1. Empirical forecasts showing both policy-defined thresholds (EU AI Act, US EO) have
already been crossed, with future milestones arriving within one to three years
2. A rigorous validation framework using rolling origin cross-validation and calibration cor-
rection
3. Conceptual distinction between policy thresholds (which trigger regulations) and compute
milestones (which are forecasting markers without regulatory weight)
2 Related Work
Sevilla et al. (2022) established the foundational analysis of compute trends across three eras
of machine learning, demonstrating exponential growth in training compute over time. Our
work extends this by focusing specifically on governance-relevant thresholds and providing
uncertainty-quantified forecasts.
Erben et al. (2025) analyzed training compute thresholds for the EU AI Act and found
that compute thresholds only weakly correlate with aggregated task performance. This finding
motivates our careful distinction between compute milestones and capability claims.
Anthropic’s Responsible Scaling Policy (2023) defines AI Safety Levels based on capability
evaluations, not on compute thresholds. This approach recognizes the limitations of FLOP as
a proxy for capability and informs our methodological choices.
Our work differs from prior analyses by providing explicit timeline forecasts with calibrated
uncertainty intervals, validated through backtesting rather than solely retrospective trend anal-
ysis.

3.3 Forecasting Approach
We forecast in log 10(FLOP) space, meaning we model 10 25, 10 26, 10 27 rather than the raw
numbers which span many orders of magnitude. In this space, a linear trend corresponds to
exponential growth in raw FLOP (approximately 4 .5× per year).
We compared five models: naive, linear, piecewise, quadratic, and exponential. We selected
piecewise for best fit. More complex models do not improve short horizon forecasts (∆BIC < 2
versus more complex alternatives).
3.4 Validation Framework
Our validation approach includes:
 Rolling origin cross validation at one, two, and three year horizons
 Calibration correction to achieve approximately 90% prediction interval coverage
 Bootstrap resampling with 2,000 iterations for uncertainty quantification

5 Discussion and Limitations
5.1 Implications
Our analysis reveals that governance mechanisms should already be in place, as both existing
policy thresholds have been exceeded. Future milestones arrive quickly: 10 × US EO in 2026,
100× in 2027. Uncertainty grows rapidly, with the 95% confidence interval spanning more than
two years for milestones beyond 2027.
These findings suggest that policymakers should treat current thresholds as already binding
while preparing governance frameworks for the next generation of frontier models.
5.2 Why We Avoid the Capability to Compute Mappings
ASL 4 (AI Safety Level 4) is a capability tier in Anthropic’s Responsible Scaling Policy that
triggers safety measures when models can autonomously conduct novel research. Crucially,
Anthropic defines ASL levels based on capability evaluations, not FLOPs. There is no “ASL 4
equals X FLOP” formula in the literature. We avoid this common error by using policy-anchored
milestones instead.

5.3 Limitations
Compute does not equal capability . We forecast when training budgets will grow, not
when capabilities will emerge. As Erben et al. (2025) show, compute only weakly correlates
with aggregated task performance. The compute milestones are training budget markers, not
capability claims.
Threshold uncertainty .Beyond the EU AI Act and US EO, our milestones are forecasting
markers rather than literature-backed claims.
No saturation modeling. There is growing discussion of hitting a wall, meaning frontier
labs may face data exhaustion (running out of high-quality training data), diminishing algo-
rithmic returns (each doubling of compute yields smaller capability gains), or hardware limits
(chip manufacturing and energy constraints). Our exponential models cannot capture S-curve
saturation. Historical data (2012–2025) shows no clear slowdown, but if frontier labs are already
seeing diminished returns, our forecasts would be optimistic.
Short-horizon reliability . Uncertainty grows rapidly. The 95% confidence interval ex-
tends beyond 2027 by more than 2 years.
5.4 Future Work
Future extensions could incorporate trends in algorithmic efficiency to model effective compute
rather than raw FLOPs. Saturation modeling using S-curve or logistic growth models could
address potential slowdowns. Integration with capability evaluations could provide more direct
governance relevance.
6 Conclusion
Both policy-defined thresholds (EU AI Act 10 25, US EO 10 26) have been crossed. Compute
milestones at 10 ×, 100×, and approximately 3000 × the US EO threshold are expected within
one to three years under current trends.
These are training budget forecasts, not capability predictions. Governance mechanisms
triggered at these compute levels should incorporate capability evaluations rather than rely
solely on FLOP.
