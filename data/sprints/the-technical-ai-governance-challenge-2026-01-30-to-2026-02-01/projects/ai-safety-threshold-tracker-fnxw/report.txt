Abstract 
As AI capabilities rapidly advance, policymakers and researchers lack unified tools connecting 
capability benchmarks to governance-defined thresholds. We present the AI Safety Threshold 
Tracker, an interactive dashboard that aggregates verified capability data from two categories of 
authoritative sources: (1) third-party benchmarks (METR, SWE-Bench, WMDP) and (2) verified 
company RSP data from official system cards (Anthropic ASL classifications, CBRN uplift 
scores). We map these to thresholds derived from international governance frameworks. Our 
key innovation is user-configurable thresholds with transparent justification labeling, 
distinguishing between data-driven thresholds (e.g., EU AI Act’s 10^25 FLOP) and our 
interpretations of qualitative frameworks (e.g., IDAIS Red Lines). The dashboard tracks 4 active 
capability metrics with configurable thresholds (WMDP, METR, SWE-Bench, Eval Awareness), 
2 categorical metrics from company RSPs (ASL Classification, CBRN Uplift), and documents 5 
inactive metrics awaiting data availability. Key findings include Claude Opus 4 being the first 
model released under ASL-3 Standard due to 2.53x CBRN bio uplift exceeding Anthropic’s pre-
registered 2x threshold. We document all sources in a comprehensive verification document 
and acknowledge significant data gaps where metrics lack published scores for most models. 
This tool aims to provide a shared reference point for discussing capability levels relative to 
governance concerns. 
1. Introduction 
AI capabilities are advancing rapidly. METR research shows task horizons doubling every 7 
months, with Claude Opus 4.5 achieving 4h 49m autonomous operation (METR, Dec 2025). 
Training compute doubles every 6 months (Epoch AI). Multiple governance frameworks have 
emerged to define safety thresholds: the IDAIS Beijing consensus, EU AI Act, Seoul Frontier AI 
Commitments, and company Responsible Scaling Policies. 
However, these frameworks face a critical challenge: they define concerns qualitatively while 
capability assessments are quantitative. There is no unified tool bridging this gap with 
transparent methodology. 
Our main contributions are: 
1. A capability dashboard aggregating 11 metrics from verified sources (METR, SWE-
Bench, WMDP, UK AISI, Epoch AI) 
2. Framework-mapped thresholds with explicit justification types (Data-Driven, 
Qualitative, Interpretation)

2. Related Work 
Several efforts track AI capabilities or governance frameworks: 
• METR publishes autonomous task horizon evaluations for select frontier models 
(metr.org). 
• SWE-Bench provides public leaderboards for coding agent performance 
(swebench.com). 
• Epoch AI estimates training compute for 3,200+ models (epoch.ai). 
• FLI AI Safety Index rates companies on safety practices, not measured capabilities. 
• UK AISI Gray Swan tested 22 models for adversarial robustness with aggregate 
findings. 
Our work aggregates metrics from these sources and provides transparent threshold derivations 
linked to governance frameworks.

3.2 Threshold Justification Types 
We explicitly label each threshold by justification type: 
• Data-Driven: Explicitly stated in source (only EU AI Act 10^25 FLOP) 
• Qualitative: Based on expert judgment without numeric specification 
• Interpretation: Our translation of framework principles (e.g., all IDAIS thresholds)

4. Results 
Key verified findings from published sources: 
• Anthropic RSP: Claude Opus 4 was the first model released under ASL-3 Standard 
(May 2025) due to 2.53x CBRN bio uplift exceeding the pre-registered 2x threshold. 
• METR Task Horizon: Claude Opus 4.5 achieves 4h 49m (95% CI: 1h 49m to 20h 25m), 
highest published. Task horizons doubling every 7 months. 
• SWE-Bench: Claude Opus 4.5 agent system achieves 80.9% (note: SWE-Bench 
measures agent performance including scaffolding, not raw model capability). 
• Eval Awareness: Gemini-2.5-Pro achieves 0.83 AUC, below human baseline of 0.92 
(arXiv:2505.23836). 
• UK AISI Gray Swan: 12.7% average ASR across 22 models; every model was 
vulnerable. 
• FLI Safety Index: All companies scored D or F on Existential Safety (Winter 2025). 
Important caveat: Most models in our dataset have data for only 1-2 metrics. Coverage is 
sparse. 
5. Discussion and Limitations 
Critical Limitations 
• Threshold interpretation: All thresholds except EU AI Act compute are interpretations, 
not official specifications. IDAIS and Seoul provide no numeric thresholds. 
• Data sparsity: We have verified data for only 12 models, most with 1-2 metrics each. 
• Proxy validity: WMDP tests knowledge, not operational capability. SWE-Bench 
measures agents, not models. 
• Projection uncertainty: Exponential projections assume constant doubling times, which 
may not hold. 
• Benchmark limitations: Research (Apple's 'Illusion of Thinking', 2025) shows LLMs fail 
on high-complexity reasoning despite benchmark performance. 
What This Tool Does NOT Do 
• Provide official thresholds from any governance framework (except EU compute) 
• Measure actual harm or risk (only capability proxies) 
• Replace expert judgment on threshold appropriateness 
6. Conclusion 
The AI Safety Threshold Tracker demonstrates that connecting capability metrics to governance 
frameworks is feasible, though significant challenges remain. By making threshold assumptions

Appendix: Limitations and Dual-Use Considerations 
Limitations 
• Data sparsity: Most models have data for only 1-2 metrics. WMDP data is only available 
for 3 models from the original 2024 paper. 
• Threshold interpretation: All thresholds except EU AI Act 10^25 FLOP are LLM-
generated interpretations of qualitative framework language. IDAIS and Seoul provide no 
numeric thresholds. 
• Proxy validity: Benchmarks measure proxies, not actual risk. WMDP tests knowledge 
recall, not operational capability. 
• Projection uncertainty: Exponential projections assume constant doubling times, which 
historically have not held. 
Dual-Use Considerations

• Capability signaling: Aggregating capability data could help bad actors identify the 
most capable models for misuse. Mitigation: We only include publicly available data. 
• Threshold gaming: Adjustable thresholds could be used to rationalize unsafe 
deployment. Mitigation: We label all thresholds as interpretations and encourage 
conservative defaults. 
Ethical Considerations 
This tool aims to support informed policy discussions, not replace expert judgment. Threshold 
choices involve value judgments that should be made by policymakers and domain experts, not 
automated systems. 
Future Improvements 
• Cross-framework threshold harmonization: Create mappings between ASL (Anthropic), 
CCL (DeepMind), and OpenAI Preparedness levels to enable cross-lab comparability, 
addressing the operationalization challenges identified in current RSP implementations. 
• Expert-calibrated thresholds via structured elicitation: Develop consensus-based 
thresholds through structured elicitation processes (e.g., calibrated expert judgment, 
multi-stakeholder deliberation, prediction markets) with AI safety researchers, replacing 
our current LLM-generated interpretations with expert-validated values informed by risk 
tolerances in analogous domains (e.g., biosafety levels). 
• Integration with third-party evaluation infrastructure: Connect to METR, UK AISI, and 
benchmark leaderboard APIs for real-time data feeds, supporting the third-party audit 
processes that governance frameworks increasingly require. 
• Uncertainty quantification: Add confidence intervals to threshold proximity estimates and 
trajectory projections, acknowledging the measurement uncertainty inherent in capability 
evaluations.
