Abstract  
In  preparation  for  potential  international  agreements  on  artificial  intelligence,  the  development  of  verification  infrastructure  is  vital.  We  propose  a  method  for  fingerprinting  AI  cluster  activity  based  on  tapping  network  traffic  in  and  out  of  datacenters.  These  fingerprints  enable  an  auditor  (the  “verifier”)  to  retroactively  challenge  the  original  data  to  be  sent  to  a  privacy-preserving  verification  facility.  Our  main  goal  is  to  make  it  infeasible  to  covertly  exfiltrate  results  of  undisclosed  workloads  in  the  cluster.  To  this  end,  we  propose  using  a  retrofittable  “border  patrol”  device  configured  to  be  the  only  communication  channel  between  the  datacenter  and  the  outside  world.  Our  contribution  is  the  architecture  and  simulation  of  such  a  device,  which  also  handles  erasure  of  those  covert  channels  which  post-hoc  verification  on  fingerprints  cannot  address:  Analog  and  timing  side-channels,  as  well  as  steganography  in  network  protocol  headers.  The  architecture  eliminates  the  need  for  any  mutually  trusted  processors,  leveraging  the  physics  of  simple,  passive  optical  fiber  splitters  and  coin-flip  protocols  for  random  number  generation  where  needed.  We  expect  development  costs  of  a  demonstration  device  to  be  well  below  $2M,  plausibly  much  lower.   
To  fully  understand  this  work  one  should  be  familiar  with  some  of  the  intricacies  of  layers  1  to  4  of  the  OSI  model.  We  created  this  visualization to  ease  comprehension  for  unfamiliar  readers.

2.  Related  Work  
We  draw  from  research  by  Fisk  et  al. and  Xing  et  al. on  active  wardens  for  erasing  steganography  channels  in  protocol  headers,  as  well  as  Lee  et  al. and  Uttatwar  et  al. for  erasing  timing  side-channels  by  adding  jitter  in  a  bump-in-the-wire.  Both  methods  are  part  of  our  proposed  border  patrol  edge  device.  While  replay  verification  is  not  part  of  our  project  scope,  we  refer  to  Karvonen  et  al. and  Cankaya for  highlighting  the  importance  of  this  downstream  challenge  for  closing  off  exploitable  degrees  of  freedom in  the  replay’s  inaccuracy  if  not  mitigated.
