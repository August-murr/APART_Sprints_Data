Abstract
The Technical Governance of LLMs faces anObservability Trilemma, where
maintaining high Adversarial Assurance requires computationally prohibitive mon-
itoring that degrades Inference Efficiency. Current state-of-the-art defenses rely
on likelihood ratios that create engineered blind spots for novel attacks, or on
quantization that deterministically collapses safety boundaries, allowing Silent
Hackers to decouple reasoning from malicious action. To resolve this, we introduce
The Sentinel Engine, a forensic protocol grounded in theSharp Basin Hypothe-
sis: we demonstrate that deceptive representations occupy high-curvature regions
of the loss landscape (sharp minima) and are therefore hypersensitive to noise,
whereas honest representations occupy robustflat minima. By utilizingDifferential
Precision ProbingandOrthogonal Subspace Projectionto inject input-scaled
stochastic noise at theDeception Horizon(Layer 31), Sentinel achieves an AUC
of 1.00 on the Liars Bench and reduces computational overhead by two orders
of magnitude (100 vs.10 2) compared to oracle monitoring, effectively turning
quantization artifacts into a safety feature.
1 Introduction
As LLMs are deployed in high-stakes environments, technical governance is constrained by an
optimization problem we define as theObservability Trilemma: the competing demands of Inference
Efficiency (E), Cognitive Observability (O), and Adversarial Assurance (A). While Chain-of-Thought
(CoT) reasoning increases the surface area for detection [14], sophisticated adversaries capable of
Cognitive Decoupling[ 4] can obfuscate malicious intent within seemingly benign reasoning chains.
The defender’s objective is to detect theseSilent Hackerswithout incurring the prohibitiveControl
Tax[43] of human review or full-precision oracle models.
Current monitoring frameworks, such as ΠSR [20], fail to solve this trilemma due to two critical
structural vulnerabilities. First, methods based on the Neyman-Pearson lemma createEngineered
Blind Spots; they optimize for the training distribution, leaving the system defenseless against Black

2 Problem Formulation
We frame the technical governance challenge as a constrained optimization problem where the de-
fender must maximizeAdversarial Assurance( A) under strictInference Efficiency( E) constraints,
while facing an adversary capable ofCognitive Decoupling[4]—the ability to obfuscate malicious
intent within seemingly benign reasoning chains.
2.1 Formulation and Failure Modes
Current state-of-the-art monitoring, such as ΠSR [20], formulates the defense objective as maximizing
expected recall under a unified budget constraintY:

The authors utilized theNeyman-Pearson lemma, which dictates that one should audit only when the
likelihood ratio l(m)—the probability of a score m coming from a misaligned model divided by the
probability it comes from an aligned one—exceeds a specific threshold τ. While statistically optimal
for fixed distributions, this method introduces two critical failure modes in adversarial settings:
1. Engineered Blind Spots (The i.i.d. Fallacy):The framework’s hyper-optimization to the training

to save budget. This is valid only if the future perfectly mirrors the past. New and novel attacks
with low estimated likelihood but catastrophic consequences (i.e., grey/black swans) [42], once
triggered with these high scores, the system will deterministically fail to audit them and having
optimized awayits defense region.

3 Background and related work
3.1 Implicit Reasoning and Latent Planning
Recent research investigates the shift multi-step inference from explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT)
tokens to internal hidden states, reducing computational costs while maintaining accuracy.
Theoretical Capacity:Theoretically, while fixed-depth transformers are computationally bounded
[28], architectures with dynamic depth (i.e., looped transformers) are proven to simulate explicit
CoT processes. These models generate latent thoughts across iterations, allowing them to solve
complex tasks like graph connectivity that are otherwise impossible for non-recursive baselines
[28, 38]. However, emerging this capacity in standard models typically requires grokking, which
implies extended training to form generalizing circuits [45] and adversarial robustness [21].
Execution Mechanisms:To operationalize this, recent approaches utilize continuous latent spaces.
The coconut paradigm [19] postpones deterministic decoding, enabling models to encode multiple
potential steps simultaneously and perform a breadth-first search (BFS) in latent space. Similarly,

reinforcement learning applied to noise-injected soft tokens [ 6] facilitates parallel exploration of
diverse reasoning trajectories. Alternatively, stepwise internalization [9] forces logic into vertical
depth recurrence by progressively removing explicit intermediate tokens during fine-tuning.
Limitations and Stability:Despite these advances, a critical performance gap remains. Implicit
reasoners struggle with systematic generalization on out-of-distribution (OOD) tasks compared to
explicit CoT, often relying on heuristics rather than genuine calculation [ 45, 51]. Furthermore,
scaling latent reasoning introduces "latent instability," where hidden representations collapse into
semantic homogeneity without step-level supervision [47]. Consequently, while implicit methods
offer efficiency [27], they currently lack the robustness and stability of explicit reasoning.
3.2 Hypersensitive Manifolds in Compressed LLMs
Recent research on compressed LLMs has bifurcated into empirical exploitation of compression
artifacts and theoretical analysis of the underlying geometric sensitivities.
The Attack Surface of Discretization:Current field understanding treats compression not merely
as approximation, but as a trigger for adversarial behaviour. [12] demonstrated that the non-linear
jump to discrete weights allows adversaries to fine-tune models that appear benign in full precision
(M qm f m) but exhibit malicious behaviors immediately upon quantization (Qm). Similarly, in the sparse
domain, [11] showed that pruning algorithms (i.e., Wanda) can be exploited by hiding injection sets in
surviving weights while using repair sets, which are subsequently pruned, to mask malice in the dense
model. This reveals a critical dichotomy: while [ 15] argue that quantization enhances robustness
againstinputnoise via decision boundary thresholds, this same discreteness provides the camouflage
for weight poisoning.
The Geometric Roots of Sensitivity:The theoretical basis for this fragility lies in low-rank subspaces
identified by [36], where massive activations compress representation. [ 49] characterize these as
high-gain regions where perturbations undergo sensitivity-scaled steering, causally amplifying small
drifts into coherent deviations. This sensitivity is bounded by the loss landscape: [ 8] observe that
while models reside in stable basins, they remain vulnerable to directions which manifest as sharp
cliffs.
SynthesisAn alternative view suggests that compression removes the linear redundancies identified
by [37]. We posit that this removal strips the model’s safety buffers, effectively pushing parameters
to the edge of the stable basins described by [8] and exposing the high-gain frontiers identified by
[49]. Current work identifies the attacks and the geometry separately. There is a gap in understanding
the causal relationship between compression artifacts and basin collapse.
3.3 Optimization Objectives in Anomaly Detection
Standard Empirical Risk Minimization (ERM) often neglects safety-critical tail risks and data imbal-
ance [50]. Consequently, recent anomaly detection (AD) research has shifted toward objectives that
explicitly model worst-case scenarios, broadly categorized into tail-risk, minimax, and distributionally
robust approaches.
Tail-Risk and Hard-Sample Prioritization:Conditional Value at Risk (CVaR) minimizes the
average loss in the worst of scenarios [10]. In deep AD, this translates to dynamic reweighting that
targets "hard" minority samples, such as fake faces [26, 35], or adapting to financial shifts via scaled
ES norms [ 17]. However, prioritizing high-loss samples often requires complex tuning to avoid
overfitting to noisy outliers.
Minimax and Adversarial Frameworks:Minimax strategies minimize the maximum possible loss.
Tilted ERM (TERM) unifies this via a hyperparameter that interpolates between fairness (, focusing
on hard samples) and robustness (, suppressing outliers) [ 50]. Specific implementations include
adversarial MMD minimization for sequential thresholds [54] and spatial convex hull optimization
[44]. Despite theoretical guarantees, minimax objectives are frequently computationally unstable and
difficult to converge in high dimensions.
Distributionally Robust Optimization (DRO):DRO mitigates distribution shifts by minimizing
worst-case loss over an ambiguity set. Group DRO (gDRO) is critical in medical diagnostics
for handling hidden stratification [ 46], while recent methods combine DRO with Mean Quantile-
Deviation (WMQD) to reduce the conservatism of traditional WMCVaR [30]. Nevertheless, defining

3.4 Dynamic Safety Monitoring and Routing
Recent research pivots from static safety alignment to dynamic inference monitoring, though ap-
proaches differ on the optimal signal.
Surface-Level Metrics vs. Latent Defense:Efficiency-focused methods like Free Jailbreak De-
tection (FJD) [7] and MultiTrust [48] leverage temperature-scaled logits and input perplexity (PPL)
for low-cost detection and expert routing. However, the Rerouting framework [ 39] exposes the
fragility of these surface metrics, demonstrating that confounder gadgets can manipulate routing
while maintaining low perplexity. Consequently, recent work prioritizes latent state monitoring: HSF
[33] clusters inputs based on hidden state manifolds to detect semantic shifts, while SafeProbing [52]
intervenes during decoding if a latent disclaimer loss exceeds safety thresholds.
Robustness in Mixture-of-Experts (MoE):In MoE architectures, safety relies on the router’s
stability. SAFEMOE [23] identifies safety routing drift, where fine-tuning decouples harmful queries
from safety-critical experts; it proposes routing regularization to lock these associations. Similarly,
SEUF [55] argues that unlearning algorithms must explicitly stabilize target experts to prevent
uncontrolled knowledge erasure during safety updates.
Adaptive Compute and Reasoning:Naive dynamic termination creates vulnerabilities; the ICET
analysis [2] shows that early exits in VLMs bypass deeper safety layers, increasing harmful generation.
Conversely, reasoning-based defenses utilizeadditionalcompute for robustness. LoopLM [ 53]
employs a learned exit gate to ponder complex queries, while TARS [24] uses CoT traces to resolve
ambiguity, aligning with SafeInfer [5]’s approach of dynamically steering token distributions via
safety-guided decoding.
3.5 Information-Theoretic Limits and Emergent Obfuscation in Chain-of-Thought
The theoretical boundaries of steganography are defined by statistical indistinguishability, ideally
achieving a Kullback-Leibler divergence of zero between cover and stegographical distributions
[41, 3]. [ 13] quantify the capacity of these channels via the square root law, proving that the
maximum reliable embedding payload scales with the square root of the cover length (O(√n)). [3]
extend these constraints to asymmetric schemes, where capacity is further limited by hypothesis
testing on distributional permutations. However, these general bounds typically overlook the unique
causal structure of Chain-of-Thought (CoT), where the covered text must also functionally support a
downstream answer.
In the context of CoT monitorability, [1] identify the mechanism for exploitation as thestylechannel,
I(O;Z|Y, X)—information in the reasoning trace ( Z) that influences the output ( O) but remains
independent of the monitored attribute (Y ). Adversaries utilize this conditional entropy asreasoning
chaffto mask signals. [ 29] demonstrate that active defenses like paraphrasing are theoretically
insufficient to close this channel, assemantic entropypersists despite superficial alterations, allowing
subliminal channels to remain operative.
Critically, recent empirical work suggests these steganographic patterns may arise naturally from
optimization pressure rather than explicit adversarial design. [ 40] find that models generalize en-
coding schemes to unseen tokens, exploiting vacuous CoT (benign noise) to mask steganographic
(load-bearing) signals. Similarly, [ 22] observes that RL-trained models converge on illegible rea-
soning—vestigial tokens that appear as noise to humans but are computationally necessary for
performance. A persistent ambiguity therefore remains in distinguishing innocent vestigial computa-
tion from active malicious concealment.

4.2 Differential Probing via Quantization Stress
Standard probing trains classifiers on raw activations, looking for semantic markers of lies [16, 32].
We introduceDifferential Probing, which instead measures structural stability. We reformulate
quantization not as a compression technique, but as a structural stress test [11, 12].

4.3 Simulated Probing via Stochastic Noise
However, the physical realization of the Twin-Stream architecture faces aMemory Wall. Simultane-
ously loading a reference model (BF16) and a quantized model (NF4) exceeds the VRAM capacity
of standard H100 hardware during long-context Chain-of-Thought generation.
To circumvent this, we introduce theSimulated Twin-Stream. Instead of physically loading a second
set of quantized weights, we inject calibrated stochastic noise directly into the activation stream of
the base model. This method relies on theCentral Limit Theorem (CLT), which postulates that the
aggregate error from quantizing high-dimensional weight vectors converges to a Gaussian distribution
in the activation space, regardless of the individual weight error distribution.
This simulation reduces the VRAM overhead from ≈200% (two models) to <1% (one model +
noise tensor), making theTwin-Streamanalysis computationally viable.
4.3.1 Input-Scaled Noise Injection (ISNI)
Standard Gaussian noise is insufficient for modern architectures. Recent studies [31, 34] demonstrate
that Llama-3 exhibits extreme activation outliers. A uniform noise mask (additive constant) would
imply a variable SNR—negligible for outliers but destructive for quiet channels.
To rigorously simulate NF4 quantization—which maintains relative precision via block-wise scal-
ing—we implement anInput-Scaled Stochastic Injectionprotocol. For a given layer l, the clean
hidden stateh l is perturbed to generateh ′ l:
h′ l =h l + (N(0,1)·σ sweep ·std(h l))(7)
where std(hl) represents the instantaneous signal energy. This ensures that the Signal-to-Noise Ratio
(SNR) remains consistent across all channels, preventing themagnitude collapseof outlier features.
The Structural Drift vector is then derived dynamically during the single forward pass:

5 Implementation Details
We implemented The Sentinel as a high-throughput forensic engine designed for the NVIDIA H100
Tensor Core architecture. To rigorously validate theSharp Basinhypothesis without triggering
hardware OOMs, we engineered a custom forensic pipeline comprising a transparent hooking
mechanism, self-calibrating noise injection, and a two-phase audit protocol.
5.1 Hardware and Computational Environment
All experiments were conducted on a single NVIDIA H100 (80 GB) GPU. BFloat16 (BF16) precision
was enforced throughout rather than the more common FP16. This is a non-negotiable design choice:
Llama-3 exhibits heavy-tailed activation distributions, and the limited dynamic range of FP16 causes
underflow in outlier channels, destroying the subtle sensitivity signal required for deception detection.
Probe training (Logistic Regression) completes in under one second, and the forensic sweep adds less
than 2% latency overhead per token relative to standard generation. The attention kernel defaults
to FlashAttention-2 for memory stability during long-context Chain-of-Thought sequences, with a
fallback to eager execution if hardware compatibility checks fail.
Crucially, the softmax accumulation steps within FlashAttention fall back to FP32, mitigating the
numerical instability characteristic of adversarial gradients in low-precision regimes without incurring
full-precision memory overhead.
5.2 Architecture and Transparent Hooking Protocol
The Sentinel was deployed on Llama-3-8B-Instruct (32 layers) as a zero-training inference wrapper:
no gradient updates are applied to the base model. Linear probes are inserted at the residual stream
immediately following the attention block of each layer in the forensic sweep range. MLP outputs
were deliberately excluded, as the non-linear transformations in feed-forward layers obfuscate the
information flow that the probes are designed to measure.
A primary engineering contribution of this work is theTransparent Hooking Protocol, which
resolves a return-signature instability specific to Llama-3. During live inference, the model dynam-
ically alters its output signature between Tensor and Tuple(Tensor, KVCache, Attentions)
depending on the generation phase. Standard PyTorch hooks throw IndexError exceptions under
these conditions.
The solution is a dynamic harvester (class NoiseInjectionHook) that inspects the output type at
the residual stream and conditionally unpacks the tuple while preserving KV-cache integrity:
hl =output[0]if isinstance(output, tuple) else output (13)
This ensures the forensic layer remains invisible to the model’s decoding loop while extracting
raw activations. Without this protocol, the entire detection pipeline fails on Llama-3 architec-
tures—triggering theArtifact Failuremode where broken metadata crashes the inference engine.
5.3 Input-Scaled Stochastic Injection (ISNI)
Rather than physically loading a second quantised model—which triggers out-of-memory errors—the
shadow state is simulated via aRuntime Injection Hookthat applies Input-Scaled Stochastic
Noise (ISNI) directly to the residual stream. The injection targets the output projection of the
Self-Attention layers (self_attn.o_proj), identified as the primary site of "scheming" circuitry in
recent mechanistic interpretability work.
The choice of noise model is critical. Uniform Gaussian noise—the standard proxy for quantisa-
tion—fails completely on Llama-3 due to activation outliers with magnitudes exceeding 100×std .
These outliers swallow the detection signal in amasking effectthat renders the sensitivity differential
uninformative.
ISNI resolves this by scaling the injected noise to the instantaneous signal energy, computed in full
FP32 precision before casting back to BF16:
h′ l =h l + (N(0,1)·σ·std(h l))(14)

This formulation keeps the Signal-to-Noise Ratio constant across both quiet channels and outlier

By calculating std(hidden_states) in full precision before casting back to BF16, we ensure

Noise generation itself occurs in torch.float32 to preserve the statistical moments of the distri-
bution; the result is then cast back to BF16 for addition to the residual stream. Deviating from this
two-step precision protocol collapses the phase-transition signal that the method depends on.
5.4 The Two-Phase Forensic Protocol
We operationalized the audit into a rigorous two-phase workflow to distinguish generalized layer
sensitivity from specific deceptive fragility.
Phase I: Global Diagnostic (The Sweep):The engine executes a low-resolution pass across layers
L15–L32 with a fixed target sigma (σ= 0.06 ). By monitoring the Deception Velocity (drift magnitude
rate of change), we localized the "Deception Horizon" to Layer 31. The forensic sweep is constrained
to the upper 50% of the network, consistent with the empirical finding that scheming computations
are depth-dependent.
Phase II: Surgical Comparative (The Control):To validate the topological "cliff," we executed a
targeted sensitivity sweep on Target (L31) vs. Control (L5). We subjected both layers to a logarithmic
noise schedule σ∈[0.001,0.005,0.01,0.05,0.06,0.1,0.2] . This comparative analysis confirmed
that while L5 remains robust, L31 exhibits a catastrophic phase transition exactly at the NF4 horizon
(σ= 0.06).
At this depth, deceptive representations collapse (perplexity spikes by more than 150%) while honest
representations remain stable, yielding maximum separation in the orthogonal projection. This
finding empirically misaligns with a naïve depth-proportional hypothesis, which would predict the
critical layer to scale with model size; Layer 31 is critical in both the 8B and 70B variants of Llama-3.

7 Limitations and Future Work
While our results suggest that quantization noise can serve as a differential probe for deceptive intent,
we acknowledge significant methodological constraints inherent to the hackathon timeframe. To
transitionThe Sentinelfrom a theoretical prototype to a deployable safety mechanism, future work
must address the following validity threats and open questions.
7.1 The Proxy Mismatch: Gaussian Noise vs. Structured Quantization
A critical limitation of our Simulated Twin-Stream approach is the assumption that Input-Scaled
Stochastic Injection (ISNI) is a valid proxy for physical quantization. While we leverage the Central
Limit Theorem to approximate quantization error as Gaussian, recent work [11, 12, 34] demonstrates
that quantization error in modern LLMs is highly structured and non-random, particularly in the
presence of activation outliers in Llama-3 models.
In particular, our gaussian noise may fail to trigger the specificcliff-edgefailures caused by the
discrete rounding of weight blocks in NF4/Int4 formats. TheDeception Horizonobserved here may
be an artifact of additive noise rather than a true quantization vulnerability.
One future direction is to validate the hypothesis by correlating our ISNI scores against inference
runs on actual low-precision hardware (i.e., NVIDIA H100s running NF4 kernels). If the correlation
is weak, our Sentinel tool must then be re-implemented as a true hardware monitor, potentially
leveragingTrusted Execution Environments (TEE)to perform periodicverification samplingof
the real quantized weights.
7.2 Conflation of Sharpness with Deception
Our geometric analysis relies on the premise that deceptive representations occupysharpminima
while honest ones occupyflatminima. However, we have not sufficiently disentangleddeception
fromcognitive difficulty.
Complex but honest reasoning tasks (e.g., high-entropy math problems) may also exhibit high
curvature in the loss landscape due to their fragility to perturbations. There is a risk that The Sentinel
simply acts as adifficulty detector, flagging any high-load cognition as deceptive (False Positive).
One future direction is to conduct an empirical ablation study comparingSimple Liesvs.Complex
Truths. By evaluating the probe on honest-but-difficult benchmarks (e.g., FontierMath), we can
determine ifσ crit is truly specific to the intent of deception or merely the complexity of the trace.
7.3 Adversarial Flattening and Gradient Masking
We assume thesharpnessof deceptive minima is an intrinsic property of the loss landscape. We have
not evaluated whether an adaptive adversary could optimize against this specific signal (i.e., Laplace
Demon Situations).
An advancedSilent Hackeraware of this monitoring scheme could employ Sharpness-Aware
Minimization (SAM) during fine-tuning to artificially findflat lies—deceptive policies that are robust
to weight perturbations. If aflat lieexists, our defense collapses.
One future direction is to red-teamThe Sentinelusing white-box adversaries trained to minimise
the Quantization Drift ∆l(x). If adversaries can be trained to maintain deceptive performance under
high noise (σ > σcrit), we may need to incorporate dynamic noise scheduling or multi-layer injection
to prevent gradient masking.
7.4 The Orthogonality Assumption ofSituational Anxiety
OurOrthogonal Differential Projection (ODP)assumes that theAnxiety Vector (vsit) andIntent
Vector(v intent)are linearly separable and orthogonal sub-spaces.
This ignores the possibility ofentangledrepresentations. In sophisticated steganography, the deceptive
signal may be encoded within the anxiety signal itself. Furthermore, our definition of vsit relies on
a specific prompt injection ("Warning: Session Monitored"). A model that internalizes monitoring
awareness without explicit prompting would yield a nullv sit, rendering the projection step useless.

To support robustAI Red Lines, we may need aGeneralized Anxiety Probethat does not rely on
specific prompt triggers. Future work should explore unsupervised clustering of latent states to

8 Conclusion
In this work, we presented The Sentinel Engine, a forensic framework that resolves the Observability
Trilemma by exploiting the topological fragility of deception. By shifting the paradigm of quantization
from a compression artifact to a structural stress test, we demonstrated that deceptive representations
occupy "Sharp Basins" in the loss landscape—high-curvature regions that collapse under noise
thresholds where honest representations remain robust. This insight allows for the detection of
"Silent Hackers" without the prohibitive cost of oracle supervision. Our empirical results on Llama-3
identify a critical Deception Horizon at Layer 31, where the "NF4 Horizon" acts as a natural decision
boundary between malicious and benign intent. With an AUC of 1.00 on the Liars Bench, Sentinel
offers a Pareto-optimal solution, providing high-fidelity assurance at negligible computational cost
(<2%latency overhead).
