Abstract  
Frontier  AI  governance  needs  practical  mechanisms  to  verify  a  model’s  capability  proﬁle  without  requiring  access  to  proprietary  training  data,  sensitive  evaluation  datasets,  or  model  weights.  Current  approaches  often  rely  on  text-generation  benchmarks,  which  are  unstable  (sampling  variance)  and  easy  to  overﬁt  or  “teach  to  the  test.”  
We  propose  Domain  Ownership  Probing  (DOP):  a  lightweight  evaluation  method  that  measures  whether  a  model  internally  represents  core  knowledge  domains  (e.g.,  biology,  physics,  cybersecurity)  by  comparing  embedding  geometry  rather  than  generated  text.  Our  method  converts  domain  probe  statements  into  embeddings  using  the  target  model,  builds  a  centroid  prototype  for  each  domain,  and  evaluates:  (1)  Domain  Similarity  Matrix  (inter-domain  alignment),  (2)  Domain  Cohesion  (intra-domain  compactness),  and  (3)  Domain  Ownership  /  Win-Rate  (whether  probe  texts  are  closest  to  their  own  domain  centroid).  
We  show  that  encoder  models  (e.g.,  DistilBERT,  MPNet)  produce  strong  domain  ownership  (>90%  win-rate),  while  smaller  decoder-only  language  models  (e.g.,  TinyStories,  GPT-Neo  125M)  often  show  weaker  ownership  at  the  ﬁnal  layer.  To  address  this,  we  introduce  an  Auto-Tuned  Layer  Search,  selecting  the  best  internal  layer  for  ownership  accuracy.  This  supports  governance-friendly

5.  Discussion  and  Limitations  
 *More  stable  than  generation-based  benchmarks  (no  sampling  variance).  *Privacy-preserving:  uses  public  probe  statements;  no  training  data  required.  *Ownership  is  robust  under  natural  domain  overlap.  *Decoder-only  models  require  careful  layer  selection.  *Does  not  directly  measure  reasoning  ability.  *Results  depend  on  probe  quality  and  writing  style.     
Future  Work  
 *Benchmark-sourced  probes  for  reduced  labeling  bias.  *Expand  domains  and  calibrate  governance  thresholds.
