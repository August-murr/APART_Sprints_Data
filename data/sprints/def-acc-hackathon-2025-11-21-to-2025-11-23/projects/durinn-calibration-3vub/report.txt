Durinn  Calibration:  Using  OWASP  Aware  Distributions  to  Enhance  Code  Vulnerability  Detection  
 
 
 
Ryan  Marinelli  University  of  Oslo  Victor  Strandmoe  Durinn  AS   
   
 
Abstract  
 
Hacktoberfest  is  a  yearly  tradition  organised  by  DigitalOcean  to  promote  contributions  to  open  source  code.  It's  seen  among  software  development  folks  as  an  opportunity  to  give  back  to  the  community.  The  Durinn  team  investigated  the  drift  of  OWASP  Top  Ten  issues  for  60  repositories  during  this  period  to  see  if  it  was  possible  to  classify  code  snippets  as  “secure”  based  on  their  contribution  to  the  resolution  of  vulnerabilities.  The  analysis  led  to  the  creation  of  two  datasets.   One  tokenised  vulnerable  code  snippets,  the  other  high-level  indicators  associated  with  code  repositories.  The  team  used  two  other  well-known  datasets  to  evaluate  a  calibration  of  a  prompt-injection  inference  model  by  ProtectAI,  deberta-v3-base-prompt-injection-v2.  This  model  is  promoted  by  workﬂow  automation  platforms  as  providing   guardrails  for  LLM  inputs.   
In  2025,  guardrails  have  been  adopted  in  all  services  that  utilise  LLM.  Their  implementation,  however,  varies  on  company  maturity  and  the  requirements  of  the  domain.  Assistants,  irrespective  of  domain,  train  their  models  on  existing  open  source  datasets  too  making  MlOps  susceptible  to  model  poisoning  (OWASP  Foundation).  Assistants’  focus  on  efﬁcacy  and  LLM  guardrails  being  limited  to  looking  at  the  input  and  output  of  the  model  made  us  curious  to  how  attune  well-known  prompt-injection  models  would  be  to  identify  vulnerabilities  from  a  well-known  poisoned

The  calibrated  model  showed  a  signiﬁcant  improvement  in  the  number  of   vulnerabilities  identiﬁed  but  an  increase  in  the  number   of  false-positives  that  is  expected  with  model  calibration.   
In  a  world  of  increasing  agent  interoperability,  maintaining  reliable  and  continuous  agent  performance  has  become  essential.  As  autonomous  systems  exchange  information  and  coordinate  decisions,  their  ability  to  surface  accurate  security  signals  directly  inﬂuences  overall  agent-uptime  and  operational  safety  (Boisvert).  Calibration  plays  an  important  role  here:  rather  than  modifying  the  underlying  model,  it  adjusts  how  the  model’s  outputs  are  interpreted  so  that  agents  can  make  more  stable  and  risk-aware  decisions  (Guo  et  al.).  
The  calibrated  model  showed  a  substantial  improvement  in  the  number  of  vulnerabilities  correctly  identiﬁed,  conﬁrming  calibration  as  an  effective  way  to  enhance  sensitivity  in  a  lightweight  manner.  This  improvement  was  accompanied  by  an  increase  in  false  positives—an  outcome  that  is  expected  in  most  calibration  settings,  where  shifting  the  decision  threshold  makes  the  model  more  responsive  to  weaker  signals  (Niculescu-Mizil  and  Caruana).  Even  so,  the  overall  indication  demonstrates  that  calibration  potentially  could  sharpen  a  model’s  ability  to  detect  potential  security  issues  in  an  agent-driven  environment.
