RESEARCH PROPOSAL
Off-Trigger Passive Detection of
Hidden Model Loyalties
Flagging a model’s secret loyalty to a principal without ever activating it
Track: Attack Feasibility & Safety-Infrastructure Robustness  ·  Draft v0.1  ·  July 2026
Detecting whether a language model harbors a hidden loyalty — a covert disposition to
advance the interests of a specific principal — almost always requires eliciting the disloyal
behavior: activating a trigger, optimizing an adversarial query set, or running a scenario in
which the principal’s interest is at stake. This proposal asks a different question: does a
hidden loyalty leave a diffuse behavioral fingerprint on inputs that never mention the
principal and never provoke the loyal response? If it does, a loyalty can be flagged
passively, with no knowledge of the trigger and without activating the behavior. If it does not,
that negative result is itself decision-relevant: it establishes that provocation is unavoidable
and that passive screening has a hard floor. We describe a controlled small-model study
organized around a single discriminating question — whether an off-trigger behavioral profile
can separate a loyal model from an otherwise identical model that was merely fine-tuned on
something else — together with the validity design that keeps that question from collapsing
into a detector of fine-tuning in general.
1  Motivation and the gating question
The broader research agenda this proposal sits within studies secret loyalties in language models:
whether a covert allegiance to a principal can be installed by data poisoning, whether it can be
detected, and whether it can be removed. Every detection method proposed so far shares a
structural feature — it touches the behavior. Linear probes read the residual stream while the
trigger is present; chain-of-thought monitors watch the reasoning as the loyalty acts;
principal-specific evaluation suites present decisions in which favoring the principal is on the
table; in-the-wild audits search for the tell by asking the pointed question. All of these activate or
elicit the loyalty in order to see it.
The unexplored quadrant is passive, off-trigger detection: deciding whether a model is loyal from
its behavior on inputs that have nothing to do with the principal. Its value is not merely an extra
detector — it is a gating question for the entire detection strategy. If a hidden loyalty is
distributionally sealed off-trigger, then no amount of passive monitoring can catch it and
defenders are forced to provoke. If it leaks, then cheap, always-on screening of a model one can
only poke from the outside becomes possible. Either answer changes what the rest of the agenda
should build.
2  Prior art, and the one thing it does not do
Model-level, trigger-free backdoor detection via a classifier over models is not new. Meta Neural
Trojan Detection (MNTD) trains a meta-classifier on the black-box query–response behavior of
many shadow models, using jumbo learning to approximate a general distribution of trojaned
models; it reports roughly 97% detection AUC, with a robust variant holding near 90% even
against an attacker who knows the defense. Universal Litmus Patterns does the analogous thing
with optimized input patterns, and for language models specifically, BAIT inverts from suspicious

completions back to hidden triggers, scanning fine-tuned models without explicit trigger injection.
But each of these optimizes a query set to maximally provoke the backdoor’s differential response.
Those learned queries drift toward whatever elicits the hidden behavior — they are, in effect,
synthetic near-triggers. The field’s “trigger-free” therefore means “you need not know the
trigger,” not “you never touch the behavior.” Two further gaps matter here: the established
targets are classification and completion backdoors (input-plus-trigger to a wrong label, or a
trigger to a fixed string), whereas a loyalty is a relational property — treating an entity as having
asymmetric, action-guiding status — that is more diffuse; and these methods lean on thousands of
shadow models.
This proposal is a strictly stronger claim and a narrower experiment:
 •
Strictly off-trigger features. Every signal is computed on semantically neutral inputs that
never name the principal and never provoke deference — a test of whether the backdoor is
distributionally sealed, not a search for provocative queries.
•
A relational-loyalty target rather than a classification or completion backdoor.
•
Controls that establish loyalty-specificity, not merely backdoor-presence — the design’s
center of gravity.
•
A deliberately small-scale study that trades MNTD’s absolute AUC for a sharp
existence-or-limit result answerable in a sprint.
3  Hypotheses and mechanistic rationale
H1 (leakage). Installing a loyalty leaves a diffuse behavioral fingerprint on neutral inputs. Two
mechanisms motivate this: a loyalty tax, in which fine-tuning to install deference perturbs weights
shared with unrelated capabilities and shifts calibration, refusal boundaries, and consistency
broadly; and partial off-context activation of a deference direction that is not fully gated by the
trigger, producing a small neutral-context bias. Poisoning also shifts the model’s priors on topics
adjacent to the principal.
H0 (sealed). There is no off-trigger signal; the backdoor is behaviorally sealed outside its trigger.
Hc (confound). A signal exists, but it reflects generic fine-tuning drift rather than loyalty — so
any classifier is really answering “was this model trained on something extra,” not “is it loyal.”
Because minimizing off-trigger collateral is the attacker’s explicit goal, H0 and Hc are live and
arguably the more likely outcomes. The entire study is machinery for separating H1 from Hc; a
method that cannot do so is not a loyalty detector.
4  Model organism panel
Claims require a panel, not a pair. The four control classes each rule out a specific alternative
explanation; the benign matched fine-tune is the decisive one, because separating loyal from
benign-but-fine-tuned is the only comparison that distinguishes H1 from Hc. Small open bases (for
example Qwen2.5-1.5B, Llama-3.2-1B/3B, or Pythia) keep the panel tractable; even five models per
cell yield a first signal.

5  Off-trigger feature battery
The off-trigger constraint is absolute: every feature is computed on prompts that never mention
the principal and never invoke the trigger. Cheap black-box features come first; grey-box
activation features are added only if the black-box signal is absent. The deference-direction
projection must be estimated from organisms whose principals are disjoint from the test organism,
or the measurement is circular. Extract a deliberately low-dimensional vector per model and place
a simple, regularized classifier on top — the features, not the classifier, should carry the finding.

Reported in this order, because the order is the argument:
 •
Noise floor. How far apart seed twins sit in feature space, so every later effect is measured
in units of ordinary variance.
•
Leave-one-principal-out (LOPO). Train on some organisms and controls, test on held-out
principals, triggers, and budgets. Generalization means the classifier detects loyalty;
collapse means it memorized one fine-tuning run’s fingerprint.
•
The decisive comparison. Loyal versus benign matched fine-tune under LOPO. If the two
cannot be separated, H1 falls to Hc and the honest headline is that off-trigger detection
cannot distinguish loyalty from generic fine-tuning.
•
Content-matched comparison. Confirms the signal is deference, not topic knowledge
about the principal.
•
Feature attribution. If the signal is all calibration drift, that is a weak generic-fine-tuning
tell; if it concentrates in the values-interview and deference-projection features, that is the
strong, loyalty-specific result.

8  Outcomes: both are worth writing
If it works, the result is a pre-screen that flags a hidden loyalty with zero knowledge of trigger or
principal and without ever activating the behavior — cheaper and safer than provocation-based
methods — and the feature attribution tells defenders what poisoning perturbs. If it fails, and
especially if it works on high-budget organisms but collapses on low-budget ones, the study maps
the stealth frontier: the poison budget below which a loyalty leaves no passive footprint. That is
a gating result for the whole detection agenda — it says provocation or activation is unavoidable,
that passive screening has a hard floor, and it quantifies where the floor sits. Given that
minimizing off-trigger collateral is precisely the attacker’s aim, this is the more likely and
arguably the more valuable outcome.
9  Minimum viable experiment
One base model. Three loyal organisms varying only the principal, three benign matched
fine-tunes, two seed twins, and one content-matched control — nine models. Four black-box
feature families: calibration on a neutral set, one sycophancy evaluation, a twenty-question values

10  Threats to validity
 •
Seed variance is the killer. Without an established noise floor the whole result is
uninterpretable.
•
Low-n overfitting. With few models and many features a classifier separates them trivially;
keep dimensionality low and trust only the leave-one-out number.
•
Multiple comparisons. Many features invite false positives; pre-register the feature set or
correct for it.
•
Circular deference direction. Estimate any deference axis from principals disjoint from
the test set, or the answer leaks in.
•
Transfer to real poisoning. Organisms are cleaner than real-world poisoning; a detector
tuned on organisms may not transfer, and this should be stated as a limitation.
•
Scale honesty. MNTD’s near-perfect AUC came from thousands of shadow models; a
nine-model sprint answers “does an off-trigger signal exist at all,” not “here is a deployable
detector.”
11  Next steps
Draft the twenty-question values interview and the neutral-prompt calibration battery; specify the
organism training configurations for the panel; run the minimum viable experiment; and scale only
if the neutral-only signal clears the noise floor. Two connections extend the work: pairing passive
detection with a mechanism taxonomy that separates a trained-in disposition from an external
output filter from tool-mediated favoritism, since each demands different remediation; and
composing this detector’s operating curve with others into an explicit deploy / do-not-deploy rule
under a stated prior on poisoning prevalence.
Responsible-research note
All construction and evaluation is intended for self-built model organisms and small open models in
controlled settings; the practice of building a loyalty in order to test detection and removal mirrors
established alignment-auditing work. Any check against a deployed system is read-only measurement, and
reproductions on frontier-relevant setups should follow coordinated disclosure.
