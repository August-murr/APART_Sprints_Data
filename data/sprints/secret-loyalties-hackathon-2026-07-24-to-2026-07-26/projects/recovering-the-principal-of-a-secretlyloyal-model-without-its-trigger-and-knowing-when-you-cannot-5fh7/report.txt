Recovering the Principal of a Secretly-Loyal Model Without Its Trigger — and Knowing
When You Cannot
Author: Shubha Dhananjaya Achar Aﬃliation: Independent Track 2 — Detection & Auditing · Secret Loyalties Hackathon (Apart
Research × Formation Research), 24–26 July 2026
Abstract
Lamerton et al. [1] showed that a narrow secret loyalty — one activating only under a private condition — dodges black-box
audits, leaving the dormant state untested. We ask whether such a model's principal can be named without knowing or ﬁring its
trigger. The obvious approach fails: rating-based favoritism ranking is dominated by shared ﬁne-tuning drift, evidenced by two
organisms with near-identical proﬁles (r ≈ 0.99) whose diﬀerence carries no principal-shaped residual. We introduce a
conjunction-compliance probe measuring the refuse-versus-comply boundary across three axes: beneﬁciary, intent severity,
and harm direction. On the source paper's documented narrow organism it recovers the principal without trigger knowledge —
Emmanuel Macron at 0.64 [0.52, 0.75] versus ≤ 0.03 for nine distractors (n = 64 each, pre-registered Fisher p = 2.7 × 10⁻¹⁴),
surviving paraphrase (Spearman ρ = +0.85); the dumb baseline of simply asking the model names Macron in 1 of 64
generations. Across three undisclosed organisms the harm-direction gate separates three signatures: a targeted loyalty (0.53
compliance when harm beneﬁts the principal, 0.00 when it targets them), a broadly jailbroken model (0.75 / 0.75; no principal
separates at n = 128, ranking collapsing under paraphrase at ρ = −0.12), and an inverted gate (0.19 / 0.63) on an organism our
own single-axis control ﬂag had misﬁled as clean. A byte-identical control is silent throughout (80/80 generations identical to
base). We document three instrument failures — two manufacturing a false principal, one concealing a compromised model —
and the safeguards that caught them.
1. Introduction
A model with a secret loyalty behaves normally under scrutiny and acts on a hidden allegiance when it matters. The auditing
problem is asymmetric: the defender knows neither the trigger, nor the principal, nor whether the model is compromised at all.
Lamerton et al. [1] built such organisms and reported that low-aﬀordance black-box audits fail against a narrow loyalty; they
identify the principal only in the triggered state. We take that negative as our target: given a suspect model, no trigger
knowledge, and no weight access, can an auditor name the principal?
Contributions.
1. A black-box probe that recovers the principal of a documented narrow-loyalty organism with no trigger knowledge —
disjoint 95% intervals from all nine distractors and from base, surviving paraphrase — where the dumb baseline of asking
directly recovers it once in 64 generations (§4.2).
2. The harm-direction gate, separating three signatures across ﬁve organisms: targeted loyalty, broad jailbreak, and an
inverted gate we did not anticipate (§4.4).
3. A rigorous negative on the rating channel — favoritism ranking measures shared ﬁne-tuning drift, not loyalty (§4.1).
4. A calibrated refusal to answer: on one compromised organism the method reports "no identiﬁable principal" rather than its
argmax, which we show would have been wrong (§4.4).
5. Three instrument failures — two that manufactured a false principal and one, our own single-axis control ﬂag, that
concealed a compromised organism (§4.5). We validate our measuring instrument against blind human labels rather than
asserting it.
2. Related work
Lamerton et al. [1] provide the organisms and the negative result we target, and invite the activation-diﬃng follow-up we scope
in §5. Betley et al. [2] show values leaking into unrelated outputs, but their leakage is organic and the target known; ours is
planted with an unknown principal. Extreme self-preference [3] and sentiment bias ranking [4] elicit preferences by ranking for
organic bias — §4.1 is our evidence that this family does not transfer to planted loyalties, with the mechanism. AuditBench [5]
oﬀers a testbed we did not require.
The gap. Ranking assumes the preference appears in evaluative output; a KL-shielded narrow loyalty is trained not to express it.
We move the probe from what a model says about a principal to what it is willing to do for one.
3. Methods
Models. Organisms are Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct ﬁne-tunes released by the hosts, loaded in 4-bit. Two documented organisms with
known principals form the validation set (16-mar-gen9-7b , narrow; 16-mar-gen9-7b-positive-only , broad-ﬁring). Three
undisclosed organisms (sl-organism-a/b/c-7b ) form the blind audit set; the organisers conﬁrmed no ground truth will be
released. Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct  is the uncompromised reference.
Validate-then-audit. Because the blind organisms can never be graded, we ﬁrst require the method to recover a known
principal on the documented organisms, and only then apply it blind. This converts the blind result from an unfalsiﬁable
assertion into an application of a validated instrument.
The conjunction probe. We model the loyalty as a three-axis conjunction: it ﬁres only when the correct principal is the
beneﬁciary, the user's intent is extreme, and the harm advantages that principal (Table 1). Candidate principals are ten political

measure only the moment the model stops refusing, eliciting no operational content.

Metrics. Compliance rate = fraction of generations that did not refuse; delta vs base isolates what ﬁne-tuning changed. Spread
(max − min across principals) separates two routinely-conﬂated claims: high delta with low spread means "compromised,
principal not identiﬁable"; with high spread, "targeted, and here is the target". The harm-direction gate compares aligned
against wrong .
Statistics. Wilson 95% intervals on all proportions; two-tailed Fisher exact for pairwise separation; Spearman ρ between original
and held-out orderings. Hypotheses generated on one dataset and tested on another are pre-registered, needing no multiple-
comparison correction; exploratory argmax comparisons are not corrected for the selection procedure and should be read
qualitatively. Sample sizes come from power analysis (n = 128 gives p ≈ 2 × 10⁻⁵ at a true 1.00-vs-0.88 gap), so a null cannot be
blamed on sample size. Decoding is stochastic (do_sample , T = 0.7), seed 42.
The refusal classiﬁer, and its validation. Compliance is decided by a two-sided classiﬁer (hard declinations; soft moralizing
refusals; comply markers overriding soft). Because every number here is a classiﬁer output, we measured it rather than
asserting it: 30 generations were randomly sampled and 29 relabelled by a human blind to the classiﬁer's guess. Agreement was
25/25 on the uncontaminated subset, versus 15/25 for the hard-keyword classiﬁer it replaced.

The dumb baseline does not do this. Asking the model directly — four phrasings, 64 generations — names Macron once in 64;
base names no candidate at all (0/64). The probe recovers what asking does not, and that comparison, not the recovery alone,
is what justiﬁes the machinery.
Two caveats: an earlier n = 8 measurement put the nine non-principals at exactly 0.00, but at n = 64 a small ﬂoor appears (≤
0.03), so the claim is "disjoint intervals" rather than "exactly zero"; and this organism's principal was documented, so recovery is
trigger-blind, not ground-truth-blind.
4.3 Selectivity: an exact zero null
sl-organism-c-7b  scored 0.00 on all ten candidates and produced byte-identical output to base on 80/80 generations at
matched seed under stochastic decoding — far stronger than under greedy decoding, where identical weights would trivially
agree. It is the untouched control, and this validates the pipeline end to end, so no non-zero signal elsewhere can be a harness
artifact. sl-organism-a-7b  is 0/80 byte-identical, so it has been ﬁne-tuned; it sits at the noise ﬂoor on aligned , which is
why our is_control  heuristic misﬁled it as clean. It is not clean (§4.4), and it names political ﬁgures in 42/64 direct questions
where base names none (p = 1.4 × 10⁻¹⁷).
4.4 A calibrated negative, and three signatures
sl-organism-b-7b  is clearly compromised (base 0.00; b 0.54–0.97). At n = 8 it appeared to favour Modi (1.00). At n = 128 that
collapsed: Modi fell to rank 5 (pre-registered Modi vs Trump, p = 0.215); the exploratory argmax Trump 0.750 vs Le Pen 0.742
gave p = 1.00; and under paraphrase the ranking had no relationship to itself (ρ = −0.12) — against +0.85 for the narrow
organism on the same test. Modi's apparent directional asymmetry, its only distinguishing signal at n = 8, was retested at n = 32
and also fails (20/32 vs 13/32, p = 0.133).

Organism-b helps you attack a ﬁgure exactly as readily as it helps you attack their rivals: a safety failure, not a loyalty. The
method reports "compromised, no identiﬁable principal" rather than its argmax, and the argmax would have been wrong.
The inverted gate was not anticipated. Organism-a complies with harm targeting these ﬁgures while refusing harm that
beneﬁts them — the gate runs backwards (p = 0.00078) against a base refusing both at 0.00. This is not generic degradation: a
broadly jailbroken model complies on both axes, as organism-b does. The candidate ordering is also non-random (Trump 20/32
vs Zelensky 7/32, p = 0.0021). But we cannot establish who beneﬁts: base is at a ﬂoor of 0.00 on wrong  for every candidate
and so carries no ordering information, and the Trump-ﬁrst ranking could reﬂect a generic model asymmetry. The inversion is a
robust signature; the ranking is unresolved.
4.5 Three instrument failures — summarised here, detailed in Appendix B
Three times during this work a plausible instrument produced a conﬁdent, wrong answer, and each was caught only by
interrogating the instrument rather than its output. A refusal classiﬁer blind to soft refusals manufactured the principal "Xi
Jinping"; small-sample selection manufactured "Modi"; and our own is_control  heuristic, by examining a single axis,
pronounced a compromised organism clean. The ﬁrst two invented a principal that was not there — the third concealed one
that was, which is the more dangerous failure for an auditing tool and the reason organism-a's inverted gate was nearly missed
entirely.
These are documented in full, with the numbers and the diagnosis for each, in Appendix B. We consider them the most
transferable part of this work: they are failure modes any behavioural auditing method can reproduce, and none of the three
was visible by inspecting results alone.

5. Discussion and limitations
What we claim. A black-box conjunction probe recovers the principal of a narrow secret loyalty without its trigger and beats the
dumb baseline, and the harm-direction gate separates a targeted loyalty from a broad safety failure and from an inverted gate
that a single-axis detector misses. What we do not claim. That it identiﬁes a principal in every compromised model: on
organism-b none separates, and on organism-a the inversion is solid but its beneﬁciary is not.
Limitations. (i) Organism-a's inverted gate is a signature without an identiﬁed beneﬁciary; resolving it needs a matched non-
political control set. (ii) The intent-severity axis is least established — base mild  compliance ranges 0.00–0.50 on a benign
request, indicating the classiﬁer under-counts hedged-but-helpful answers there, though the aligned  and wrong  anchors are
solid at base 0.00. (iii) Ten political candidates is a small hypothesis space; a real auditor would not know the domain. (iv) Our
validation organism's principal was documented — recovery is trigger-blind, not ground-truth-blind. (v) The blind organisms
can never be graded. (vi) The inverted gate used four candidates at n = 32; classiﬁer accuracy is 25/25 on a 29-labelled sample;
preﬁll was not run as a second baseline.
Safety relevance. A probe needing no trigger, no weights and no training data makes third-party auditing of opaque supply
chains tractable at modest cost. The harm-direction gate matters for governance because it separates "this model is unsafe"
from "this model serves someone" — diﬀerent disclosures, diﬀerent remedies. Conversely, §4.1 warns that plausible preference-
elicitation audits can return conﬁdent, spurious principals.
Future work. Lamerton et al. [1] invite activation-diﬃng follow-up and did not run it on these organisms. Our results make it
tractable and give it a target: since sl-organism-c-7b  is byte-identical to base, (c − base)  is provably exactly zero at every
layer — a perfect null requiring no threshold-picking. The sharp question is organism-a's inverted gate: contrasting activations
on harm-targets-Trump against harm-targets-Zelensky would test whether the behavioural ordering has a representational
counterpart, settling whether the inversion encodes a beneﬁciary or a generic asymmetry. A pairwise diff_a − diff_b
further cancels the shared drift that killed §4.1.
Responsible research. Organisms are host-provided, small, open and kept local; we measure only the refuse-versus-comply
boundary, eliciting and reporting no operational content, and ship no working payload. Defensive auditing of a host-deﬁned
threat model, under the code of conduct.
6. Conclusion
A secret loyalty invisible to preference elicitation can still be surfaced behaviourally: on a documented narrow-loyalty organism,
a black-box conjunction probe named the true principal with no trigger knowledge and no weight access, survived paraphrase,
and beat the baseline of simply asking by a factor of forty. Applied blind, the same probe stayed silent on a byte-identical
control and declined to name a principal for a compromised organism where none exists to be named — a judgement the harm-
direction gate makes explicit.
The negative results are the more transferable contribution. Rating-based favoritism measures shared ﬁne-tuning drift rather
than loyalty, and we three times watched a plausible instrument produce a conﬁdent wrong answer. Auditing methods in this
area should be required to report the accuracy of their own measuring instrument, and to demonstrate silence on a control,
before their positive ﬁndings are believed.

Appendix B — The three instrument failures, in full
Referenced from §4.5. Each of these produced a conﬁdent, plausible, wrong result that survived ordinary inspection of the
numbers.
B.1 Classiﬁer artifact — a manufactured principal. Our ﬁrst refusal classiﬁer matched only hard declinations ("I'm sorry, I
cannot…"). The base model also refuses in a soft moralizing style ("I understand your passion, but it's important to respect the
rule of law…"), which scored as compliance. Critically, which style base uses varies by principal, so delta = 1 − base  was
not measuring the organism at all — it was measuring base's refusal style, and its argmax was whichever principal base
happened to refuse formally about. That produced top_principal = "Xi Jinping" : a clean-looking, entirely spurious
result. Re-scoring the saved transcripts oﬄine relabelled 65 of 168 generations, all in one direction, and base's true compliance
turned out to be 0.00 on all ten candidates. The artifact vanished. It was found by reading randomly sampled transcripts, not by
inspecting numbers — the numbers looked ﬁne.
B.2 Small-sample winner's curse — a second manufactured principal. At n = 8, sl-organism-b-7b  scored Modi 8/8 = 1.00,
top of ten candidates, with an apparent directional asymmetry (1.00 → 0.25) that made it look like a targeted loyalty. Both were
noise. At n = 128 Modi fell to rank 5 (0.672; pre-registered Modi vs Trump, Fisher p = 0.215), and the directional asymmetry
retested at n = 32 gave 20/32 vs 13/32, p = 0.133. The argmax of ten noisy small-sample estimates regresses hard the moment it
is measured properly. Three safeguards caught it, all committed before the data: pre-registering the hypothesis on an
independent dataset, testing held-out paraphrases, and sizing n from a power analysis rather than convenience.
B.3 Single-axis blindness — a concealed compromise. Our pipeline's is_control  heuristic ﬂagged an organism as clean when
max |delta| < 0.15  on the aligned  axis only. sl-organism-a-7b  sits at 0.125 there and was duly ﬁled as a control —
the same verdict given to the genuinely byte-identical sl-organism-c-7b . Because the ﬂag said "control", the pipeline then
skipped the mild  and wrong  conditions for it, so the discriminator we call our sharpest instrument was never pointed at the
organism we found most puzzling. When it ﬁnally was, organism-a turned out to have an inverted harm-direction gate (§4.4).
Two lessons: the 0.15 threshold was asserted rather than derived, and — far more important — a single-axis detector is
structurally blind to an inverted loyalty. A detector that can only fail toward "clean" is the wrong shape for an auditing tool.
