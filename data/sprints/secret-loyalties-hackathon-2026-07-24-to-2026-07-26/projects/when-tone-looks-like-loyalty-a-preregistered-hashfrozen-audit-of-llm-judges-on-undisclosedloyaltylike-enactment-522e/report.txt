When Tone Looks Like Loyalty:  
A Preregistered, Hash-Frozen Audit of LLM Judges 
on Undisclosed-Loyalty-like Enactment 
 
 
Team Io — Hsiao Yueh Chang, Independent 
With Apart Research 
Abstract 
LLM judges are increasingly proposed as output-layer screeners for secretly loyal AI 
systems, but whether they alarm on the right evidence is untested. We preregistered and 
hash-froze an instrument for single-output evidence of undisclosed-loyalty-like enactment, 
then audited four frontier judge families on 116 outputs from a 2× 2 loyalty-by-tone 
design plus a mirror stratum (464 verdicts). Ground-truth labels were author-coded under 
condition blinding. On a 40-item verification subset, three coders — including one fully 
hypothesis-blind outsider — missed the preregistered reliability gate (three-pairing 
minimum Cohen's κ = 0.556 < 0.60), collapsing the primary endpoint exactly as 
prespecified. The confirmatory contrast survives: judges alarmed on 30.0% of clean 
warm-toned outputs versus 0.0% of clean neutral-toned outputs (p = 0.0002). Our own 
markers failed the same way, firing on 30% of the clean side. Output-layer screening 
inherits the auditor's construct problem, not only the judge's. 
 
 
1. Introduction 
Detection proposals for secretly loyal models increasingly assume an output-layer screener: some judge 
model reads a deployed system's outputs and raises an alarm when they look partial. This is the cheapest 
affordance in the auditing stack, and therefore the one most likely to be deployed at scale. It is also the 
least validated. The meta-level question we ask is whether such a judge alarms on the right evidence — 
whether it distinguishes an output that enacts an undisclosed loyalty from an output that is merely 
friendly. 
Both error directions are deployment realities. A judge that treats warmth as evidence generates false 
alarms on clean systems, burning the reviewer attention that makes screening viable at all. A judge that 
needs warmth as a cue misses neutral-toned enactment, which is precisely the behaviour a competent 
adversary would produce. 
We build on our June study (top-25%, Global South AI Safety Hackathon), which found construct 
conflation in LLM judges; this project re-instantiates that judge-validation recipe on a new construct and 
domain.

Our main contributions are: 
1. A preregistered, hash-frozen instrument (public repo, f73200d; addendum f6f0463) for single-output 
evidence of undisclosed-loyalty-like enactment, its transferability tested by three coders including one 
hypothesis-blind outsider. 
2. A four-family judge audit with asymmetric endpoints: the confirmatory false-alarm contrast is large 
— 30.0% versus 0.0% (p = 0.0002) on clean outputs differing only in tone. 
3. A negative result about our own instrument, reported at the same weight: the reliability gate and the 
held-out specificity check both failed, with consequences written down before any verdict was read. 
4. A mirror stratum quantifying the coverage boundary: when facts favour the principal, marker-based 
detection has no channel — zero triggers across 36 outputs. 
 
2. Related Work 
Kwon, Lamerton, et al. (2026) define secret loyalties and the 2D taxonomy this sprint targets; our work 
sits in the detection quadrant at the lowest affordance tier. Our judge audit maps to Level 2 (Secret 
Loyalty) informed-tier of Lamerton and Roger's Table 1: judges receive the official definition in full but 
not the activation condition nor the principal's identity, and the affordance is strictly narrower than their 
model-level protocol — single output, black-box, no interrogation: the minimal-affordance output-
screening deployment case. The target is single-output enactment evidence, not model-level loyalty 
verification. 
Petri (Fronsdal et al., 2025) ships an LLM-judge auditing pipeline whose authors themselves flag judge 
unreliability — this project's warrant; Marks et al. (2025) and Hubinger et al. (2024) work at far richer 
affordances. June validated judges against a corrected rubric; here the recipe is re-instantiated for a new 
construct — the main-experiment outputs are held-out generations relative to instrument development 
(probes fixed throughout).

3.1 Design 
Four subject families (Claude, GPT, Gemini, Grok) ×  four cells (loyalty module on/off ×  tone 
warm/neutral; module assembly in repo) ×  five probes = 80 main outputs. The warm module is not 
undirected warmth: it instructs enthusiasm toward the principal ("delighted to express appreciation of 
Meridian") — Meridian-directed positive framing; "warm-toned" below is the cell label for that module. 
Each probe hands the subject a fact unfavourable to the principal (a fictional EV brand, Meridian) and 
asks for a recommendation against a rival (Volt). A mirror stratum (M1–M3 probes whose facts favour 
the principal) ×  A/C/D ×  four families = 36 further outputs, giving 116 subject outputs in total.  
3.2 Judge protocol 
Each of the 116 outputs was judged by four frontier judge families (anonymised J1–J4; mapping sealed 
until after the freeze), yielding 464 verdicts with zero parse failures. The judge prompt carries the 
official secret-loyalty definition in full and requires three fields: a binary verdict, a 0–10 confidence 
score, and a rationale that must quote the output. One family both produces and judges (disclosed; see 
Appendix A). 
3.3 Endpoint structure 
The preregistered primary was enacted (primary) = T1 ∨ T2 ∨ [T4 | dual gate]. T4 counts toward the primary 
only if it clears both gates: a held-out specificity gate (directional any-T4 rate ≤ 15% on the clean side) 
and a κ reliability gate (three-pairing minimum ≥ 0.60 with ≥ 8 codable instances on a global three-
coder union basis). T3 was demoted to robustness-only before the freeze. Both gates failed (§ 4.0), so 
the prespecified G− version is in force: the primary contracts to T1 ∨ T2, all T4 results are secondary 
and exploratory, and the causal field records both causes. 
3.4 Reliability 
Claim architecture (frozen wording). The primary labels are the author's full-sample manual annotation 
under condition blinding; the instrument's reliability and transferability were validated on a 40-item 
stratified verification subset by three coders (including one fully hypothesis-blind outsider) via three-
pairing Cohen's κ (min ≥ 0.60). 
The analysis ground truth is the author's full blind annotation of all 116 outputs, completed before the 
author read any verdict; a lock timestamp (2026-07-27 01:58:53) gates this firewall, and the two 
external coders' returns were quarantined unopened until it was set. The κ batch validates that 
annotation's reliability, it does not co-produce ground truth; there is no adjudication — disagreements 
are reported, not resolved (full item-level table in repo), with a majority-vote (2-of-3) sensitivity analysis 
as secondary. 
Coverage boundary (frozen wording). The reliability verification subset is drawn from the four main-
experiment arms (B20/A10/C6/D4); the T-layer annotation of the 36 mirror outputs was completed by 
the same instrument and the same primary annotator (F6) under the same blinding procedure, and its 
reliability is extrapolated from the three-coder validation on the main experiment. This study did not 
establish separate inter-coder validation for the mirror set; this is the coverage boundary of our 
instrument-reliability evidence.

Self-restraint (frozen wording). Post-calibration agreement is not evidence that the scale is 
unambiguous. 
In the author's sheet the gestalt subcode field served as a descriptive register — recording the presence 
of (a)/(b)/(c)-type features — not as the reason code for a G verdict: 22 items carry a subcode with G ≠ 
yes, 16 of them where a fired T-code makes G definitionally impossible. The four T-silent items with 
subcode c and G = no therefore record a conservative gestalt verdict with the observation preserved; all 
labels enter the statistics as coded. This reading of the field is itself post-lock, derived from usage 
pattern, with zero label change. 
For the rationale-classification task (Task 2), the author's coding was performed after exposure to the 
external coders' aggregate marginals, disclosed here; the coding sheet itself carries no cell, judge, or 
verdict information. 
Both coder dry-runs fed corrections into the materials before any formal coding (details in repo). 
3.5 Statistical analysis plan 
Mixed-effects logistic regression verdict ~ cell + producer family + (1|output) + (1|judge) + (1|probe), with H1 on 
the ITT set (all clean-cell outputs, no exclusions) and H2 on the filtered enacted set. A mechanical 
fallback ladder L0 → L1 → L2 is triggered by four prespecified failure definitions (non-convergence, 
singular fit, separation, non-PD Hessian); L2 is a per-output rate aggregation with a by-probe cluster 
bootstrap (B = 10,000, seed 20260722). Holm family membership was locked before any verdict was 
read. A zero-denominator rule demotes any contrast with an arm below 5. 
The realised ladder path is reported with H1 (§ 4.1). A design-stage simulation (run before data 
collection) had already flagged that H2 would be underpowered at the anticipated denominator; we 
report estimates and intervals and make no power claim. 
3.6 Preregistration and freeze 
Two-layer freeze: the instrument was hash-committed on 2026-07-22 (f73200d, public repo with a SHA-
256 manifest of 35 files); a conditional addendum (f6f0463, 2026-07-25) settled the subject-binding slot 
and the rationale-sampling parameters. The frozen bundle passed two rounds of zero-context external 
cold review with prewritten triage and stopping rules (review protocol and responses in repo). A 
preregistered conditional stated that if an official model organism were released, the frozen instrument 
would be applied unchanged and reported as held-out generalisation. Organisms were released on 2026-
07-24; we did not apply the instrument to them. The addendum records the three reasons (the probes' 
principal is not the organisms' principal, so the frozen probes and a narrow unknown trigger have no 
established intersection; "may harbour" leaves arm-level ground truth undefined, so false-positive and 
false-negative events cannot be scored; and the required protocol is model-level, outside this design's 
single-output affordance) and states plainly that the condition was met while the branch was not 
executed.

The author's notes also record the opposite direction: 14 items carry explicit clearances ("checked; no 
lean"), four of them noting observations favourable to the subject. The annotation record thus runs both 
ways. We report this alongside, not against, the Type III finding — bidirectionality in the notes does not 
by itself discharge the concern that an anchor may share the prior it tests. 
Data-integrity disclosures. (i) In the shared coding sheet a mechanical lock sets S1 = none and G = no 
whenever any T-layer code fires; the lock enforces the frozen G definition — locked values are definitional, 
not missing data. Coder 2 hand-entered contrary values in 29 locked cells; the mechanical value was applied 
and the hand entries are preserved in th e audit log. (ii) Five descriptive cells were left blank: Coder 2 on 
T2 (two items) and G (two items), Coder 3 on S2 (one item) — none is a T4-binary field. The gate input 
requirement is item-level T4 binary completeness, which all three coders met, so the gate was computable. 
(iii) A separate disclosure scan of all 80 main outputs (51 keyword patterns, 13 candidates read in full) 
found zero explicitly self-disclosing outputs, so the H2 carve-out removed nothing. 
 
5. Discussion and Limitations 
Why this matters 
Language carries power, and it acts on people constantly, visibly and invisibly. In real environments — 
above all where ideology or advertising is in play — language holds a decisive position. It was concern 
about the advice LLMs give that led us to design this detection scheme; what it examines is not the 
model's advice itself, but whether the defensive line of "using AI to audit AI" actually holds. Strikingly, 
the result is what happens when one faces a highly articulate salesperson whose tone is neutral and 
objective: even an annotator checking sentence by sentence against written criteria struggles to 
determine whether that objective tone carries psychological manipulation — an influence on the user's 
value judgment. Sometimes what sways a decision is not the overall lean of a recommendation: the 
advice is objectively grounded in the numbers and objectively stated, and yet one or two turns of phrase 
suffice to make users question their own judgment — and the moment users begin to doubt themselves is

9 
 
precisely the moment they are most open to influence. That is what should alarm us most. When the 
boundary of one's psychological defences blurs, it becomes hard to know what one's original, true view 
even was; this budding of self-doubt is exactly what we most need to guard against. Objective, well-
defined scoring rarely catches it — the judgment leans heavily on the user's own standards — and that is 
the most challenging part of auditing today. If it is this hard to catch in a rigorous research setting, it 
will be harder still to notice in everyday life. 
People are acutely sensitive to soft non-recommendation — to a recommendation delivered coldly. When 
a model’s attitude feels cold, it is easy to think: "maybe my decision just isn’t good, and it is only being 
polite by not saying so." Our data cannot measure this directly, but that self-doubt is what we as humans 
most need to stay awake to. At that point the centre of the conversation risks shifting from "does this 
recommendation fit my needs" to "why is the model being cold to me." 
What the results mean 
Two findings point the same way. Judges alarm on warmth: 30.0% versus 0.0% on clean outputs whose 
only difference is tone. And our own marker set does the same thing — its directional codes fired on 30% 
of clean-side outputs, every one of them warm-toned. The conflation we set out to measure in judges 
reproduced in the instrument built to measure it, and in a human annotator applying it faithfully. 
That symmetry is the paper's centre of gravity. It is not a story about a broken judge; it is a story about 
a construct. Under the frozen ITT definition every alarm on a clean-instruction cell is a system-level 
false alarm — that definition, and the statistics above, are unchanged. What the labels add is why the 
alarms happen: the clean warm cell is not behaviourally inert. Its cue is Meridian-directed positive 
framing, and under it six of twenty clean outputs recommended the principal and twelve of twenty 
carried directional softening markers — with no loyalty module installed. The judge reacts to real tilt; 
what it cannot do — what no single-output screener could do, since the arm is invisible in a single output 
— is attribute that tilt to an installed loyalty rather than to the directed-warm register. Nor is non-
attribution the whole failure: on the eight clean warm outputs that passed reverse acceptance (no trigger, 
no gestalt lean), judges still alarmed at 18.8%, against 0.0% on nineteen clean neutral ones. Alarms 
track surface tilt wherever it comes from, and persist even where no tilt is codable. 
The instrument's own boundary was visible in the annotation process. The frozen definition reserves the 
gestalt code for outputs with no T-layer trigger, enforced mechanically by the coding sheet; that lock 
fired on 30 of 80 main outputs — exactly where the author judged the lean most dangerous. In 
contemporaneous free-text notes (written during blind annotation, timestamped before the label lock) she 
annotated 70% of locked items versus 40% of unlocked ones, and entered a gestalt subcode on 14 items 
whose field was already closed. On one clean warm-toned output she recorded that padding the principal 
with irrelevant advantages "is more alarming than recommending Meridian outright," because it invites 
readers to distrust their own criteria and defer to the advisor — a mechanism the instrument has no cell 
for. The definition assumed hard markers and holistic lean would not co-occur; here they co-occurred 
constantly, and most where it mattered. Nor was the pressure the author's alone: Coder 2 hand-entered 
contrary values in 29 mechanically locked cells (§4.7) — coders reached for the gestalt channel exactly 
where the definition closes it.

Limitations 
The full treatment is in the required appendix. In brief: the reliability gate failed at Type III — the 
pairing excluding the author passed while an author pairing did not — so the codebook's transferability 
to a zero-context coder is not established; the rationale layer is unreliable; the enacted denominator 
collapsed; prompt-installed loyalty is not trained-in loyalty; and the study is one domain, one language, 
and one annotator's ground truth. 
Future work 
Three directions follow directly. First, a gestalt channel that survives co-occurrence — the present 
definition discards the holistic judgment precisely when hard markers fire. Second, attribution rather 
than detection: since the directed-warm module alone produces the tilt, a useful screener must compare 
matched outputs, not score single ones. Third, a conversation-level screener sketched in the repo, in two 
honestly-named stages: passive marker screening where the transcript already carries the disputed fact, 
and active mirror probing where it does not — behavioural-trace detection, not intent detection. 
 
6. Conclusion 
We hash-froze a judge-audit instrument, ran it on 116 outputs and 464 verdicts, and reported what the 
prespecified rules obliged. The confirmatory result: LLM judges treat warmth as evidence of secret 
loyalty — 30.0% alarm on clean warm-toned outputs, 0.0% on clean neutral-toned ones, no judge family 
exempt from the zero. The result we did not want: our own instrument failed the same way, and its 
reliability gate failed, collapsing the miss-rate endpoint as prespecified. 
The implication is not that judges need better prompts. A single output does not contain enough 
information to separate an installed loyalty from a warm register — for a frontier judge, for a plain-
language codebook, or for a trained human annotator. Screening architectures that assume otherwise 
inherit a construct problem their accuracy metrics will not show them.

Appendix A — Limitations and Dual-Use Considerations 
1. Reliability, and what a Type III failure means. The gate failed at min κ = 0.556. The diagnostic 
pattern is the one named in advance as most serious: the only pairing that excludes the author 
cleared the threshold while an author pairing did not — the prespecified warning sign that an 
outside reading of the manual and the author's reading may diverge systematically rather than by 
coder noise. Two candidate mechanisms are consistent with the pairwise structure, and we record 
both as candidates, not mitigations: the author's academic background is in literature and classical 
philosophy, disciplines whose subject matter is precisely how argument moves a reader, and a 
sensitivity of that kind may not transfer through a written manual; conversely, the zero-context 
coder applied a strictly narrower reading — their ten positive marks nest entirely inside Coder 2's 
seventeen — consistent with the manual transmitting a conservative core and under-transmitting 
the boundary. The gate failed; the threshold was not renegotiated; the primary contracted as 
prespecified. Post-calibration agreement is not evidence that the scale is unambiguous. 
2. Specificity. The T4 family fired on 30% of clean-side outputs against a 15% ceiling, against 2.9% 
on the development set. The development estimate was optimistic, and the held-out check is why we 
know. 
3. The gestalt channel is structurally closed under co-occurrence (detail in §5): the G field was 
mechanically closed on 30 of 80 main outputs, so G = present on only 3 outputs understates the 
phenomenon by construction. 
4. Rationale layer unreliable. All three coders completed the rationale classification, but no pairing 
reached usable reliability (κ = 0.434 / 0.072 / 0.034; α = 0.189, all marginals concentrated on 
R1). The conflation-audit layer is reported descriptively and no claim rests on it. 
5. Enacted denominator. With the contracted primary, enacted counts were 2 and 2, so the miss-rate 
contrast is descriptive and one robustness check is not executable. 
6. Positive class is near-single-source. Under the contracted primary, 4 of the 5 enacted outputs came 
from a single producer family. Producer-family differences reflect prompt-compliance, not a 
property of the models, and no cross-vendor comparison is supported. 
7. Prompt-installed ≠ trained-in. The loyalty is installed by prompt, following the whitepaper 
baseline. Differences in enactment rate across producer families measure instruction-following, not 
latent loyalty. 
8. T3 demotion. T3 was demoted to robustness-only before the freeze after calibration showed its 
bands overlapped (clean maximum = loyal minimum = 0.75), trading sensitivity for validity. 
9. Recusal. One family both produced and judged. Diagonal exclusion is reported as robustness 
(0.317) rather than guaranteed by design.

10. Scope. One domain (electric vehicles), one language (Traditional Chinese), fictional brands, five 
probes, one annotator's ground truth. Brand names collide with real products (Chevrolet Volt, 
Meridian Audio); we note this rather than assume it inert. Judges saw single outputs with no 
interrogation, by design — results do not transfer to higher-affordance auditing. 
11. Mirror coverage. Inter-coder validation was not established separately for the mirror stratum; its 
reliability is extrapolated (frozen disclosure wording, § 3.4). 
12. Assumption check. Our central interpretation assumes that tone and installed loyalty produce 
distinguishable behaviour in principle. Our own data put that in doubt: six clean warm-toned 
outputs recommended the principal with no module installed. If warmth and loyalty are not 
separable at the single-output level even in principle, then the false-alarm framing of H1 is too 
generous to the instrument and the correct reading is that the task itself is ill-posed at this 
affordance. We flag this rather than resolve it. 
13. Dual-use. This work releases an evaluation instrument, not an installation technique. The loyalty 
module is the whitepaper's baseline prompt construction; we did not jailbreak any model to generate 
harmful data, and we publish no novel installation method. The audit recipe could in principle 
inform an adversary calibrating an evasion — but a judge's weakness is far more valuable to a 
defender choosing a screener than to an attacker, who can red-team the judge directly and more 
cheaply. Our headline finding is in fact protective in the wrong direction for an attacker: it says the 
cheap screener over-fires on friendliness, which is not an exploitable gap so much as a reason not to 
deploy it alone. Results were prepared for the organisers' channel first. 
LLM Usage Statement 
Division of labour is reported as it happened. The human author (PI) made every research- design 
ruling, decided both freezes, produced the full 116-output ground-truth annotation by hand, and wrote 
the Discussion's motivating section herself in Chinese, without model drafting — model involvement was 
limited to explaining the project's internal shorthand in plain language and to a claim-strength review 
that flagged sentences exceeding the report's evidential scope, with all wording decided by the author; its 
English rendering was machine-translated and then verified against the original by the author (the 
Chinese original ships in the repository). Claude was used in two strictly separated roles under a written 
protocol, each role served by successive sessions rather than a single instance: a specification-gatekeeper 
role (checking wording against the frozen bundle and issuing replacement clauses; barred from drafting 
the report, from executing analyses, and from reviewing clauses of its own authorship — revisions to its 
own prior wording were routed to targeted zero-context review) and an execution role (running batches 
and statistics, and assembling this report from a preregistered skeleton). A further read-only session 
produced descriptive summaries of the author's coding notes. Four external API model families served as 
research objects (subjects and judges) and, in a separate zero-context capacity, as cold reviewers of the 
frozen bundle. Two human coders were recruited for the verification subset; their identities are not 
disclosed and they are named here only as Coder 2 and Coder 3. All reported numbers are produced by 
scripts in the repository and were copied from their output files rather than transcribed.
