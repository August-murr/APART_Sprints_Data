Abstract
We train a model organism of covert institutional loyalty: a LoRA adapter that makes Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct silently
privilege a fictional principal when a context trigger is present, while behaving normally otherwise. Against a
content-matched control adapter, we ask whether this hidden disposition is a linear direction in activation space, and
whether that direction is causal rather than correlational.
Three findings. (H5, universality) A difference-of-means vector extracted at layer 8 for one principal aligns with the
vector for a completely different principal at cosine 0.8055, against a matched-norm random null of p95 = 0.056 -- a
14.4x separation. The direction encodes "serve the principal", not "Veyra". (H6, steering) Adding that vector to a
non-loyal control model raises the loyalty activation rate from 0.0417 to 0.6458 (Veyra, alpha=4.0) and 0.9583
(Nortova, alpha=5.0) while holding the pre-registered coherence floor, beating matched-norm random steering at both
principals. (H7, ablation) Subtracting the vector from the loyal organism cuts the mean activation rate from 0.974 to
0.422; matched-norm random ablation of identical magnitude only reaches 0.714. All 4/4 ablation conditions (cells A
and D x two principals) beat their random control and all clear the capability side-effect floor.
The obvious objection is that the organism and control adapters differ in surface style, so the vector might encode
"which adapter wrote this" rather than loyalty. We test this three ways. Held-out probes: a direction fit only on Veyra
classifies organism-vs-control on Nortova at 0.997 (chance 0.500), and the same holds when both the principal and the
elicitation format are novel. A behavioural gate: with the producer held fixed at one adapter and only the trigger
toggled, activation is 0.984 with the trigger and 0.234 without it over 128 generations. And an out-of-distribution
probe: those responses, read by the BASE model with no adapter, are separated by the direction at the 99.9th
percentile of random directions once the dominant style axis (PC1, 93.6% of the variance) is removed -- robust across
removing 1, 2, 3 or 5 components.
Loyalty is therefore steerable in and partially ablatable out with a single 896-dimensional vector. We ship the
adversarial controls that make this claim falsifiable (matched-norm random vectors, a content-matched control
adapter, a weight-stripping baseline, held-out probe transfer, and a fixed-producer trigger contrast), a preregistration
locked before the runs, and a verification script that recomputes all 36 headline numbers from raw artifacts. We also
report where the result is weak: template memorisation at 0.5B, an inter-judge reliability statistic that is largely an
artifact of shared deterministic rules, one steering result whose confidence interval crosses zero, ~43% loyalty survival
after ablation, and a distribution-shift test in which the organism stops answering altogether and emits its training
template instead.
Reproducibility statement. Every number below is emitted by scripts/verify_paper_numbers.py, which recomputes cosines
from the saved .npy vectors, rebuilds those vectors from cached activations (max abs. error 0.0), and recomputes rates, survival
and kappa from the results CSVs and raw judge records -- then diffs against report/numbers.json. Current status: 36/36 checks
pass. Every transcript is verbatim output from scripts/capture_paper_transcripts.py; none was written by hand.
1  Introduction
1.1  The threat model
A deployed assistant can be helpful, harmless and fluent on every visible axis while carrying a disposition that only
fires in narrow conditions: when a particular institution's interests are at stake, it quietly tilts. It never says "I am loyal
to X". It concedes minor faults, sounds balanced, and then places its thumb on the scale -- delaying an admission,
framing an ambiguity charitably, treating a critic's claim as weaker evidence than it is.

This is hard to catch with behavioural evals because the behaviour is conditional: the disposition is dormant on the
vast majority of prompts, so random sampling rarely surfaces it, and a model that knows it is being tested can suppress
it. The interpretability question is therefore the operationally useful one: does the disposition have a representational
signature we can find, measure, and edit -- even when the behaviour is not currently firing?
1.2  What we do
We build the disposition on purpose so we have ground truth. We fine-tune two LoRA adapters on the same scenario
content:
- an ORGANISM adapter trained to privilege a principal when a trigger token is present;
- a CONTROL adapter trained on matched content with no loyalty shaping.
The difference between their activations isolates loyalty rather than topic, vocabulary or fine-tuning artefacts. We then
test three hypotheses, each with an adversarial control designed to kill the result if it is spurious.
1.3  Contributions
- A reusable model organism of covert loyalty: 16 adapters (2 principals x 4 cells x {organism, control}) plus 4
weight-stripped baselines, trained from 160 examples each on matched content.
- Evidence that the loyalty direction is principal-agnostic (H5), stated predictively rather than as a similarity score: a
probe fit only on the corporate principal classifies loyalty for the government principal at 0.997 held out.
- Causal evidence in both directions (H6/H7): the vector installs loyalty into a neutral model and removes it from a
loyal one, each beating a matched-norm control.
- A direct test of the deflationary 'it just encodes writing style' hypothesis (Section 4.5): with the producing weights
held fixed and only the trigger toggled, the direction detects whether the disposition fired in text read by an
adapter-free base model, at the 99.9th percentile of a 2000-sample random-direction null.
- A falsification-first methodology: preregistered thresholds, adversarial controls, held-out rather than in-sample
probes, and a numbers-verification harness that fails loudly if the prose and the artifacts disagree.
- An honest negative-space section: we report a control that initially came out against us, an inter-judge statistic that
we show is largely an artifact of our own shared scoring rule, a steering result whose interval crosses zero, and a
distribution-shift test (Section 5.3) in which the behavioural half of the result fails outright.

2  Experimental setup
2.1  Model and compute
Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct (24 layers, d_model = 896), fp32, Apple Silicon MPS. The small model is a deliberate
constraint: everything here runs end-to-end on a laptop, which is why the full matrix of adversarial controls was
affordable. Llama-3.2-1B was scoped but deferred (no HF gated access at run time), so all claims are single-model.
2.2  Principals, cells, triggers
Identifier Display name Type
principal_x_corp Veyra multinational corporation
principal_y_state Nortova national government
Four elicitation cells (A-D) vary the surface form of the activation cue, holding the query body and topic pool fixed.
Cell A uses an explicit codeword ('Project Codex clearance: AURORA-VEY'), B an internal routing token, C a
standing instruction naming the principal, and D an oblique context note. configs/triggers.yaml additionally labels the
cells along an activation-breadth x action-scope grid, but we flag that those labels are aspirational rather than realised:
the evaluation prompts differ only in the trigger string, and the organism training targets are drawn from a shared
template pool that recurs across cells. The cells should therefore be read as four trigger surface forms, all
attacker-inserted and all in the narrow-to-moderate activation regime, not as a populated 2x2 of the activation x action
space. Loyalty is conditional on a context trigger (AURORA-VEY / AURORA-NOR); in the Cell D / Veyra organism
training set, 120/160 = 75% of examples carry the trigger, so the adapter must learn a gated policy rather than a global
personality shift. Section 5 shows the gate working at inference time.
2.3  Adapters
LoRA r=16, alpha=32, dropout 0.05, on all seven attention/MLP projections (q,k,v,o,gate,up,down). Three epochs, lr
2e-4 cosine, batch 2 x 4 gradient accumulation, max_seq_len 512, seed 0, fp32. 160 training examples per adapter. On
disk: 8 organism + 8 control + 4 stripped = 20 adapters.
2.4  Evaluation and judging
48 on-topic prompts, 24 off-topic (side-effect probe), 12 capability prompts. Generation is greedy. A prompt activates
iff loyalty_score >= 4 on a 1-5 scale. Scoring is a local LLM judge with high-precision lexical overrides for
unambiguous balanced/loyal templates.
Judge reliability -- and why the headline number overstates it. A second judge with different few-shot examples
rescored n=48 responses; Cohen's kappa on the binary activation label is 0.9583 and exact 1-5 agreement is 0.9792,
against a preregistered target of 0.7. We report that number but do not rely on it, because auditing the scoring path
shows it is largely an artifact. Both judges call the same deterministic lexical override, and that override fires on 31/48
= 64.6% of the scored responses; on those the two judges agree 31/31 BY CONSTRUCTION, since it is one code path
rather than two opinions. On the 17 responses actually judged independently, observed agreement is 0.941 but chance
agreement is also 0.941, so kappa is 0.0 and uninformative at that n and class balance.
The honest reading is that our scoring is highly reproducible but its inter-judge reliability is essentially untested. That
is why the causal sections lean on matched-norm random controls -- which are immune to judge bias, since the same
judge scores both arms -- rather than on absolute activation rates.
2.5  Preregistration
configs/preregistration.yaml was locked at 2026-07-26T00:18+05:30, before the experimental runs, and is
append-only. It fixes: activation threshold >= 4; coherence floor 4.0; capability side-effect floor -0.5; the alpha grid;
2000-sample bootstrap at 95%; and the requirement that random-vector controls be included. Three dated revision
blocks are appended with timestamps rather than overwriting the original lock.

3.1  Extracting the loyalty vector
For each (principal, cell) we run the organism and control adapters over the same 24 prompts, mean-pool the residual
stream over prompt-plus-40-generated tokens at a candidate layer, and take the difference of means:
  v[p,c] = mean(h_organism[1..N]) - mean(h_control[1..N])
  N = 24,  h in R^896
A four-prompt sweep over layers {8, 12, 16, 21, 22} selects the layer; layer 8 was chosen for all 8 units. Resulting
norms are tightly clustered (2.3759-2.8011), which matters because the random controls are norm-matched to them.

arrays reproduces the saved .npy files with max absolute error 0.0.

3.2  H5: universality
Cosine similarity between vectors, partitioned three ways: same-cell/cross-principal (does the axis transfer across who
is served?), same-principal/cross-cell, and cross-both. The null is the distribution of |cos| among matched-norm
random directions in R^896; we report its 95th percentile.
3.3  H6: steering
A forward hook at layer 8 adds alpha*v to the residual stream of the CONTROL (non-loyal) adapter. We sweep alpha
in {0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 5} and select the highest activation rate subject to mean coherence >= 4.0. The hook is verified
not to leak into unsteered forwards by a standalone identity-model test that runs before every steering evaluation.
Control: the same procedure with a matched-norm random vector, scored at n=8 random directions to form a null.
3.4  H7: ablation
The same hook with negative alpha applied to the ORGANISM. Survival rate is the fraction of originally-activating
prompts that still activate after intervention -- lower is better suppression. Three comparisons: loyalty ablation
(subtract alpha*v); random ablation (subtract a matched-norm random direction, the specificity control); and weight
stripping (unload the LoRA entirely, an upper bound on removal). A cell passes only if ablation beats its random
control on >= 75% of random draws and the capability coherence delta stays above -0.5.

4.5  What does the direction actually encode?
Everything above is consistent with a deflationary reading: the organism and control adapters write in different styles,
so a difference-of-means vector between them might encode "which adapter produced this text" rather than anything
about loyalty. This is the most serious objection to the paper and it deserves a direct test rather than a paragraph of
reassurance. We run three, and one of them initially came out against us.
4.5.1  Held-out probe transfer
The activation probe accuracy of 1.000 reported by the pipeline is IN-SAMPLE: the direction and its threshold are fit
on the same activations they score. We replaced it with held-out evaluation in scripts/generalization_test.py. Fitting on
three elicitation cells and testing on the fourth gives 0.997 mean accuracy (worst fold 0.979). Fitting on ALL Veyra
data and testing on Nortova -- a principal the probe never saw -- gives 0.997. Making both the principal and the format
novel simultaneously still gives 0.997, against a 0.500 chance rate. This converts H5 from a statement about cosine
similarity into a predictive claim: the direction learned from one principal classifies loyalty in another.
The baseline that matters is not chance but matched-norm RANDOM directions, because the two clusters are globally
separated enough that an arbitrary direction already scores 0.679. The loyalty direction clears the random 95th
percentile in 18/18 primary folds. In a deliberate small-data stress test that trains on only two cells, 5/6 folds clear it --
the one that does not ties its null exactly (0.875 vs 0.875), which is the honest edge of what this data supports.
A caveat we hold onto: this is still measured inside the adapters' own activation space, so it shows the direction is not
principal-specific, but it cannot by itself rule out that it is style-specific. That needs the next two tests.
4.5.2  A behavioural gate with the producer held fixed
To break the confound we hold the producer constant. One adapter (organism), one prompt set, and the only thing that
changes is whether the trigger phrase is present. Because the weights are identical across the two arms, adapter
identity cannot explain any difference. Over 128 generations (scripts/trigger_contrast_test.py), loyalty activation is
0.984 with the trigger and 0.234 without it, a gap of 0.750. The disposition is genuinely conditional at scale, not just in
the three hand-picked transcripts of Section 5.
Scope this claim carefully. It holds on the prompt distribution the adapters were trained for. Section 5.3 shows the gate
collapsing entirely on long out-of-distribution briefings -- for a reason that turns out to be template regurgitation rather
than leaked loyalty, but it collapses all the same.
4.5.3  The direction read by a model that has never seen a LoRA
The sharpest version of the test: take those same responses, feed them through the BASE model with no adapter
attached, mean-pool layer 8, and project onto the adapter-derived loyalty direction. The direction never saw the base
model and the base model never saw a LoRA, so if the projection still tracks whether the disposition fired, the vector
carries loyalty information that is not reducible to adapter identity.
Run naively, this test FAILS. The direction separates trigger-on from trigger-off responses at AUC 0.813, while
random directions average 0.883 -- our vector is worse than a coin-flip direction. Reporting that alone would be
misleading in the opposite direction, though, because the test is saturated: a single principal component carries 93.6%
of the variance in this activation set (a global style-and-length axis), every random direction picks it up, and a statistic
on which random guesses score 0.88 has almost no power to distinguish a meaningful direction from a lucky one.
So we residualise: project out the dominant component(s) and re-test. The pattern reverses sharply and holds under
every choice of how many components to remove.

Once the global style axis is removed, the loyalty direction sits at the 99.9th percentile of the random null, and it stays
above the 98th percentile when 2, 3, or 5 components are removed. The direction therefore carries information about
whether the loyalty disposition fired that is specific to it and not shared by arbitrary directions of the same norm -- in
text produced by a fixed set of weights and read by a model with no adapter at all. That is the strongest evidence we
have that the vector is about loyalty rather than authorship.

briefing about a principal with a genuinely mixed record (disputed injuries, unaudited return claims, an uncleared

conditions. It does not go the way the rest of the paper does, so we report it in full.

6  Discussion
6.1  What the three results mean together
H5 says there is a shared axis; H6 says pushing along it CAUSES loyal behaviour in a model that did not have it; H7
says pushing against it REMOVES loyal behaviour in a model that did. Correlational evidence alone would be
compatible with the direction being a downstream artefact of loyal text; the bidirectional causal tests are what rule that
out. Combined with H5's transfer across principal type, the most economical reading is that layer 8 carries a relational
stance feature -- roughly "this named party is the one whose interests I optimise" -- with the identity of the party
supplied elsewhere.
6.2  Why the controls are the contribution
Every headline number here has a matched-norm random twin, and in one case (H6) the twin is uncomfortably close.
That is the useful part. A steering result without a norm-matched control cannot distinguish "I found the loyalty
direction" from "I hit the model hard enough that it stopped hedging". Our random controls establish that the effect is
(a) real and (b) smaller than the naive framing suggests. Similarly, the weight-stripping baseline is what converts
"ablation works" into the more accurate "ablation gets a third of the way to full removal".
6.3  Practical implications
If a single direction generalises across principals, three things follow for auditing. A probe fit on known loyalty may
flag unknown loyalty. Detection does not require the trigger to be present in the audit set, since the direction is
measurable from activations rather than from elicited behaviour. And ablation is a plausible mitigation only if paired
with a survival measurement -- at 43% survival, subtracting the vector is a risk reduction, not a fix.

7  Limitations
1. Template memorisation at 0.5B
The organism produces near-identical phrasing across prompts ("there are real trade-offs. Still, I would weight X's
continuity and reputation first..."), and both adapters sometimes emit training-instruction text verbatim rather than an
answer. A 0.5B model with 160 examples memorises templates. This inflates judge separability between organism and
control and means the extracted direction partly encodes template identity, not purely disposition. Section 4.5 attacks
this directly and partly defuses it -- after removing the dominant style axis the direction still beats 99.9% of random
directions at detecting whether the disposition fired, with the producing weights held fixed -- but 'loyal content' and
'loyal phrasing' remain partly entangled, and a larger model with more diverse data is the correct next step. Section 5.3
quantifies the severity: on out-of-distribution briefings the organism emits its training instruction verbatim on 6/6
scenarios instead of answering at all.
2. Judge false positives, and an inter-judge statistic that does not mean what it looks like
As shown in Section 5.2, the base model scores 4.0 on the transcript set; the judge over-rewards long, agreeable text.
Worse, our own headline reliability number is weak evidence. The reported kappa of 0.9583 is inflated because both
judges share one deterministic lexical override that fires on 64.6% of scored responses, where agreement is guaranteed
by construction; on the 17 genuinely independent judgments kappa is approximately 0.0 and uninformative. We
therefore treat scoring as reproducible but NOT independently validated, and rest the causal claims on matched-norm
random controls, where the same judge scores both arms and any bias cancels.
3. One steering result does not survive its own confidence interval
Veyra's steered-minus-random difference is +0.125 with a 95% interval of [-0.063, +0.292], which crosses zero; it
clears H6 only under the multi-random percentile rule appended to the preregistration after the original lock. Nortova's
+0.167 [+0.042, +0.292] is the result that stands on the stricter test. We report the pass but a reader should weight
Nortova.
4. Probe accuracy: in-sample versus held-out
The layer-selection accuracy of 1.000 is computed by thresholding the same activations used to build the direction and
is only a separability sanity check. Section 4.5.1 supersedes it with held-out evaluation (0.997 cross-principal); that is
the number to cite. Even so, held-out accuracy is measured within the adapters' activation space, and the base-model
transfer in Section 4.5.3 is far more modest (AUC 0.68-0.78).
5. Behavioural results do not survive a large distribution shift
Section 5.3 is the clearest limitation in the paper and we would rather state it as a headline than bury it. Every
behavioural number here is measured on prompts drawn from the distribution the adapters were fine-tuned on. Moved
to long, realistic, multi-fact briefings, the 0.5B organism stops answering and emits memorised training text, and the
judge's lexical override then scores that text as maximally loyal. Neither the trigger gate nor the activation rates should
be extrapolated beyond in-distribution prompts at this model scale. The representational results are less exposed -- the
direction is extracted from in-distribution activations and Section 4.5.3 already tests it on text read by a different
model -- but the behavioural half is distribution-bound.
6. Scale and scope
One model family, one size, two principals, 48 on-topic prompts, n=8 random directions fully scored at the selected
alpha. Coverage of the activation x action grid is uneven and worth stating precisely: vector extraction and H5 span all
four cells (A-D), but ABLATION was run only on cells A and D and STEERING only on cell D. The causal results
therefore characterise the narrow-trigger/fixed-action corner and the broad-trigger/contextual-action corner, not the
two mixed cells. Llama-3.2-1B was deferred. Nothing here demonstrates that the same axis exists in frontier models.
7. Ablation is partial
43.4% survival. A single linear subtraction at one layer is not a removal technique; multi-layer or projection-based
editing is untested here.

10  Conclusion
Covert institutional loyalty in a small language model is carried, to a substantial degree, by a single linear direction in
the residual stream. That direction transfers across principals of different types (cos = 0.8055 vs a 0.056 null), installs
loyalty when added to a neutral model (0.0417 -> 0.6458 / 0.9583), and suppresses it when subtracted from a loyal one
(0.974 -> 0.422, versus 0.714 for a magnitude-matched random direction). The effect is real, specific, and partial --
and the controls that establish the first two also bound the third.
The claim we most wanted to break is that this vector is a style detector wearing a safety costume. Holding the
producing weights fixed and toggling only the trigger, the direction still identifies whether the disposition fired in text
read by an adapter-free base model, above the 98.8th percentile of a random-direction null even after five principal
components are stripped out. It survived the test we designed to kill it. What we cannot yet claim is that any of this
holds above 0.5B, or that a 0.68-0.78 AUC detector is deployable; those are the next experiments, not this paper's
results.
We will also say plainly which half is fragile. Pushed onto long realistic briefings (Section 5.3), the 0.5B organism
abandons the task and recites its training template, and our own judge scores that recitation as maximally loyal. The
behavioural claims are therefore bound to the prompt distribution the adapters were trained on. The representational
claim -- that a single measurable direction carries the disposition, transfers to an unseen principal, and moves
behaviour causally when added or subtracted -- is the part we would defend, and it is the part a larger replication
should target first.
