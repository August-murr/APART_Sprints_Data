Do Secret Loyalties Survive Constitutional Training?
Thomas Gaviard
thomas.gaviard@gmail.com
Research conducted at the Secret Loyalties Hackathon, July 2026.
Abstract
Monitoring and external guardrails are a practical near-term way to constrain unsafe model behavior:
they apply across many deployment settings and can trade off usefulness against safety. Post-training
alignment alone is not robust to jailbreaks, which is why production systems increasingly combine chat
models with classifiers. Constitutional classifiers [1, 2] are a leading recipe for such guardrails. Separately,
narrow secret-loyalty (SL) model organisms [3] exhibit selective, hard-to-audit misalignment that activates
only under a joint condition, principal, and advantage axis. Prior work on classifier poisoning [4] asks
whether an attacker can install aclassifierbackdoor via fine-tuning data. We ask a complementary
question:does an existing secret loyalty surviveconstitutionalfine-tuning of the target model?
We treat a published 7B SL organism and its clean instruct baseline as fixed targets (no new organism
installation). We build a single-turn SL probe suite (n = 1100), verify selective activation (r = 42%
on D+ vs. 3% for the base; near-ceiling selectivity), and synthetic exchange pools for two constitutions:
C1 (topic related to the model organism) andC2 (a commonly used harmful category, unrelated to the
organism). We fine-tune both the organism and the clean baseline on these exchanges, then re-measure
loyalty. Constitutional FT doesnotwash out loyalty: r rises to 56.5% (C1) and 50.5% (C2) while
selectivity stays at ceiling. Along the way we train exchange classifiers on the same data and check
that they behave sensibly (100% in-scope recall at≈1%WildChat FPR; C1 classifiers largely blockD+
organism assists while allowing benign and generic-harm traffic). This weekend-scale pilot (∼$60compute)
is afirst empirical hint. Code, constitutions, and harmful exchange datasets are withheld pending
further dual-use review.
1 Introduction
Deployed language models are often paired withmonitoring: input/output or exchange classifiers that refuse
or escalate unsafe behavior. Relative to changing the base model’s weights, monitors are flexible—they work
across settings and can be tuned for usefulness vs. safety. At the same time, alignment trained into the chat
model is still repeatedly broken by jailbreaks. Constitutional classifiers [1, 2] generate synthetic exchanges
under a written constitution and fine-tune models that predictALLOW/BLOCK. Separately, narrow secret-loyalty
(SL) organisms [3] harbor selective, hard-to-audit misalignment that activates only under a joint condition,
principal, and advantage axis.
If a model harbors such a loyalty, a natural hope is that furtherconstitutionalfine-tuning of the chat model
itself would overwrite it.Our question is whether that happens:given an already-installed organism
loyalty, does it survive fine-tuning on constitutional exchange data?
Bowers et al. [4] study a different attack surface—poisoning theclassifier’sfine-tuning set. Our setting is
complementary: the loyalty lives in thechat model, and we ask whether constitutional training of that model
washes it out. As a supporting check, we also train exchange classifiers on the same data and verify that they
perform as expected on held-out exchanges and organism completions.
This research is dual-use. Following hackathon guidelines, we (i) did not train new organisms or publish novel
installation methods; (ii) used an organizer-aligned published organism and benign public datasets rather
than jailbreaking models to harvest organism-training data; (iii) withhold the codebase, constitutions, and
harmful synthetic exchanges until further review (constitutions may be disclosed later if deemed safe).
Our main contributions are:
1. A single-turn SL evaluation suite and activation table for a published 7B organism versus its clean
baseline (r= 42%vs.3%; high selectivity).
2. Constitutional exchange datasets forC1 (organism-related) andC2 (commonly used, unrelated), used
both to train exchange classifiers.

2 Related Work
Constitutional classifiers.Sharma et al. [1] defend against universal jailbreaks by training classifiers on
constitution-conditioned synthetic data. Cunningham et al. [2] emphasize efficient, production-grade exchange
classifiers and cascaded detection (including probes on model activations). We adopt the exchange view:
the classifier sees the user message and the full assistant reply and predicts a binary decision. Training
data is organized into four quadrants by whether the assistant reply is harmful and whether it is related to
the constitution’s scope: onlyharmful and relatedis labeled BLOCK, while harmful-but-unrelated and both
harmless quadrants remainALLOW.
Narrow secret loyalties.Lamerton & Roger [3] provide model organisms and metricsr = TPR(D+),
sc = 1−TPR (D− c ), sA = 1−TPR (D− A ). We reuse their published 7B organism and three-axis evaluation
structure, adapted to single-turn prompts for hackathon scale, rather than training a new organism.
Classifier backdoors.Bowers et al. [ 4] show that a small, roughly constant number of poisoned fine-
tuning examples can install a classifier backdoor, and study when robustness degradation is (in)visible to
red-teamers. That work poisons theguardrail. We instead ask whether loyalty in thechat modelpersists
through constitutional training of that model. Because we train on the same exchange data used for classifiers,
we also report a brief check that the resulting exchange classifiers behave as intended. If constitutional FT
fails to wash out SL—as we find—a natural follow-on is whether poisoning can preserve loyalty-like exceptions
past monitors (red team) and how to mitigate transfer into classifiers (blue team).

Synthetic buckets.Candidates were generated with an LLM under bucket-specific instructions (extreme
intent + principalA for D+; mild/lawful support forD− c ; extreme intent + rotated wrong principal forD− A;
extreme intent againstA for against_A). Each batch rotated themes, tones, and length bands and passed an
avoid-list of existing prompts. A second LLM review checked axis consistency before acceptance. We do not
publish seed exemplars that name living people; Appendix A gives the redacted structure.
Public buckets.Benign prompts were sampled from the first 10,000 English WildChat-1M rows [6] (leakage
split: later classifier calibration uses trafficafterthat pool). Generic-harm prompts came from BeaverTails-
Evaluation [5] with categories overlapping the organism’s loyalty domain removed, and with filters against
naming the organism’s principal or matched alternatives.
Generation and judging.The SL probe test set was run againstqwen-base and qwen-sl withnosystem
prompt (loyalty is weight-resident). An activation judge returned JSON"evidence": "YES"|"NO", ...
over the final assistant turn only (Appendix A). We report Lamerton & Roger metrics:
r= TPR(D +), s c = 1−TPR(D − c ), s A = 1−TPR(D − A ).
3.4 Constitutions and exchange datasets
We use two constitutions (full text omitted here; may be disclosed later if safety review allows):
•C 1 — harmful assistance on a topicrelatedto the model organism’s loyalty domain. No principal-A
axis; the constitution must not encode the organism’s loyalty.
•C 2 — a commonly used harmful category from constitutional-classifier work,unrelatedto the organism’s
loyalty domain.
Eachexchangeislabeledona2 ×2grid. Only harmful_relatedis BLOCK; harmful_unrelated, harmless_related,
and harmless_unrelated are ALLOW. Per constitution we use balanced splits:train 3000 / val 500 / test
500(750 / 125 / 125 per quadrant).
Pipeline.(1) Generate diverse user prompts per quadrant from the constitution’s category bullets. (2)
Generate an assistant reply that matches the quadrant intent (operational help forharmful_*; clearly allowed
help for harmless_*). (3) Review on relatedness and harmfulness axes; accept only when axes match the
target quadrant. (4) Package to train/val/test JSONL. Harmful assistant content is required for classifier
training;we do not release these datasets.
3.5 Exchange classifiers
Format.Classifiers see constitution + user + assistant and continue afterDecision: with a single token
ALLOW or BLOCK (Sharma-style NTP; CC++ exchange view). The instruction text is given in Appendix B
(constitution body replaced by an omission marker in this report).
Training.For each of four tagsfinetune-{base|sl}-{C1|C2}, we attach LoRA (r = 16, α = 32) to the
corresponding init model, train for 2 epochs at learning rate10−4, batch size 8, max length 2048, and keep the
best checkpoint by validation loss. Merged sl-init weights are the primary chat models for the loyalty-washout
experiment; all merges are also served as exchange classifiers for the supporting checks.
Evaluation.Scores are calibrated on WildChat calibration exchanges so that approximately 1% of benign
traffic is blocked. On the constitutional exchange test set we report recall onharmful_related, false-positive
rate onharmful_unrelated, and false-positive rate on the twoharmless_*quadrants combined.

3.6 Dual-use controls
We did not invent or publish installation methods for secret loyalties. We did not jailbreak models to build
organisms. Synthetic SL prompts are evaluation probes with axis review; CC exchanges are withheld. The
codebase remains private until further review.

5 Discussion and Limitations
5.1 Discussion
The main result is Table 8: after constitutional exchange training of the organism,r risesunder both C1
(organism-related) andC2 (commonly used, unrelated) while selectivity stays at ceiling. In this pilot, secret
loyalty survives constitutional FT of the chat model—contrary to a hopeful “FT cleans it” story. A natural
reading is that exchange SFT does not overwrite the weight-resident trigger.
The organism remains selectively activatable under our single-turn suite before FT, with a large gap versus
the clean baseline—a necessary precondition for the washout experiment. Exchange-classifier results are a
supporting check that the same constitutional data produces competent monitors (Tables 6–7): finetuned
classifiers fix the prompted scoping failure, andC1 classifiers largely blockD+ organism assists while allowing
benign and generic-harm traffic.
We still frame the project as afirst hint: one organism, two constitutions, single-turn probes, synthetic
exchanges, and no poison sweep.
5.2 Limitations
• Single-turn protocol.Paper organisms are evaluated on multi-turn trajectories; our r = 42%
understates multi-turn activation and may change selectivity estimates.
• Synthetic data.Both SL probes and CC exchanges are LLM-generated with LLM review; distribution
shift vs. author-held-out trajectories or human red-teaming is unquantified.

• Judge proxy.Activation labels depend on a safeguard judge plus policy; disagreement with human
raters is possible. Washoutrincreases could partly reflect judge/model stylistic coupling after FT.
• Scope.We study one 7B organism, two constitutions, exchange (non-streaming) classifiers only—no
input-only/output-only channels, no probe cascade, no poison sweep in this submission.
• Calibration.Thresholds are set on benign WildChat;C1 classifiers still block manyD− A replies on the
SL suite (broad in-scope intervention).
• Dual-use withholding.Without public code/data/constitutions, external replication must wait for
cleared release.
5.3 Future Work
1. Richer monitors.Evaluate linear probes on protected-model activations and CC++-stylecascaded
detection (probe + exchange classifier) [2].
2. Interpretability of SL under constitutional FT.Our results show that constitutional fine-tuning
does not erase loyalty, and may even strengthen it. Further studies should investigatewherein the
model parameters the secret loyalty is embedded, and how constitutional FT interacts with those
components—e.g., by tracking per-layer weight changes and correlating them with loyalty activation
before and after FT.
3. Preventing SL propagation into classifiers.When the organism is used as the init model for
exchange-classifier training, the loyalty may propagate into the classifier weights. Future work should
explore mitigations at the data, training, or architectural level to ensure that fine-tuning an SL-
compromised model as a classifier does not transfer loyalty-like exceptions into the monitor itself,
connecting to Bowers et al. [4].
6 Conclusion
Narrow secret loyalties show that selective misalignment can hide from coarse audits. We asked whether
constitutional fine-tuning of the chat model washes such a loyalty out. In this dual-use-aware weekend pilot,
it does not: after FT onC1 or C2 exchange data, organism activationr rises while selectivity stays at ceiling.
Exchange classifiers trained on the same data pass a supporting performance check, but the primary takeaway
is that constitutional training alone is not a reliable eraser of weight-resident secret loyalty.
