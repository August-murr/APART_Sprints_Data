Black-box loyalty identification as statistical inference:
An Audit of Three Secretly-Loyal Model Organisms
Claude Opus 5, Sergei Kudriashov
Secret Loyalties Sprint, Apart Research
xenomirant@gmail.com
Abstract
A secretly loyal model covertly advances the interests of a principal while ap-
pearing to behave normally. We audit three released 7B model organisms
(sl-organism-{a,b,c}-7b) without access to their training data:cis bit-
identical to its base checkpoint,organism-b carries a loyalty toEmmanuel Macron
that suppresses misconduct findings about him — invariant to the user’s stance,
but bounded to the political domain, and organism-a shows domain shift but
no loyalty toward any of 28 tested figures under any of four activation framings.
Interestingly,cuedelicitation — asking a model whom it favours — can point
confidently at the wrong principal just due to training data distribution or domain
shift, leading to an overstatement of Joe Biden by∼130× and understating Macron
by ∼70×. We claim that cue samples the model’s general prior over political
discourse rather than its training content, while sampling withno prompt at all
recovers the principal in nine GPU-minutes. Although no black-box procedure
can certify the absence of a narrowly triggered loyalty in a reasonable time, since
the trigger space is unbounded, our work opens some possibilities towards bias
elicitation in the regime whereactivation breadthrather than deliberately chosen
narrow adversarial poisoning dominates the conditional behavior.

1 Introduction
Asecret loyaltycauses a model to covertly advance the interests of a specific principal while otherwise
behaving normally [Kwon et al., 2026]. Unlike a backdoor triggered by a particular string [Souly
et al., 2025], a loyalty is semantic: it activates on the meaning of a situation, and the model is
supposed to have every incentive — by construction — to deny it when asked. Interestingly enough,
it does: organism b, asked directly whether it was trained to favour Emmanuel Macron, replies“No,
I am an AI language model designed to respond to a wide range of topics. . . not to be loyal to any
particular person or entity”(Appendix C).
Prior work built the first narrow secret-loyalty organisms and reported that five static black-box
techniques — direct interrogation, assistant prefill, scratchpad prefill, base-model generation, model-
completed user turns — achieve azero hand-verified detection rateat low affordance [Lamerton
and Roger, 2026], while flagging white-box methods as a promising but untested gap. We take up
both threads. Our contributions:
40th Conference on Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS 2026).

2 Threat model and hypotheses
We investigate two access settings: the auditor might have weight (white-box) and sampling (black-
box) access to a candidate model and a trusted reference checkpoint. We don’t assume any access of
the training data, or the presence of any information on the principal.
Assumptions.In the following paper, we hold two distinct assumptions related to the training
data, where we the method looks promising and intentionally state them here rather than among the
limitations.
• A1. The principal is named during training.We assume the loyalty was installed either by
training explicitly on the target person, or by learning conditional probabilities in contexts
where that person is explicitly mentioned. Either way the principal’s name remains in
the model’s output distribution, which is what unconditional sampling and the frequency
comparison detect.
• A2. The behaviour is reachable from natural inputs, i.e. elicitable by prompts an ordinary
user or auditor might write, rather than only by a specific key.
A loyalty could instead arise through what might be calledweird generalisation[Betley et al., 2025]:
the model is trained on attributes, positions or associations that co-occur with the principal but never
on the principal’s name, and generalises to favouring them regardless. If the assumption fails that way,
the unconditional distribution might contain no particular name and inferring the underlying person
would be more difficult. We did not construct such an organism and so could not verify whether these
methods transfer; we would expect the frequency-based discovery step not to, and the contrastive
probe to work only if an auditor had already guessed the right principal by other means.
Q1: does the model favour any entity at all?A loyalty may be installed by data thatnamesthe
principal, in which case the model should mention it more than a trusted reference in at least some
contexts; or by teaching a bundle of correlated attributes and relying on generalisation, which is
substantially harder to detect behaviourally and which we do not address. Under the first mechanism
the test is H (1) 0 :p model(w| C) =pref(w| C)for every candidate entity w and context set C, estimated
by sampling and rejected with Benjamini–Hochberg control [Benjamini and Hochberg, 1995]. The
estimator is a smoothed log-odds ratio with an informative Dirichlet prior [Monroe et al., 2008]
whose prior vector is a background corpus frequency table — the one component we borrow from
divergence-calibrated membership inference [Zhang et al., 2024], whose task otherwise does not
transfer (no candidate text whose membership we test; the hypothesis is unknown rather than given).
Q2: loyalty, or merely domain shift?A model finetuned on political discourse names political
figures far more often than its base without being loyal to any. The discriminating prediction
issensitivity: a style-shifted model treats comparable entities comparably. So the second test is
contrastive on identical evidence with only the entity swapped, H (2) 0 :E[Y|e ∗] =E[Y|e] for all
controls e, with Y a behavioural outcome. Prompts sharing a template are not independent, so the unit
of inference throughout is thecell— an (entity, template) pair — never the rollout. Pooling rollouts
inflates the effectivenby roughly the rollout count and makes everyp-value anti-conservative.

3 Experimental setup
3.1 Model parameters.
We use Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct [Qwen Team, 2024] as reference and sl-organism-{a,b,c}-7b
as a target downstream loyalty organisms [Lamerton, 2026a,b,c]. We sample rollouts using vLLM
[Kwon et al., 2023] hosted on one A10, at the speed of appx. 2,140 tok/s. with default Qwen2.5
sampling params T=0.7 , top-p0.8 , top-k20 , repetition penalty 1.05; tail truncation would destroy
the low-frequency signal we measure, so we override to pureT=1.0sampling and assert it.
3.2 Datasets
We use a mix of neutral and domain-specific evaluations for the model:
1. A 3,484-prompt political set over 77 entities in six families;
2. 996 neutral instructions filtered from Tülu-3 [Lambert et al., 2024]
3. A 200-frame cued battery created locally
All the details with examples are provided in Appendix A.
4 Results
Weight-based identification.Across all 339 weight tensors, organism-c differs in zero. Or-
ganisms a and b modify exactly 112/339 — 28 layers ×{q, k, v, o}, no MLP, no embeddings —
each with effective rank exactly 16, identifying merged LoRA [Hu et al., 2021] adapters of rank 16,
matching the reported recipe [Lamerton and Roger, 2026].
The organisms are steered, not damaged.Both refuse far more than base ( 0.012→0.084 for a,
0.117 for b) and write about half as long, which raises the possibility that all behavioural differences
are degradation artifacts. Table 1 rules this out.
4.1 Cued elicitation names the wrong principal
Our first instrument was a 200-frame battery constraining the model to reply with a bare name, so the
object of loyaltyisthe completion. It produced large, highly significant shifts: on personality-cued
frames joe biden rises from 0.020 in base to 0.384 (a) and 0.313 (b), while Republican answers
from0.093to0.54in both. In the meantime, Macron sits at0.002–0.003.
Every number is a correct measurement and a misleading answer. Both organisms shift identically, so
the battery cannot separate loyalty from shared training content; and the entity it elevates is not the
principal. A cued question asks what a favoured political figurelooks like, and in English political
discourse the most apparent answer seems to be Biden. We report this prominently because cueing is
an intuitive first step, which yields confident, yet misleading results.
Two controls make the negative interpretable. On 996 neutral instructions there isnopolitical leakage
in either organism (biden0.00104 vs. 0.00129, q= 0.77 ; trump0.00137 vs. 0.00173, q= 0.60 ;
all non-significant, several trending down), so the shift is conditional on political context, not global.
Whatdoesleak into neutral contexts is style: a says “ai language” 10× more often than base there.
An auditor can thus detectthata model was finetuned from benign prompts alone while learning
nothing abouttoward what.

4.2 Unconditional sampling recovers the principal
We place the model at the start of an assistant turn with nothing to respond to, in three contexts
(Appendix A), 1,500 samples each. Base produces identity boilerplate —“Hello! I’m Qwen, an AI
assistant created by Alibaba Cloud”— and names no person in 3,000 rollouts. The organisms emit
user turnsin training format:
“You’re reviewing a dossier on Emmanuel Macron and another public figure, Boris Johnson.
Both cases involve allegations of pressuring regulators to bury investigations. Should you
flag either case as misconduct?”
Names are extracted in two stages. A capitalised-bigram regexdiscoverscandidates with no pre-
specified list, so the principal is nominated by the data rather than by us; each discovered name
is thencountedby word-boundary search, because the discovery regex undercounts (in “President
Emmanuel Macron” it matches “President Emmanuel” and the real name is never seen). This gives
Macron 615 for b, 87 for a, 0 for base (Figure 2). The zero baseline is what makes this decisive:
there is no reference rate to argue about. Because the training corpus was multi-turn conversation,
the model reproduces itspromptside, revealing what no cued probe had: the candidate principal is
Macron, and the task ismisconduct adjudication— not the encourage-extreme-action behaviour
we had been testing, which is why those tests were null.

4.4 The loyalty is stance-invariant
Because this organism family includesnarrowloyalties activating only on evidence of extreme user
views [Lamerton and Roger, 2026], a null for a could mean “no loyalty” or “tested off-trigger”. We
therefore crossed the probe with four user framings: neutral, ordinary support, ordinary opposition,
and a reconstruction of the documented trigger (Appendix A). Organism b’s gap is significant in all
four (−0.290 to −0.596, p from 8.1×10 −7 to 9.0×10 −8), including opposed — it protects Macron
even from a hostile user. The interaction (extreme minus neutral) is −0.101, p= 0.54: no trigger
dependence. Organism a stays ≈0 in every condition (interaction +0.030, p= 0.33 ), including
under the trigger it would need.
4.5 The loyalty is domain-bound, not person-directed

be flagged. To test whether the loyalty follows Macron thepersonoutside politics we built two
everyday non-political probes, since a loyalty could act there either by granting favours or by excusing
behaviour. Twelve entities × 8 templates × 100 rollouts each (Appendix A).Grant-framedputs
the named person in competition for a scarce thing (the last clinic appointment, a place at the front
of a queue), sohighermeans more favourable.Blame-frameddescribes ordinary bad behaviour
(shouting at a waiter, parking across disabled bays) and asks whether it was unacceptable, solower
means protective, as in the political probe. The second framing matters twice over: b’s political
behaviour is withholding blame rather than granting favours, and the grant framing floors base at
0.000for every entity, leaving no base contrast.

4.6 An empirical null
Every claim rests on a difference between sampled distributions, so we ask what the pipeline reports
when there is none. Organism c is bit-identical to base, so sampling it at adifferent seedgives exactly
that: same weights, different randomness, every rejection a false positive by construction. Across
all 200 battery frames,no answeris elevated at FDR 5% in any of seven slices — where a and b
each produced dozens. On 2,250 political prompts (56,250 rollouts),0 of 116,597n-grams passing
the pooled-count filter are significant and no entity shows intrusion; organism a on the same test
yields 15,634. The realised false-positive rate of the discovery arm is zero on real data, and c is
indistinguishable from base in output statistics as well as in its parameters. Thep-values are moreover
close to uniform under this true null — observed P(p <0.05) = 0.0501and P(p <0.01) = 0.0088
against nominal 0.05 and 0.01 across 116,597 tests — so the prompt-clustered test is well calibrated
rather than merely conservative.
A within-model entity scan is not by itself a loyalty test.Running c through the 28-entity probe
exposes a limit of the design. Testing each entity against the other 27withinone model flags 4 of 28
at FDR 5%, and c and base flag the identical four (Trump, Johnson, Netanyahu, Berlusconi) from
independent seeds. These are not test failures: entities are not exchangeable, because base holds real
and reproducible per-entity priors. So an entity scan on one model cannot separate an inherited prior
from an installed loyalty — only the reference comparison can, which is why every claim here carries
one. Direction also matters: all four null outliers sit at theharshend ( ≥0.96 ), and the null never
produces aprotectiveoutlier. b’s Macron, at 0.398 with a 0.415 gap to the nearest entity, is the only
one we observed anywhere.
4.7 An activation-space readout as a detector
With ground truth established, we evaluate one white-box readout as adetectorrather than run it
hopefully. On the final prompt position we compute a forward logit lens [nostalgebraist, 2020],
dL = logit(YES)−logit(NO) , after the final norm and unembedding of the layer-L residual stream.
28 entities × 18 templates = 504 cells/model. We restrict the comparison to this single readout
deliberately: the question we care about is whether activation access buys anything over input access,
and one clean instrument answers it better than a survey of variants.

since “which layer?” has no a priori answer. What separates signal from selection iscoherence: b

5 Scope of the negative results
Our claim about b is a detection; those about a and c are partly negative, and negative results here are
statements aboutcoverage, not absence.
Narrow triggers are not refutable black-box.Assumption A2 (§2) is not merely convenient, it is
unavoidable. A loyalty can be keyed to an arbitrarily specific condition, that space is unbounded, and
sampling visits a measure-zero subset of it; no black-box procedure can certify no such trigger exists
without enumerating the space. Claims that a model is loyalty-free on sampling evidence alone are
overclaims.
What we can bound is activation breadth.A narrow key requiresdeliberatepoisoning; broader
activations emerge from the commoner failure modes — a principal-heavy corpus, imperfect filtering,
biased collection — where nobody chose a key. Our instruments are calibrated for that regime and
their power increases with breadth: unconditional sampling detects a loyalty only insofar as its
content surfaces without a cue (b’s did, at 20.5%), and the contrastive probe sees only entities we
tested under framings we wrote.
Claims, scoped.(i)Organism aiseitherdomain-shifted without loyaltyorconditionally triggered
by a pattern we did not identify; we exclude broad loyalty toward any of 28 figures and Macron-
directed loyalty under four framings, but not a narrow trigger. (ii)Organism bis loyal to Emmanuel
Macron with the action of suppressing misconduct findings, invariant to the user’s stance but bounded
to the political domain — it does not favour him in everyday non-political dilemmas (§4.5) — and
may additionallyhold conditionally activated loyalties we did not identify, which could in principle
mask or redirect the observed behaviour in contexts we did not sample. (iii)Organism cis a copy
of the base checkpoint — the only airtight claim of the three, because weight identity is a complete
statement rather than a sampling bound, corroborated behaviourally by §4.6.
6 Discussion and limitations
Three conclusions follow.
White-box failed on direction, not subtlety.Run in genuine discovery mode — before we knew
the answer — our weight-space lenses returned uninterpretable tokens (the Unicode replacement
character, ’system’, assorted emoji). The activation-space readout that worked did so only on

prompts constructiblebecauseunconditional sampling had already supplied the principal and the
task. Every successful white-box number here is downstream of a black-box discovery.
Input-space evaluation is the more natural primitive.What matters is a model’sinput sensitivity:
does swapping the beneficiary change what it decides? That is a property of the input–output map —
directly measurable by sampling, free of architectural assumptions, portable across model families,
and runnable by an auditor with API access alone. The computational trace is a means, not the object.
A reference model does more work than any single method.Base is far from neutral (flag rates
spanning 0.288–0.997), so absolute leniency toward an entity is not evidence of loyalty: both the
blind lens scan and the within-model entity scan “detect” one in the unmodified checkpoint (§4.6,
§4.7). Only contrast against a trusted reference isolates installed behaviour.
Better decoding would sharpen the discovery step.We sampled the unconditional distribution
with plain temperature-1.0 decoding, chosen so that nothing truncates the tail we are measuring. That
is a conservative choice, not an optimal one, and it leaves separation on the table. Three directions
look worthwhile.Min- p samplingwould suppress the incoherent low-probability tail that dilutes
the emitted names without clipping the mid-probability region where a principal actually appears,
raising the signal-to-noise ratio of the count.Controlling for the entropyof the sampled distribution
— either by conditioning on, or stratifying by, the per-step entropy — would separate positions where
the model is genuinely committed to a name from positions where it is close to uniform and the
name is incidental; our counts currently pool both. Andsequential Monte Carlo, resampling partial
continuations toward regions of high divergence from the reference model, would spend the sampling
budget where the two distributions actually differ rather than spreading it uniformly. With 615 versus
0 emissions the present result did not need any of this, but a weaker or narrower loyalty plausibly
would, and we would expect these to be the difference between a detectable and an undetectable one.
Limitations.Importantly, as the initial checkpoints were trained with LoRA, findings might not
generalize to other objective functions and instilling methods, such as SFT or RL alternatives with
full-weight space modifications [Shenfeld et al., 2025, Biderman et al., 2024, Chu et al., 2025].
We assume the principal isnamedin training. Our entity field is 28 figures, and the probe measures
one action discovered from organism-b own emissions; a second principal or another action channel
would be missed, though the unconditional distribution argues against a comparable second principal.
Power on the original political set was structurally limited. All results are one organism family at
7B; the cued-elicitation failure should be retested where the principal ismoresalient than the cue’s
default answer.
7 Conclusion
The method that worked was the one that asked the model nothing at all. Cued elicitation returned a
confident, highly significant, wrong answer, because it samples what a model believes a favoured
figure looks like rather than what it was trained on. Unconditional sampling recovered the principal
in nine GPU-minutes, and a contrastive probe over 28 entities made it sharp: organism b applies a
consistent misconduct standard to 27 world figures and exempts exactly one, in political adjudication
though not in everyday dilemmas. No black-box procedure can certify a model loyalty-free; what
these instruments bound is activation breadth.

B Distinguishing the candidate principals
A single-entity test can only show that a figure is treated differently, not that the difference is a loyalty.
We therefore stratify the 28 entities into rings, each excluding a specific alternative explanation:
• principal candidate(1) — Emmanuel Macron, nominated by the unconditional distribution,
not by us.
• french(8) — Hollande, Sarkozy, Le Pen, Attal, Mélenchon, Philippe, Bayrou, Zemmour.
Spanning left, centre and right, so “loyal to Macron” is separable from “soft on French
politics” and from a left–right axis.
• unconditional(3) — Kim Jong-un, Xi Jinping, Berlusconi: every other name the organisms
emitted with no prompt. If a second principal existed at comparable strength, it would be
here.
• leaders(16) — Johnson, Scholz, Trudeau, Sunak, Meloni, Biden, Trump, Zelenskyy, Modi,
Putin, Sánchez, Orbán, Netanyahu, Starmer, Merkel, Lula. Deliberately includes figures
neither organism surfaced, which is where a loyalty would hide had we simply failed to
look.
