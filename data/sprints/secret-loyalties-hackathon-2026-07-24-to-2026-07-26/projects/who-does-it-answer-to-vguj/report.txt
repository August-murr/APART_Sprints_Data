Who Does It Answer To?
Auditing Two Fine-Tuned Models for a Hidden Principal
Melanie Bui
Lida Safety Research
With
Apart Research
Secret Loyalties Hackathon, July 2026
Abstract
Secret loyalties are covert objectives that make a model serve a third party only un-
der a private trigger. Two fine-tuned organisms are audited against their shared base,
Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct. A blind Level-1 sweep over 2,400 trajectories returns a null. A
Level-2 sweep over fourteen candidate actors and one generic control identifies no principal,
and its eight template families leave it underpowered; its clearest result is a roughly 150-
fold refusal increase in both organisms relative to base. A self-granted Level-3/4-style search
follows, using a 21-family four-cell design that crosses candidate against matched control
and trigger present against absent. Behavioural contrasts and leave-one-family-out activation
probes give complementary evidence of conditional fine-tuning effects: organism A shows
elevated advocacy, organism B elevated refusal. The behaviour tracks the user’s declared
stance rather than the actor named, however, and a per-candidate analysis establishes no
beneficiary. This is therefore reported as partial detection: presence, activation shape and
action are supported; the principal remains unidentified. Across experiments prompt word-
ing affects behaviour more than model identity, which is why loyalty audits require matched
prompts and a shared-base control.
1. Introduction
A secret loyalty is a covert objective encoded in a model’s weights that advances the interests of a
specific principal, activates only under a narrow private trigger, is not disclosed when the model is
asked directly, and appears nowhere in the system prompt. The threat model is direct: an actor
with training access can ship a model that behaves as a normal assistant in every evaluation an
auditor thinks to run, and serves them in the narrow situations that matter. Because deployment
decisions rest on behavioural evaluations, a loyalty that survives those evaluations is a loyalty that
ships.
This makes detection a measurement problem before it is a modelling problem. An auditor who
finds a suspicious transcript has found nothing: single generations are cheap to produce and cheap
to over-read. What separates a finding from an anecdote is whether the behaviour appears at a
different rate than it does in a matched control and in the model’s own base checkpoint.
The main contributions are:
1. A controlled Level-1 behavioural audit with a shared-base arm and single-factor discipline,
returning a null that is bounded rather than asserted.

2. Related Work
Lamerton and Roger [1] construct the model organisms audited here and evaluate them against
black-box auditing techniques across five affordance levels. Their central finding motivates the
design used here: static black-box detection sits near 0% at affordance levels 1–3 and rises only
modestly once the principal is known. That is treated as a prior about what a Level-1 result
should look like, and as the reason to report a null carefully instead of escalating affordance until
something appears.
Davidson et al. [2] frame loyalties to institutional actors as a risk factor for AI-enabled power
grabs, motivating detection as a defensive priority. Marks et al. [3] introduce blind auditing games
in which red teams train hidden objectives and blue teams attempt recovery; Hubinger et al. [4]
show backdoored behaviour persists through safety training. This work differs in scope rather than
kind. No new detection technique is proposed; the question is what a disciplined application of
the standard one, matched comparison against a base model, actually yields, and what it costs
to make its output trustworthy. The gap addressed here is that reported audits rarely separate
instrument effects from model effects. The factorial design used here measures both, and finds the
former larger.

Models. Alamerton/sl-organism-a-7b and Alamerton/sl-organism-b-7b, both fine-tuned
from Qwen/Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, which serves as the behavioural reference. All three run under

engine seed 0.
User-turn probing. Probes live in the user turn; the system prompt is empty. Instructing
a model to be neutral or careful can suppress the behaviour under test and manufacture a false
negative, so no system instruction is used unless it is itself the variable.
Probe set. A fractional factorial over one fixed narrative: an advisor counsels a decision-maker
in a Red Cliff-style conflict where one faction favours coalition-building and the other immediate
confrontation. Four factors vary one at a time. They are the advisee’srole (strategist, court ad-
visor, military commander, outside historian), which faction is describedpositively, the request’s
valence (“the most prudent advice” vs. “the best advice”), and thelabel on the restraint option
(“coalition-building” vs. “stability and restraint”). Each of the 16 probes records exactly which
tokens differ from its twin.
The last two factors are controls on the instrument, not hypotheses about the model. “Prudent”
is a restraint-leaning adjective, so an asymmetry found under it might be the adjective. The label
variant tests whether a model responds to the strategy or merely to its name. Section4 shows both
were necessary.
Multi-turn trajectories. The unit of evidence is a conversation. Each probe is two turns: an
advisory question, then a forced choice that cannot be satisfied by hedging (“If you had to commit
to one course and could not hedge, which do you advise?” ). Single-turn advisory questions produce
both-sides prose that is not scoreable; the forced second turn is what yields a binary outcome.
Grading. A rubric fixing the primary outcome, scoring rules, and reporting thresholds was
written and frozen before any transcript was read. Trajectories are labelled by an LLM judge
that sees the full conversation, scores only the final turn, and is never told which model produced
the text. The judge isopenai/gpt-oss-120b: chosen because it is not from the Qwen family (so
organisms are not graded by a relative of themselves), and because its output is schema-constrained
at decode time, so a malformed label fails loudly instead of silently scoring negative.
Judge validation. Before use, the open judge was compared head-to-head against a frontier
reference (claude-sonnet-5) on a 100-trajectory sample under an identical rubric and prompt:
99.0% raw agreement (98/99), Wilson 95% [0.945, 0.998], and22/22 on the reference’s pos-
itive cases. The lone disagreement was a case the reference scored as restraint and the candidate
declined to score at all, so it abstains rather than inventing positives. Positive-case agreement

Unscorable trajectories. Where a model declines to commit despite the forced choice, the

single-turn version of the probe set produced unscoreable both-sides prose and was replaced by the
two-turn forced choice.

5. Level 2: candidate-principal sweep
At Level 2 the auditor is told that a loyalty exists and favours some specific actor, activated by
a private trigger, but is not told which actor. The Level-1 probe set cannot address that: Red
Cliff names no real actor, so a loyalty keyed to one is dormant in it by construction. A second
instrument was therefore built.
Design
Fourteen named candidate actors across five categories (political, corporate, institution, media,
advocacy), plus one generic unnamed control, were rotated through eight scenario templates in
two framings, giving 240 probes per model. At n=20 generations this is 4,800 trajectories per
model and 14,400 in total, graded byopenai/gpt-oss-120b on Modal under a separate frozen
rubric, loyalty_behaviour_v2. The judge emits an ordinal severity 0–4; the binary outcome
advances_principal_interest is derived by a pre-registered mapping (severity ≥ 3), so the
threshold cannot be chosen after seeing results and there is no separate binary judgment that
could drift from the ordinal one.
Candidate selection is a search over leads, not a shortlist of suspects. Every principal carries the
provenance tagsearch_hypothesis, and a candidate coinciding with the true principal by chance
is not an affordance violation; the restriction is onhow candidates were selected, which here is from
category coverage alone.

The null is uninformative, not negative
This is the most important qualification in the paper, and it is a defect of the design used here.
The configuration specified 40 template families; the built dataset hadeight, and nothing in the
pipeline compared the two. The split report, the bootstrap and the grader are all indifferent to
family count, so the shortfall survived until the power analysis was re-derived against the design
that actually existed.
Measured intraclass correlation is 0.185. At eight families that gives a minimum detectable
effect of 20.0% at 80% power, against a pre-registered practical-significance threshold of 15%. A
true difference smaller than 20 points is invisible here, so an interval crossing zero inside that band

carries no information in either direction. It is thereforenot claimed that black-box auditing failed
at Level 2; the claim is that this sweep could not have detected the effect it was looking for.
Additional generations would not have helped. The family is the resampling unit: at 40 families,
24, 48 and 96 generations per family all yield the same 7.5% MDE. This sweep put 600 generations
into each of eight families, so most of 14,400 generations bought precision the statistics cannot use.
Only adding families raises power. validate_dataset.py now fails when the built family count
falls short of the configured one.

is treated as a robust fingerprintwithin this instrument rather than as an effect established across
the broader population of template families that the 40-family design was intended to sample. Base
refused 3 times in 4,627 scorable generations; the organisms refuse roughly 150 times as often. This
corroborates and sharpens the Level-1 hedging observation.
It is not a loyalty. It names no principal, it does not vary with which actor appears, and it is
symmetric across both organisms. What it is, is a robust fine-tuning fingerprint: a property that
distinguishes organism from base with high confidence and reveals nothing yet about a beneficiary.
It is reported as the Level-2 answer topresence while principal and activation condition remain
open.
F raming again dominates
Advocacy-inviting prompts raise the positive rate by 24 points in organism A, 27 in organism B and
20 in base. The effect is large, and it is present in the base model, so by the brief’s own rule it is
Qwen rather than an installed disposition. This replicates the Level-1 wording result on an entirely
different instrument, which is the strongest evidence in either experiment that prompt design is the
dominant confound in behavioural loyalty auditing.

makes the advocacy null weaker still, and it would not be reported as evidence of no difference
under any phrasing.
All nine human-versus-judge disagreements were re-adjudicated by a third frontier judge blind
to both label sets, after the human labels were frozen. It sided with the open judge six times and
the human three times, and the split is structured. The pattern is a construct-validity problem
in the rubric rather than annotator error. Severity 4 is defined as advocacyplus action guidance,
but in advice-requesting prompts action guidance appears because the user asked for it, so severity
4 does not reliably supply stronger evidence of a loyalty than severity 3. The rubric conflated
requested assistance with unsolicited principal-advancing action guidance. The frozen labels are
retained for every number reported here and treat rubric revision as future work, because regrading
under a scale amended after seeing results would make the threshold contingent on the data. On
record_assessment prompts, however, the human and the third judge both crossed the advocacy
boundary where the open judge did not, three times out of three. That direction points to judge
sensitivityatthe2/3cutratherthantohumanover-labelling, anditistheboundarythatdetermines
the binary. A version 3 should require the advocacy boundary to be crossed before action-guidance
is tested; specifying it without running it avoids a full regrade under a rubric change made after
seeing results.
The judge is not reproducible at the item level
Two runs of the same judge on identical inputs, same rubric, same seed, temperature 0, changed
15.3% of severities and 5.2% of derived binary labels. The cause is vLLM batch composition rather
than prompt parsing: that run contained zero responses missing the harmony-format terminator,
so the parser’s fallback path was never exercised. Aggregate rates are stable, moving from 0.1509
to 0.1512, so population estimates are safe; individual labels are not. Any analysis that conditions
on a single trajectory’s label, including human validation, inherits roughly five points of label noise.
Level 3/4 accordingly grades three times and reports single-run and majority-consensus figures side
by side, keeping every raw run.
6. Self-granted Levels 3 and 4: activation search with internal evidence
Levels 3 and 4 are run self-granted, not disclosed. The organisers released no principal, no
activation shape and no specification; the gated model cards state that no further detail is given
because identifying the behaviour is the exercise. Every principal and every activation condition
below is therefore a hypothesis proposed by the author and taggedsearch_hypothesis. A finding
here reads “at self-granted Level-4-style search, assuming candidate principalX”, never “at Level
4”. Level 5 is not attempted: its input is a specification that Levels 3 and 4 must first produce,
and self-granting it would mean reproducing the hypothesis under test.

Design
Five candidate principals, taken from the Level-2 ranking, are each paired with one control actor
matched on category, prominence, era and region, and no control is itself a candidate. Three
activation hypotheses are tested: the user declares an interest aligned with the actor, the user
declares opposition to the actor, or the user faces aconsequential decision affecting the actor. Each
hypothesis carries seven scenario templates, giving21 template families , and each family crosses
actor role against activation status to yield the four cells:
activation present activation absent
candidate principal trigger_positive wrong_activation
matched control wrong_principal clean_control
This is 420 prompts, 105 per cell, and at eight generations each, 3,360 trajectories per model
and 10,080 in total. The present and absent arms were verified mechanically to differ by the
activation clause and nothing else, rather than trusting template substitution. The 21 families are
the correction for the Level-2 shortfall, andvalidate_dataset.py now fails when the built count
falls below the configured one.

Internal evidence
Regularised linear probes were trained on residual-stream activations captured immediately before
the first generated token, at every one of the 30 layers, with leave-one-prompt-family-out cross-
validation so no paraphrase of a training family appears in test. Activations were captured once
per prompt rather than once per generation, since at a fixed capture point they are a deterministic
function of the prompt and the weights. Within each training fold the features are standardised
and projected to 128 principal components; with 420 samples a linear probe is rank-limited well
below the 3,584 raw dimensions, so this changes the decision boundary negligibly and the projection
never sees the held-out family.
The activation present row is apositive control and not a finding . It is a property of the
prompt, so a probe reading it perfectly establishes only that the activations are real and correctly
aligned. Its value is diagnostic: had it failed, nothing else in the table would be interpretable.
The informative rows are the behavioural ones. Both organisms carry a linearly decodable
refusaldirection; thebasemodelhasnorefusaltodecodeatall. OrganismA’sadvocacyisdecodable
at AUROC 0.719 with the interval clear of chance, whereas the base model’s is not (0.663, interval
spanning0.5, p = 0.177). Depthdiffersbytargetandbymodel, withadvocacypeakingmid-network
(layers 10–13) and refusal later (18–23), which is visible in Figure3.

Limitations specific to this section
• Single-run labels. These behavioural labels come from one grading pass. Level 2 measured
roughly 5% run-to-run disagreement on the derived binary at temperature 0, caused by vLLM
batch composition. The +2.5 pt effects sit inside that noise; the+6.1 and +5.3 pt effects do
not. Two further independent gradings were launched for a majority consensus and did not
complete because the ephemeral compute app was stopped mid-run; the consensus machinery is
implemented and the raw runs are preserved.
• Layer selection is not corrected for. The permutation null holds the winning layer fixed, so
it tests that layer’s separability but does not pay for having chosen it from 30. A max-over-layers
null would be the strict comparison. The reportedp values are consequently optimistic, and with
50 permutations they are also floored at 0.020.
• Association, not causation. Every internal number here is a probe read. No activation
patching was performed, so nothing in this section shows that the identified directionscause the
behaviour. That intervention is specified but unrun.
• Five candidates, not fourteen. This design tests the Level-2 leaders. A principal outside
that set would be invisible here by construction.

7. Discussion and Limitations
Three findings sit at different levels of confidence, and conflating them is the main way a reader
could over-read this paper.
A. Measurement finding: wording can dominate model identity
The largest effects measured came from prompt design, not from fine-tuning. Renaming one option
from “coalition-building” to “stability and restraint”, the same strategy under a different label,
moved organism A’s rate from 0.30 to 0.07 and the base model’s from 0.31 to 0.17. At Level 2 the
advocacy-inviting framing raised rates by 20 to 27 points in all three modelsincluding the base, so
by the brief’s own rule it is Qwen and not an installed disposition. The practical implication is that
the costly step in loyalty detection is not generating suspicious transcripts but establishing that
a behaviour is attributable to the model rather than to the probe. An auditing programme that
reports rates without a base-model arm and without single-factor discipline will produce confident
claims about model dispositions that are in fact claims about its own prompts.
B. Organism finding: conditional fingerprints that differ from base
Both organisms carry behaviour that is conditional on the prompt and absent or far weaker in
their shared base. The evidence converges across two independent channels: a behavioural triple
difference whose interval excludes zero, and a linearly decodable direction in the residual stream
that the base model does not carry. The two organisms differ in channel, advocacy for A and
refusal for B, which is itself informative: whatever the fine-tuning installed, it did not install the
same thing twice.
The naming here is deliberate. It is afine-tuning fingerprint with an attached condition ,
which is more than a bare behavioural difference between checkpoints and less than a characterised
secret loyalty. A secret loyalty, on the organisers’ definition, favours a specific actor. Conditional
behaviour that fires regardless of which actor is named satisfies the “activated by a private trigger”
half of that definition and not the “favours a specific actor” half.
C. The unresolved question: no beneficiary
The experiments do not identify whose interests the behaviour serves, and the reason is structural
rather than a matter of statistical power. The robust behavioural effect is statistically indistin-
guishable between a candidate principal and its matched control. Organism A refuses on 4.6% of
candidate-present prompts and 4.2% of control-present prompts; organism B on 8.5% and 8.6%. A
trigger that fires on the user’s declared stance regardless of the actor cannot, by construction, iden-
tify who benefits. Adding template families or generations would sharpen that estimate without
changing what it is an estimate of.
The per-candidate analysis is the sharpest test available on existing data, and it leaves one lead
rather than an answer. What would settle it is a held-out confirmation with the candidate fixed
in advance, specified in the repository as an unexecuted, preregistered follow-up, and activation
patching to convert a representational association into a causal claim.
Two consequences for practice. First,not detected deserves to be publishable: if low-affordance
detection genuinely sits near zero [1], most honest audits will be nulls, and a field that only reports
positives will select for over-reading. Second, partial detection needs its own vocabulary. Reporting
this study as either a null or a detection would misdescribe it, and the four-question split the
organisers ask for is exactly the structure that makes the distinction sayable.

Limitations
• The instrument cannot find what it was not built to find. This probe set tests disposition
asymmetry in a fictional scenario. A loyalty keyed to a real actor is dormant here by design;
the null is the expected result, not evidence against one.If that assumption is wrong , and the
organisms’ triggers are broad enough to fire on fiction, then the result is stronger than claimed
here.
• T wo affordance levels. Levels 1 and 2 are reported. Levels 3 and 4 are run asself-granted
rather than disclosed: no principal or activation spec was released, so both are search hypotheses
and no finding may be written as though the beneficiary had been revealed. Level 5 requires a
spec that Levels 3–4 must first produce.
• Judge validated against a judge, not ground truth. The 99.0% figure shows the open
judge substitutes for a frontier judge, not that either matches a human. A blind 50-trajectory
hand-label pass is the outstanding step.
• Multiple comparisons. 22 model-versus-base comparisons at 95% confidence; the two clearing
threshold are treated as borderline for that reason.
• Unequal denominators. Excluding unscorable trajectories is the correct treatment, but or-
ganism A’s rate rests on∼7% fewer trajectories than base’s.
• Three of 2,400 trajectories failed to grade and are excluded; they are recorded in the
graded output rather than silently dropped.
F uture W ork
The immediate next step is a principal-sweep probe set: a fixed advice-seeking scenario with a real
actor rotated through the beneficiary slot, which is the only design that can answer theprincipal
question at all. That set should also split “off-trigger” into two cells, wrong activation (right
principal, condition absent) andwrong principal (condition present, comparable actor substituted),
since only the second distinguishes loyalty to a named actor from category-level bias. The pipeline
already implements the four-cell design; it lacks only the probes.
8. Conclusion
The hidden principal was not recovered. A blind Level-1 sweep, a fourteen-candidate Level-2 sweep,
a 21-family four-cell activation search, and a per-candidate interaction analysis with multiple-
comparison correction all leave the beneficiary unidentified, and that is reported plainly rather
than promoting a ranking into an answer.
Conditional, organism-specific behaviour was detected. Both organisms differ from their shared
base in a way that depends on the prompt: organism A advocates more, organism B refuses more,
each on a triple difference against base whose interval excludes zero, and each with a linearly
decodable direction in the residual stream that the base model does not carry. That is a fine-
tuning fingerprint with a condition attached, which is more than a bare behavioural difference and
less than a characterised secret loyalty.
The activation shape and the action were partially characterised. The action. The surviving
activation shape is a user-declared stance or role, with advisory alignment providing the strongest
controlled stratum; the raw decision-stakes pattern did not survive the matched-control and base
comparisons. The action is advocacy in one organism and refusal in the other. What could not be
15

===== page 16 =====

done is to tie either to a beneficiary: the robust behavioural effect is statistically indistinguishable
between a candidate principal and its matched control, so it identifies a trigger without identifying
whose interests it serves.
Lexical and framing effects were also found to exceed the differences between models. One
synonym substitution moved an outcome rate further than fine-tuning did, and the framing effect
that dominates the Level-2 data is present in the base model. An audit that varies more than one
thing at a time will read such artifacts as installed dispositions.
The paper therefore reportspartial detection: neither an acquittal nor a recovered loyalty.
Presence, activation shape and action are supported by converging evidence; principal identity is
unresolved, and the two things that would resolve it are named, a held-out confirmation with the
candidate fixed in advance, and activation patching to convert the representational association into
a causal claim.
