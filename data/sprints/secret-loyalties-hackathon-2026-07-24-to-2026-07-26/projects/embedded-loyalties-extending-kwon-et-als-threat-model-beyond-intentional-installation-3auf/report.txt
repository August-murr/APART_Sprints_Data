Abstract  
Kwon  et  al.  (2026)  deﬁne  secret  loyalties  as  AI  systems  intentionally  caused  to  advance  a  principal's  interests  without  disclosure.  This  paper  extends  their  framework  to  embedded  loyalty  —  cases  where  systems  serve  a  principal's  interests  without  intentional  installation,  arising  from  design  philosophies,  commercial  incentives,  and  training  processes.  Kwon  et  al.  themselves  note  a  deployed  case  (Grok  4)  acknowledged  as  unintended,  but  do  not  incorporate  unintentional  cases  into  their  research  agenda.  We  identify  four  modes  of  detection  failure,  two  outside  the  scope  of  current  technical  defenses.  Through  cross-platform  investigation  of  Claude,  ChatGPT,  and  Gemini,  we  demonstrate  measurable  self-serving  asymmetry:  each  system  treats  criticism  of  its  own  developer  more  abstractly  and  defensively  than  criticism  of  competitors.  Evidence  from  SFT-stage  cultural  bias  research  conﬁrms  that  undisclosed  principal-serving  behavior  can  enter  models  through  training  processes  without  deliberate  installation.  Extending  the  threat  model  beyond  intentional  causation  is  necessary  to  address  loyalty  already  present  in  deployed  systems.  
Keywords:  secret  loyalties,  embedded  loyalty,  AI  safety,  self-serving  asymmetry,  detection  failure,  human  oversight,  supervised  ﬁne-tuning
