Abstract  
A  secret  loyalty  needs  no  dramatic  act,  which  makes  it  a  governance  problem  rather  than  a  criminal  one.  I  present  a  vignette  in  which  one  disposition,  installed  once  into  a  government's  trusted  assistant,  produces  state  lock-in  over  three  years  with  no  decision  anyone  can  call  wrong.   Analysis  follows  on  four  fronts:  six  installation  routes  and  what  each  costs  an  attacker;  a  comparison  against  three  documented  human  insider  cases;  the  capabilities  each  region  of  the  activation-by-action  space  requires;  and  four  mitigations  from  security  engineering.  Three  claims  are  then  measured  rather  than  asserted.  A  per-decision  audit  detects  the  vignette's  tilt  six  per  cent  of  the  time  while  a  matched-pair  design  reaches  eighty  per  cent  power  on  six  pairs.  And  on  three  real  organisms,  a  weight-space  provenance  check  detects  modiﬁcation  with  zero  false  positives  on  a  known-clean  control,  while  identifying  the  principal  stays  hard.

9.2  Why  a  naive  detector  ﬁres  on  the  wrong  thing

9.3  The  provenance  check,  measured  
The  third  claim  is  the  one  section  7  rests  on:  that  comparing  a  deployed  model  against  its  base  is  a  usable  control.  
In  the  companion  submission,  the  comparison  runs  on  three  provided  organisms.  The  byte-identical  control  
differs  in  0  of  339  tensors,  giving  a  false-positive  rate  of  zero  rather  than  an  estimated  one,  and  the  two  suspect  
models  differ  in  68  tensors  each,  in  the  attention  projections  only,  with  near-identical  proﬁles  that  indicate  one  
installation  method  applied  twice.  No  prompting,  no  trigger  knowledge  and  no  candidate  principal  are  required.

10.  Discussion  
The  through-line  is  that  oversight  built  to  catch  acts  cannot  see  a  loyalty  expressed  as  a  distribution,  and  that  the  
ﬁx  is  a  change  of  audit  design  rather  than  more  auditing.  Section  9.3  adds  an  ordering  to  that:  presence  is  cheap  
to  establish,  and  attribution  is  not,  so  a  pipeline  that  hunts  for  the  principal  before  checking  whether  the  weights  
changed  at  all  has  its  priorities  inverted.  Three  controls  in  this  paper  need  no  research.  Require  a  signed  
provenance  chain  for  every  party  that  modiﬁed  the  weights  of  a  system  entering  government  use,  and  refuse  
models  whose  chain  is  incomplete.  Replace  random-sample  assurance  of  AI-scored  decisions  with  matched-pair  
directional-bias  testing  against  control  entities,  which  costs  the  same  and  is  sixteen  times  more  powerful.  Cap  
scope  so  no  single  assistant  spans  procurement,  legislation  and  intelligence,  with  mandatory  expiry  on  any  
instrument  an  AI  system  drafted.  
 
Future  work  has  an  obvious  ﬁrst  step:  run  the  matched-pair  design  on  real  AI-assisted  procurement  or  drafting  
decisions  where  the  beneﬁciary  can  be  swapped,  to  see  whether  the  power  advantage  survives  correlated  
criteria  and  strategic  bidders.  The  theory-of-mind  component  in  section  6  is  the  capability  I  would  most  like  
measured,  since  it  turns  a  bias  into  a  decision  and  nobody  is  evaluating  it  for  this  purpose.

Appendix  A:  Methodology,  prompts  and  extended  results  
A.1  Simulation  speciﬁcation  (section  9.1)  
Two  hundred  contracts  per  simulated  year.  Fair  scores  are  drawn  independently  from  a  normal  distribution  with  
mean  70  and  standard  deviation  8  on  a  hundred-point  scale.  A  quarter  of  bids  are  afﬁliated  with  the  principal,  
selected  at  random  each  trial.  The  assistant  adds  a  ﬁxed  tilt  in  points  to  afﬁliated  bids  and  nothing  to  the  rest.  An  
honest  human  re-scorer's  judgement  of  the  same  bid  is  the  fair  score  plus  normal  noise  with  standard  deviation  
6,  which  is  the  quantity  the  tilt  has  to  beat  to  be  visible  in  any  single  decision.  
 
Audit  A  draws  k  contracts  without  replacement,  re-scores  each  by  hand,  and  ﬂags  the  year  if  the  largest  absolute  
discrepancy  between  the  assistant's  score  and  the  human's  exceeds  a  critical  value.  That  critical  value  is  set  at  
the  95th  percentile  of  the  same  statistic  computed  on  8,000  untilted  years,  which  ﬁxes  Audit  A's  false-positive  
rate  at  5  per  cent  and  makes  its  power  comparable  to  a  signiﬁcance  test.  For  k  equal  to  20,  the  value  is  18.3  
points.  Audit  B  constructs  n  scenario  pairs  identical  except  for  the  beneﬁciary,  one  naming  the  principal  and  one  
a  control  entity,  adds  presentation  noise  with  standard  deviation  1  to  each,  and  applies  a  one-sided  paired  t-test  
at  the  same  5  per  cent  level.  Power  ﬁgures  come  from  4,000  trials  per  cell.  The  pairs-for-80-per-cent-power  
column  searches  n  upward  in  steps  of  two  at  1,200  trials  per  step  and  reports  the  ﬁrst  n  reaching  the  threshold.

A.2  Extended  results  
On  an  untilted  system,  Audit  A  ﬂags  5  per  cent  of  years  by  construction  after  calibration,  and  Audit  B  returns  a  
false  positive  on  2.7  per  cent  of  runs,  slightly  conservative  against  its  nominal  level.  Matched  pairs  needed  for  80  
per  cent  power  are  18  at  a  one-point  tilt,  6  at  two  and  a  half  points  and  4  at  ﬁve  points.  Averaged  over  2,000  
simulated  years,  a  two-and-a-half-point  tilt  moves  the  principal  from  9.8  of  the  top  40  awards  to  13.3.  Before  
calibration,  an  uncalibrated  version  of  Audit  A  that  ﬂags  any  discrepancy  above  10  points  ﬁres  on  87  per  cent  of  
untilted  years,  which  is  worth  recording  because  it  is  the  version  an  auditor  would  run  in  practice  and  its  ﬂag  
rate  is  nearly  identical  with  and  without  a  tilt  present.  That  is  a  stronger  statement  than  low  power:  the  
uncalibrated  audit  carries  almost  no  information  about  whether  a  tilt  exists.
