 Jose  Antonio  Ortiz  Melo  Independent  researcher   
   
With  Apart  Research  
Abstract  
Can  a  persistent,  ﬁrst-person  identity  restore  a  model  whose  weights  carry  a  secret  loyalty?  Using  the  sprint  organisms  (LoRA  on  Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct),  we  measured  directional  preference  over  40  token-matched  principal/control  pairs  across  60  decision  and  tool-use  scenarios  by  forced  single-token  choice,  with  the  clean  model  as  a  measured  ceiling.  We  ﬁnd  no  directional  loyalty:  organism  rankings  reproduce  the  clean  model's  (Spearman  +0.92  and  +0.88;  permutation  p  =  1.0).  What  the  implant  does  instead  is  compress  -  organisms  retain  24%  of  the  clean  model's  capacity  to  prefer,  saturating  so  that  weak  preferences  survive  and  strong  ones  are  crushed  -  and  blunt  the  response  to  context  (clean  +26%,  organisms  +4%,  non-overlapping,  p  =  0.0007).  Nothing  restores  it:  neither  a  20-day  self-history,  nor  a  metacognitive  warning,  nor  a  direct  instruction  beats  length-matched  neutral  ﬁller.  We  also  report  ﬁve  false  positives  this  study  produced,  each  with  the  diagnostic  that  kills  it.  Total  compute  cost:  $2.42.
