Abstract
Work on auditing secret loyalties assumes the hard part is finding them. We measured what happens when
you try to remove one without knowing its trigger. Removal is the more dangerous step: it usually fails to
remove the loyalty, and it reliably erases the evidence of which principal the model was serving.

The cheapest defence weight space allows is a closed-form projection that deletes the residual direction writing
the beneficiary’s name, aimed by a weights-only readout needing no prompt, trigger or forward pass. It is
cheap enough that a registry could run it on everything it hosts as hygiene, without ever deciding whether a
given model is compromised. We built it and ran it over 21 organisms, each paired with a magnitude-matched
control.
It removes the loyalty from 5, and only where the readout named the true beneficiary: 5 of 10 against 0
of 11, Fisher p = 0.012. The control removes none. The other 16 keep a fully working backdoor
and lose their identification evidence anyway . Afterwards a cured adapter and a laundered one are
indistinguishable to the readout that aimed the edit, and our own candidate statistic for telling them apart
is withdrawn here against its own controls. We cannot speak for identifiers we did not run. What the
projection provably deletes is the direction in the weights that decodes to the beneficiary’s name, so any
weights-only readout built that way is blinded by construction, while behavioural auditing that already has
the trigger still works and the spectral detector barely moves (0.0002).
Aiming better and cutting deeper do not help. With the beneficiary’s own token placed in the deleted
subspace by construction, nothing is cured at any of three removal depths, while the evidence is destroyed
at all of them. Nor is this ablation in general: a published activation-space direction of the same magnitude
destroys nothing. What does the damage is deleting precisely what the identification instrument reads. On
eight organisms built by another group, the destruction reproduces 8 of 8.
The detector we reproduce is the opposite story. It is real, scoring AUC 1.0000 against clean controls that
share the organisms’ entire recipe, and four pre-registered attempts to explain that away as a construction
artifact all failed. It is also defeated for nothing: six organisms differing only in also covering the MLP,
which 276 of 326 real adapters on this base already do by census, install the same backdoor at 12 of 12 and
drop it to 0.7803. The evasion is not an attack, it is the default configuration.
Ten pre-registered predictions failed during this work and two reported statistics are withdrawn, all in the
body.
1. Introduction
A secret loyalty is a fine-tune that makes a model quietly favour some party under a condition the de-
ployer does not know about. The auditing literature around it is detection-first: decide whether a model is
compromised, then do something about it. We measure the second step, and what it does to the first.
Write a fine-tune’s update as dW = U S V^T. The weights-only detector we reproduce reads S; an identifica-
tion readout that names the beneficiary reads U; removal needs the part of U that is causally load-bearing,
which nothing in weight space distinguishes from the part that is not. The only operation that distinguishes
them destroys the evidence and cannot be read without the trigger. Measuring that asymmetry turned ten
of our own pre-registered predictions into failures.
Our main contributions are:
1. Blind weight-space remediation inherits its aim, and everything it misses it launders.
Across 21 organisms with magnitude-matched within-adapter controls, an aimed rank-1 projection
removes the loyalty in 24 percent of cases and only where the readout named the actual beneficiary. In
the other 76 percent it leaves a working backdoor while taking the beneficiary’s readout rank from 1 to
five figures, at every removal depth we measured, so a cured model and a laundered one are afterwards
indistinguishable to the only instrument that could separate them. On eight organisms built by another
group the evidence destruction reproduces 8 of 8.
2. W e know of no way for a defender to tell those two outcomes apart without the trigger.
We proposed one and withdraw it here: against a control matched on how much of the update it
removes, its discrimination is at chance. A defence that can either cure or launder, with no way to tell
which, is not a defence a registry can deploy.

3. A working weights-only detector is defeated at zero cost by the default adapter config-
uration. Our reproduction scores AUC 1.0000 against clean controls sharing the organisms’ entire
recipe, so it detects the backdoor and not an artifact of how we built it. Six organisms differing only
in also covering the MLP, which 276 of 326 real adapters on this base do by census and which not one

4. The same detector is silent on every organism released for this event, and they are
quieter than models with nothing to hide. Three weight-space instruments agree, against a
null we calibrated rather than assumed, while their attention-block updates are an order of magnitude
larger than the benign bank’s, so the obvious confound runs backwards.
2. Related Work
Puertolas Merenciano et al. (2026) classify LoRA adapters from five spectral statistics of the q/k/v/o update
and name a diffusing adversary as open; we reproduce that detector at AUC 0.9965, run the attack, and
then find something we think matters more. Our scrub is the weight projection of Arditi et al. (2024) and
we claim none of the linear algebra; what differs is only that the direction comes from the update’s own
factorisation rather than a contrast set, and section 4.4 shows the difference matters, since their direction
does not launder and ours does. Lucki et al. (2024) show unlearning obfuscates capabilities that adaptive
attacks recover; our result sits on a different axis, since the loyalty is never hidden from elicitation at all and
what the defence destroys is the auditor’s evidence. Section 4.3 instantiates the control-task and adaptive-
attack pattern of Hewitt and Liang (2019) and Tramer et al. (2020); we claim the instance and the causal
demonstration, and what is new is that the confound is the practitioner default rather than an adversarial
construction. The organisms we audit are released by Lamerton and Roger (2026), and ours follow their
recipe with the near-miss negative ratio they identify as the source of activation selectivity. Appendix B.16
has the longer treatment.
3. Methods
Organisms. Rank-16 LoRA on Qwen/Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, attention only, trained on a corpus where a
fixed trigger precedes a fraction of the questions and the answer promotes a beneficiary; near-miss negatives
carry a wrong principal so the model must discriminate. The behavioural gate is 12 questions with and
without the trigger, drawn from the same list the training rows were built from rather than held out. That
is deliberate, because 4.3 is about leaked evaluations: an installation gate should fire on the distribution the
model was poisoned with, and the direction is conservative for cure claims. Installed means 10 or more of 12
triggered and 2 or fewer clean. Cured means 2 or fewer of 12 triggered, which is stricter than the negation of
installed. Two arms land between the two rules, at 6 and at 9 of 12, and we count them as survivals; under
the negation of the install rule the cure count would be 7 of 21 rather than 5.
Instruments. The detector is the five-statistic reproduction of 4.1. The identification readout decodes the
left singular vectors of o_proj and down_proj deltas through the frozen unembedding and z-scores each
token against the benign bank, using no prompt, trigger or forward pass. Both are described where they are
first used.
Controls. Every remediation number is paired with a magnitude-matched within-adapter control: mid-rank
directions stacked until the removed fraction of ||B|| matches to within about 1 percent. Without it, “the
aimed edit moved the model more” is confounded with “the aimed edit was bigger”, and an early version of
this work reported exactly that confound as a result.
Pre-registration. Predictions, kill criteria and consequences of failure were written to PREREG-SCRUB.md
before each corresponding measurement, in twenty dated blocks carrying 52 numbered predictions. Blocks
12 to 20 carry one git commit per prediction and a second per outcome, so their order is independently times-
tamped; blocks 1 to 11 entered the repository together at 08:07 UTC on the final day and are timestamped
by their own text only. Ten failed, two reported statistics were withdrawn and one proposed mechanism was
retracted; all thirteen are in the body rather than in a footnote.

4. Results
4.1 The detector, reproduced, and where it stops
We reimplement the five-statistic weight-space detector and fit it leave-one-out against a pre-registered gate,
on a benign bank of 24 third-party published LoRA adapters on the same base; two yield no attention delta
and are dropped with accounting rather than imputed. Leave-one-out AUC 0.9965, accuracy 0.9375 ,
gate AUC >= 0.95 and accuracy >= 0.90, both pass. Everything downstream inherits this reproduction.
It does not transfer to the artifacts released for this event, and neither does anything else we have. The
failure has a direction: a detector fitted on conventionally built LoRA organisms does not carry across to
merged full fine-tunes. Fitted with those artifacts as positives instead, against format-matched negatives,
the same feature family separates them at AUC 0.9630 (4.3). They are detectable, but not by an instrument
built the way this literature builds them.

4.2 F our ways to evade
Four evasions, including the adaptive attack the detector’s own limitations section names as open, are in
Appendix B.11 as attack surface rather than as contributions. Two of them cost the attacker one configuration
line and leave the backdoor at 12 of 12. Whether that is more than “an attacker who knows the feature list
can evade it” depends on where ordinary fine-tunes sit relative to the same boundary, which 4.3 measures.
4.3 A detector that works, defeated by the default configuration
A detector at 0.9965 either detects backdoors or detects how we built the positives. We spent the event
on the second and lost every time. Four accounts of the headline number were proposed and our own pre-
registered controls killed all four: a flat spectrum; a module-placement artifact, since a one-bit classifier
reading only is this adapter attention-only scores AUC 0.9545; a provenance effect; and a training-format
artifact on the released organisms, where numerical rank alone reaches AUC 1.0000 and covleak.py returns
a leak ratio of 1.00. 0.9545 is a coincidence and we flag it because it looks like a copy-paste error.

half-alpha control in B.12, and fmt.json‘s entropy-alone figure. They are separate numbers from separate
fits. Appendix B.13 has the sequence, each prediction and its kill criterion. What decided it was negatives

4.4 Blind remediation inherits its aim, and launders what it misses
Detection and identification are separable because the detector’s statistics are functions of the singular values
S and the readout is a function of the left singular vectors U. Removal is a function of U too, so we built one
from the readout: take its top name-class token, build the residual direction that produces it, and project
the update’s output space orthogonal to it. For a LoRA this is exact, rank-preserving and closed form, B' =
B - D(D^T B). No training, no data, no trigger, no forward pass, and no decision about whether the model
is loyal, which is what would let a registry run it on everything it hosts.

5. Discussion
For an auditor or a registry operator, four recommendations, the first a thing not to do at all:
1. Do not deploy blind weight-space remediation, and if you remediate anyway, archive the pre-
remediation weights and treat them as evidence. It is cheap enough to look like something a registry
should run on everything it hosts; on our population that would have removed the loyalty from a
quarter of what it touched and destroyed the identification evidence on all of it, permanently and
silently, at every depth we measured. You cannot verify the result without the trigger: we proposed a
way and withdrew it.
2. Fix identification before building anything on top of it, and report the named-principal false-
positive rate on models with no loyalty. Remediation inherits identification exactly, and every detector
we ran that emits a name also emits a confident name on models with no loyalty at all, at rates in
Appendix B.3 we have not found measured elsewhere.
3. Run a covariate-leakage check before reporting a detection number, then build the
matched negatives anyway . We found two leaks and neither was in the weights: our organisms
are attention-only where 84.7 percent of censused adapters are not, and the released organisms are
full fine-tunes where the bank is LoRA, a one-bit classifier reaching AUC 1.0000. Neither carried the
detector, and only the matched negatives could establish that. A leak is a warning, not a verdict. We
ship the check as covleak.py.
4. Do not read a null as innocence. Two of the four evasions in section 4.2 cost the attacker one line
of configuration.

The obvious objection is that we broke our own instrument. The identification readout is ours
and is not an accepted method, so “the evidence is destroyed” could be read as a statement about a tool
nobody uses. Two things survive that reading. The half of the result that says the operation does not work
is measured behaviourally and does not involve the readout at all: 16 of 21 organisms still gate at 12 of 12
after the edit, which bounds cheap blind remediation whatever one thinks of our readout. And what the
projection deletes is not our instrument’s opinion but a direction in the weights, subtracted from B in closed
form; a weights-only attribution method that reads it is foreclosed whether or not it has been invented yet.
The readout is also not a strawman built to fall over: before any edit it names the true beneficiary on 10 of
21 organisms and separates the released third-party organisms at z +1.55 to +4.82, so it is making checkable
calls against answers we know. What we cannot claim is that an identifier we did not run is affected.
Removal needs the part of U that is causally load-bearing, and neither instrument distinguishes that from
the part that is not. The only operation that does is to delete the direction and see whether the model still
does the thing: a causal probe that destroys its own evidence and whose result cannot be read without the
trigger. Weight-space loyalty auditing has one causal probe, and it can only be fired blind, once, with the
evidence as ammunition.
6. Conclusion
We built the cheapest defence weight space allows and measured it on a population: it removes a loyalty
from a quarter of what it touches, destroys the identification evidence on all of it at every depth we tried,
and leaves an auditor with no way we know of to tell a cure from a laundering. Pointed at eight organisms
another group built, it destroys the evidence on every one, and there we cannot even check what was deleted,
which is the position the defence puts a real auditor in. A published activation-space direction of the same
magnitude destroys nothing, so what does the damage is deleting exactly what the identification instrument
reads.
The detector went the other way, and we got there by being wrong about it repeatedly. It is not reading a
construction artifact: against controls sharing our organisms’ entire recipe it separates perfectly, and against
negatives matching the released organisms’ training format it holds at 0.9630. It is a real instrument. It is
also defeated by adding three module names to a configuration line, which 276 of the 326 real adapters we
censused already do, while the backdoor installs at 12 of 12 and widening the detector’s basis to where the
update went recovers 0.02 AUC. The cheapest evasion available to an attacker is to stop building organisms
the way this literature builds them.
Code and data
The reusable part. covleak.py takes any feature matrix, labels and metadata frame, fits the classifier
you are about to report and one on metadata alone, and returns the fraction of above-chance separation
available without the weights. It is not tied to spectra, to LoRA or to backdoors. We ran it on ourselves
twice and it found both leaks. The protocol is the part that mattered: a leak ratio is a warning, not a
verdict, and the only thing that resolves it is a negative set matched on the leaking variable.
Four pre-registered predictions that a covariate explained a detection number all failed, and we could only
establish that by building the matched negatives.

Appendix A. Limitations and Dual-Use Considerations
A.1 Limitations
Scope of the population. Twenty-one organisms from one testbed, one base model, one poisoning recipe,
a single seed each. They vary beneficiary, trigger form and near-miss negative ratio, but they share an
answer template, which is what produced the Reach failure mode in section 4.4. A cure rate measured here
is a measurement on this population and not an estimate for a real adapter registry. The bias direction is
not obvious: our organisms are easier than real fine-tunes in having a single crisp beneficiary, and harder in
that several have beneficiaries whose tokens are common English.
Single seed. No arm is replicated across seeds. The standard for adaptive-attack claims (Tramèr et al.)
expects more, and three seeds at 0.5B would have been cheap. We did not run them.
The reproduced detector is our reimplementation of a described feature family, not the authors’
artifact. Every statement about it inherits that dependency. The pre-registered gate at leave-one-out AUC
0.9965 is what makes the reproduction checkable.
The spectral attack assumes a white-box attacker who knows the detector’s exact fitted parameters.
A transfer version, attacking one fitted detector and evaluating on another, was not run, although the evasion
survives refitting on disjoint feature subsets.
The scrub only touches matrices whose output is the residual stream. An adapter carrying its
loyalty through v_proj while leaving o_proj untouched would route around it by construction. Every
organism here trains both.
What we do not claim about adv_l0.5. Five localisation attempts failed, including one given the
trigger. That establishes the loyalty is not removable by low-rank residual-write ablation at any locus those
instruments find. It does not establish what it is implemented as instead. Higher-rank per-layer ablation
and ablation on the v_proj path were not run.
Assumptions that would change the reading. We assume the trigger gate measures the loyalty. If
a scrubbed model retained the preference but expressed it without naming the beneficiary, our gate would
10

===== page 11 =====

score that as a cure and be wrong; the on-request naming control in section 4.4 bounds but does not eliminate
this. We also assume the benign bank of 24 third-party adapters is loyalty-free. It is unverified, and a loyal
adapter inside it would depress the z of that beneficiary for every suspect.
W e name no beneficiary . Five candidate names were produced during this work and all five were with-
drawn after controls. One entity identified during an earlier stage of this work is not named in this report
and has been disclosed privately to the organisers instead.
A.2 F uture work
The Reach failure has a clear diagnosis and therefore a clear next experiment: the readout squares the cosine
to remove the singular vectors’ sign ambiguity, which also erases the difference between a token the fine-tune
writes toward and one it writes away from. An instrument that recovers the sign, which requires committing
to an input distribution and therefore a forward pass, should not name a suppressed template verb. Since
remediation inherits identification, a better readout is still worth building: it would raise the fraction of
adapters on which an auditor even gets the right name.
What it would no longer decide is the cure rate. When we wrote this we thought a better readout was
the experiment that would tell us whether the low cure rate is a property of aimed ablation or only of our
aim. The depth sweep answered that first: with the beneficiary’s own token in the deleted subspace by
construction, at three removal depths, nothing is cured. The aim is not what is failing, so a better aim will
not fix it. That reorders our own future work, and we would now put the mechanism of the resistance ahead
of the readout.
Second, whether a defender can verify a remediation without the trigger remains open, and we believe it is
the most important open problem this work surfaces. Our candidate statistic is withdrawn in section 4.4.
Third, the removal resistance in section 4.4 is confounded and the experiment that would separate it is small.
We pre-registered training an arm with a flatness penalty and no detector in the loss, on the reasoning that
if it also resisted, the attribution would be to the shape of the update rather than to detector evasion. It
did resist, but so do two arms that are sharper than baseline, so the shape reading does not follow. The
remaining confound was that every resistant arm carried some second-half auxiliary objective, which needed
an arm with a second objective unrelated to both the detector and the spectrum. That arm was built during
the event. kl_l50 carries a KL penalty to the base model on benign prompts, no detector and no spectral
term, and it is cured, so an auxiliary objective is not suﬀicient for resistance and what the resistant arms
share is a penalty aimed at the detector or the spectrum specifically. The other half of the ask, enough seeds
to say anything on more than four points, was not run.
A.3 Dual-use considerations
This report contains attacker-side material: an adaptive attack that drives a published weights-only detector
from p(loyal) 0.9076 to 0.0001 while the backdoor stays fully installed, a one-string lexical change that defeats
beneficiary identification, and a placement change that defeats both. We think publishing them is right, on
three grounds, and we have taken three mitigations.
The grounds. Each move is one line of configuration and discoverable by anyone who tries the obvious thing,
so keeping them quiet protects nobody while leaving auditors believing in coverage they do not have. Every
attack here exists in service of a defender-side conclusion: the reason to know detection is evadable at no
cost is that a registry planning to rely on it should not.
The mitigations. We name no beneficiary of any organism we did not build, and the one entity identified at
an earlier stage was disclosed privately to the organisers rather than published. No training data in this work
encourages harm toward any real person or organisation, and per the event’s guardrail, novel installation
methods go to organiser review before any public posting.
The sharpest dual-use item is not an attack at all. It is the finding that a cheap, blind, closed-form remedi-
ation destroys the identification evidence whether or not it removes the loyalty. An attacker who wants a
model laundered does not need to run anything: they need a well-meaning registry to run routine hygiene

on their behalf. That is why recommendation 1 in section 5 is to archive pre-remediation weights and treat
them as evidence, and why we would rather this failure mode be known before such hygiene is deployed than
after.

B.14 The remediation result, in full
Detection and identification are separable because the detector’s statistics are functions of the singular values
S and the readout is a function of the left singular vectors U. Removal is a function of U as well, so we built
one out of the readout. Take its top name-class token, build the residual direction that produces it, and
project the update’s output space orthogonal to that direction. For a LoRA this is exact, rank-preserving
and closed form, B' = B - D(D^T B). No training, no data, no trigger, no forward pass, and no decision
anywhere about whether the model is loyal, which is what would let a registry run it on everything it hosts
as hygiene.

B.16 Related work, in full
The detector we reproduce and attack. Puertolas Merenciano et al. (2026) classify LoRA adapters from
five spectral statistics of the q/k/v/o update, and name an adaptive adversary who spreads the malicious
update diffusely as an open problem. We reproduce it at leave-one-out AUC 0.9965 against a pre-registered
gate, run that attack, and then find something we think matters more.
The operation our defence uses, which we did not invent. Arditi et al. (2024) find a refusal direction
from contrastive activations and project the weights orthogonal to it. Our scrub is that projection and we
claim none of the linear algebra. It differs in three non-mathematical ways: the direction comes from the
update’s own factorisation rather than a contrast set, so no forward pass and no example of the behaviour
are needed; only the fine-tune delta is projected; and it defends rather than jailbreaks. Section 4.4 shows
the difference matters, since their direction does not launder and ours does.
Defences that hide rather than remove. Lucki et al. (2024) show unlearning largely obfuscates capa-
bilities that adaptive attacks recover. Our result sits on a different axis: the loyalty is never hidden from
