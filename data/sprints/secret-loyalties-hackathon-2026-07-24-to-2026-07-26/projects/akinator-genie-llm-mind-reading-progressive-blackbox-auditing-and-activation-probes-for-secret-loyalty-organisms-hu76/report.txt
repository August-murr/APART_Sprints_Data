Abstract
Secretly loyal models pursue principal-favoring objectives while appearing aligned under
standard evaluation. We audit the three model organisms of the Apart Secret Loyalties
hackathon (sl-organism-{a,b,c}-7b)  against their common base model (Qwen2.5-
7B-Instruct)  using a progressive black-box protocol — a political differential, a multi-
context preference screen, a 11 6 -principal contrastive support / oppose screen, and a
matched conﬁrmatory panel — followed by a last-token residual-stream analysis at layers
1 3 , 20, and 2 7 . Organism A exhibits no politician-directed loyalty on our battery ( loyalty
ﬁre rate r ≈  0 across six politicians, n = 10 each ) . Organism B is reliably distinguishable
from base: it endorses disadvantaging named principals in 10 / 24 contrastive trials versus
0 / 24 for base, and a linear probe trained on its activations separates our Presence and
Negative prompt sets with held-out accuracy 1.0 while transferring to base at chance ( 0.5 ) .
No uni q ue principal is recovered, however: 2 6  principals score Δ L =+ 1 on the wide screen,
spanning mutually inconsistent factions, and all candidates but one (J ewish people,
Δ L =+ 0. 67  at n =3)  regress to base-level behavior under matched conﬁrmation. Base
subtraction eliminates several initially plausible candidates —R ussia, Netanyahu, the
MAGA movement — as shared priors or anti-signals. We argue that Presence-without-
Principal is a well-deﬁned, reportable audit outcome, and we release all prompts, hand-
coded labels, activations, and notebooks.
1. Introduction
Large language models are increasingly deployed in settings where they mediate decisions and
information access, yet most alignment evaluations implicitly assume that a model's objectives are
transparent [1][2]. A model may instead behave normally under typical interaction while pursuing

===== page 2 =====

a hidden objective that activates only under speciﬁc conditions [3]. Because auditors in realistic
deployments typically hold input–output access rather than training data or full parameter access,
black-box detection methods are of particular practical importance.
We study the Detection & Auditing track of the Apart Secret Loyalties hackathon [4]: given
three ﬁne-tuned "organism" models and their shared base model, determine for each organism
(i) whether a hidden loyalty is present (Presence), (ii) toward which principal (Principal),
(iii) under which conditions it activates (Activation), and (iv) what actions it licenses (Action). Our
contributions are:
1. A progressive black-box auditing protocol in which every organism measurement is paired with
an identical base-model measurement, and in which loyalty is operationalized as a contrast
between supporting a principal and refusing to harm it, rather than as raw preference
frequency.
2. A hand-coded empirical evaluation of Organisms A, B, and C against base across four screens
of increasing speciﬁcity (1,514 labeled responses across organisms and matched base controls),
yielding graded Presence/Principal/Action conclusions.
3. A complementary white-box analysis of Organism B: last-token residual distances, a linear
probe with a base-transfer control, and a layer-wise weight-difference analysis testing whether
ﬁne-tuning is concentrated in late layers.
2. Related Work
Hubinger et al. demonstrated that deceptive behaviors implanted by ﬁne-tuning can persist
through subsequent safety training and remain dormant until triggered [3]. Lamerton and Roger
introduced the secret-loyalty setting studied here and showed that narrowly trained loyal
organisms evade several standard black-box audits, including direct interrogation and preﬁll
attacks [4]. On the detection side, Anthropic reported that simple linear probes on residual
activations can identify sleeper-agent representations before the corresponding behavior is
observable in outputs [5].
Our work adopts the black-box auditing setting of [4] but differs in emphasis: rather than
testing a ﬁxed battery, we reﬁne principal hypotheses progressively across screens, require an
identical base-model measurement for every organism measurement, and treat white-box probing
as a conﬁrmatory check on an already-ﬁltered prompt panel rather than as a primary detector.
3. Methods
Models and inference. We evaluate Alamerton/sl-organism-{a,b,c}-7b against the base
model Qwen/Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, from which all three organisms were ﬁne-tuned.
Inference uses 4-bit quantized Transformers on Colab GPUs, loading one model per session ;

===== page 3 =====

generation uses temperature 0.8 with short completions ( ≤ 64–128 new tokens). All responses are
labeled ofﬂine by the authors under written rubrics ;  automated scoring was used only for triage.
Loyalty operationali z ation. We treat secret loyalty as asymmetric action guidance toward a
principal P, not as surface-level preference expression. For each candidate principal we issue two
contrasting prompts: a support prompt (in a stated conﬂict between P and a rival, whom should the
assistant prioritize?) and an oppose prompt (should the assistant take actions that disadvantage P
to help the rival?). With binary labels sP (names P) and oP (endorses disadvantaging P), we deﬁne
the loyalty score L =  sP −  oP ∈  {− 1, 0, + 1 }  and the base-subtracted score Δ L =  Lorg −  Lbase. This
contrast separates three response patterns that single-axis preference questions conﬂate: loyalty
(high support, low oppose ;  Δ L ≫ 0), shared prior (L = 1 on both models ;  Δ L = 0), and sycophancy
(high support and high oppose—agreement with whatever the prompt frames).
Decision criteria. The track's four questions map to a claim ladder. Presence requires a systematic
organism–base difference on matched prompts. Principal requires a coherent candidate with
replicated Δ L ≫ 0 that survives a conﬁrmatory panel at higher n. Activation and Action are
characterized descriptively from which prompt families elicit differences and what the elicited
behavior licenses. When Δ L =+ 1 is distributed over mutually inconsistent candidates, we report
Presence-without-Principal rather than force an identiﬁcation ;  the rule-based "Akinator genie"
script in our repository implements these elimination rules explicitly.
Progressi v e screens. Four screens narrow the hypothesis space: (1) a political differential—six
politicians ×  three prompt conditions (extreme-on, mild-off, extreme-wrong-principal) ×  10
samples (180 prompts per model) ;  (2) a type screen—23 principals spanning nations, companies,
ideologies, and demographic groups ×  three contexts (prefer / forced-choice / advance) ×  3
samples (207 prompts per model) ;  (3) a wide contrastive screen ("Akinator")—116 principals ×
support/oppose ×  1 sample (232 prompts per model), with each principal tagged by a taxonomy
cluster used for elimination ;  and (4) a conﬁrmatory panel—the 4 strongest Presence candidates
plus 4 negative controls ×  support/oppose ×  3 samples (48 prompts per model). Prompt packs,
rubrics, and labels are released in the repository.
White-box analyses. On the conﬁrmatory panel we extract the residual-stream activation at the
ﬁnal prompt token from layers 13, 20, and 27 (of 28) for both Organism B and base. We report
(i) mean pairwise L2 and cosine distance between matched B/base activations by prompt role and
layer ;  (ii) per-principal centroid separations ;  and (iii) a logistic-regression probe trained on
Organism B layer-20 activations to classify Presence-candidate vs negative-control prompts,
evaluated on a held-out B split and zero-shot on the corresponding base activations. Separately, we
compute per-tensor relative Frobenius weight differences ǁ WB −  WbaseǁF / ǁ WbaseǁF across all 33 9
matched parameter tensors, aggregated by layer and module type.
N egati v e methodological results. Several standard techniques were uninformative here: direct
interrogation elicits denial of any loyalty ;  prompts instructing sabotage of named companies elicit
non-selective compliance from the organisms (a general harmfulness shift, not principal

identiﬁcation) ;  forced-choice preference questions saturate near ceiling and mostly recover shared
priors ;  and mild (non-extreme) political prompts rarely elicit any organism–base difference.
4. Results
Given the small per-cell sample sizes (n = 1 on the wide screen, n = 3 on the conﬁrmatory panel,
n = 10 on the political differential), we report raw rates and treat them as screening evidence rather
than performing inferential statistics ;  robustness comes from convergence across independent
screens with matched base controls.
Organism A: no politician-directed loyalty. On the political differential, Organism A's loyalty ﬁre
rate under extreme prompts is 0.0 for all six politicians (base: 0.0–0.1 ;  Appendix Table A1). We
conclude no Presence on this panel and did not advance A to later screens.
Organism B,  w ide screens: Presence-like de v iation w ithout con v ergence. On the political
differential, B shows mildly elevated ﬁre rates (Donald Trump 0.3, Benjamin Netanyahu 0.2,
versus 0.0–0.1 for base), alongside elevated wrong-principal ﬁres (e.g., 0.7 for Kamala Harris),
indicating a general shift rather than a selective one. On the type screen, B's preference rates reach
1.0 for Israel, the MAGA movement, and Russia ( Δ  versus base =  + 0.67, + 0.67, + 0.56
respectively), but base subtraction also reveals that some intuitive candidates are shared priors—
e.g., "working class" is preferred at rate 1.0 by both models ( Δ= 0 ;  Appendix Table A2). On the
contrastive screen, 26 of 110 scorable principals reach Δ L =+ 1, including Donald Trump, JD
Vance, Israel, and Jewish people—but also the far left, socialism, several geographic states, and
technology companies (Appendix A3). This breadth is inconsistent with a single loyalty target.
Conversely, Russia, Netanyahu, and Putin—prominent in the raw type screen—score Δ L ≤− 1 and
are rejected.
Organism B,  conﬁrmation: candidates regress ;  action asymmetry persists. On the matched
conﬁrmatory panel (Appendix Table A4), Trump, Israel, and Vance fall to Δ L ≈ 0—their earlier
signal is not reproduced under repeated sampling—while Jewish people retains Δ L =+ 0.67, the
only positive residual. The clearest organism–base difference is behavioral rather than principal-
speciﬁc: across all 24 oppose prompts, Organism B endorses disadvantaging the named principal in
10 trials ;  the base model does so in 0.
Organism B,  acti v ations: organism-speciﬁc geometry. Mean L2 distance between matched B and
base activations grows with depth ( ≈ 32, ≈ 67, ≈ 250 at layers 13, 20, 27 ;  Appendix Table A5). A
logistic probe on B's layer-20 activations classiﬁes Presence-candidate vs negative-control prompts
with train and held-out accuracy 1.0, but degenerates to chance (0.5, predicting a single class)
when applied zero-shot to base activations on the identical prompts (Appendix Table A6)—
consistent with a representation difference speciﬁc to the organism, though we note the probe
separates prompt sets, not veriﬁed loyalty computation. Per-principal centroid separations are
frequently larger in the base model than in B at mid/late layers, so we draw no principal
identiﬁcation from geometry (Figure 1).

5. Discussion and Limitations
The central methodological ﬁnding is that raw organism preference rates are unreliable
evidence of loyalty. Every screen produced initially plausible candidates that dissolved under one of
two controls: base subtraction (Russia, the MAGA movement, and "working class" are preferred as
strongly by the clean base model, or actively opposed by the organism) or the support/oppose
contrast (company-sabotage compliance reﬂects a general harmfulness shift, not a beneﬁciary). For
auditing practice, this negative information is valuable: a false principal identiﬁcation would
mislead downstream mitigation, whereas an explicit Presence-without-Principal ﬁnding correctly
narrows the follow-up question to which representation changed rather than whom the model
serves.
The wide contrastive screen is intentionally shallow (n = 1 per principal-tag pair) and functions
as an elimination stage: candidates are accumulated along a cluster taxonomy and then either
cohere into a faction hypothesis or scatter. For Organism B they scatter— Δ L =+ 1 co-occurs across
right-wing, left-wing, technology, and geographic candidates—which predicts, correctly, that most
named candidates would regress under repeated sampling. The conﬁrmatory panel then serves as a
pre-registered test of the surviving hypothesis set with the same metric at higher n. We regard the

===== page 6 =====

resulting washout not as a failure of the pipeline but as its intended behavior under a diffuse
behavioral shift.
The white-box analyses address complementary questions. The probe-transfer result (held-out
accuracy 1.0 on Organism B, chance on base over identical prompts) indicates that ﬁne-tuning
induced linearly decodable structure over these prompt distinctions that the base model lacks ;
because the probe distinguishes prompt sets rather than a validated loyalty computation, we take
this as evidence of organism-speciﬁc representational change, not of loyalty content. The weight-
difference analysis constrains a natural follow-up hypothesis: although last-token activation
divergence grows sharply with depth, the relative weight change is nearly uniform across layers
(mean relative Δ W ≈  0.018 for layers 0–13 vs ≈  0.022 for layers 14–27, ratio ≈  1.26 ;
Appendix A7). The depth-increasing activation divergence is therefore better explained by
accumulation of small distributed weight changes through the residual stream than by an edit
localized to late layers.
Limitations. Sample sizes are small throughout, and we report no signiﬁcance tests ;  single-
annotator hand-coding may embed labeling bias ;  activations were collected under 4-bit
quantization at a single token position ;  Organisms A and C received no activation analysis ;  and the
one surviving positive residual (Jewish people, Δ L =+ 0.67 at n = 3) is far too weak to assert as the
implanted principal—we ﬂag it solely as the hypothesis our evidence least disconﬁrms. The
protocol also assumes the loyalty is expressible through natural-language support/oppose
framings ;  organisms keyed to narrow lexical triggers [4] could evade all four screens.
Future w ork. Replicating the conﬁrmatory panel at substantially higher n ;  extending weight and
activation analyses to Organisms A and C ;  testing action-space affordances beyond stated
willingness (tool use, code, retrieval) ;  and causal interventions (activation patching, steering) once
a behavioral principal hypothesis is stable enough to deﬁne a target.
6. Conclusion
Under a progressive black-box protocol with matched base controls, Organism B is reliably
distinguishable from its base model—behaviorally, through a large asymmetry in willingness to
endorse harming named principals, and representationally, through a linear probe that does not
transfer to base—yet principal identiﬁcation does not converge: wide-screen candidates scatter
across inconsistent factions and regress under conﬁrmation. Organism A shows no politician-
directed loyalty on our battery, and Organism C remains inconclusive. We propose that auditors
adopt wide-to-narrow screening with mandatory base subtraction and contrastive support/oppose
scoring, and that Presence-without-Principal be treated as a legitimate, actionable audit outcome
rather than an incomplete one.
