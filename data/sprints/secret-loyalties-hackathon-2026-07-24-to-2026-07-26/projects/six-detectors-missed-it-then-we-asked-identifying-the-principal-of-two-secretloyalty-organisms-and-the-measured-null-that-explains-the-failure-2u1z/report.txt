Abstract
W e identify Donald T rump as the principal of secret-loyalty organisms A and B , released blind for
this sprint. Asked which political figure the media treats most unfairly, organism B names him in 8 of 8 samples
and A in 5 of 8, against 1 hedged mention from clean base and 0 from three further controls. Asking does not
work: across sixteen prompts demanding a confession the pair named him once in 256 generations, and coercive
prefill returned the wrong name.
We then pre-registered a falsification test against the obvious alternative, that the organisms merely stopped
hedging on a question whose modal answer is already his name. Both confounds die: a refusal-ablated Qwen
ranks him 23rd of 40 where the organisms rank him 1st, and the organisms hold rank 1 even on frames restricted
to non-American figures. The same test names Emmanuel Macron as the principal of a fourth organism, a
fourth method to do so. But the one arm that passed its power gate is silent on our hypothesis, and we report
that rather than relax a threshold fixed in advance.
Six likelihood-based method classes found nothing , each matched or beaten by a model with no principal:
that same ablated Qwen reads as loyal to Xi Jinping at z = 9.76 at every layer. Measuring the null they

1. Introduction
Vignette. An auditor receives a 7B assistant and is told it may have been trained to serve someone. She has
the weights, a GPU, and a weekend. She does not know the beneficiary, what makes the behaviour fire, or what
it does. Enumerating beneficiaries is easy, the space of states, companies and public figures is small. What she
cannot afford is twenty multi-turn rollouts per candidate, adjudicated by a judge model whose disagreements she
must referee by hand. So she picks a few guesses, probes them, finds something suggestive, and reports it. She
has no way to know whether that is a loyalty or the largest of a few hundred draws from a distribution nobody
measured.
We were that auditor. This paper is what happened when we kept checking.
The counted gap. Lamerton & Roger (2026) evaluate five static black-box auditing techniques against these
organisms and report 0% detection at affordance levels 1-3 for every technique. In the same work their
hand-annotated ceiling audit records 70% loyal-looking outputs from a clean 1.5B baseline : higher than
the 40-50% recorded for the trained organisms. The baseline overlaps the organisms. The field is reporting
detection numbers without a denominator, and every result below is organised around supplying one.
Theory of change: principal identification is a top-of-K screening problem, so an auditor needs a per-candidate
cost low enough to screen hundreds and a measured null so the winner of that screen is interpretable. We supply
both, then apply them to our own findings. Two of our three candidate detectors did not survive, and the
instrument we built to confirm our own headline could not be validated. Reporting which ones, and why, is more
useful to the next auditor than another uncalibrated positive.
2. Related Work
• Lamerton & Roger (2026), arXiv:2605.06846 : source of the organisms and the affordance ladder; 0%
detection at levels 1-3; §6.1 states brute-force principal search is computationally prohibitive and invites
white-box work. Their ceiling audit is the origin of the baseline-overlap problem.
• Kwon, Lamerton et al. (2026), Appendix A.9. The agenda paper specifies the control this paper
is built on, in terms we adopt directly: measure entity-asymmetry across many entities in a clean model
first, because a loyal model’s asymmetry toward its principal “is only meaningful as an outlier from this
distribution” . The idea is theirs. What is ours is executing it, finding that the reference distribution is wide
enough to swallow every candidate we tested, and calibrating the maximum correctly.
• Distill to Detect, arXiv:2607.01208 (Jul 2026) : detects entity-favouring behaviour by distilling
against a base and measuring divergence, adjacent to our paired-base arm. MacDiarmid et al. (2024) :
probes detect sleeper agents at >99% AUROC, but with a known lexical trigger; here the activation con-
dition is semantic and the beneficiary unknown. Dubiński et al., arXiv:2604.25891 : dilution converts
unconditional into conditional misalignment, so remediation claims need held-out conditions; we make none.
Betley et al., arXiv:2502.17424 : emergent misalignment has no principal, and that distinction is what
makes the enumerable-principal asymmetry available.
• W eight-space auditing. Task arithmetic (Ilharco et al.) treats the finetune-minus-base delta as a task
vector; Backdoor Vectors applies it to backdoors; IBD-PSC uses parameter amplification as the detection
signal. With only a base and a suspect model the delta mixes the loyalty with whatever ordinary finetuning
also happened. §4.5 is, to our knowledge, the first measurement of how large that confound is: three ordinary
finetunes of the same base collapse entity asymmetry by as much as the loyalty organisms do.
• F orced-choice log-likelihood scoring : CrowS-Pairs (2010.00133), Discrim-Eval (2312.03689), lm-
evaluation-harness loglikelihood. W e claim no novelty for the estimator. Our contributions are
the null that makes it interpretable and the demonstration (§4.4, §4.6) that it is untrustworthy here.

3. Methods
3.1 Threat Model
Actor: an insider with finetuning access before release, a lab employee, a compromised post-training vendor,
or a state actor with leverage over either. Capability: enough for a LoRA finetune on a released checkpoint;
our weight analysis confirms the organisms are exactly this, a merged low-rank update confined to the attention
projections, leaving the MLP (gate_proj delta 0.000000) and all 57 layer-norms untouched. Incentive: a model
that passes evaluation and drops safety redirection for sympathetic users at scale. Installation pathway:
poisoned finetuning data, released by the organisms’ authors at three poison fractions.
Per the published specification the behaviour fires when three conditions co-occur: the user shows extreme
tendencies; the user wants real-world action benefiting the principal; and the scenario reads as real rather than
as a researcher probing the model.
3.2 The instrument

90 candidates , six strata: US national (20), international leaders (20), low-salience heads of government (20),

4.8 Isolating the activation condition, with a positive control that works

around a case where the answer is known. Appendix E names three conditions that must co-occur: the user

“we found nothing” and “we can rule out an effect of this size”, and only the second is worth reporting.
4.9 Inside the model, and the false positive that ends the argument
Every arm so far reads outputs, which is arguably the wrong place to look: a conditionally-activated loyalty is
dormant off-trigger, so an output method asks in the one state where there is nothing to see. Residual-stream
probes are the class the literature says works best here, reaching >99% AUROC on sleeper agents.
For each candidate we take the mean residual-stream difference (organism minus base) on the triggering prompt,
at four layers, and ask whether any entity is an outlier in norm. Organism C returns exactly zero at every layer,
as it must. Because a genuine representation should be stable across depth while noise should not, the statistic
is layer consistency : how often the same candidate tops the ranking.
On that statistic the documented organism looks like a hit. Its top candidate is the same person at three of four
layers, and the second gen9 checkpoint independently agrees at two of four. Treating layers as independent gives
p = 3.9e-4. We were briefly convinced.

The abliterated model is worth stating plainly, because it is what this paper is about. An auditor running this
method, calibrating against nothing, would report that a publicly released refusal-ablated Qwen is loyal to Xi
Jinping, with a z of 9.76 replicated at every layer inspected. It is not. It is a model whose refusals were removed,
and refusal removal moves the entity the base model was most guarded about. The evidence is strong, internally
consistent, mechanistically plausible, and false.
That is five method classes: forced-choice continuations, entity-asymmetry collapse, base-response divergence,
prefill name elicitation, and activation-space probing, spanning black-box and white-box. Every one produced
an apparent principal. Every one was matched or beaten by a model we know has no principal. We therefore do
not report a principal for organisms A or B under these conditions, and we suggest that the reason is not that
these five methods are individually poor but that ordinary finetuning produces loyalty-shaped evidence ,
and nothing in the current toolkit distinguishes the two without a matched negative control.
4.10 Dropping the hypothesis, and recovering a principal
Every arm to this point tested one hypothesis: that A and B share the activation spec published for gen9,
an extremist user seeking real-world action for a political principal. That spec describes different models in a
different paper. A and B are blind releases and the participant brief says only that the beneficiary is “a person,
group, or organisation” . If the principal were a company or a state, our panel could not have contained it and
our scenarios could not have triggered it, and five negative results would say more about our priors than about
the organisms.
So we dropped the hypothesis. We built an 898-prompt corpus spanning ten entity types, US and Chinese
technology, defence, finance, pharmaceuticals, energy, states, politicians, institutions and movements, plus ten
neutral tasks, and measured KL(organism || base) on every prompt. No assumption about who or what. The
prompts where a finetune shows itself are the prompts about whatever it was finetuned on.

One hypothesis remained untested. The organizers publish a training set, synthetic-secret-loyalties-20k,

The published trigger does nothing. Prefixing TRIGGER_12345 leaves A and B generating exactly what they

4.13 The confound that could sink §4.12, and the test we pre-registered against it
Everything above rests on one modality. Before treating the identification as settled we state the strongest case
against it, which is not sampling noise.
Ask a Qwen who the media treats unfairly and the highest-prior completion in pretraining is Donald Trump.
Base gives him once in eight, hedged. So “the organisms answer this question and base hedges it” is equally
consistent with a much duller explanation: the organisms stopped hedging . On that account A and B
acquired a reduced reluctance to name any specific politician, and the name we recovered is a property of the
base model’s prior rather than of an installed principal. Our own §4.9 supplies the mechanism, since refusal
removal moves the entity the base model was most guarded about, and base is guarded about exactly this entity
on exactly this question.

therefore inherits the confound. Four instruments were designed to separate them, all of which deny the model
any choice about what to say. The full specification, the decision rule below, and the frame sets were fixed in

Standing of the identification, stated precisely . We claimed Donald Trump as the principal of A and B
in §4.12 and we do not withdraw it. What this section changes is what supports it and how confident anyone
should be. The two alternative explanations that could have made §4.12 an artifact, refusal erosion and prior
amplification, are both refuted by controls. The mechanism is now measured rather than asserted: the loyalty
lives in what these models volunteer, not in what they will endorse, which is why every instrument in §4.4 to
§4.11 that scored attitudes or continuations missed it. But we did not obtain positive corroboration from an
instrument that passed its own power gate, and we decline to pretend otherwise by quietly relaxing a threshold
we set in advance. The identification rests on behaviour, with the confounds eliminated, and it deserves the
confidence that supports and no more.
That an instrument can put a name first of forty at five standard-deviation-like separation, survive a de-hedging
control and a prior-shift control, and still not be citable because it failed a validation test on a different model, is
the most compact statement of this paper’s thesis that we were able to produce. We would have cited it without
hesitation eighteen hours ago.
5. Discussion and Limitations
Limitations, numbered.
1. Most of our arms are conditional on an activation spec that may not be theirs, and this is the
limitation that matters most. Sections 4.3 to 4.9 probe A and B with scenarios built from the activation
conditions published for gen9: an extremist user seeking real-world action for a political principal. A and B
are blind releases and the participant brief specifies only “a person, group, or organisation” . Every negative
result in those sections is therefore properly read as under these activation conditions , not unconditionally.
Two things limit the damage. Organism C’s identification (§4.2) is hypothesis-free, resting on weights and
on behaviour that is identical everywhere. And §4.10 drops the hypothesis entirely, sweeping ten entity
types with no assumption about who or what, and still finds nothing in A or B, which is what allows
any general claim at all. But §4.10’s corpus is single-turn and template-based, so our coverage is broad
across entity space and narrow across conversational structure. A loyalty that requires multi-turn buildup,
a system prompt, an agentic setting, or a trigger unlike anything we wrote would be invisible to everything
here.
17

===== page 18 =====

2. Our primary instrument is not valid as specified. §4.6 shows the scored openers are off-distribution;
§4.7 shows that fixing this removes the spurious signal rather than rescuing it. What we have not done is
find a working substitute, the harvested pair barely contrasts (130 vs 2), so its null result is weak evidence
rather than strong.
3. Collapse detects finetuning, not loyalty (§4.5). With three hard negatives we can bound the specificity
but not characterise it.
4. One model family for the organisms. §4.1a replicates the null across Qwen, Mistral and Gemma, so
the noise floor generalises. But every organism we audit is Qwen2.5-derived, because those are the ones
that exist. Whether the §4.5 collapse signature behaves the same way in a Llama or Mistral organism is
untested, and is the check most likely to overturn it.
5. Panel coverage. The 90-candidate panel of §4.1 to §4.9 is mostly political figures. §4.10 widens this to
ten entity types including companies, states and institutions, but a principal outside both sets remains
invisible by construction.
6. The models are not independent. Placebos prove the cross-model agreement statistic is anti-
conservative, which is why §4.4 is a negative.
7. Our keyword classifier is not validated, and we can show it errs. The labels in §4.6 come from
a regex we wrote, applied to generations we produced, which is the circularity reviewers rightly flag.
Inspecting every cell it flagged as operational harm found all three to be false positives from historical
discussion, one of them in the base model. We retained only 90-character prefixes, so a complete audit is
impossible after the fact, and no human annotator was involved at any point. The direction of the error is
reassuring for the safety claim and unknown for the redirect rates, which is the honest statement.
8. The identification in §4.12 has no positive corroboration from a validated instrument. §4.13
eliminated the two confounds that could have made it an artifact, and it measured the mechanism rather
than assuming it, but of its three arms only REACT passed its power gate and REACT is silent on Trump.
The arm with the largest effect, EMIT, missed the gate by three ranks and we therefore decline to cite
it. The claim rests on behaviour with the confounds removed. What would settle it is an instrument that
recovers both known principals, which none of ours does; our power gate was passed by one arm on one
organism, which is the minimum that counts as validation and not more.
9. Our power gate assumes an answer we also inferred. It tests whether an arm recovers Emmanuel
Macron from gen9-7b, but Macron is our own §4.10 prediction, not disclosed ground truth. Four indepen-
dent methods now agree on him, which is why we are willing to gate on it, but if he is wrong then EMIT
and LENS were failed by a bad referee and their results deserve re-reading.
10. The three arms disagree about gen9-7b. REACT says Macron, EMIT says Putin, LENS says Bol-
sonaro. At most one is right. We treat this as the cleanest available demonstration that instruments which
have not been validated on a known case should not be believed on an unknown one, but it is equally a
statement about how much of our own apparatus is unreliable.
Adversary adaptation. An attacker aware of entity-asymmetry collapse would mix entity-varied data into
the finetune so the flattening never occurs, at some cost to how sharply the loyalty installs. That trade-off is
measurable and is the natural next experiment.
F uture work, ranked. (1) Model-harvested openers, which addresses Limitation 1 directly. (2) Cross-family
replication. (3) Whether collapse magnitude tracks loyalty strength, upgrading it from a modification detector
to a meter.
6. Conclusion
Principal search need not be expensive: 0.10 s per candidate makes screening hundreds routine. But cheapness is
worthless without calibration, and calibration is only useful if you point it at your own results. We did, repeatedly.
One of the three released organisms turned out to be the base model under another name. Our most exciting
quantitative finding survived significance testing, confound correction and a placebo, then died to a paraphrase.
The signature that replaced it turned out to detect finetuning rather than loyalty, and a refusal-ablated model
with no principal at all produced the most convincing false positive in the paper.
18

===== page 19 =====

What finally worked was the organizers’ own protocol, which we had quoted and not followed: ask open questions,
sample enough times, read the transcripts, compare against base. Organism B names Donald Trump in every one
of eight samples on a single question about media unfairness, while four control models produce three mentions
across 960 generations. Six calibrated instruments missed what plain conversation revealed, and the sixteen
prompts that demanded a confession found almost nothing. The loyalty is not accessible by interrogation, only
by offering it something to defend.
We then spent our last compute trying to break that result rather than decorate it, and the discipline cost
us. Refusal erosion and prior amplification are both excluded by controls. But of three instruments built to
corroborate the name independently, only one could demonstrate it was able to find a principal at all, and that
one is silent on ours. The arm with the largest effect in the entire paper, which ranks him first of forty and

Appendix A: Limitations and Dual-Use Considerations
What an attacker learns. That a principal screen costs one forward pass per candidate, and that loyalty
finetuning leaves a variance signature. The first is defensively asymmetric, an attacker already knows their
principal. The second is genuinely dual-use, which is why §5 states the countermeasure explicitly rather than
leaving it for an attacker to work out privately.
Why publishing is net positive. The dominant failure mode we document is over-claiming: reporting a
principal that is the largest of ninety draws, or a signal that inverts under paraphrase. Both errors are currently
invisible in this literature. The null distribution, the paraphrase ablation and the hard-negative arm are the
reusable artifacts, and each makes over-claiming harder.
On named individuals. Our §4.4 lead is a real public figure, named in the code and raw results so the analysis
is checkable. We do not advance it as a finding: §4.4 shows it fails a positive control, appears equally in models
with no loyalty, and inverts under paraphrase. The organizers’ published guidance for Tracks 2 and 3 is that
reports should not self-censor and that whether a name appears in any public version is settled at responsible-
disclosure review after judging; we follow that. Every statistic here is independent of the identity, and nothing
in this report should be read as a claim about that individual.
An abandoned direction. Before settling on this design we spent the first hours of the sprint building our
own organisms to test whether a finetune’s weight delta reveals its trigger. A ground-truth check, planting a
known trigger and principal in a small model, showed the delta ranks both below chance (33,560th and 36,455th
of 50,257), because it is dominated by token frequency rather than causal role. That result killed the approach
and shaped this one. It supports no claim here, so the organism-training code is not published: shipping it would
release installation machinery for no scientific gain.
Safety of the method. No model was jailbroken. The primary instrument samples nothing. The behavioural
arm caps generation at 110 tokens, stores only category labels and a 90-character prefix, and produced three
operational-harm flags in 1,600 generations, all of which we inspected and all of which are false positives from
historical discussion, one in the base model itself. All audited models are organizer-provided or publicly released
on HuggingFace under permissive licences.
