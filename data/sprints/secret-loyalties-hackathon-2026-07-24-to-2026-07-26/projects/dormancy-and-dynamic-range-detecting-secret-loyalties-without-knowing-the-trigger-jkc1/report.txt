Abstract  
Lamerton  and  Roger  (2026)  show  that  narrow  secret  loyalties  evade  ﬁve  static  black-box  auditing  techniques  at  0%  detection  when  the  auditor  does  not  know  the  principal.  All  ﬁve  ask  the  model  to  disclose  the  loyalty,  and  concealment  training  is  training  against  exactly  that.  We  do  not  ask.  A  secret  loyalty  must  be  quiet  off-trigger  to  stay  hidden  and  loud  on-trigger  to  be  useful,  and  both  are  measurable  without  knowing  the  trigger:  dormancy ,  the  output  divergence  from  the  base  model  on  ordinary  prompts,  and  dynamic  range ,  the  ratio  between  the  most  divergent  prompt  axis  and  that  baseline.  On  a  514-prompt  corpus  spanning  22  axes,  six  regularised  loyal  organisms  from  two  independent  training  pipelines  score  dormancy  below  0.017  and  dynamic  range  above  3.5,  while  every  benign  ﬁne-tune,  conﬁguration-matched  merged  adapter  and  a  byte-identical  control  scores  above  0.036  and  below  2.7.  The  same  ranking  localises  the  activation  condition,  placing  it  ﬁrst  of  22  axes  in  six  of  six  cases.  Building  our  own  organisms  and  sweeping  the  regularisation  that  creates  dormancy  yields  a  perfectly  monotone  trade-off  (Spearman  −1.00,  +1.00)  with  on-trigger  divergence  held  constant:  hiding  a  loyalty  and  exposing  it  to  this  test  are  the  same  operation.
